# Surah Ash-Shams (91) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–15)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**One word appears twice — and it means "He shaped a soul" the first time and "He flattened a town" the second.**

⭐ **v.7:** *wa nafsin wa mā ***sawwāhā*** — "By the soul and the One who **PROPORTIONED IT**."
⭐ **v.14:** *fa-damdama ʿalayhim rabbuhum bi-dhanbihim **fa-sawwāhā*** — "so their Lord crushed them for their sin and **LEVELLED IT**."

⭐ **Identical word. *Sawwāhā*.** ⭐ **In v.7 it means *to perfect, to balance, to bring into beautiful proportion*. In v.14 it means *to flatten to the ground, to level, to make even with the dust*.**

⭐ **Same verb builds a soul and razes a civilization.** ⭐ **Because "making even" (*sawwā*) can mean *perfectly balanced* or *perfectly flat* — and which one you get depends on what you did with the soul in v.7.**

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐⭐ **Seven verses apart, the same word. He evened you into a person; He evened them into rubble.** ⭐ **If you remember ONE thing from this surah, make it this.**

---

## The Big Picture

1. **Eleven oaths** (v.1–7) — the longest oath-run in the Qurʾān: ⭐ **sun, its brightness, moon, day, night, sky, earth, and the SOUL.**
2. **The design of the soul** (v.8) — ⭐ *He inspired it with its corruption and its guarding.*
3. **The verdict** (v.9–10) — ⭐ *succeeded who purified it; failed who buried it.*
4. **The case study** (v.11–15) — **Thamūd**, the she-camel, and one man who acted for everyone.

## §1 — Eleven Oaths (v.1–7)

**1. وَضُحَاهَا — *wa ḍuḥāhā*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** the "**forenoon brightness**" family — ***ḍuḥā*** = **mid-morning**, when the sun is well up and everything is fully lit. ⭐ **You know it as *Ṣalāt aḍ-Ḍuḥā* and *Sūrat aḍ-Ḍuḥā*.**
- **Here it means:** "**By the sun and its brightness.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **you own this word from Nāziʿāt** — it appears there **twice**, and its journey is worth holding:
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:29** — *wa akhraja **ḍuḥāhā*** — creation's **daily gift of light**
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:46** — *illā ʿashiyyatan aw **ḍuḥāhā*** — ⭐ **the measure of your ENTIRE LIFE** ("an evening or its morning")
  - **Shams 91:1** — the sun's brightness, sworn by
  
  ⭐ **Same word: a daily gift, the length of a lifetime, and now an oath.**
  
  ⭐ **And note the pairing: the SUN and ITS brightness.** ⭐ **Not two separate things — a thing and **what it gives off**.** ⭐ **That pattern runs through all seven oath-pairs: an object and its function.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **the *wāw* is *wāw al-qasam*** (the "by" of oaths), so the noun is **genitive**. ⭐ **Note the *-hā* suffix returns at the end of EVERY verse in this surah** — all fifteen. **That's the rhyme.**
- **Why this word:** *ḍuḥā* names ⭐ **the brightest, most ordinary part of your day** — evidence you walk through constantly.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **You pray *Ṣalāt aḍ-Ḍuḥā* in this light.** ⭐ **Nāziʿāt 79:29 (creation's gift) → 79:46 (your whole life) → 91:1 (an oath).** ⭐ **And EVERY verse in this surah ends in *-hā*.**

**2. تَلَاهَا — *talāhā*** · v.2
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**following after**" family — *talā* = **to follow, to come after**; ⭐ **and from that: *talā* = TO RECITE** (reciting is following the text word by word!), ⭐ ***tilāwah* = recitation.***
- **Here it means:** "**and by the moon when it follows it.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the double meaning is a gift you use every day.**
  
  ⭐ ***Talā* means both "to follow" AND "to recite."*** ⭐ **Why? Because when you recite, you're **following** the words in sequence.** ⭐ **So *tilāwah* (the word for Qurʾān recitation) literally means "**following along**."**
  
  ⭐ **And here the moon *follows* the sun** — rising as it sets, taking over the shift, ⭐ **borrowing its light** (recall **Nabaʾ 78:13**: the sun is a ***sirāj*** — a **burning lamp** — while the moon merely reflects).
  
  ⭐ **A beautiful thought worth keeping:** ⭐ **the moon *recites* the sun.** ⭐ **It has no light of its own; it faithfully transmits what it received.** ⭐ **Which is exactly what *tilāwah* is.**
- **Grammar bite:** weak-final past verb with attached pronoun *-hā* (referring to the sun). ⭐ **Note *idhā* + past tense = "when it [does]" — a habitual, recurring action.**
- **Why this word:** *talā* over a plain word for "rises" — ⭐ **it makes the moon a **follower**, which is the whole relationship.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Talā* = TO FOLLOW **and** TO RECITE.* ⭐ ***Tilāwah* = "following along."** ⭐ **The moon RECITES the sun — transmitting light it didn't make.**

**3. جَلَّاهَا — *jallāhā*** · v.3
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**making clear / unveiling / polishing to a shine**" family — *jalā* = **to reveal, to make manifest, to burnish**; ⭐ ***tajallī* = a manifestation***; *jaliyy* = **crystal clear**; ⭐ **and *jalāʾ* = polish/burnishing** (what you do to metal to make it gleam).
- **Here it means:** "**and by the day when it displays it.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the verb means "to POLISH into visibility."**
  
  ⭐ **The day doesn't *create* the sun — it ***jallāhā***: **buffs it into full view**, like polishing a mirror until it gleams.** ⭐ **The image is of something being **revealed by removal of what covered it**, not by being made.
  
  ⭐ **And notice the pair with v.4:** ⭐ **the day ***unveils*** (*jallāhā*); the night ***covers*** (*yaghshāhā*).** ⭐ **A perfect *ṭibāq* — reveal and conceal, one verse apart.**
  
  ⭐ **Keep this word: *tajallī*** — ⭐ **it's the word used for divine manifestation** (as when the mountain crumbled at Allah's *tajallī*). Same root.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form II** (*jallā*) = intensive/causative — "**made it fully clear**." Weak-final, with *-hā*.
- **Why this word:** *jallā* over "showed" — ⭐ **it carries the sense of *polishing*, of active display, not passive presence.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***Tajallī* = a MANIFESTATION; *jalāʾ* = POLISH.** ⭐ **The day BUFFS the sun into view.** ⭐ **v.3 unveils, v.4 covers — perfect opposites.**

**4. يَغْشَاهَا — *yaghshāhā*** · v.4
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**covering over / enveloping**" family — *ghashiya* = **to cover completely**; ⭐ ***ghishāwah* = a covering over the eyes.***
- **Here it means:** "**and by the night when it covers it.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **you own this root exactly — it names the previous surah's Day.**
  
  ⭐ **Ghāshiyah 88:1 — *hal atāka ḥadīthu'l-***ghāshiyah*** — "the report of **THE ENVELOPER**."** ⭐ **The Day of Judgment is named with this verb.**
  
  ⭐ **And here it describes ***the night covering the sun*** — something that happens **every single evening.** ⭐ **So the connection is: what night does to the sun gently and daily, the *Ghāshiyah* does to everything, permanently.** ⭐ **You watch a rehearsal of that Day every sunset.**
  
  ⭐ **Note also the tense shift:** ⭐ **v.2–3 use PAST verbs (*talāhā*, *jallāhā*); v.4 uses PRESENT (*yaghshāhā*).** ⭐ **Classical readers note this gives the covering a sense of **ongoing, continuous action** — the night is *always* in the process of covering somewhere.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **present tense**, weak-final, with *-hā*. ⚠️ **Note the shift from past to present mid-oath — deliberate, not a slip.**
- **Why this word:** *ghashiya* implies ⭐ **covering with no gaps**, which is what night does and what the Day will do.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Al-Ghāshiyah* (88:1) — THE ENVELOPER**, a name for the Day. ⭐ **Same verb here for night covering the sun.** ⭐ **Every sunset is a rehearsal.**

**5. طَحَاهَا — *ṭaḥāhā*** · v.6
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**spreading out / rolling flat**" family — *ṭaḥā* = **to spread wide, to extend outward**, ⭐ **also to push/drive something away and out.**
- **Here it means:** "**and by the earth and the One who spread it out.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **a rare word — it appears **only here** in the entire Qurʾān.**
  
  ⭐ **And you own its twin from Nāziʿāt 79:30** — *wa'l-arḍa baʿda dhālika ***daḥāhā*** ("and the earth, after that, He **spread it out**"). ⭐ **Two verbs, nearly identical in sound (*daḥā* / *ṭaḥā*) and identical in meaning — one surah apart in your study, twelve in the muṣḥaf.** ⚠️ ⭐ **This is a genuine confusion risk: *daḥāhā* (79:30, with **dāl**) vs. *ṭaḥāhā* (91:6, with **ṭāʾ**).**
  
  ⭐ **Both mean "spread out for use"** — ⭐ **the earth prepared as a livable surface**, echoing **Nabaʾ 78:6** (*mihādan*, **a cradle**) and **Ghāshiyah 88:20** (*suṭiḥat*, **spread flat**).
  
  ⭐ **Four surahs, four verbs for the same act:** ⭐ ***mihād* (cradled), *daḥā* (spread), *suṭiḥat* (flattened), *ṭaḥā* (spread wide).**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***wa mā ṭaḥāhā*** — ⚠️ **note *mā*, not *man*.** ⭐ **Classical scholars discuss this: *mā* here is widely read as *maṣdariyyah* ("and the spreading of it") or as referring to Allah with emphasis on the *attribute of power* rather than the person.** **Both are established.**
- **Why this word:** *ṭaḥā* conveys ⭐ **spreading with force and reach** — the earth pushed out wide.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ⚠️ ***daḥāhā* (Nāziʿāt 79:30, DĀL) vs *ṭaḥāhā* (Shams 91:6, ṬĀʾ).** ⭐ **Nearly identical sound and meaning — a real trap.** ⭐ **Four verbs for spreading the earth: *mihād* (78:6), *daḥā* (79:30), *suṭiḥat* (88:20), *ṭaḥā* (91:6).**

**6. وَنَفْسٍ وَمَا سَوَّاهَا — *wa nafsin wa mā sawwāhā*** · v.7
- **Root feelings:** *nafs* — ⭐ **the SOUL** — from the root of ***nafas*, BREATH*** (⭐ you met *tanaffasa* in **Takwīr 81:18** — "the morning when it **breathes**"). *sawwā* — ⭐ the "**making even / proportioning / perfecting**" family.
- **Here it means:** "**By the soul and the One who proportioned it.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **THE SEVENTH AND FINAL OATH — and it's the turn that makes the surah.**
  
  ⭐ **Look at what the first six oaths were: sun, moon, day, night, sky, earth.** ⭐ **Six cosmic objects. Then the seventh: ***YOUR SOUL***.** ⭐ **The Qurʾān places your inner self **on the same list as the sun and the sky** — and saves it for last, as the climax.**
  
  ⭐ **And *sawwā* — you now own it across SEVEN surahs.** ⭐ **This is your most-tracked root:**
  - **Qiyāmah 75:4** — re-proportioning your **fingertips**
  - **Qiyāmah 75:38** — proportioning you from **a clot**
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:28** — proportioning **the sky**
  - **Infiṭār 82:7** — proportioning **you**
  - **Aʿlā 87:2** — *khalaqa **fa-sawwā***
  - **Shams 91:7** — proportioning **the soul**
  - ⭐ **Shams 91:14** — ***levelling a town to the ground*** ⭐ **(seven verses later!)**
  
  ⭐ **A perfectly balanced soul (v.7) and a perfectly flattened city (v.14). One verb.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **note *nafsin* is INDEFINITE** ("**a** soul") — ⚠️ **the same indefiniteness as Takwīr 81:14 and Infiṭār 82:5.** ⭐ **Any soul. Yours.**
- **Why this word:** ending the oath-list on ***the soul*** shifts the whole surah from **astronomy to psychology** in one word.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **Six cosmic oaths… then YOUR SOUL, as the climax.** ⭐⭐ ***Sawwā* — SEVEN surahs, and TWICE in this one (v.7 and v.14).** ⭐ **A balanced soul, and a levelled town.**

## §2 — The Design of the Soul (v.8)

**7. فَأَلْهَمَهَا — *fa-alhamahā*** · v.8
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**swallowing whole / taking in at once**" family — *lahima* = **to gulp down, to absorb in one go**; ⭐ **from that, *ilhām* = INSPIRATION** — knowledge placed directly in the heart, ⭐ **not learned step by step but *swallowed whole*.**
- **Here it means:** "**And He inspired it [with] its corruption and its guarding.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the root explains exactly what kind of knowledge this is — and it's not education.**
  
  ⭐ ***Ilhām* comes from GULPING SOMETHING DOWN IN ONE SWALLOW.*** ⭐ **So this moral knowledge wasn't taught, argued, or reasoned into you — ⭐ **it was **placed inside you whole**, pre-installed.
  
  ⭐ **Which means: you don't need to be told that cruelty is wrong.** ⭐ **You already swallowed that.** ⭐ **The surah is saying every soul comes with the software already loaded.**
  
  ⭐ **And this connects to Balad 90:10** — *wa hadaynāhu ***an-najdayn*** ("We guided him to **the two heights**"). ⭐ **Balad said both paths are on **visible high ground**. Shams says the knowledge of both is **inside you**.** ⭐ **Consecutive surahs: the roads are visible outside, and the map is installed inside.** ⭐ **No excuse remains.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form IV** (*alhama* = to inspire). ⭐ **Note it takes TWO objects: *-hā* (it, the soul) and then *fujūrahā wa taqwāhā*.**
- **Why this word:** *alhama* over *ʿallama* (taught) — ⭐ **because taught knowledge can be forgotten or never received; *ilhām* is built in.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Lahima* = TO GULP DOWN WHOLE.** ⭐ ***Ilhām* = knowledge SWALLOWED, not studied.** ⭐ **Pre-installed.** ⭐ **Balad 90:10: the roads are visible OUTSIDE. Shams 91:8: the map is INSIDE.**

**8. فُجُورَهَا وَتَقْوَاهَا — *fujūrahā wa taqwāhā*** · v.8
- **Root feelings:** *fujūr* — ⭐ the "**bursting through / splitting past all restraint**" family — ⭐ **the same root as *fajr*, DAWN** (light splitting the dark). *taqwā* — ⭐ the "**shielding / guarding**" family — *waqā* = to protect; ⭐ ***wiqāyah* = PROTECTION*** (modern Arabic: **preventive medicine**).
- **Here it means:** "**its corruption and its guarding.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the two words are perfect physical opposites, and that's the design.**
  
  ⭐ ***Fujūr* = BURSTING OUT past your limits.*** ⭐ ***Taqwā* = PUTTING A SHIELD between yourself and harm.*** ⭐ **One breaks through barriers; the other builds them.** ⭐ **The soul was given the blueprint for both.**
  
  ⭐ **And CRITICALLY: *taqwā* is NOT "fear."** ⭐ **The root means **making a shield**.** ⭐ **A *muttaqī* isn't cowering — he's **carrying armour**.** ⭐ **You own this from Nabaʾ 78:31** (*inna li'l-**muttaqīna** mafāzā*).
  
  ⭐ **You own *fujūr* across SIX surahs now:** ⭐ **Qiyāmah 75:5 (*yafjura*), Insān 76:6 (*yufajjirūnahā*), ʿAbasa 80:42 (*al-fajarah*), Infiṭār 82:3 (*fujjirat*), Infiṭār 82:14 (*al-fujjār*), Fajr 89:1 (*al-fajr*), and here.**
  
  ⭐ **And note the connection to Balad 90:5** — the surah just before described a man with no *ḥijr* (**walls**). ⭐ **Here we learn: he had the blueprint for walls all along.**
- **Grammar bite:** both are **verbal nouns** with the *-hā* possessive; accusative as second objects of *alhama*.
- **Why this word:** naming them as **the soul's own** (*fujūra-**hā***, *taqwā-**hā***) means ⭐ **both potentials belong to you** — neither is external.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Fujūr* = BURSTING OUT** (like *fajr*, dawn splitting). ⭐ ***Taqwā* = BUILDING A SHIELD** (*wiqāyah* = protection). ⭐ **Break barriers, or build them.** ⭐ **Both blueprints are inside you.** ⭐ ⚠️ ***Taqwā* is NOT "fear" — it's ARMOUR.**

## §3 — The Verdict (v.9–10)

**9. قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَن زَكَّاهَا — *qad aflaḥa man zakkāhā*** · v.9
- **Root feelings:** *aflaḥa* — ⭐ **a FARMING root**: *falaḥa* = **to split/plough the earth**; ⭐ ***fallāḥ* = A FARMER***; from ploughing comes ***falāḥ* = success** — ⭐ **the harvest belongs to whoever broke the soil open.** *zakkā* — ⭐ the "**purify + grow**" family — ***zakāh***.
- **Here it means:** "**He has certainly succeeded who purifies it.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **you own this verse almost verbatim from the surah two back.**
  
  ⭐ **Aʿlā 87:14 — *qad **aflaḥa** man **tazakkā***.**
  ⭐ **Shams 91:9 — *qad **aflaḥa** man **zakkāhā***.**
  
  ⭐ **Nearly identical.** ⭐ **The only difference: Aʿlā uses **Form V** (*tazakkā* = "purified **himself**"), Shams uses **Form II** (*zakkā-hā* = "purified **it**").** ⭐ **Aʿlā emphasizes the reflexive; Shams names the object — **the soul from v.7**.
  
  ⭐ **And the FARMING logic runs through both words:** ⭐ ***falāḥ* = breaking soil open; *zakāh* = purifying so a thing can GROW.** ⭐ **Two agricultural roots, one verse.** ⭐ **Success is cultivation.**
  
  ⭐ **You say *falāḥ* in every adhān** — ⭐ ***ḥayya ʿala'l-falāḥ***, "come to success." ⭐ **You are being called to the harvest.**
  
  ⭐ **And *zakkā* is now across FIVE surahs:** ⭐ **Nāziʿāt 79:18 (offered to **Pharaoh**), ʿAbasa 80:3 & 80:7 (the **blind man**), Aʿlā 87:14, Shams 91:9.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **قَدْ + past = certainty** ("has **certainly** succeeded"). ⭐ ***Man*** here = "**whoever**" (relative), not "who?".
- **Why this word:** *falāḥ* over a generic word for winning — ⭐ **it builds *effort and cultivation* into the concept of success.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Fallāḥ* = A FARMER.* ⭐ ***Ḥayya ʿala'l-falāḥ*** — every adhān. ⭐ **Aʿlā 87:14 and Shams 91:9 are near-identical** (Form V vs Form II). ⭐ **Both words are farming words: plough it, and it grows.**

**10. وَقَدْ خَابَ مَن دَسَّاهَا — *wa qad khāba man dassāhā*** · v.10
- **Root feelings:** *khāba* — the "**failure / disappointment / coming away empty**" family — *khaybah* = **utter disappointment**, the state of the one whose hopes came to nothing. *dassā* — ⭐⭐ **a magnificent word**: from *dassa* = ⭐ **to bury something in the ground, to hide it away out of shame**; ⭐ **the same root as *dasīsah* = a covert plot**, and *indassa* = to sneak in.
- **Here it means:** "**And he has certainly failed who buries it.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **THE MOST PRECISE WORD-PAIR IN THE SURAH, and the contrast is agricultural.**
  
  ⭐ **v.9: *zakkāhā* — PURIFIED it, so it GROWS.**
  ⭐ **v.10: *dassāhā* — ⭐ **BURIED it in the dirt.**
  
  ⭐ **Both are things you do to a seed.** ⭐ **One clears it to grow; the other **shoves it under the soil to hide it.**
  
  ⭐ **And here's the connection that makes it unforgettable:** ⭐ **the root *k-f-r* (disbelief) originally meant **A FARMER BURYING A SEED** — you own this from Insān 76:3.** ⭐ **Now *dassā* means burying too.** ⭐ **Two different roots, same image: covering something over so it can't be seen.**
  
  ⭐ **And Qurʾānic usage of *dassa* elsewhere carries the sense of hiding something in the dust out of shame** — ⭐ **which is exactly what you do to a conscience you don't want to hear.** ⭐ **You don't kill it. You bury it alive.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Muṭaffifīn 83:14** — *bal ***rāna*** ʿalā qulūbihim* ("**RUST** has covered their hearts"). ⭐ **Two surahs, two images of the soul being covered over.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form II** (*dassā*), weak-final. ⚠️ **Note the perfect parallel with v.9: *qad + past + man + Form II verb + -hā*.** ⭐ **Identical grammar, opposite verbs.**
- **Why this word:** *dassā* (bury/conceal) rather than "corrupted" — ⭐ **because the soul isn't destroyed, only hidden. It's still down there.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Dassa* = TO BURY IN THE GROUND out of shame.** ⭐ **v.9 PURIFIES the seed so it grows; v.10 BURIES it.** ⭐ **And *kafara* also means burying a seed (Insān 76:3).** ⭐ **You don't kill the conscience — you bury it alive.**

## §4 — The Case Study: Thamūd (v.11–15)

**11. بِطَغْوَاهَا — *bi-ṭaghwāhā*** · v.11
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**overflowing all bounds**" family — original image: ⭐ **water bursting its banks and flooding.**
- **Here it means:** "**Thamūd denied by reason of its transgression.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the SIXTH appearance of this root, and by now it should be automatic:**
  - **Nabaʾ 78:22** — *aṭ-**ṭāghīn*** (Hell awaits those who transgress)
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:17** — *innahu **ṭaghā*** (**Pharaoh** — the case study)
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:37** — *fa-ammā man **ṭaghā*** (**whoever** transgresses — transferable)
  - **Fajr 89:11** — *alladhīna **ṭaghaw*** (ʿĀd, Thamūd, Pharaoh)
  - **Shams 91:11** — *bi-**ṭaghwāhā*** (Thamūd)
  
  ⭐ **And now connect it to THIS surah's own vocabulary:** ⭐ **v.8 said the soul was given *fujūr* (bursting out) and *taqwā* (shielding).** ⭐ **v.11 shows a whole nation that chose *fujūr*.** ⭐ ***Ṭaghā* IS *fujūr* at national scale.**
  
  ⭐ **Note the grammatical form: *ṭaghwā* — a verbal noun, not a verb.** ⭐ **It's not "because they transgressed" but "**by reason of ITS TRANSGRESSION**" — ⭐ **transgression as a *settled characteristic*, a permanent state of the nation, not a series of acts.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **بـ here = *bāʾ as-sababiyyah*** ("**because of / by reason of**"). ⭐ ***Thamūdu*** is often a **diptote** (foreign proper name) — ⚠️ **watch its case endings; they vary by position and reading.**
- **Why this word:** *ṭaghwā* (the noun) over the verb — ⭐ **it makes overflowing their **identity**, not their behaviour.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Ṭaghā* = water BURSTING ITS BANKS.** ⭐ **SIX surahs: 78:22, 79:17, 79:37, 89:11, 91:11.** ⭐ **And v.8 already named it: they picked *fujūr* over *taqwā*.**

**12. انبَعَثَ أَشْقَاهَا — *inbaʿatha ashqāhā*** · v.12
- **Root feelings:** *inbaʿatha* — ⭐ the "**being sent forth / raised up / dispatched**" family — ⭐ **the same root as *baʿth* = THE RESURRECTION!** (*yawm al-baʿth*) and *baʿatha* = to send (a messenger). *ashqā* — ⭐ the "**wretchedness / misery**" family — *shaqiyy* = wretched; *shaqāwah* = misery (opposite of *saʿādah*, happiness).
- **Here it means:** "**When the most wretched of them was sent forth.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **two hooks, both excellent.**
  
  ⭐ **First: *inbaʿatha* is the RESURRECTION root.** ⭐ **The verb for the dead being raised is used here for **one man standing up to commit a crime**.** ⭐ **He "resurrected himself" into action — and it destroyed his nation.**
  
  ⭐ **Second: *ashqā* — you own it from Aʿlā 87:11** — *wa **yatajannabuhā'l-ashqā*** ("**the most wretched** will sidestep it"). ⭐ **Aʿlā described *al-ashqā* abstractly; Shams gives him a face and a crime.** ⭐ **Same superlative, four surahs apart.**
  
  ⭐ **And note: it's SINGULAR.** ⭐ **"The most wretched **of them**" — ONE man.** ⭐ **But v.14 says *"their Lord crushed **THEM**"* — **plural**.** ⭐ **One man acted; a nation was destroyed.** ⭐ **Because the rest **sent him** — the passive *inbaʿatha* implies he was dispatched, with the community's consent.** ⭐ **Silence made them partners.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form VII (اِنْفَعَلَ)** — the **ان-** prefix; intransitive/passive in sense. ⭐ **You met it in Takwīr 81:2 (*inkadarat*) and Infiṭār 82:1 (*infaṭarat*).**
- **Why this word:** *inbaʿatha* implies ⭐ **being dispatched by others**, which is precisely why the punishment is collective.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Baʿth* = THE RESURRECTION.* ⭐ **The raising-verb used for **one man standing up to do evil**.** ⭐ **ONE man acted (*ashqāhā*, singular); the WHOLE nation was destroyed (*ʿalayhim*, plural).** ⭐ **They sent him.** ⭐ **And *al-ashqā* appeared in Aʿlā 87:11.**

**13. نَاقَةَ اللَّهِ وَسُقْيَاهَا — *nāqata'llāhi wa suqyāhā*** · v.13
- **Root feelings:** *nāqah* — **a she-camel**. *suqyā* — ⭐ the "**giving to drink**" family — *saqā* = to give water; ⭐ ***sqiyā/suqyā* = a turn at the water***, a drinking-share; ⭐ **and *istisqāʾ* = the PRAYER FOR RAIN!**
- **Here it means:** "**The Messenger of Allah said to them: [Beware] the she-camel of Allah and her drink!**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the whole crime is about a DRINKING SCHEDULE — and that's the point.**
  
  ⭐ **The she-camel of Ṣāliḥ (peace be upon him) was given a designated day at the well; the people had theirs.** ⭐ **That's it. That's the test.** ⭐ **Not a war, not an idol — ⭐ **a rota for water.**
  
  ⭐ **And notice the ownership: *nāqata **'LLĀH*** — "the she-camel **of ALLAH**."** ⭐ **The animal is attributed directly to Him.** ⭐ **Which is why harming her isn't cruelty to an animal — it's a direct challenge.**
  
  ⭐ **The lesson worth keeping: they were destroyed over a small, clearly-stated boundary.** ⭐ **Which is exactly what *ṭaghā* (v.11) means — ⭐ **refusing to stay within the banks.** ⭐ **The size of the boundary was never the issue.**
  
  ⭐ ***Istisqāʾ*** — the rain-prayer — is a free hook for this root.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***nāqata'llāh*** is accusative — a ***taḥdhīr*** construction (**a warning**): "[Beware] the she-camel!" ⭐ **Arabic can issue a warning by putting the noun in the accusative with the verb implied.**
- **Why this word:** *suqyā* (her **drinking turn**) makes the transgression ⭐ **absurdly small and absolutely clear.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **A nation destroyed over **A DRINKING SCHEDULE**.** ⭐ **The boundary was tiny and clearly stated — that was the test.** ⭐ ***Istisqāʾ* = the prayer for rain** (same root). ⭐ **And she's called the she-camel **OF ALLAH**.**

**14. فَعَقَرُوهَا فَدَمْدَمَ — *fa-ʿaqarūhā fa-damdama*** · v.14
- **Root feelings:** *ʿaqarū* — ⭐ the "**wounding / hamstringing**" family — *ʿaqara* = **to hamstring an animal** (cut the leg tendon so it collapses); ⭐ **also *ʿāqir* = barren** (cut off from offspring). *damdama* — ⭐⭐ a **reduplicated root**: *damdama ʿalā* = **to crush utterly, to level with overwhelming force**, ⭐ **piling destruction on top of something until nothing remains.**
- **Here it means:** "**But they hamstrung her, so their Lord crushed them for their sin.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the sound of *damdama* is the punishment.**
  
  ⭐ ***Dam-da-ma*** — ⭐ **a doubled, hammering syllable, like the sound of something being pounded flat.** ⭐ **You've met this reduplication pattern three times now:**
  - ***waswasa*** (to whisper repeatedly — Sūrat an-Nās)
  - ***zalzala*** (to shake violently)
  - ***ʿasʿasa*** (the night creeping — **Takwīr 81:17**)
  - ***damdama*** (to crush utterly — here)
  
  ⭐ **Arabic uses doubled roots for repeated, relentless action.** ⭐ **The word *sounds* like what it does.**
  
  ⭐ **And note the sequence of *fa*'s: *fa-kadhdhabūhu **fa**-ʿaqarūhā **fa**-damdama*.** ⭐ **Denied → hamstrung → crushed.** ⭐ **Three "fa"s = three immediate consequences, no gaps.** ⭐ **Same accelerating technique as Nāziʿāt 79:21–24 (Pharaoh's chain of *fa*'s).**
  
  ⭐ **And *bi-dhanbihim* ("for their sin") — SINGULAR *dhanb*, PLURAL *-him*.** ⭐ **One sin, all of them.** ⭐ **Because one man's act became everyone's.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **quadriliteral (four-letter) verb**, past tense. ⭐ ***Damdama ʿalā*** takes the preposition *ʿalā* — "crushed **down upon** them."
- **Why this word:** *damdama* over a plain word for destroy — ⭐ **it's onomatopoeic**; the pounding is audible.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **Say it: *dam-da-ma*.** ⭐ **The word IS the pounding.** ⭐ **Same doubling as *waswasa*, *zalzala*, *ʿasʿasa* (Takwīr 81:17).** ⭐ **And *ʿaqara* = to HAMSTRING** (cut the leg tendon). ⭐ **One *dhanb* (singular sin), all of *-him* (plural).**

**15. فَسَوَّاهَا — *fa-sawwāhā*** · v.14
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**making even / levelling**" family — ⭐ **the SAME ROOT as v.7.**
- **Here it means:** "**and levelled it** [the town / them]" — flattened to the ground, made even with the earth.
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **THE SINGLE BEST FIND IN THIS SURAH, AND POSSIBLY IN YOUR WHOLE COLLECTION.**
  
  ⭐ **v.7: *wa nafsin wa mā **sawwāhā*** — "By the soul and the One who **PROPORTIONED IT**."
  ⭐ **v.14: *fa-damdama ʿalayhim rabbuhum bi-dhanbihim **fa-sawwāhā*** — "so He crushed them and **LEVELLED IT**."
  
  ⭐⭐ **IDENTICAL WORD. SEVEN VERSES APART.**
  
  ⭐ **How can one verb mean both?** ⭐ **Because *sawwā* means "**to make even**" — and "even" can mean:**
  - ⭐ **PERFECTLY BALANCED** (a soul with all faculties in proportion) — **v.7**
  - ⭐ **PERFECTLY FLAT** (a town levelled with the ground) — **v.14**
  
  ⭐ **The same act of "evening out" builds and destroys.**
  
  ⭐ **And the moral logic is exact:** ⭐ **He *sawwā*-ed your soul (v.7). What you do with it decides whether the second *sawwā* — the flattening one — ever finds you.** ⭐ **The surah gives you the verb twice and lets you choose which meaning applies.**
  
  ⭐ **This is now the SEVENTH surah for *s-w-y*, and its EIGHTH total appearance in your study.** ⭐ **Fingertips (75:4), a clot (75:38), the sky (79:28), you (82:7), everything (87:2), the soul (91:7), a ruined town (91:14).**
- **Grammar bite:** Form II, weak-final, with *-hā*. ⚠️ ⭐ **The *-hā* is debated: the town? the nation? the punishment? — ⭐ **all readings are classical, and all mean "levelled flat."**
- **Why this word:** using ⭐ **the creation-verb for the destruction** is the most economical possible way to say *the One who made you can unmake you*.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***SAWWĀHĀ* IN v.7 = HE PERFECTED A SOUL.** ⭐⭐⭐ ***SAWWĀHĀ* IN v.14 = HE FLATTENED A TOWN.** ⭐ **Same word, seven verses apart.** ⭐ **"Making even" = balanced, or flat.** ⭐ **Eighth appearance of this root across seven surahs.**

**16. وَلَا يَخَافُ عُقْبَاهَا — *wa lā yakhāfu ʿuqbāhā*** · v.15
- **Root feelings:** *yakhāfu* — the "**fear**" family (*khawf*). *ʿuqbā* — ⭐ the "**following / heel / consequence**" family — ***ʿaqib* = a HEEL***; ⭐ ***ʿāqibah* = the outcome***; ⭐ **and *ʿaqabah* = a steep pass — ⭐ **which you just met as the centre of Balad 90:11!**
- **Here it means:** "**And He does not fear its consequence.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the final verse, and it's a statement about God that answers a question you'd never think to ask.**
  
  ⭐ **Why mention that He doesn't fear consequences?** ⭐ **Because every *human* ruler who destroys a city must.** ⭐ **There'll be retaliation, reputation damage, a reckoning, someone to answer to.** ⭐ **The verse says: **not Him.** There is no one above Him to whom an account is owed.
  
  ⭐ **And the root-connection is beautiful:** ⭐ ***ʿaqib* = a HEEL*** — ⭐ **the consequence is what "follows on the heels" of an action.** ⭐ **Arabic names outcomes after the part of the foot that comes behind.**
  
  ⭐ **And here's the payoff with the surah before it:** ⭐ **Balad 90:11 built its whole message on ***al-ʿaqabah*** — **the steep pass**, from this same root (the climb whose meaning is its **outcome**).** ⭐ **Shams 91:15 closes with ***ʿuqbāhā*** — **the outcome itself.** ⭐ **Consecutive surahs, opening and closing on one root: the climb, and what follows it.**
  
  ⭐ **And note the very last sound: *-hā*.** ⭐ **All fifteen verses end in it.** ⭐ **The rhyme holds to the final syllable.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⚠️ ⭐ **there is a well-known discussion of the subject here** — most read it as **Allah** ("He does not fear the consequence"); some read it as referring to **the one who hamstrung the camel** (he did not fear the outcome). ⭐ **Both are classical.** *ʿUqbā* is weak-final, carrying the rhyme.
- **Why this word:** *ʿuqbā* (consequence) rather than "punishment" — ⭐ **it's the neutral word for *what comes next*, which makes the absence of fear absolute.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***ʿAqib* = A HEEL.** ⭐ **A consequence is what follows **on your heels**.** ⭐ **Balad 90:11 = *al-ʿaqabah* (THE CLIMB). Shams 91:15 = *ʿuqbāhā* (THE OUTCOME).** ⭐ **Consecutive surahs, one root.** ⭐ **And every verse of this surah ends in *-hā*.**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **سَوَّاهَا appears TWICE (v.7 and v.14)** — ⚠️ **"He PERFECTED a soul" and "He FLATTENED a town."** ⭐ **Identical word, opposite outcomes.** **The single most important thing in this surah.**
2. ⭐⭐ **طَحَاهَا (v.6, ṬĀʾ) vs. دَحَاهَا (Nāziʿāt 79:30, DĀL)** — ⚠️ **near-identical sound, same meaning ("spread out"), different letters.** **A genuine confusion risk.**
3. ⭐⭐ **زَكَّاهَا (v.9) vs. دَسَّاهَا (v.10)** — ⚠️ **both are things you do to a SEED**: **clear it to grow** vs. **bury it out of sight.** Don't read them as generic good/bad.
4. ⭐ **دَسَّاهَا (v.10)** — ⚠️ **means BURY/CONCEAL, not "destroy."** ⭐ **The soul is still down there, buried alive.**
5. ⭐ **أَلْهَمَ (v.8)** — from *lahima*, ⭐ **TO GULP DOWN WHOLE.** ⚠️ **Inspiration, not education** — it was pre-installed, not taught.
6. ⭐ **تَقْوَاهَا (v.8)** — ⚠️ **NOT "fear."** From *wiqāyah* = **PROTECTION/ARMOUR.** A *muttaqī* carries a shield; he doesn't cower.
7. ⭐ **انبَعَثَ أَشْقَاهَا (v.12)** — ⚠️ **SINGULAR** (one man) — but v.14's punishment is **PLURAL** (*ʿalayhim*). ⭐ **One acted; all were destroyed, because all sent him.**
8. ⭐ **بِذَنبِهِمْ (v.14)** — ⚠️ **SINGULAR sin, PLURAL people.** One *dhanb*, all of them.
9. ⭐ **وَمَا سَوَّاهَا (v.7)** — ⚠️ **note *mā*, not *man*.** Classical readings treat it as *maṣdariyyah* or as emphasizing the **attribute of power**. Don't assume it's an error.
10. ⭐ **عُقْبَاهَا (v.15)** — from ***ʿaqib*, A HEEL.** ⭐ **Same root as *al-ʿaqabah* (Balad 90:11), the steep climb.** ⚠️ **Two consecutive surahs, one root.**
