# Surah Al-Infiṭār (82) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–19)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**It's Takwīr's twin — and comparing them is the fastest way to lock both in.**

| | **Takwīr (81)** | **Infiṭār (82)** |
|---|---|---|
| Openers | **12** *idhā* clauses | **4** *idhā* clauses |
| Sky | *kushiṭat* — **flayed off** | *infaṭarat* — **split open** |
| Stars | *inkadarat* — **dimmed & fell** | *intatharat* — **scattered like beads** |
| Seas | *sujjirat* — **stoked like an oven** | *fujjirat* — **burst open** |
| Payoff | *ʿalimat nafsun **mā aḥḍarat*** | *ʿalimat nafsun **mā qaddamat wa akhkharat*** |

⭐ **Look at that last row: "ʿalimat nafsun" is IDENTICAL in both surahs.** Same two words, same position, same job — the punchline after the *idhā* stack. ⭐ **Learn one, and you own half the other.**

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐ **Takwīr takes twelve hammer-blows to reach the point. Infiṭār takes four.** Same architecture, shorter runway.

---

## The Big Picture

1. **Four Undoings** (v.1–4) — sky, stars, seas, **graves**. ⭐ Notice the fourth: Takwīr never mentioned graves. Infiṭār goes **underground**.
2. **The Payoff** (v.5) — *a soul will know what it sent ahead and what it left behind.*
3. **The Question** (v.6–8) — ⭐ **"What DECEIVED you about your generous Lord?"** The gentlest, most devastating question in the Qur'an.
4. **The Recorders** (v.9–12) — you are never unwatched, and the watchers are **noble**.
5. **Two Groups, Two Places** (v.13–16) — *al-abrār* in bliss, *al-fujjār* in blaze.
6. **The Day Nobody Can Help Anybody** (v.17–19).

## §1 — The Four Undoings (v.1–4)

**1. انفَطَرَتْ — *infaṭarat*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**splitting open / cleaving apart**" family — *faṭara* = to split. **And here's the treasure:** the same root gives ***fiṭrah*** (your **original, natural disposition** — the state you were "split into" at creation), ***fāṭir*** (the **Originator** — one who splits something into being), and ⭐ ***Ifṭār*** — **breaking your fast** (splitting the fast open)! Also ***ʿĪd al-Fiṭr***.
- **Here it means:** "When the sky **is cleft open**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the root's double life is the whole point.** *Faṭara* means both **to split** and **to originate**. Why? Because ⭐ **creating something is splitting it out of nothingness.** So when the sky is *infaṭarat*, it's being **un-originated** — the splitting that made it is now the splitting that ends it. ⭐ **The verb of your beginning is the verb of your ending.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form VII (اِنْفَعَلَ)** — the **ان-** prefix means it happens **to** the subject with no doer named. Same shape as **Takwīr 81:2** (*inkadarat*) and **Inshiqāq 84:1** (*inshaqqat*). ⭐ **Three consecutive surahs open with a Form VII passive-sense verb.**
- **Why this word:** *infaṭarat* over "broke" — because the root **carries creation inside it**, making the destruction feel like a **reversal**, not an accident.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **You say this root every Ramadan: *Ifṭār* = breaking (splitting) your fast, *ʿĪd al-Fiṭr*.** And ***fiṭrah*** = your **original nature**. ⭐ **Same root: to split = to originate.**

**2. الْكَوَاكِبُ انتَثَرَتْ — *al-kawākibu intatharat*** · v.2
- **Root feelings:** *kawākib* — plural of *kawkab*, a **planet or bright star**. *intatharat* — ⭐ the "**scattering / strewing**" family — *nathara* = to scatter loose things; ***nathr* = PROSE** (scattered words, as opposed to *shiʿr*, poetry with a thread through it!). ⭐ And you own this root from **Insān 76:19**: *luʾluʾan **manthūrā*** — "**scattered** pearls."
- **Here it means:** "and when the stars **are scattered**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the connection to Insān is exquisite, and it's an exact reversal.**
  - **Insān 76:19** — the servants of Paradise are like ***scattered pearls*** (*manthūrā*) — beautiful, abundant, joyfully strewn.
  - **Infiṭār 82:2** — the stars are ***scattered*** (*intatharat*) — a **necklace snapping**, order collapsing.
  
  ⭐ **Same root: scattering as GENEROSITY there, scattering as RUIN here.** Picture pearls tossed at a wedding vs. **a string of pearls breaking and rolling across the floor.**
  
  ⭐ **And compare Takwīr 81:2** (*inkadarat* = **dimmed and fell**). **Takwīr's stars go MURKY; Infiṭār's stars go LOOSE.** Two different failures.
- **Grammar bite:** **Form VIII** (the **-ta-** slipped inside). Note: **v.1 is Form VII, v.2 is Form VIII** — small variation keeping the rhythm from flattening.
- **Why this word:** *intatharat* pictures **loss of arrangement**, not loss of light — the sky's *design* comes apart.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***nathr* = PROSE** (scattered words). ⭐ **Insān 76:19: pearls scattered in JOY. Infiṭār 82:2: stars scattered in RUIN.** Same root — **a necklace snapping.**

**3. الْبِحَارُ فُجِّرَتْ — *al-biḥāru fujjirat*** · v.3
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**bursting open / cleaving forth**" family — you own this root **three times over**: ***fajr*** (**dawn** splitting the dark), **Qiyāmah 75:5** (*li-**yafjura** amāmah* — bursting into sin), **Insān 76:6** (*yufajjirūnahā* — the righteous make the spring **gush**), and **ʿAbasa 80:42** (*al-**fajarah*** — the wicked who burst past limits).
- **Here it means:** "and when the seas **are burst forth**" — barriers broken, waters released.
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **compare with Takwīr 81:6 (*sujjirat*, "stoked like an oven").** Both describe the seas, both are Form II passive, **and they're describing the same event from two angles**: ⭐ **Takwīr says the seas BURN; Infiṭār says the seas BURST.** Fire and flood — the two ways water can betray you.
  
  ⭐ **And the root-echo with Insān 76:6 is the sharpest one:** there, the righteous **burst forth** springs *at will* — mastery, control, blessing. Here, the seas **burst forth** *uncontrolled* — the barriers that held them are gone. ⭐ **Same verb: yours to command in Paradise, no one's to command on that Day.**
- **Grammar bite:** **Form II passive** (*fuʿʿila* shape) — same as *sujjirat, kuwwirat, suyyirat*. ⭐ **Intensity + no named doer.**
- **Why this word:** *fujjirat* keeps the **fajr/bursting** family running through five surahs — a root worth owning completely.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Same root as *fajr* (dawn splits the dark).** ⭐ **Takwīr 81:6: seas STOKED. Infiṭār 82:3: seas BURST.** Fire vs. flood, same seas.

**4. الْقُبُورُ بُعْثِرَتْ — *al-qubūru buʿthirat*** · v.4
- **Root feelings:** *qubūr* — plural of *qabr*, **a grave** — ⭐ you own it from **ʿAbasa 80:21** (*fa-**aqbarah***, "He **gave him a grave**"). *buʿthirat* — ⭐ the "**overturning and scattering out**" family — *baʿthara* = to **turn something upside down and dump its contents**, to ransack. (Related in sense to *baʿatha*, to send forth/resurrect.)
- **Here it means:** "and when the graves **are turned inside out**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the fourth undoing, and it's the one Takwīr never made.** Takwīr's twelve stayed **above ground** — sky, stars, mountains, seas, beasts, records. ⭐ **Infiṭār's fourth verse goes DOWN, and empties the earth.**
  
  And *baʿthara* is beautifully chosen: it's the verb for ⭐ **turning a container upside-down and shaking everything out** — like emptying a bag onto a table. **The graves aren't opened. They're INVERTED.**
  
  ⭐ **And connect it to ʿAbasa 80:21:** there, being **given a grave** was listed as a **blessing** (*aqbarah*). ⭐ **Here that same gift gets tipped over.** The resting-place you were honored with becomes the thing that empties you out.
- **Grammar bite:** a **quadriliteral** verb (four root letters: ب-ع-ث-ر), in the passive. ⭐ **Quadriliterals often carry a sense of jumbled, messy, repeated action** — like *zalzala* (shake), *waswasa* (whisper). The **shape itself sounds chaotic.**
- **Why this word:** *buʿthirat* over "opened" — because **opening is orderly; overturning is not.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ *baʿthara* = **to tip a container upside down and dump it out.** ⭐ **ʿAbasa 80:21 GAVE you a grave; Infiṭār 82:4 TIPS IT OVER.** And ⭐ **this is the one Takwīr didn't have — Infiṭār digs.**

**5. عَلِمَتْ نَفْسٌ مَّا قَدَّمَتْ وَأَخَّرَتْ — *ʿalimat nafsun mā qaddamat wa akhkharat*** · v.5
- **Root feelings:** *qaddama* — "**to send forward**" (*muqaddimah* = an introduction, what comes first). *akhkhara* — "**to put back / defer**" (*ākhir* = last; *al-ākhirah* = **the Last** life).
- **Here it means:** "**A soul will know what it sent ahead and what it left behind.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **THIS VERSE IS A DOUBLE JACKPOT — it links to TWO surahs at once.**
  - ⭐ **"ʿalimat nafsun" is IDENTICAL to Takwīr 81:14.** Same two words, same position, same function: **the answer to the *idhā* stack.** Twelve clauses there, four here, **same landing.**
  - ⭐ **"mā qaddamat wa akhkharat" is nearly identical to Qiyāmah 75:13** — *yunabbaʾu'l-insānu yawmaʾidhin **bimā qaddama wa akhkhar***. **Same pair of verbs, same meaning.**
  
  ⭐ **So this one verse is Takwīr's opening + Qiyāmah's closing, fused.** If you learned those two, **you already knew this verse before you read it.**
  
  And the **ṭibāq** (opposite-pair) does its classic job: ⭐ **"sent forward" + "left behind" = the ENTIRE ledger**, nothing outside it. Two words covering a whole life.
  
  ⭐ **One more difference worth catching:** Takwīr said *mā **aḥḍarat*** ("what it **brought with it**" — luggage). Infiṭār says *mā **qaddamat wa akhkharat*** ("what it **sent ahead** and **deferred**" — investments and omissions). ⭐ **Takwīr weighs what you carried; Infiṭār weighs what you shipped AND what you skipped.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***nafsun* is INDEFINITE** — "**a** soul," not "every soul." Singular, anonymous. ⭐ **Same move as Takwīr 81:14, Nabaʾ 78:40 (*al-marʾ*), and ʿAbasa 80:34 (*al-marʾ*).** **Four surahs in a row narrow to ONE unnamed person.** Note also the **past tense for a future event** = certainty.
- **Why this word:** the **forward/backward pair** makes omissions count as much as actions — you're accountable for **what you didn't do**, too.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **Takwīr 81:14 + Qiyāmah 75:13 = this verse.** ⭐ **"Sent ahead" (what you did) and "left behind" (what you didn't).** Both on the bill.

## §2 — The Gentlest Devastating Question (v.6–8)

**6. مَا غَرَّكَ — *mā gharraka*** · v.6
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**deceiving by making something look better than it is**" family — *gharra* = to delude, beguile; ***ghurūr*** = **delusion, false glitter**; and ⭐ ***gharīr*** = someone **naive**, easily taken in. Related sense: *ghirr* = inexperienced.
- **Here it means:** "O man! **What deceived you** concerning your generous Lord?"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **This is one of the most remarkable questions in the entire Qur'an, and everything is in the word choice.**
  
  Look at what it does **NOT** say:
  - Not *"How dare you?"*
  - Not *"What is wrong with you?"*
  - Not *"Why did you disobey?"*
  
  ⭐ **It says: "What DECEIVED you?"** — which quietly assumes ⭐ **you must have been TRICKED**, because no one who saw clearly would do this. **The question grants you an excuse while removing it.** It treats your rebellion as **a case of being conned**.
  
  ⭐ **And then look at the last two words: *bi-Rabbika'l-KARĪM*** — "concerning your **GENEROUS** Lord."
  
  ⭐ **Of all the names of Allah, this verse picks *al-Karīm* — the Generous.** Not *al-Jabbār* (the Compeller), not *al-Muntaqim*, not *al-ʿAzīz*. ⭐ **The rebuke is delivered through the name of GENEROSITY.** The logic: *I have been nothing but generous to you — so what could possibly have talked you out of that?*
  
  ⭐ **A famous scholarly observation:** some said the name *al-Karīm* is mentioned here **to teach the answer** — as if man might reply, *"Your own generosity deceived me"* (i.e., You kept giving despite my sins, so I assumed there'd be no reckoning). **The verse names the very attribute the deluded person exploited.**
- **Grammar bite:** *mā* = interrogative "what." ⭐ **Note the address: *yā ayyuha'l-insān*** — "O **man**" (humanity at large), not "O disbelievers." ⭐ **Nobody is excluded from the question.**
- **Why this word:** *gharra* over "made you disobey" — it targets the **self-deception**, which is the actual mechanism.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***ghurūr* = false glitter, being conned.** ⭐ **"What CONNED you about your GENEROUS Lord?"** — the rebuke arrives dressed as **concern**, and it's delivered through the name **al-Karīm.**

**7. سَوَّاكَ فَعَدَلَكَ — *sawwāka fa-ʿadalak*** · v.7
- **Root feelings:** *sawwā* — ⭐ the "**proportioning / making even**" family — **you own this THREE times already**: **Qiyāmah 75:4** (*nusawwiya* — re-proportion your fingertips), **Qiyāmah 75:38** (*sawwā* — proportioned you from a clot), **Nāziʿāt 79:28** (*fa-sawwāhā* — proportioned the sky). *ʿadala* — ⭐ the "**justice / balance / symmetry**" family — ***ʿadl*** = **justice**; *iʿtidāl* = equilibrium, moderation.
- **Here it means:** "**Who created you, then proportioned you, then balanced you.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the answer to v.6's question is delivered as a résumé of favors — and *ʿadalak* is the stunning one.**
  
  ⭐ ***ʿAdala* is the JUSTICE root.** Applied to your body it means **symmetry** — two eyes level, two arms matched, limbs in proportion. ⭐ **Your physical body is described using the word for JUSTICE**, because **balance and justice are the same idea in Arabic.**
  
  ⭐ **Now feel the argument:** *"What deceived you about your generous Lord — the One who built JUSTICE into your very limbs?"* ⭐ **You are walking evidence of balance, arguing against the Day of Balance.**
  
  And the **three-verb chain** (*khalaqaka → sawwāka → ʿadalak*) with **فَـ** between each = **immediate sequence**: created, **then instantly** proportioned, **then instantly** balanced. ⭐ **No gap, no rough draft.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form II (*sawwā*) = careful, deliberate crafting.** *ʿAdala* is Form I. Note the **relative pronoun الَّذِي** opening the verse — it's an extended description of *Rabbika'l-Karīm*, i.e. ⭐ **the answer to "what deceived you?" is a list of what He did for you.**
- **Why this word:** *ʿadalak* over a plain word for "shaped" — because **justice** is exactly what the deniers are rejecting, and it's **built into their bones**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***ʿadl* = JUSTICE.** Your **symmetrical body** is described with the **justice** word. ⭐ **You are physical proof of balance — denying the Day of Balance.** And *sawwā* you've now met in **four surahs.**

**8. فِي أَيِّ صُورَةٍ ... رَكَّبَكَ — *fī ayyi ṣūratin… rakkabak*** · v.8
- **Root feelings:** *ṣūrah* — **a form, shape, image** (*taṣwīr* = **photography** in modern Arabic; *muṣawwir* = **the Fashioner**, a name of Allah). *rakkaba* — ⭐ the "**assembling / composing / mounting**" family — *rakkaba* = **to put parts together**; ***tarkīb*** = **composition, structure, assembly**; and ⭐ ***rakiba*** = **to ride/mount** (climbing onto something and joining with it).
- **Here it means:** "In **whatever form** He willed, **He assembled you**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ ***Rakkaba* is ENGINEERING vocabulary.** It's the word for ⭐ **fitting components together into a working whole** — assembling a machine, composing a structure. Modern Arabic uses *tarkīb* for **chemical composition** and **grammatical structure**.
  
  ⭐ **So the three verbs across v.7–8 form a complete build sequence:**
  1. ***khalaqaka*** — **created** you (brought into being)
  2. ***sawwāka*** — **proportioned** you (got the measurements right)
  3. ***ʿadalak*** — **balanced** you (made it symmetrical)
  4. ***rakkabak*** — **assembled** you (fitted every part together)
  
  ⭐ **Four verbs, a full manufacturing line — and the person who came off it is asking "will I really be rebuilt?"**
  
  ⭐ **And "fī ayyi ṣūratin mā shāʾa" ("in whatever form He willed") is the quiet answer to human vanity:** your face, your height, your features — **you didn't pick any of it.** The variety among humans is **His choice**, not an achievement of yours.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form II** again (*rakkaba*) — intensive, deliberate assembly. *Ayyi* = "whichever"; *mā* here is *mā zāʾidah* (**extra *mā* for emphasis**), a common construction.
- **Why this word:** *rakkaba* frames you as **an assembled machine** — and machines have **makers**, and makers can **rebuild**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***tarkīb*** = **assembly / composition** (chemistry, grammar). ⭐ **Four-step build: created → proportioned → balanced → ASSEMBLED.** And **you didn't choose the design.**

## §3 — The Noble Recorders (v.9–12)

**9. تُكَذِّبُونَ بِالدِّينِ — *tukadhdhibūna bi'd-dīn*** · v.9
- **Root feelings:** *kadhdhaba* — ⭐ the "**calling something a lie**" family — **you own it** from **Nabaʾ 78:28** (*kidhdhābā*), **Qiyāmah 75:32**, **Nāziʿāt 79:21**. *dīn* — ⭐ **a double-life root**: *dīn* = **religion**, *and* *dayn* = **a DEBT**, and *dāna* = **to judge / to requite**. ⭐ ***Yawm ad-Dīn* = the Day of Judgment / the Day the DEBT comes due.**
- **Here it means:** "**No! Rather, you deny the Recompense.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the root's double meaning is the surah's engine.** ***Dīn* and *dayn* (debt) share three letters** — because ⭐ **religion is understood as an obligation you owe and will be asked to settle.** So *yawm ad-dīn* isn't just "Judgment Day" — it's ⭐ **the day the account is settled.**
  
  ⭐ **Now the logic snaps together:** v.6 asked *what deceived you?* Verses 7–8 listed **four favors**. Verse 9 gives the real answer: ⭐ **you deny that there's a bill.** Nothing "deceived" you cleverly — **you just decided the debt wasn't real.**
  
  And ⭐ **كَلَّا** ("No!") is the stern rebuttal-particle you own from **Qiyāmah 75:11, Nabaʾ 78:4, ʿAbasa 80:11.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **notice the sudden shift from SINGULAR to PLURAL.** Verses 6–8 addressed *you* (singular): *gharraka, sawwāka, rakkabak*. Verse 9 switches to *tukadhdhibūn* (**you all**). ⭐ **The private question becomes a public verdict.** This shift (*iltifāt*) is deliberate.
- **Why this word:** *dīn* over a word for "judgment" — because the **debt** sense makes the denial sound like **defaulting**, not disagreeing.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***dīn* (religion) and *dayn* (DEBT) — same three letters.** ⭐ *Yawm ad-Dīn* = **the day the bill comes due.** And ⭐ **watch the "you" go from singular (v.6–8) to plural (v.9).**

**10. لَحَافِظِينَ — *la-ḥāfiẓīn*** · v.10
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**guarding / preserving / memorizing**" family — *ḥafiẓa* = to guard, to preserve; ⭐ ***ḥāfiẓ*** = **one who has memorized the Qur'an** (a "preserver"!); *maḥfūẓ* = protected (as in ***al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ***, the Preserved Tablet — **Burūj 85:22**); *ḥifẓ* = memorization.
- **Here it means:** "And indeed, **over you are guardians**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the word you already use for yourself.** A ***ḥāfiẓ*** is someone who **preserves the Qur'an in memory**. ⭐ **These angels are *ḥāfiẓīn* of YOU** — preserving your life in a record the way a ḥāfiẓ preserves a text.
  
  ⭐ **And notice the tone:** the root isn't menacing. *Ḥifẓ* means **protection** as much as surveillance — the same word for **guarding a treasure**. ⭐ **You're not being spied on; you're being PRESERVED.** Which is worse in one way and kinder in another: **nothing you did is lost.**
  
  ⭐ **Cross-link:** the same root appears in **Ṭāriq 86:4** — *in kullu nafsin lammā ʿalayhā **ḥāfiẓ*** ("there is no soul but has over it a guardian"). ⭐ **Two surahs apart, same statement.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **triple emphasis**: *wa **inna** ʿalaykum **la**-ḥāfiẓīn* — *inna* + the **lām of emphasis** + the fronted *ʿalaykum*. **Arabic underlining, three layers.**
- **Why this word:** *ḥāfiẓīn* over "watchers" — the **preservation** sense makes the record feel **permanent**, not merely observed.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **A *ḥāfiẓ* memorizes the Qur'an — preserves it perfectly.** ⭐ **These angels are *ḥāfiẓ* of your LIFE.** Same root as ***al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ*** (Burūj 85:22).

**11. كِرَامًا كَاتِبِينَ — *kirāman kātibīn*** · v.11
- **Root feelings:** *kirām* — ⭐ the "**nobility / generosity**" family — **you own it from ʿAbasa 80:16** (*kirāmin bararah* — "noble, righteous" scribes) and **Takwīr 81:19** (*rasūlin karīm*), and **Makkah al-Mukarramah**. *kātibīn* — the "**writing**" family (*kataba* = to write; *kitāb* = a book; *maktab* = an office).
- **Here it means:** "**Noble, recording** [angels]."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **The word *kirām* here is doing enormous emotional work, and it's the same word used four verses earlier for Allah Himself (*Rabbika'l-**Karīm***, v.6).**
  
  ⭐ **Think about why "noble" matters for a record-keeper.** A **noble** scribe:
  - doesn't **exaggerate** your sins
  - doesn't **invent** anything
  - doesn't **gossip** about what he writes
  - records with **dignity**, not relish
  
  ⭐ **You're being watched — but by someone honorable.** Compare that to being recorded by an enemy. ⭐ **The surah about accountability keeps inserting mercy: a GENEROUS Lord (v.6), NOBLE scribes (v.11).**
  
  ⭐ **And here's the strongest link:** ⭐ **ʿAbasa 80:16 described the angels of revelation as *kirāmin bararah* (noble, righteous). Infiṭār 82:11 describes the angels of record as *kirāman kātibīn* (noble, writing).** ⭐ **Same adjective, two jobs — the ones who bring the message and the ones who record your response are BOTH noble.**
- **Grammar bite:** both accusative, describing *ḥāfiẓīn* in v.10. ⭐ **Note the *faʿīl→kirām* plural — same broken-plural shape as *ʿiẓām, ṭiwāl.***
- **Why this word:** *kirām* over "strict" or "watchful" — it turns surveillance into **honorable witness**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **v.6: your Lord is *al-Karīm* (Generous). v.11: the scribes are *kirām* (noble).** ⭐ **Same root, four verses apart.** And ⭐ **ʿAbasa 80:16 used it for the revelation-angels.** **Noble on both ends.**

## §4 — Two Groups, Two Places (v.13–16)

**12. الْأَبْرَارَ ... نَعِيمٍ — *al-abrāra… naʿīm*** · v.13
- **Root feelings:** *abrār* — ⭐ the "**solid, dependable goodness**" family — **you own it from Insān 76:5** (*inna'l-**abrāra** yashrabūn*) and **ʿAbasa 80:16** (*bararah*), and ***birr*** = **devotion to parents**. *naʿīm* — ⭐ the "**blessing / comfort**" family — **you own it from Insān 76:20** (*raʾayta **naʿīman** wa mulkan kabīrā*); related to *niʿmah* (a favor) and even *naʿam* (**yes!**).
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, **the righteous will be in bliss**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **this exact phrase — *inna'l-abrāra la-fī naʿīm* — appears VERBATIM in Muṭaffifīn 83:22.** ⭐ **Next surah, same sentence.** Free vocabulary, and worth noticing when you get there.
  
  ⭐ **And the pairing with v.14 is a textbook *muqābala* (mirror-antithesis):**
  
  | v.13 | v.14 |
  |---|---|
  | *inna'l-**abrāra*** | *wa inna'l-**fujjāra*** |
  | *la-fī **naʿīm*** | *la-fī **jaḥīm*** |
  
  ⭐ **Identical structure, word for word, with only the two key nouns swapped.** And ***naʿīm* / *jaḥīm*** even **rhyme** — the sound makes the two fates feel like **two doors in the same wall.**
- **Grammar bite:** *inna* + accusative subject + *la-fī* (**lām of emphasis** on the predicate). ⭐ **Same frame in both verses — that's what makes the contrast land.**
- **Why this word:** *abrār* (dependable goodness) over a word for "believers" — it emphasizes **consistency**, matching *birr*.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Insān 76:5 (*abrār*) + Insān 76:20 (*naʿīm*) → both land in this one verse.** ⭐ **And this exact sentence repeats in Muṭaffifīn 83:22.**

**13. الْفُجَّارَ ... جَحِيمٍ — *al-fujjāra… jaḥīm*** · v.14
- **Root feelings:** *fujjār* — ⭐ the "**bursting past all restraint**" family — **you own it from ʿAbasa 80:42** (*al-kafaratu'l-**fajarah***) and **Qiyāmah 75:5** (*li-**yafjura** amāmah*), and ***fajr*** (dawn splitting). *jaḥīm* — the "**fiercely blazing**" family — **you own it from Nāziʿāt 79:36, 39** and **Takwīr 81:12**.
- **Here it means:** "And indeed, **the wicked will be in a blaze**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **note the plural form is different from ʿAbasa's.** ʿAbasa 80:42 used ***fajarah*** (*faʿalah* pattern); here it's ***fujjār*** (*fuʿʿāl* pattern). ⭐ **Both are plurals of *fājir*, but *fujjār* is the more INTENSIVE shape** — the same *fuʿʿāl* family as the intensive forms you learned in Nabaʾ.
  
  ⭐ **And here's the beautiful full-circle:** v.3 said the **seas** were ***fujjirat*** (burst open). v.14 says the **people** are ***fujjār*** (burst past limits). ⭐ **SAME ROOT, eleven verses apart, in the same surah.** The seas broke their barriers; **so did these people.** ⭐ **The cosmos does at the end what they did their whole lives.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***fuʿʿāl* is an intensive plural pattern** — *kuffār, fujjār, kuttāb, ḥujjāj*. **Recognize the doubled middle letter + long ā.**
- **Why this word:** *fujjār* over a milder word — it names them by **the mechanism**: no brakes, no boundaries.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **v.3: the SEAS burst (*fujjirat*). v.14: the PEOPLE burst (*fujjār*).** Same root, same surah. ⭐ **They lived like a sea with no shore.**

**14. يَصْلَوْنَهَا — *yaṣlawnahā*** · v.15
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**roasting / being scorched by fire**" family — *ṣaliya* = to be burned/roasted; and ⭐ ***muṣallā*** — no, careful: that's a **different root** (ṣ-l-w for prayer). This one is ص-ل-ي: to **roast**.
- **Here it means:** "**They will [enter to] burn in it** on the Day of Recompense."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **this root runs through the next three surahs** — worth collecting now:
  - **Infiṭār 82:15** — *ya**ṣla**wnahā* (they burn in it)
  - **Muṭaffifīn 83:16** — *la-**ṣāl**ū'l-jaḥīm* (they will burn in the Blaze)
  - **Inshiqāq 84:12** — *wa ya**ṣlā** saʿīrā* (he will burn in a Blaze)
  
  ⭐ **Three consecutive surahs, same verb.** Learn it once here.
  
  And the specific sense — **roasting, being exposed to fire** — is more **prolonged** than "burning." ⭐ **It's the word for meat over flame**, not a quick flash.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **weak-final verb** (ص-ل-ي). Note the plural: *yaṣlawna* — the final weak letter **shifts to a *w*** before the plural ending. Watch how weak-final verbs behave in the plural.
- **Why this word:** *yaṣlawna* over "enter" — because it names **the sensation**, not the location.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ṣaliya* = to ROAST over fire.** ⭐ **Same verb in Infiṭār 82:15, Muṭaffifīn 83:16, Inshiqāq 84:12 — three surahs running.**

**15. بِغَائِبِينَ — *bi-ghāʾibīn*** · v.16
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**absence / hiddenness**" family — *ghāba* = to be absent; ⭐ ***al-ghayb*** = **the Unseen** (which you own from **Takwīr 81:24** — *wa mā huwa ʿala'l-**ghaybi** bi-ḍanīn*).
- **Here it means:** "And they will **not be absent** from it."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the double negative is the point — and it's chilling.** "They will not be **absent**" is a strange way to say "they will stay." ⭐ **Why phrase it as absence?**
  
  Because ***ghāʾib* is the word for someone who steps out** — takes a break, is excused, misses a session. ⭐ **The verse pre-emptively closes the door on any interruption:** no leave, no pause, no time off, **not even briefly out of the room.**
  
  ⭐ **And there's a haunting echo with *al-ghayb* (the Unseen):** they spent their lives treating the Hereafter as ***ghayb*** — absent, unreal, out of sight. ⭐ **Now THEY are the ones who can never be absent from it.** The thing they called "not there" becomes the thing they can never leave.
- **Grammar bite:** another ***bāʾ zāʾidah*** (**extra *bāʾ***) after negation for emphasis — ⭐ **you own this from Takwīr 81:22, 24, 25** (*bi-majnūn, bi-ḍanīn, bi-qawl*). **Four instances across two surahs.**
- **Why this word:** *ghāʾibīn* over "they will remain" — it forecloses **every form of relief**, including a moment's break.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***al-ghayb* = the Unseen** (Takwīr 81:24). ⭐ **They treated the Hereafter as ABSENT; now they can never be absent FROM it.**

## §5 — The Day Nobody Can Help Anybody (v.17–19)

**16. وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ — *wa mā adrāka*** · v.17–18
- **Root feeling:** the "**knowing by perception**" family — *darā* = to become aware; **Form IV *adrā*** = to make someone know.
- **Here it means:** "**And what can make you know** what the Day of Recompense is?" — asked **twice**.
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **MAJOR GRAMMAR GEM — and it's a direct contrast with a word you already own.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare ʿAbasa 80:3: *wa mā **YUDRĪKA*** (present tense) with Infiṭār 82:17: *wa mā **ADRĀKA*** (past tense).**
  
  There's a recognized Qur'anic pattern here:
  - ⭐ ***mā yudrīka*** (**present**) → introduces something the addressee **will NOT be told** — *"how could you know?"* (ʿAbasa 80:3: *perhaps he would purify himself* — unknowable to you.)
  - ⭐ ***mā adrāka*** (**past**) → introduces something that **IS about to be explained** — *"and what made you know…?"* followed by the answer.
  
  ⭐ **Two surahs apart, the same construction in two tenses, doing two opposite jobs.** Catch this once and you'll read it correctly forever.
  
  ⭐ **And the REPETITION here is the balāgha.** Verse 17 asks it. **Verse 18 asks the exact same thing again**, with **ثُمَّ** (*then*) added. ⭐ **Same escalation technique as Nabaʾ 78:4–5** (*kallā sa-yaʿlamūn* / *thumma kallā sa-yaʿlamūn*). **Ask, pause, ask again — heavier.** The repetition says: **you didn't grasp it the first time.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **مَا أَدْرَاكَ = past, followed by explanation. مَا يُدْرِيكَ = present, followed by nothing.** Learn the pair.
- **Why this word:** asking **twice** without answering **either time** makes the Day feel **beyond description** — which is the answer.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **ʿAbasa 80:3 = *yudrīka* (present, you WON'T be told). Infiṭār 82:17 = *adrāka* (past, you WILL be told).** ⭐ **And it's asked TWICE — same escalation as Nabaʾ 78:4–5.**

**17. لَا تَمْلِكُ نَفْسٌ لِّنَفْسٍ شَيْئًا — *lā tamliku nafsun li-nafsin shayʾā*** · v.19
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**ownership / control / power over**" family — *malaka* = to own, to have power over; ***malik*** = **a king**; ***mulk*** = **dominion**; ***mamlūk*** = owned.
- **Here it means:** "**A day when no soul will possess [the power to do] anything for another soul.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **Look at the exact repetition: *nafsun li-nafsin* — "a soul… for a soul."** The **same word twice**, both **indefinite**, side by side. ⭐ **No names, no titles, no relationships.** Not "no father for his son," not "no friend for his friend" — just ⭐ **soul… to soul… nothing.** The grammar **strips away every label** you might have leaned on.
  
  ⭐ **And connect it to ʿAbasa 80:34–36**, where the fleeing was listed: *brother → mother → father → wife → children*. ⭐ **ʿAbasa showed you the RUNNING; Infiṭār gives you the REASON: nobody owns anything they could give.**
  
  ⭐ **Then the final clause is the release: *wa'l-amru yawmaʾidhin lillāh*** — "and the command, that Day, **belongs to Allah**." ⭐ **The *lām* of possession** (*li-llāh*) sits against *lā tamliku* — ⭐ **nobody OWNS anything; He owns everything.** The surah that began with the sky splitting ends with **ownership settled.**
  
  ⭐ **And note the word *al-amr*** — you own it from **Nāziʿāt 79:5** (*al-mudabbirāti **amrā*** — the angels who **manage the affair**). ⭐ **There, angels managed the command. Here, the command reverts entirely to its Owner.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***lā tamliku* + *li-*** — "possesses [nothing] **for** someone." The **لِـ** marks the beneficiary. Note *shayʾan* (accusative, indefinite) = "**anything at all**" — **the smallest possible unit is denied.**
- **Why this word:** *malaka* (to own/control) rather than "help" — because **intercession, wealth, status are all forms of OWNERSHIP**, and the verse cancels the whole category at once.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***malik* = a KING; *mulk* = dominion.** ⭐ **"A soul… for a soul… nothing."** No names, no titles. ⭐ **And the last word of the surah: *lillāh* — it all belongs to Him.**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐ **Infiṭār vs. Takwīr — the twin surahs.** Sky: *infaṭarat* (**split**) vs. *kushiṭat* (**flayed**). Stars: *intatharat* (**scattered**) vs. *inkadarat* (**dimmed**). Seas: *fujjirat* (**burst**) vs. *sujjirat* (**stoked**). ⚠️ **Easy to swap. Learn them as a table, not separately.**
2. ⭐⭐ **مَا أَدْرَاكَ (past) vs. مَا يُدْرِيكَ (present)** — v.17 vs. **ʿAbasa 80:3**. **Past = an explanation follows. Present = it doesn't.** ⚠️ **One letter and a vowel apart.**
3. ⭐ **فُجِّرَتْ (v.3) and الْفُجَّارَ (v.14)** — **same root**, same surah, eleven verses apart. **Seas burst their limits; so did they.**
4. ⭐ **الْكَرِيمِ (v.6) and كِرَامًا (v.11)** — same root. ⭐ **The Lord is generous; the scribes are noble.** Both mercies inside a warning.
5. ⭐ **The address switches from singular to plural at v.9** — *gharra**ka**, sawwā**ka**, rakkaba**k*** (you) → *tukadhdhibū**n*** (you all). ⚠️ **Don't miss the shift.**
6. ⭐ **عَدَلَكَ (v.7)** — this is the **JUSTICE** root (*ʿadl*), used for **bodily symmetry**. ⚠️ **Not just "shaped you."**
7. ⭐ **بُعْثِرَتْ (v.4)** — a **quadriliteral** verb: **turned upside down and dumped out**, not merely "opened."
8. **الدِّينِ (v.9, 15, 17, 18)** — appears **four times**. Remember: ***dīn* / *dayn* (debt)** — **the day the account settles.**
