# Surah Al-Balad (90) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–20)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**It asks a question, refuses to answer it, and then answers it with four rhyming words.**

⭐ **v.11:** *"But he has not attempted **the steep pass**."*
⭐ **v.12:** *"And what can make you know what **the steep pass** is?"*
⭐ **v.13–16:** **the answer** — and it isn't a mountain at all:

> ***Fakku raqabah*** — freeing a **neck** (*raqabah*)
> ***Miskīnan dhā matrabah*** — a poor man in the **dust** (*matrabah*)
> ***Yatīman dhā maqrabah*** — an orphan of **near kin** (*maqrabah*)
> ⭐ *and later*, ***aṣḥābu'l-maymanah*** — the companions of the **right** (*maymanah*)

⭐ **Listen to the rhyme: *ʿaqabah… raqabah… masghabah… maqrabah… matrabah… maymanah… mashʾamah*.** ⭐ **Seven words, one drumbeat.** ⭐ **The "steep pass" is defined by words that *sound like it*.**

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐ **The mountain you have to climb is made of people.** ⭐ **A slave's neck, a hungry man's stomach, an orphan's need. ⭐ That's the *ʿaqabah*.**

---

## The Big Picture

1. **An oath by Makkah** (v.1–3) — ⭐ *and you, [O Prophet], are free of restriction in this city.*
2. **Man was made in toil** (v.4) — ⭐ *kabad* — struggle is the design, not a malfunction.
3. **Two arrogant assumptions** (v.5–7) — *does he think none has power over him? Does he think no one saw?*
4. **Three gifts, then the choice** (v.8–10) — two eyes, a tongue, two lips… and **the two paths**.
5. **The steep pass** (v.11–16) — ⭐ **freeing a neck, feeding on a day of hunger.**
6. **Two companies** (v.17–20) — the **right hand** and the **ill-omened**.

## §1 — An Oath by Makkah (v.1–4)

**1. لَا أُقْسِمُ بِهَٰذَا الْبَلَدِ — *lā uqsimu bi-hādha'l-balad*** · v.1
- **Root feelings:** *uqsimu* — ⭐ the "**dividing / apportioning**" family (*qismah* = division) — an oath **settles** a disputed matter. *balad* — **a town, a city, a country**.
- **Here it means:** "**I swear by this city.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **you own this construction from THREE surahs already:**
  - **Qiyāmah 75:1** — *lā uqsimu bi-yawmi'l-qiyāmah*
  - **Takwīr 81:15** — *fa-lā uqsimu bi'l-khunnas*
  - **Inshiqāq 84:16** — *fa-lā uqsimu bi'sh-shafaq*
  - **Balad 90:1** — *lā uqsimu bi-hādha'l-balad*
  
  ⭐ **Four surahs, same opening move.** ⭐ **The **لا** either (a) **rebuts an objection** before the oath lands, or (b) **intensifies** it. Both readings are classical.
  
  ⭐ **And note *hādhā* — "THIS city."** ⭐ **The demonstrative makes it immediate and physical: the ground under the Prophet's ﷺ feet as he spoke.** ⭐ **Not "a city" — *this one*, right here.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **the بـ is *bāʾ al-qasam*** (the "by" of oaths), which is why *al-balad* is **genitive**. ⭐ **Present tense *uqsimu* (not *aqsamtu*) = a living, renewed oath.**
- **Why this word:** *balad* over "Makkah" — ⭐ **it's the ordinary word for a town**, which makes v.2's twist land harder.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **FOUR surahs open this way: 75:1, 81:15, 84:16, 90:1.** ⭐ **And *hādhā* — "THIS city," the ground he was standing on.**

**2. وَأَنتَ حِلٌّ بِهَٰذَا الْبَلَدِ — *wa anta ḥillun bi-hādha'l-balad*** · v.2
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**untying / making lawful / releasing**" family — *ḥalla* = to untie a knot, to settle in a place; ⭐ ***ḥalāl* = lawful*** (literally "**untied**," released from restriction) — the opposite of *ḥarām* (bound/forbidden). ⭐ **And *iḥlāl* = leaving iḥrām** — the moment restrictions are **lifted** in Hajj!
- **Here it means:** "**and you are free of restriction in this city**" — several classical readings, explored below.
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **one of the most layered verses in this juzʾ, and the root's meanings all fit:**
  - **(a) A prophecy of the Conquest** — ⭐ **the Prophet ﷺ would one day enter Makkah with fighting made lawful for him for a period**, though he entered with famous mercy. Makkah is *ḥaram* (**sacred, restricted**) for everyone — ⭐ **and this verse foretells the exception.**
  - **(b) "You are a dweller in this city"** — from *ḥalla* = **to settle/reside.**
  - **(c) A note of pathos** — ⭐ **"they treat you as *ḥalāl* (fair game) in this sacred city"** — the sanctuary protects animals and plants, but not the Prophet ﷺ.
  
  ⭐ **The root-irony is the point either way:** ⭐ **Makkah is the *ḥaram* — the place where everything is BOUND.** ⭐ **And in that bound place, this word means UNBOUND.**
  
  ⭐ **The Hajj hook is free:** ⭐ ***iḥrām* = binding yourself; *iḥlāl* = releasing.*** ⭐ **You do both every pilgrimage. Same root.**
- **Grammar bite:** *ḥillun* is a **verbal noun used adjectivally** — indefinite nominative, *khabar* of *anta*. ⭐ **Note the verse **repeats** *bi-hādha'l-balad* verbatim from v.1** — an emphatic echo.
- **Why this word:** *ḥill* is chosen ⭐ **precisely because Makkah is *ḥaram*.** The whole verse lives on that contrast.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Ḥalāl* = "UNTIED."* ⭐ ***Iḥrām* (bound) / *iḥlāl* (released)** — you do both in Hajj. ⭐ **Makkah is the place where everything is BOUND — and this word means UNBOUND.**

**3. وَوَالِدٍ وَمَا وَلَدَ — *wa wālidin wa mā walad*** · v.3
- **Root feeling:** the "**begetting / giving birth**" family — *wālid* = **a father/parent**; *walad* = **a child**; *wilādah* = birth.
- **Here it means:** "**and by a begetter and what he begot.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the third oath, and it's deliberately unspecified.**
  
  ⭐ **Note both are INDEFINITE: "a begetter" and "what he begot" — no names.** ⭐ **Many commentators read it as **Ibrāhīm and Ismāʿīl**, given the Makkan setting (they built the Kaʿbah). ⭐ **Others read it universally: *every* parent and *every* child.**
  
  ⭐ **And the universal reading connects straight to v.4:** ⭐ **swear by every parent and child — then say "We created man in *kabad* (toil)."** ⭐ **Because the very first thing any parent and child experience together is **labour and pain.** ⭐ **The oath is the evidence for the claim.**
  
  ⭐ **Note the shift: *wālid* is masculine ("a begetter") and *mā walad* uses *mā* ("WHAT he begot"), not *man* ("who").** ⭐ **Some read this as emphasizing the *nature* of the offspring rather than identity.**
- **Grammar bite:** both genitive after the oath-*wāw*. ⭐ ***Mā walad*** is a relative clause — "**that which** he begot."
- **Why this word:** the **indefiniteness** lets the oath work at both levels — the specific founders and every family that ever existed.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **"A begetter and what he begot" — no names given.** ⭐ **Possibly Ibrāhīm & Ismāʿīl; possibly every parent and child.** ⭐ **Either way it sets up v.4: parents and children know about TOIL.**

**4. فِي كَبَدٍ — *fī kabad*** · v.4
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ a superb root — ***kabid* = THE LIVER***, and by extension ⭐ **the middle/core of something**. From that: *kabad* = ⭐ **hardship, strain, toil** — the kind that hits you **in the gut**. (Arabic locates deep feeling in the liver the way English locates it in the heart.)
- **Here it means:** "**We have certainly created man in toil / hardship.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **THE THESIS OF THE SURAH, and the word choice is anatomical.**
  
  ⭐ **It doesn't say man *encounters* hardship. It says he was created ***fī kabad*** — **IN it**, as his native environment.** ⭐ **Struggle isn't an interruption of your life. It's the medium of it.**
  
  ⭐ **And *kabid* = the LIVER** — ⭐ **the deep interior organ.** So *kabad* means hardship felt ⭐ **in your core**, not on the surface.
  
  ⭐ **Why does this matter so much?** ⭐ **Because it pre-empts the complaint of Fajr 89:16** — *"my Lord has humiliated me!"* ⭐ **If you know you were *built* for toil, you stop reading hardship as a verdict.** ⭐ **Fajr showed the man who misread the test; Balad explains why he misread it.**
  
  ⭐ **And it sets up v.11:** ⭐ **you were made for climbing — so climb the *ʿaqabah*.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **لَقَدْ + past = strong affirmation** ("We have **certainly**"). ⭐ ***Fī*** here means **immersed in**, not merely "with."
- **Why this word:** *kabad* over a generic word for difficulty — ⭐ **it names hardship by the organ that feels it.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Kabid* = THE LIVER** — hardship felt **in your gut**. ⭐ **Not "man MEETS hardship" — man was created IN it.** ⭐ **Struggle is the design, not the malfunction.** ⭐ **This answers Fajr 89:16's complaint.**

## §2 — Two Arrogant Assumptions (v.5–7)

**5. أَيَحْسَبُ أَن لَّن يَقْدِرَ عَلَيْهِ أَحَدٌ — *a-yaḥsabu an lan yaqdira ʿalayhi aḥad*** · v.5
- **Root feelings:** *yaḥsabu* — ⭐ the "**counting / reckoning / calculating**" family (*ḥisāb* = accounting) — by extension, **to suppose** (the mind "computing" a conclusion). *yaqdira* — ⭐ the "**power + precise measuring**" family — you own it from **Qiyāmah 75:4**, **Insān 76:16**, **ʿAbasa 80:19**, **Aʿlā 87:3**, **Fajr 89:16**.
- **Here it means:** "**Does he think that no one has power over him?**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **you own this opening EXACTLY — it's the same word that opens and closes Qiyāmah.**
  - **Qiyāmah 75:3** — *a-**yaḥsabu**'l-insānu allan najmaʿa ʿiẓāmah*
  - **Qiyāmah 75:36** — *a-**yaḥsabu**'l-insānu an yutraka sudā*
  - **Balad 90:5** — *a-**yaḥsabu** an lan yaqdira ʿalayhi aḥad*
  - **Balad 90:7** — *a-**yaḥsabu** an lam yarahu aḥad*
  
  ⭐ **Qiyāmah asks it twice (opening and closing). Balad asks it twice (v.5 and v.7).** ⭐ **Four instances of the same rebuking question across two surahs.**
  
  ⭐ **And the irony is built into the root:** ⭐ **the *calculating* creature (*yaḥsabu*) makes a fatal *miscalculation* about the Day of *calculation* (*ḥisāb*).**
  
  ⭐ **Note the *qadara* root again — FIFTH+ appearance.** ⭐ **Here it's the "**power**" sense: *no one has power over him*.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **أَ = *hamzat al-istifhām*** fused to the verb. ⭐ ***Lan* + present = emphatic future negation** ("will never"), which makes his assumption absolute — ⭐ **and therefore absolutely wrong.**
- **Why this word:** *yaḥsabu* (to conclude by reasoning) rather than *ẓanna* (a loose guess) — ⭐ **it targets his confident verdict**, which the root itself indicts.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***A-yaḥsabu* opens AND closes Qiyāmah (75:3, 75:36), and appears TWICE here (90:5, 90:7).** ⭐ **The *calculating* man miscalculates the day of *calculation*.**

**6. أَهْلَكْتُ مَالًا لُّبَدًا — *ahlaktu mālan lubadā*** · v.6
- **Root feelings:** *ahlaktu* — ⭐ **Form IV of *halaka* (to perish)** = **to destroy / squander**; *halāk* = destruction. *lubad* — ⭐ the "**piling up / matting together**" family — *labada* = to stick together in a mass; ⭐ ***lubdah* = a matted clump*** (as of felted wool).
- **Here it means:** "**He says, 'I have squandered vast wealth.'**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the verb he chooses convicts him without any comment needed.**
  
  ⭐ **He's boasting.** ⭐ **But the word he uses for "spent" is ***ahlaktu*** — literally ⭐ **"I DESTROYED / I caused to perish."** ⭐ **He thinks he's saying "look how much I spent"; the Qurʾān lets his own verb say "look how much I annihilated."**
  
  ⭐ **He is proud of *destruction*.** ⭐ **And note what's missing: no mention of what it was spent ON.** ⭐ **The boast is about the *amount*, not the *purpose*.**
  
  ⭐ **And *lubad* means "heaped, matted, piled in a solid mass"** — ⭐ **wealth so abundant it clumps together.** ⭐ **Compare Fajr 89:20** — *tuḥibbūna'l-māla **ḥubban jammā*** ("you love wealth with an **overflowing** love"). ⭐ **Two consecutive surahs, both on excessive attachment to *māl*.**
- **Grammar bite:** **Form IV** past, 1st person. *Lubadā* is an adjective for *mālan*, accusative, carrying the rhyme.
- **Why this word:** *ahlaktu* over *anfaqtu* (spent) — ⭐ **because he's not spending, he's destroying, and the Qurʾān makes him say so.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **He boasts: "I DESTROYED vast wealth."** ⭐ **He picked the verb *ahlaka* — to annihilate.** ⭐ **Proud of destruction.** ⭐ **And *lubad* = wealth so much it CLUMPS.** ⭐ **(Compare Fajr 89:20's *ḥubban jammā*.)**

**7. أَن لَّمْ يَرَهُ أَحَدٌ — *an lam yarahu aḥad*** · v.7
- **Root feeling:** *raʾā* — **to see** (*ruʾyah* = seeing; *raʾy* = an opinion, a "view").
- **Here it means:** "**Does he think that no one saw him?**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the second assumption, and it's more damning than the first.**
  
  ⭐ **v.5 was about POWER: "no one can control me."** ⭐ **v.7 is about OBSERVATION: "no one saw me."** ⭐ **Together they're the two pillars of every wrongdoing: *I can't be stopped* and *I can't be caught*.**
  
  ⭐ **And v.7 is the more revealing.** ⭐ **A man who boasts about squandering (v.6) implies he wants to be SEEN spending — but assumes he wasn't seen doing whatever he actually did.** ⭐ **He wants witnesses for his generosity and none for his life.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Fajr 89:14** — *inna rabbaka la-**bi'l-mirṣād*** ("your Lord is at the **lookout**") — ⭐ **and Ṭāriq 86:4** — *in kullu nafsin lammā ʿalayhā **ḥāfiẓ*** ("over every soul there is a **guardian**"). ⭐ **Three consecutive surahs answering the same delusion.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **لَمْ + jussive = past negation** ("did not see"). ⭐ **Note the contrast with v.5's *lan* (future).** ⭐ **v.5 denies future power; v.7 denies past observation. Both tenses covered.**
- **Why this word:** the plain verb "see" is stronger than any theological term — ⭐ **it's the simplest possible refutation.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **v.5 = "no one can STOP me" (*lan*, future). v.7 = "no one SAW me" (*lam*, past).** ⭐ **The two pillars of wrongdoing.** ⭐ **Answered by Fajr 89:14 (*mirṣād*) and Ṭāriq 86:4 (*ḥāfiẓ*).**

## §3 — Three Gifts, Then the Choice (v.8–10)

**8. عَيْنَيْنِ ... لِسَانًا وَشَفَتَيْنِ — *ʿaynayn… lisānan wa shafatayn*** · v.8–9
- **Root feelings:** *ʿaynayn* — **two eyes** (dual of *ʿayn*; ⭐ *ʿayn* also means **a spring** and **the essence** of a thing). *lisān* — **a tongue** (⭐ also **a language**! — *al-lughah al-ʿarabiyyah* / *lisān ʿarabī*). *shafatayn* — **two lips** (dual of *shafah*).
- **Here it means:** "**Have We not made for him two eyes, and a tongue and two lips?**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the answer to v.7, delivered as a body inventory — and the logic is precise.**
  
  ⭐ **He said "no one SAW me" (v.7).** ⭐ **The reply: *"didn't We give you EYES?"*** ⭐ **The One who **manufactured sight** is being told that seeing is impossible. ⭐ **The refutation is in your own face.**
  
  ⭐ **And notice the DUALS: two eyes, two lips — but ONE tongue.** ⭐ **Classical scholars noted this: two eyes to see with, two lips to close over the tongue, ⭐ **and only one tongue — kept behind two gates.**
  
  ⭐ **The three gifts also map onto the surah's demands:** ⭐ **eyes (to SEE the orphan and the poor), tongue and lips (to speak up for them, and to *urge one another* — the very thing Fajr 89:18 said they failed to do).**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **DUAL nouns in the accusative: *ʿaynayni*, *shafatayni*** — ⚠️ ⭐ **note the ending *-ayn* (accusative/genitive) vs *-ān* (nominative).** ⭐ **You met this in Qiyāmah 75:39 (*az-zawjayn*).** ⭐ **And *a-lam najʿal* = a rhetorical question expecting "yes."**
- **Why this word:** naming **sensory equipment** answers a claim about **being unobserved** — ⭐ **the reply is physical, not philosophical.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **He said "no one SAW me" → *"didn't We give you EYES?"*** ⭐ **TWO eyes, TWO lips, ONE tongue** — the tongue kept behind **two gates**. ⚠️ ⭐ **Dual: *-ayn* (acc./gen.) vs *-ān* (nom.).**

**9. النَّجْدَيْنِ — *an-najdayn*** · v.10
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**high, elevated, open ground**" family — ***najd* = raised land, an elevated plateau*** (hence **Najd**, the highland region of Arabia!). ⭐ **Also *najdah* = help/rescue** (from the sense of standing out prominently to aid someone).
- **Here it means:** "**And We guided him to the two heights / the two paths.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the word for "path" here is a word for HIGH GROUND, and that's the whole design.**
  
  ⭐ **Most translations say "the two ways," which loses the image.** ⭐ ***Najd* means an ELEVATED, VISIBLE path** — ⭐ **high ground you can see clearly from a distance.** ⭐ **So both roads — good and evil — are described as **obvious**. ⭐ **Neither is hidden.**
  
  ⭐ **That answers a possible excuse before it's raised:** ⭐ **"I didn't know which way to go."** ⭐ **Both routes are on high ground. You could see them both.**
  
  ⭐ **And it sets up v.11 with a mountaineering metaphor already running:** ⭐ **you were shown two heights (*najdayn*), and you didn't attempt the steep pass (*ʿaqabah*).** ⭐ **The vocabulary of terrain runs straight through.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **DUAL, accusative (*an-najdayni*)** — third dual in three verses (*ʿaynayn, shafatayn, najdayn*). ⭐ **The surah is drilling the dual form.**
- **Why this word:** *najd* over *sabīl* (path) — ⭐ **because *najd* means you can SEE it.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Najd* = HIGH, VISIBLE GROUND** (like the region **Najd**). ⭐ **Both paths are on high ground — neither is hidden.** ⭐ **"I didn't know" isn't available.** ⭐ **Third dual in a row: eyes, lips, paths.**

## §4 — The Steep Pass (v.11–16)

**10. فَلَا اقْتَحَمَ الْعَقَبَةَ — *fa-la'qtaḥama'l-ʿaqabah*** · v.11
- **Root feelings:** *iqtaḥama* — ⭐ a **magnificent** word: the "**plunging in headlong / storming through with difficulty**" family — *iqtiḥām* = ⭐ **to throw yourself into something hard and frightening**, to charge in. (Modern Arabic uses *iqtiḥām* for **storming a building**.) *ʿaqabah* — ⭐ **a steep mountain pass**, a difficult uphill road — from *ʿ-q-b*, the "**following / consequence / heel**" family (*ʿāqibah* = **the outcome**; *ʿuqbā* = the final end — ⭐ **which closes Shams 91:15!**).
- **Here it means:** "**But he has not attempted the steep ascent.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the two words together are the surah's centre of gravity.**
  
  ⭐ ***Iqtaḥama* is not "climbed" — it's "PLUNGED IN HEADLONG."** ⭐ **It's the word for charging into something you'd normally avoid.** ⭐ **Which tells you the *ʿaqabah* is not gentle. It requires a running start and a decision.**
  
  ⭐ **And *ʿaqabah*'s root is the "consequence" root** — ⭐ **so the steep pass is literally named after **what comes after**.** ⭐ **It's the climb whose whole meaning is its outcome.**
  
  ⭐ **Now connect it to v.4:** ⭐ **man was created *fī kabad* (IN TOIL).** ⭐ **So you're already climbing — you're built for it.** ⭐ **The charge in v.11 isn't "climbing is too hard for you." It's "you're toiling anyway, and you didn't spend the toil on THIS."**
  
  ⭐ **And you'll meet the root again as the last word of Shams 91:15** (*ʿuqbāhā*, "its consequence"). ⭐ **Two consecutive surahs, same root, opening and closing.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⚠️ ⭐ **the *lā* here is a NEGATION of the past** ("he has **not** attempted"), a known construction ⭐ **often read as implying "he *ought* to have."** ⭐ **Some readers take it as a wish/incitement: "would that he had!"** Both are attested.
- **Why this word:** *iqtaḥama* frames righteousness as ⭐ **something you charge at**, not something you drift into.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Iqtiḥām* = STORMING a building** in modern Arabic. ⭐ **Not "climbed" — PLUNGED IN HEADLONG.** ⭐ **And *ʿaqabah* ← the root of *ʿāqibah* (the OUTCOME).** ⭐ **You were made *fī kabad* (v.4) — you're climbing anyway.**

**11. فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ — *fakku raqabah*** · v.13
- **Root feelings:** *fakk* — ⭐ the "**undoing / unlocking / releasing**" family — *fakka* = to untie, to unfasten, ⭐ **to break something open**; *infikāk* = separation. *raqabah* — ⭐ **A NECK** — and by a beautiful Qurʾānic convention, ⭐ **"a neck" means "a slave"** (the part standing for the whole, because the neck bore the collar). ⭐ **Also *raqīb* = a watcher** (one who watches over — from craning the neck!), and *murāqabah* = surveillance.
- **Here it means:** "**[It is] the freeing of a neck.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the first answer to "what is the steep pass?" — and it's stunningly concrete.**
  
  ⭐ **The question in v.12 was cosmic-sounding: *"what can make you know what the steep ascent is?"*** ⭐ **You expect something vast.** ⭐ **The answer is: **untie a neck.**
  
  ⭐ **And the *kināya* (metonymy) is powerful:** ⭐ **the human being is called simply "a NECK"** — ⭐ **because that's all a slave was reduced to: the part you put a collar on.** ⭐ **The word itself carries the indignity, which is why freeing it matters.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Insān 76:4** — *aʿtadnā li'l-kāfirīna salāsila wa **aghlālā*** (**chains and shackles**), where *ghull* is specifically **the neck-collar**. ⭐ **Insān: necks bound in Hell. Balad: unbind a neck now.** ⭐ **What you do to necks here is what happens to yours there.**
  
  ⭐ **And listen: *ʿaqabah* → *raqabah*.** ⭐ **The answer RHYMES with the question.** ⭐ **The mountain and the neck sound the same.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***iḍāfah*** ("the freeing **of** a neck"); *fakku* is a **verbal noun** (*maṣdar*). ⭐ **Note there's a well-known variant reading as a verb (*fakka raqabatan*) — both are established.**
- **Why this word:** *raqabah* (a neck) rather than *ʿabd* (a slave) — ⭐ **because it names the *point of bondage*, not the person's status.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Raqabah* = A NECK = a slave** (the collared part). ⭐ **The cosmic-sounding question gets answered: "UNTIE A NECK."** ⭐ ***ʿaqabah* → *raqabah* — the answer RHYMES with the question.** ⭐ **And Insān 76:4 had *aghlāl* — neck-collars in Hell.**

**12. يَوْمٍ ذِي مَسْغَبَةٍ — *yawmin dhī masghabah*** · v.14
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**severe hunger**" family — *saghiba* = to be famished; ⭐ ***masghabah* = a state or occasion of hunger*** — a noun of place/state built on the *mafʿalah* pattern.
- **Here it means:** "**or feeding on a day of severe hunger.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the timing is the entire moral point, and it's easy to miss.**
  
  ⭐ **It doesn't say "feeding the hungry."** ⭐ **It says feeding ***on a day of hunger*** — ⭐ **a day when EVERYONE is starving, including YOU.** ⭐ **A famine day. A day when giving food away means going without.**
  
  ⭐ **That's why it belongs in the *ʿaqabah*.** ⭐ **Feeding someone from surplus is easy. Feeding them when you're hungry is a mountain.**
  
  ⭐ **And you own the exact scene from Insān 76:8** — *wa yuṭʿimūna'ṭ-ṭaʿāma **ʿalā ḥubbihi** miskīnan wa yatīman wa asīrā* ("they feed, **despite loving it**, the poor, the orphan, and the captive"). ⭐ **Insān showed people doing it. Balad names it as the climb.** ⭐ **Same three-category structure too.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***ذِي* = "possessor of"** — ⭐ **the five special nouns again** (genitive: *dhī*). ⭐ **Fourth appearance across your surahs (Takwīr 81:20, Fajr 89:5, 89:10, here).** ⭐ **The *mafʿalah* pattern (*masghabah, maqrabah, matrabah*) recurs three times in three verses.**
- **Why this word:** *masghabah* names ⭐ **a *day* of hunger**, not a hungry person — which is what makes the giving costly.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **Not "feed the hungry" — feed **ON A DAY WHEN EVERYONE IS STARVING**, including you.** ⭐ **That's why it's a mountain.** ⭐ **And Insān 76:8 showed exactly this: feeding *despite loving the food*.**

**13. يَتِيمًا ذَا مَقْرَبَةٍ — *yatīman dhā maqrabah*** · v.15
- **Root feelings:** *yatīm* — **an orphan** — you own it from **Insān 76:8** and **Fajr 89:17**. *maqrabah* — ⭐ the "**nearness / kinship**" family — *qarīb* = **near** and **a relative**; *qarābah* = **kinship**; ⭐ ***al-muqarrabūn* = those brought near*** (Muṭaffifīn 83:21, 28).
- **Here it means:** "**an orphan of near relationship.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the detail "of near kin" is doing something specific and uncomfortable.**
  
  ⭐ **Why specify a RELATED orphan?** ⭐ **Two classical readings, both excellent:**
  - **(a) The reward is greater** — you've combined charity with maintaining family ties (*ṣilat ar-raḥim*).
  - **(b) ⭐ It's HARDER.** ⭐ **A relative's orphan may be entitled to inheritance you're holding.** ⭐ **Helping a stranger costs money; helping a related orphan may cost you an argument, a claim, and your pride.**
  
  ⭐ **And reading (b) connects directly to Fajr 89:19** — *wa **ta'kulūna't-turātha** aklan lammā* ("**you devour inheritance**, sweeping it all up"). ⭐ **Fajr accused them of eating the orphan's inheritance. Balad, the very next surah, names the opposite as the steep climb.** ⭐ **Consecutive surahs, one continuous argument.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***dhā* — accusative** of the five special nouns (compare *dhī* genitive in v.14). ⭐ **Same word, different case, one verse apart — a free grammar lesson.**
- **Why this word:** *maqrabah* (kinship) rather than a general word — ⭐ **because proximity makes it harder, not easier.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **A RELATED orphan — harder, because he may have a CLAIM on you.** ⭐ **Fajr 89:19 accused them of devouring the orphan's inheritance; Balad 90:15 names the reverse as the climb.** ⭐ ⚠️ **Note *dhā* (acc., v.15) vs *dhī* (gen., v.14).**

**14. مِسْكِينًا ذَا مَتْرَبَةٍ — *miskīnan dhā matrabah*** · v.16
- **Root feelings:** *miskīn* — ⭐ the "**stillness / being stuck**" family (*sakana* = to be still) — ⭐ **someone so poor they can't move up.** *matrabah* — ⭐⭐ **the DUST root!** — ***turāb* = dust/earth*** — you own it from **Nabaʾ 78:40** (*yā laytanī kuntu **turābā***) and **Nabaʾ 78:33** (*atrābā*).
- **Here it means:** "**or a poor person in the dust.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **THE BEST CROSS-SURAH CONNECTION IN THIS SURAH — and it's a genuine payoff.**
  
  ⭐ ***Matrabah* literally means "a state of DUST"*** — ⭐ **someone so destitute they're **clinging to the bare earth**, with nothing between them and the ground. ⭐ **No mat, no bedding, no possessions — just dust.**
  
  ⭐ **Now recall the *t-r-b* root's journey:**
  - **Nabaʾ 78:33** — ***atrāb*** — "**well-matched companions**" in Paradise (from children who **played in the same dust**)
  - **Nabaʾ 78:40** — ***turābā*** — the denier's wish: "**I wish I were DUST**"
  - **Balad 90:16** — ***matrabah*** — the poor man **lying in the dust**
  
  ⭐ **One root: the company of Paradise, the wish to be nothing, and the person you're being told to lift out of the dirt.**
  
  ⭐ **And the moral logic is beautiful:** ⭐ **the denier in Nabaʾ *wishes* he were dust to escape.** ⭐ **The poor man in Balad *is* in the dust, right now, and you can do something about it.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***matrabah*** — same ***mafʿalah*** pattern as *masghabah* and *maqrabah*. ⭐ **Three in three verses, all rhyming with *ʿaqabah* and *raqabah*.** ⭐ **The rhyme IS the structure.**
- **Why this word:** *matrabah* over "very poor" — ⭐ **it puts him physically on the ground**, which is what makes lifting him a climb.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Matrabah* = "IN THE DUST"** — from ***turāb***. ⭐ **Nabaʾ 78:33 (*atrāb*, Paradise's companions), Nabaʾ 78:40 (*turābā*, "I wish I were dust"), Balad 90:16 (*matrabah*, the man in the dust).** ⭐ **Three uses, one root.** ⭐ **He's already in the dust — go lift him.**

## §5 — Two Companies (v.17–20)

**15. تَوَاصَوْا بِالصَّبْرِ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالْمَرْحَمَةِ — *tawāṣaw bi'ṣ-ṣabri wa tawāṣaw bi'l-marḥamah*** · v.17
- **Root feelings:** *tawāṣaw* — ⭐ the "**enjoining / advising / bequeathing**" family — *waṣiyyah* = ⭐ **a WILL/bequest**; *awṣā* = to enjoin. ⭐ **Form VI = MUTUAL: advising ONE ANOTHER.** *marḥamah* — ⭐ the "**mercy / womb**" family — ***raḥmah* = mercy**; ⭐ ***raḥim* = THE WOMB***; *ar-Raḥmān/ar-Raḥīm*.
- **Here it means:** "**Then he was of those who believed and enjoined one another to patience and enjoined one another to compassion.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the *ʿaqabah* turns out to require a COMMUNITY, not just an individual — and *tawāṣaw* is how you know.**
  
  ⭐ **Form VI (*tawāṣaw*) is MUTUAL** — ⭐ **not "he advised" but "they advised **each other**."** ⭐ **The climb can't be done alone; you need people urging you.**
  
  ⭐ **And this is the exact answer to Fajr 89:18** — *wa lā **taḥāḍḍūna** ʿalā ṭaʿāmi'l-miskīn* ("you do not **urge one another** to feed the poor"). ⭐ **Both are Form VI mutual verbs.** ⭐ **Fajr's charge: you don't urge each other. Balad's solution: they urge each other.** ⭐ **Consecutive surahs, problem and answer.**
  
  ⭐ **And *marḥamah* shares a root with *raḥim*, THE WOMB** — ⭐ **so "compassion" in Arabic is built from the organ that carries a child.** ⭐ **The mercy being asked for is **womb-mercy**: the instinctive, protective kind.** ⭐ **Which is exactly right for a passage about orphans.**
  
  ⭐ **Note also *thumma* ("THEN he was of those who believed")** — ⭐ **many scholars note that *thumma* here isn't chronological but indicates **rank**: this is the higher, weightier step.** ⭐ **The deeds of v.13–16 without *īmān* don't complete the climb.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form VI (تَفَاعَلَ) = mutual** — ⭐ **you've now met it three times: *yatasāʾalūn* (78:1), *taḥāḍḍūn* (89:18), *tawāṣaw* (90:17).** ⭐ **Spot the *ta-* + long *ā*.** Note *tawāṣaw* is weak-final, so the plural ending contracts.
- **Why this word:** *marḥamah* (the noun of the *raḥim* root) rather than a generic word for kindness — ⭐ **it names mercy at its most instinctive.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Raḥim* = THE WOMB.** Compassion = **womb-mercy**. ⭐ **And *tawāṣaw* is FORM VI (mutual) — the exact answer to Fajr 89:18's *taḥāḍḍūn*.** ⭐ **The climb needs a community.**

**16. أَصْحَابُ الْمَيْمَنَةِ — *aṣḥābu'l-maymanah*** · v.18
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**right hand / blessing / good fortune**" family — ***yamīn* = the RIGHT HAND*** (and an **oath**, sworn with the right hand!); ⭐ ***yumn* = blessing, good fortune***; ⭐ **and *al-Yaman* — the country — from the same root** (the land "on the right" as you face east, and the "blessed" land).
- **Here it means:** "**Those are the companions of the right hand.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the word carries BOTH meanings simultaneously and you shouldn't choose:**
  - ⭐ **"the right side"** — where the record is handed to you (⭐ **Inshiqāq 84:7** — *man ūtiya kitābahu **bi-yamīnih***!)
  - ⭐ **"blessedness / good fortune"** — from *yumn*
  
  ⭐ **So *aṣḥāb al-maymanah* = simultaneously "the people of the right hand" AND "the people of blessing."** ⭐ **In Arabic these are the same idea.**
  
  ⭐ **And you own the receiving-scene from Inshiqāq 84:7–9** — ⭐ **the man given his record in his right hand, reckoned easily, returning to his family *masrūrā* (delighted).** ⭐ **Balad names the group; Inshiqāq shows the moment.**
- **Grammar bite:** *aṣḥāb* = plural of *ṣāḥib* (**companion**) — ⭐ **you own it from ʿAbasa 80:36 (*ṣāḥibatih*, his wife) and Takwīr 81:22 (*ṣāḥibukum*, "your companion").**
- **Why this word:** *maymanah* over a plain word for the saved — ⭐ **it embeds *blessing* into the very name of the group.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Yamīn* = RIGHT HAND; *yumn* = BLESSING; *al-Yaman* = the country.** ⭐ **All one root.** ⭐ **"People of the right" = "people of blessing" — same word.** ⭐ **See the moment in Inshiqāq 84:7.**

**17. أَصْحَابُ الْمَشْأَمَةِ — *aṣḥābu'l-mashʾamah*** · v.19
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**left hand / ill omen / misfortune**" family — *shuʾm* = ⭐ **bad luck, ill omen**; *ash-shimāl* = the **left**; ⭐ **and *ash-Shām* (Greater Syria) — from the same root** (the land "on the left" as you face east).
- **Here it means:** "**they are the companions of the left / of ill fortune.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **a perfect mirror of v.18, built the same way:**
  
  | v.18 | v.19 |
  |---|---|
  | *aṣḥābu'l-**maymanah*** | *aṣḥābu'l-**mashʾamah*** |
  | **right hand** + **blessing** | **left hand** + **ill fortune** |
  | *al-**Yaman*** (the country) | *ash-**Shām*** (the country) |
  
  ⭐ **Both words carry a direction AND a fortune, and both gave their names to a country.** ⭐ **Arabic's geography and its moral vocabulary run on the same roots.**
  
  ⭐ **And note the grammatical difference:** ⭐ **v.18 says *ulāʾika hum* ("THOSE are they") for the right; v.19 says *hum* ("THEY are") for the left.** ⭐ **Both use the separating pronoun for emphasis — the two groups are being sharply distinguished.**
  
  ⭐ **You'll also see the pairing in Inshiqāq 84:10** — *man ūtiya kitābahu **warāʾa ẓahrih*** ("given his record **behind his back**"). ⭐ **Right hand vs. behind the back.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **note the *hamzah* in *mashʾamah*** (م-ش-أ-م) — ⚠️ **don't confuse the root with *sh-m-l* (*shimāl*, left), though they're related in meaning.**
- **Why this word:** *mashʾamah* embeds **misfortune** into the group's name, exactly as *maymanah* embeds blessing.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Al-Yaman*** (right/blessed) **vs. *ash-Shām*** (left) — ⭐ **two countries named from these two roots.** ⭐ **Direction and fortune, one word each.** ⭐ **And Inshiqāq 84:7 vs 84:10: right hand vs. behind the back.**

**18. نَارٌ مُّؤْصَدَةٌ — *nārun muʾṣadah*** · v.20
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**closing / shutting tight / sealing**" family — *awṣada* = **to shut a door firmly and bolt it**; ⭐ ***wiṣād* = the bolt or covering of a door.***
- **Here it means:** "**Over them will be a Fire closed in upon them.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the final word of the surah, and it closes a door the surah opened.**
  
  ⭐ ***Muʾṣadah* = shut, bolted, sealed over.*** ⭐ **The horror isn't the fire — it's the LID.** ⭐ **A fire in the open has an escape direction. A fire with the door bolted has none.**
  
  ⭐ **And here's the connection that makes it land:** ⭐ **the surah opened with v.2 — *wa anta ***ḥillun*** bi-hādha'l-balad* — "you are UNBOUND, RELEASED (*ḥill*) in this city."** ⭐ **It ends with a fire that is **BOLTED SHUT** (*muʾṣadah*).** ⭐ **Released at the start; sealed at the end.** ⭐ **The surah's first and last images are a knot untied and a door locked.**
  
  ⭐ **And one more:** ⭐ **v.13 asked you to perform ***fakk*** — an **UNTYING** (freeing a neck).** ⭐ **v.20 shows what happens to those who wouldn't: a door ***muʾṣadah*** — **SHUT**.** ⭐ **Untie a neck, or find the door bolted. The surah's whole ethic in two words.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **passive participle** (Form IV, *awṣada → mūṣad/muʾṣad*). ⚠️ **Note there are two established recitations of the spelling here (*muʾṣadah* / *mūṣadah*) — both correct.**
- **Why this word:** *muʾṣadah* focuses on ⭐ **the sealing**, which is worse than the burning.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Awṣada* = TO BOLT A DOOR SHUT.** ⭐ **The horror is the LID, not the fire.** ⭐⭐ **v.13: ***fakk*** — UNTIE a neck. v.20: ***muʾṣadah*** — the door BOLTED.** ⭐ **Untie, or be sealed in. That's the surah.**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐ **فَكُّ (v.13) and مُّؤْصَدَةٌ (v.20)** — ⚠️ **the surah's ethical spine.** *Fakk* = **UNTIE**. *Muʾṣadah* = **BOLTED SHUT**. ⭐ **Untie a neck, or find the door sealed.**
2. ⭐⭐ **مَتْرَبَةٍ (v.16)** — from ***turāb*, DUST** — ⚠️ **not a random word for poverty.** ⭐ **Same root as Nabaʾ 78:33 (*atrāb*) and 78:40 (*turābā*).**
3. ⭐ **أَهْلَكْتُ (v.6)** — ⚠️ **he says "I DESTROYED," not "I spent."** ⭐ **His own verb convicts him.**
4. ⭐ **حِلٌّ (v.2)** — from the root of ***ḥalāl* ("untied")** and ***iḥlāl*** (leaving iḥrām). ⚠️ **Multiple valid classical readings — don't flatten it to one.**
5. ⭐ **النَّجْدَيْنِ (v.10)** — ⚠️ **means "the two HIGH GROUNDS," not just "two ways."** ⭐ **Both paths are VISIBLE — that's the point.**
6. ⭐ **اقْتَحَمَ (v.11)** — ⚠️ **not "climbed" — PLUNGED IN HEADLONG** (modern *iqtiḥām* = **storming a building**).
7. ⭐ **ذِي (v.14) vs. ذَا (v.15)** — ⚠️ **same word, genitive vs. accusative**, one verse apart. **The five special nouns show case with a LONG vowel.**
8. ⭐ **الْمَيْمَنَةِ / الْمَشْأَمَةِ (v.18–19)** — ⚠️ **each carries BOTH a direction and a fortune.** ⭐ ***al-Yaman* and *ash-Shām* come from these roots.**
9. ⭐ **تَوَاصَوْا (v.17)** — **Form VI = MUTUAL**, like *yatasāʾalūn* (78:1) and *taḥāḍḍūn* (89:18). ⚠️ **Not "he advised."**
10. ⭐ **كَبَدٍ (v.4)** — from ***kabid*, THE LIVER.** ⚠️ **Man was created *IN* toil, not merely *with* it.**
