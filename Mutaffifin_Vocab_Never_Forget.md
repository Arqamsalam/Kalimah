# Surah Al-Muṭaffifīn (83) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–36)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**It opens with the smallest sin in the Qur'an — and treats it as a catastrophe.**

Not murder. Not idolatry. ⭐ **Shorting someone a few grams at the market.** Slightly heavy on the scale when you're buying; slightly light when you're selling. **Nobody dies. Nobody notices. It's practically nothing.**

And the surah opens: ***"WOE to the shortchangers."***

⭐ **Then it does something extraordinary: it uses that tiny sin to build the entire architecture of the Hereafter** — two registers (*Sijjīn* and *ʿIlliyyīn*), two drinks, two groups, two sets of laughter. ⭐ **A few grams on a scale opens onto the cosmic balance.**

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐ **The surah's whole logic: if you'd cheat someone by two grams, you've already told me everything about what you think of the Day of Reckoning.**

---

## The Big Picture

1. **The Two Grams** (v.1–6) — the sin, and the one question that explains it: *don't these people think they'll be raised?*
2. **The Wicked's Register** (v.7–17) — *Sijjīn*, the sealed record; the **rust** on the heart; and the worst punishment in the Qur'an: **being VEILED from your Lord.**
3. **The Righteous' Register** (v.18–28) — *ʿIlliyyīn*, the high places; the **sealed musk-wine**; the spring of *Tasnīm*.
4. **The Laughter Reversed** (v.29–36) — ⭐ they mocked; now **the believers sit on couches and watch.**

## §1 — The Two Grams (v.1–6)

**1. وَيْلٌ — *waylun*** · v.1 *(and v.10)*
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ a word of **doom, ruin, destruction** — often translated "woe." Classically also understood as a **valley in Hell**, but primarily it functions as an **exclamation of ruin**.
- **Here it means:** "**Woe to the shortchangers!**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **starting a surah with *wayl* is a slap.** No introduction, no oath, no *"O mankind."* ⭐ **The very first word is a curse.** Compare the openings you know: Nabaʾ began with a *question*, Takwīr with *"when,"* Infiṭār with *"when."* ⭐ **This one opens with the verdict already delivered.**
  
  And ⭐ **it repeats at v.10** (*waylun yawmaʾidhin li'l-mukadhdhibīn*) — ⭐ **framing the whole first section.** Woe at the start, woe again nine verses later.
  
  ⭐ **Compare Nāziʿāt's *nakāl* and Qiyāmah 75:34's *awlā laka fa-awlā*** — the Qur'an has a whole family of doom-idioms. ⭐ ***Wayl* is the bluntest.**
- **Grammar bite:** *waylun* is **indefinite nominative** — a standalone exclamation, with *li-* marking who it's aimed at. ⚠️ ⭐ **Don't confuse *wayl* (woe) with *walī* (ally) or *wāl* (governor)** — different roots entirely.
- **Why this word:** the harshest available opener, applied to the **mildest available sin** — that mismatch **is the message**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **The surah's FIRST WORD is a curse.** ⭐ **And it repeats at v.10** — bookending the section.

**2. الْمُطَفِّفِينَ — *al-muṭaffifīn*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ **a gorgeous root** — *ṭaff* means **the tiny bit at the brim**, the little that's **barely there**. *Ṭaffafa* = to give **just under** the full measure. Related: *ṭafīf* = **a trifling, negligible amount**.
- **Here it means:** "the **shortchangers**" — those who skim a little off the measure.
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **THE ROOT ITSELF IS THE ARGUMENT, and it's brilliant.**
  
  ⭐ ***Ṭaff* means "the negligible amount."*** So *al-muṭaffifīn* literally means ⭐ **"the people of the trifling bit"** — those who cheat by an amount **so small it has its own word for being insignificant.**
  
  ⭐ **The Qur'an names them by exactly the excuse they'd use.** *"It's nothing. It's a couple of grams. It doesn't matter."* ⭐ **Yes — and that's your NAME now.**
  
  And the **Form II** (*ṭaffafa*) is doing work too: it's **intensive/repeated** — ⭐ **this isn't one slip, it's a habit**, done again and again, each time negligibly.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form II active participle, plural** (*mufaʿʿil*). Recognize the shape: **mu- + doubled middle letter**. Same shape as *mukadhdhibīn* (v.10), *muqarrabūn* (v.21).
- **Why this word:** naming them after **"the trivial amount"** means their **defense is built into their indictment**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***ṭaff* = the tiny bit at the brim, the negligible amount.*** ⭐ **They're literally called "the people of the barely-anything."** Their excuse **is** their name.

**3. اكْتَالُوا ... يَسْتَوْفُونَ — *iktālū… yastawfūn*** · v.2
- **Root feelings:** *iktālū* — the "**measuring**" family (*kayl* = a measure; *mikyāl* = a measuring vessel); **Form VIII** = ⭐ **to measure FOR ONESELF, to take one's measure**. *yastawfūn* — ⭐ the "**fulfilling completely**" family — **you own this root from Insān 76:7** (*yūfūna bi'n-nadhr* — "they **fulfil** the vow"); **Form X** = ⭐ **to demand/exact the FULL amount**.
- **Here it means:** "who, **when they take measure from people, exact it in full**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the two verb-forms do all the work here, and this is a beautiful grammar lesson.**
  - ⭐ **Form VIII (*iktālū*) = doing it FOR YOURSELF.** Not "they measure" but "**they take their own measure**."
  - ⭐ **Form X (*yastawfūn*) = SEEKING/DEMANDING the complete thing.** Not "they receive fully" but "**they insist on every last grain.**"
  
  ⭐ **So the picture is precise: when RECEIVING, they're meticulous — leaning over the scale, demanding every particle.** The forms themselves paint a **finicky, exacting customer.**
  
  ⭐ **And the contrast with Insān 76:7 is quietly devastating:** there, the righteous ***yūfūna*** — they **fulfil** what they promised, in full, to others. ⭐ **Here, the same root is used for demanding fullness FOR THEMSELVES.** **Same root: giving completely vs. taking completely.**
  
  ⭐ **One more subtlety: *ʿala'n-nās*** — "**from** people" uses **عَلَى** (*upon*), which carries a hint of ⭐ **taking at someone's expense**, weighing down on them.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form VIII = reflexive benefit ("for oneself"). Form X = seeking/demanding.** ⭐ **You own Form X from ʿAbasa 80:5 (*istaghnā*) and Takwīr 81:28 (*yastaqīm*).**
- **Why this word:** the forms make **greed grammatical** — you can feel the leaning-over-the-scale in the morphology.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Form VIII = "for MYSELF." Form X = "give me ALL of it."** ⭐ **Insān 76:7: fulfilling FOR OTHERS. Here: demanding FOR SELF.** Same root, opposite direction.

**4. كَالُوهُمْ ... يُخْسِرُونَ — *kālūhum… yukhsirūn*** · v.3
- **Root feelings:** *kālū* — the same **measuring** root, but ⭐ **Form I now** — plain, unemphatic. *yukhsirūn* — ⭐ the "**loss**" family — **you own it from Qiyāmah 75:12** (*karratun **khāsirah***, "a losing return"); **Form IV** = ⭐ **to CAUSE someone loss**.
- **Here it means:** "but when **they measure out for them** or **weigh for them**, **they cause loss**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **The grammar drop is the entire verse — and it's one of the finest touches in the Qur'an.**
  
  ⭐ **v.2 (receiving):** *ikt**ā**lū* — **Form VIII**, *yast**awf**ūn* — **Form X**. **Elaborate. Emphatic. Effortful.**
  ⭐ **v.3 (giving):** *k**ā**lū* — **plain Form I**. **Bare. Minimal. No effort at all.**
  
  ⭐ **The verb forms themselves show the double standard.** When it's **their** gain: complex, intensive, demanding forms. When it's **someone else's**: the simplest possible verb. ⭐ **You can HEAR the carelessness in the morphology.**
  
  And ⭐ ***yukhsirūn* is Form IV = CAUSATIVE*** — they don't just "give less," they ⭐ **actively inflict loss.** The verse makes them the **agents** of someone else's *khusrān*.
  
  ⭐ **Cross-link:** Qiyāmah 75:12 had the deniers sneering *"tilka idhan karratun **khāsirah**"* ("that would be a losing return"). ⭐ **They were worried about THEIR loss; here they're busy causing others'.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form IV (أَفْعَلَ) = to CAUSE X.** You own it from **ʿAbasa 80:21–22** (*amātahu, aqbarahu, ansharahu*). Here: ***akhsara*** = **to cause loss**.
- **Why this word:** *yukhsirūn* over "they give less" — it names the **victim's experience**, not the cheat's action.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **v.2 (taking) = Forms VIII & X — elaborate. v.3 (giving) = plain Form I — lazy.** ⭐ **The GRAMMAR is the double standard.**

**5. أَلَا يَظُنُّ ... مَّبْعُوثُونَ — *alā yaẓunnu… mabʿūthūn*** · v.4
- **Root feelings:** *ẓanna* — ⭐ a **double-edged** root: usually "to suppose/think," but **you own its stronger sense from Qiyāmah 75:28** (*wa **ẓanna** annahu'l-firāq* — where it means **CERTAINTY**). *mabʿūthūn* — the "**sending forth / raising**" family (*baʿatha* = to send, to resurrect; *baʿth* = the Resurrection).
- **Here it means:** "**Do those not think** that they will be **resurrected**?"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **This one verse is the hinge of the entire surah, and it explains everything.**
  
  ⭐ **The surah just described a trivial commercial sin. Now it asks ONE question — and the question is not about honesty.** It's: ⭐ ***"Don't these people realize they'll be RAISED?"***
  
  ⭐ **That's the diagnosis: cheating on the scale is not a business problem. It's a THEOLOGY problem.** Nobody who genuinely expected to stand before a perfect Reckoner would shave two grams off a bag of dates. ⭐ **The small sin exposes the big absence.**
  
  And ⭐ ***ẓanna* here is beautifully ambiguous:*** *"don't they even SUSPECT?"* — ⭐ **the bar is set as low as possible.** Not "don't they *believe*," not "don't they *know*" — ⭐ **"doesn't it even CROSS THEIR MIND?"** Even a **passing suspicion** would have stopped them.
  
  ⭐ **And *alā*** is a particle of ⭐ **attention-grabbing astonishment** — "**Do they not…?!**"
- **Grammar bite:** *alā* = *hamzah* of questioning + *lā* of negation = a **rhetorical question expressing amazement**. *Mabʿūthūn* is a **passive participle** — ⭐ **they will be *raised*, not raise themselves.**
- **Why this word:** *ẓanna* (mere supposition) sets the **lowest possible threshold** — and they didn't even clear that.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **The whole surah's logic in one verse: "Don't they even SUSPECT they'll be raised?"** ⭐ **The bar is set at *suspicion* — and they missed it.**

**6. يَقُومُ النَّاسُ لِرَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ — *yaqūmu'n-nāsu li-Rabbi'l-ʿālamīn*** · v.6
- **Root feelings:** *yaqūmu* — the "**standing**" family — **you own it from Nāziʿāt 79:40** (*maqāma rabbih*) and **Takwīr 81:28** (*yastaqīm*). *ʿālamīn* — **all the worlds** — ⭐ **you own it from Takwīr 81:27, 29.**
- **Here it means:** "**The Day mankind will stand before the Lord of all the worlds.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the answer to a merchant's cheating is a courtroom image — and the scale-imagery is still running underneath.**
  
  ⭐ **Notice the title chosen: *Rabb al-ʿĀlamīn*** — **the Lord of ALL the worlds.** ⭐ **Not "your Lord," not "the Judge."** The man cheating over a handful of grain is being reminded he'll stand before ⭐ **the Owner of every world there is.** **The scale he tampered with belongs to Him too.**
  
  ⭐ **And it's the SAME phrase that closes Takwīr (81:29) and opens al-Fātiḥah.** ⭐ **Three occurrences you now own.**
  
  ⭐ **One more:** *yaqūmu* (**standing**) is doing quiet work — ⭐ **a merchant stands behind his scale; on that Day he stands in front of it.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **the *lām* in *li-Rabb*** = "**before / for**" — standing **at His disposal**. Note *yawma* is **accusative** (an adverb of time attached to v.5's "a great Day").
- **Why this word:** *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* over any narrower title — because **the offense was local; the Judge is universal**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Same phrase as Takwīr 81:29 and al-Fātiḥah.** ⭐ **He cheated on a small scale; he stands before the Lord of ALL scales.**

## §2 — The Wicked's Register (v.7–17)

**7. سِجِّينٍ — *sijjīn*** · v.7–8
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**imprisonment**" family — ***sijn*** = **a prison**; *sajjān* = a jailer; *masjūn* = imprisoned. ⭐ **You know *sijn* from Sūrat Yūsuf** (*yā ṣāḥibayi's-**sijn***, "O my two prison companions").
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, the record of the wicked is in **Sijjīn**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the *fiʿʿīl* pattern is an INTENSIVE — so *Sijjīn* means something like "the intensely confining place."**
  
  ⭐ **The name does double duty:**
  - It's a **place** (low, confined, sealed).
  - And it describes ⭐ **what the record DOES to them** — the file itself is a **prison**. Your deeds, locked in, inescapable.
  
  ⭐ **And notice the surah's structural symmetry — this is one of its most beautiful features.** Watch v.7–9 against v.18–20:
  
  | **The Wicked (v.7–9)** | **The Righteous (v.18–20)** |
  |---|---|
  | *kitāba'l-**fujjār*** | *kitāba'l-**abrār*** |
  | *la-fī **Sijjīn*** | *la-fī **ʿIlliyyīn*** |
  | *wa mā adrāka mā Sijjīn* | *wa mā adrāka mā ʿIlliyyūn* |
  | ***kitābun marqūm*** | ***kitābun marqūm*** |
  
  ⭐ **Word-for-word mirrored structure, eleven verses apart.** And *Sijjīn* (**LOW, prison**) vs. *ʿIlliyyīn* (**HIGH, elevated**) — ⭐ **the names are spatial opposites.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **the *mā adrāka* construction** — **you own it from Infiṭār 82:17–18** and **Ṭāriq 86:2**. ⭐ **Remember: PAST tense *adrāka* → an explanation follows.** And here it does (v.9).
- **Why this word:** *Sijjīn* over "a record" — because **the file and the sentence are the same object**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***sijn* = PRISON*** (Sūrat Yūsuf). ⭐ **Their record IS their cell.** And ⭐ ***Sijjīn* (low/prison) vs. *ʿIlliyyīn* (high) — mirrored structure, v.7–9 ↔ v.18–20.**

**8. كِتَابٌ مَّرْقُومٌ — *kitābun marqūm*** · v.9 *(and v.20)*
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**marking / numbering / inscribing**" family — ***raqm*** = **a number/digit** (modern Arabic: *raqm* = a number, *raqmī* = **digital**!); *marqūm* = **written, marked, numbered**.
- **Here it means:** "**A book inscribed / numbered.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **This is the perfect word for THIS surah, and it's easy to miss why.**
  
  ⭐ **The sin was tampering with NUMBERS on a scale.** The punishment-record is ⭐ ***marqūm* — "NUMBERED, precisely marked."*** **Same conceptual field: quantities, digits, exactness.**
  
  ⭐ **They fudged the figures; their record is figure-perfect.** *Raqm* is literally the word for **a digit**. ⭐ **You can't shave a gram off a *marqūm* book.**
  
  And ⭐ **the phrase is IDENTICAL in v.9 and v.20** — used for **both** *Sijjīn* and *ʿIlliyyīn*. ⭐ **Both records are equally precise.** The system doesn't change; only what's in it does.
- **Grammar bite:** **passive participle** (*mafʿūl*) — "**that which has been** inscribed." ⭐ **Someone wrote it; you didn't.**
- **Why this word:** *marqūm* (numbered) over *maktūb* (written) — because ⭐ **the sin was numerical.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***raqm* = a NUMBER (raqmī = digital).*** ⭐ **They cheated the numbers; their file is *numbered* perfectly.** ⭐ **Same phrase for BOTH registers (v.9, v.20).**

**9. مُعْتَدٍ أَثِيمٍ — *muʿtadin athīm*** · v.12
- **Root feelings:** *muʿtad* — ⭐ the "**exceeding / transgressing**" family — *iʿtadā* = **to cross the line, to overstep**; ***ʿudwān*** = aggression. ⚠️ ⭐ **Note: same letters as *ʿadala* (justice) in Infiṭār 82:7 — ع-د-ل vs ع-د-و — different roots, related idea of a LINE** (one keeps it, one crosses it). *athīm* — the "**sin**" family (*ithm* = sin) — ⭐ **you own it from ʿAbasa 80:? — no; from *āthiman* in Insān 76:24!**
- **Here it means:** "And none denies it **except every transgressor, sinner**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the pairing is a two-part diagnosis, and it's precise:**
  - ***Muʿtad*** = **he crosses lines** (a **behavioral** problem — boundaries)
  - ***Athīm*** = **he is steeped in sin** (a **character** problem — the *faʿīl* intensive pattern means **habitual, saturated**)
  
  ⭐ **Crossing lines outward, rotting inward.** ⭐ **Compare ʿAbasa 80:42's closing pair: *kafarah fajarah* (covering inward, bursting outward).** ⭐ **Both surahs use a two-word diagnosis; both split into an inner and outer failure.**
  
  And ⭐ ***kullu* ("every")*** is doing heavy lifting: *"none denies it except **EVERY** transgressor"* — ⭐ **the denial is universal among that type, and exclusive to it.** If you deny the Day, **you're in that category, no exceptions.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***muʿtadin* is a *manqūṣ* noun*** — **it drops its final yāʾ** and takes tanwīn on the *dāl*. ⭐ **You own this rule from Qiyāmah 75:27 (*rāqin*) and Takwīr 81:16 (*al-jawāri*).** **Third instance.**
- **Why this word:** naming **both** the behavior and the character closes off the excuse *"I just made a mistake."*
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***iʿtadā* = to CROSS THE LINE.*** ⭐ **Two-part diagnosis: crosses lines (outward) + saturated in sin (inward).** ⭐ **And *muʿtadin* is *manqūṣ* — the yāʾ drops (like *rāqin*, *al-jawāri*).**

**10. أَسَاطِيرُ الْأَوَّلِينَ — *asāṭīru'l-awwalīn*** · v.13
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ *asāṭīr* — plural of *usṭūrah*, **a legend, a tall tale**. **The root ****س-ط-ر**** means to WRITE IN LINES** — ***saṭr* = a LINE of text** (modern Arabic: *saṭr* = a line, *musaṭṭar* = ruled/lined paper). So *asāṭīr* = **things written down** → **old stories, fables**.
- **Here it means:** "When Our verses are recited to him, he says: **'Legends of the ancients!'**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **The irony here is delicious and structural.**
  
  ⭐ ***Asāṭīr* comes from *saṭr* — "a written LINE."*** He dismisses the revelation as ⭐ **"just old lines on a page."**
  
  ⭐ **But look at what surrounds him in this very surah:** his own deeds are being recorded in a ***kitābun marqūm*** (v.9) — **a numbered book.** ⭐ **He calls God's writing "old lines" while God's scribes are writing HIS lines.**
  
  ⭐ **He mocks a book. He's living in one.**
  
  And notice: ⭐ **this is his ONLY argument.** The verse gives him one line of dialogue, and it's not a refutation — it's a **dismissal**. ⭐ **He doesn't answer the Qur'an; he labels it.**
- **Grammar bite:** *asāṭīr* is a **broken plural**; *al-awwalīn* = "the former peoples" (from *awwal*, first). ⭐ **The whole phrase is his quoted speech** — the Qur'an lets him convict himself.
- **Why this word:** *asāṭīr* is exactly the sneer a **literate, worldly** man would use — dismissive, cultured, and empty.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***saṭr* = a LINE of writing** (lined paper = *musaṭṭar*). ⭐ **He calls revelation "old lines" — while his OWN lines are being written down** (v.9).

**11. رَانَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِم — *rāna ʿalā qulūbihim*** · v.14
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**rust / film / covering stain**" family — *rāna* = **to overlay with a dark film**, the way ⭐ **rust builds on iron** or grime settles on a mirror. ***Rayn*** = that covering layer.
- **Here it means:** "**No! Rather, what they used to earn has RUSTED over their hearts.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **One of the most famous and useful images in the entire Qur'an — and there's a hadith that explains it exactly.**
  
  ⭐ **The Prophet ﷺ described (reported in Tirmidhī, Ibn Mājah, Aḥmad) that when a servant commits a sin, a BLACK SPOT is placed on his heart. If he repents, it is polished away. If he returns to the sin, it increases until it covers his heart — and that is the *rān* Allah mentions in this verse.** ⭐ **The hadith names this verse directly.**
  
  ⭐ **Why *rust* is the perfect metaphor:**
  - Rust doesn't appear **all at once** — it **accumulates**, invisibly, over time.
  - Rust comes from **the metal's own exposure** — ⭐ **you don't add it; it forms from what you do.**
  - Rust makes a mirror **stop reflecting** — ⭐ **which is exactly the problem: the heart can no longer SEE.**
  - ⭐ **And rust can be POLISHED OFF** — which is why the hadith mentions repentance.
  
  ⭐ **Now connect it to v.15** (coming next): their hearts are **rusted over** *here*, and they are **VEILED** from their Lord *there*. ⭐ **The veil in the Hereafter is just the rust of this life, made permanent.** **Same barrier, two stages.**
  
  ⭐ **And "mā kānū yaksibūn" ("what they used to EARN") is chosen with care:** ⭐ ***kasb* is COMMERCIAL vocabulary — "earnings, gain."*** In a surah about **cheating in trade**, ⭐ **their sins are described as their PROFITS.** They earned rust.
- **Grammar bite:** *rāna* is a **hollow verb** (weak middle و/ي). *Mā kānū yaksibūn* = **habitual past** ("what they used to keep earning") — ⭐ **ongoing, accumulated, not a one-off.**
- **Why this word:** *rān* over "sealed" or "hardened" — because rust is ⭐ **gradual, self-generated, and removable**, which makes it both a warning and an invitation.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***rān* = RUST.*** ⭐ **The hadith: a black spot per sin, polished by repentance, or it spreads until it covers the heart.** ⭐ **Rust = a mirror that stops reflecting.** And ⭐ **their sins are called their "earnings" — in a surah about trade.**

**12. لَّمَحْجُوبُونَ — *la-maḥjūbūn*** · v.15
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**veiling / screening / barring**" family — ***ḥijāb*** = **a veil, a screen, a barrier**; *ḥajaba* = to block from view, to bar entry; *ḥājib* = **a doorkeeper** (one who screens who gets in).
- **Here it means:** "**No! Indeed, from their Lord, that Day, they will be VEILED.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **Many scholars consider this the most severe verse about punishment in the Qur'an — and it doesn't mention fire at all.**
  
  ⭐ **Read the sequence in this surah:** v.16 mentions the Blaze. ⭐ **But v.15 comes FIRST — and it's worse.** The order is deliberate: ⭐ **the veiling is named before the fire, because it IS the greater punishment.**
  
  ⭐ **Why?** Imām al-Shāfiʿī is famously reported to have argued from this verse that ⭐ **the believers WILL see their Lord** — because if being **veiled** is singled out as the punishment of the wicked, then ⭐ **being UNveiled must be the reward of the righteous.** ⭐ **The verse proves the opposite by stating the negative.**
  
  ⭐ **And you already own the other half of this.** **Qiyāmah 75:22–23:** *wujūhun yawmaʾidhin **nāḍirah** · ilā rabbihā **nāẓirah*** — ⭐ **"faces radiant, LOOKING at their Lord."** ⭐ **Qiyāmah shows you the seeing; Muṭaffifīn shows you the veil.** **Two surahs, one doctrine, opposite sides.**
  
  ⭐ **And here's the connection to v.14 that makes it perfect:** their hearts were ***rusted*** (*rāna*) — **a veil they built themselves, one sin at a time.** ⭐ **On that Day the veil is simply... kept.** **They spent a lifetime building a screen; they get to keep it forever.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **triple emphasis**: *inna* + **lām** + fronted *ʿan rabbihim*. ⭐ **And the fronting is the key:** "**from their Lord** they are veiled" — ⭐ **putting "from their Lord" first makes HIM the thing being withheld**, the emphasis of the sentence.
- **Why this word:** *maḥjūbūn* over "punished" — because ⭐ **the deprivation is the punishment.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***ḥijāb* = a VEIL/SCREEN.*** ⭐ **Named BEFORE the fire — because it's worse.** ⭐ **Qiyāmah 75:23: the righteous LOOK at their Lord. Here: the wicked are VEILED.** ⭐ **And the veil is just v.14's rust, made permanent.**

**13. لَصَالُو الْجَحِيمِ — *la-ṣālu'l-jaḥīm*** · v.16
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**roasting**" family — **you own this verb from Infiṭār 82:15** (*yaṣlawnahā*), and you'll meet it again in **Inshiqāq 84:12** (*yaṣlā saʿīrā*).
- **Here it means:** "Then indeed, **they will burn in the Blaze**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **note the ORDER once more — it's the surah's quiet argument.** v.15: **veiled from their Lord.** v.16: **then** the fire. ⭐ **The ثُمَّ ("then") makes the fire the SECOND item.** **The separation comes first; the burning is the consequence.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***ṣālū* is a plural active participle from a weak-final root, and it DROPS ITS NŪN*** because it's in *iḍāfah* (possessive construction) with *al-jaḥīm*. ⭐ **Original: *ṣālūna* → *ṣālū'l-jaḥīm*.** **Same nūn-dropping rule you met in ʿAbasa 80:36 (*banīh*).**
- **Why this word:** keeping the same roasting-verb across three surahs makes it a **fixed term** — learn it once.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Third surah with this verb (82:15, 83:16, 84:12).** ⭐ **And notice: the VEIL comes first, the FIRE second.**

## §3 — The Righteous' Register (v.18–28)

**14. عِلِّيِّينَ — *ʿilliyyīn*** · v.18–19
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**height / elevation / loftiness**" family — *ʿalā* = to be high; ***ʿālī*** = high; ***al-ʿAliyy*** = **the Most High** (a name of Allah); *aʿlā* = highest — ⭐ **you own *al-aʿlā* from Nāziʿāt 79:24** (Pharaoh's blasphemous *"anā rabbukumu'l-**aʿlā**"*).
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, the record of the righteous is in **ʿIlliyyīn**" — **the high places**.
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the exact mirror of *Sijjīn*, and the names alone tell the story:**
  - ⭐ ***Sijjīn*** — from **prison** → **LOW, sealed, confined**
  - ⭐ ***ʿIlliyyīn*** — from **height** → **HIGH, open, elevated**
  
  ⭐ **And the *fiʿʿīl* intensive pattern is the same in both** (*Sijjīn* / *ʿIlliyyīn*) — ⭐ **matched names for matched opposites.** They even **rhyme**.
  
  ⭐ **Notice the direction words in this surah keep pointing:** the *muṭaffifīn* **lowered** the measure; the *fujjār* end up in the **low place**; the *abrār* end up in the **high place**. ⭐ **The surah about tipping scales is organized by UP and DOWN.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **note v.19 says *mā **ʿilliyyūn*** (nominative) while v.18 said *fī **ʿilliyyīn*** (genitive after *fī*).** ⭐ **It behaves like a sound masculine plural: -ūn / -īn.** **A small but excellent case-ending lesson.**
- **Why this word:** naming the register by **elevation** answers the sin of **lowering** the measure.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***al-ʿAliyy* = the Most High; Pharaoh claimed *al-aʿlā* (79:24).*** ⭐ ***Sijjīn* (prison/low) ↔ *ʿIlliyyīn* (heights).** Matched names, matched rhyme, opposite directions.

**15. الْمُقَرَّبُونَ — *al-muqarrabūn*** · v.21 *(and v.28)*
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**nearness**" family — *qaruba* = to be near; ***qarīb*** = near; ***qurbān*** = **a sacrifice** (something offered to draw near!); *taqarrub* = seeking closeness.
- **Here it means:** "**Witnessed by those brought near**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **This word is the perfect answer to v.15, and the pairing is the surah's spine.**
  
  | **v.15** | **v.21** |
  |---|---|
  | *la-**maḥjūbūn*** | *al-**muqarrabūn*** |
  | **VEILED** from their Lord | **BROUGHT NEAR** |
  
  ⭐ **Distance vs. nearness. That's the real currency of this surah** — not fire and gardens, but **how close you get.**
  
  ⭐ **And *muqarrabūn* is a PASSIVE participle (Form II)** — ⭐ **"those who have been BROUGHT near."** **They didn't climb; they were drawn in.** ⭐ **Compare *maḥjūbūn* — also passive: "those who have been veiled."** ⭐ **Both are done TO them. Same grammar, opposite results.**
  
  ⭐ **And you own this root from Hajj:** ***Muzdalifah*** is from *zulfah* (nearness) — **Takwīr 81:13** (*uzlifat*, Paradise **brought near**). ⭐ **Two different roots, same idea: nearness is the reward.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form II passive participle** (*mu-faʿʿal*): *muqarrab, mukarram, muṭahhar*. ⭐ **You own this shape from ʿAbasa 80:13–14** (*mukarramah, marfūʿah, muṭahharah*).
- **Why this word:** *muqarrabūn* over "the believers" — because it names them by ⭐ **their proximity**, which is exactly what the wicked lost.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **v.15: *maḥjūbūn* (VEILED). v.21: *muqarrabūn* (BROUGHT NEAR).** ⭐ **Both passive. Both done to them. Opposite results.** ⭐ ***qurbān* = a sacrifice, i.e. drawing near.**

**16. الْأَرَائِكِ ... نَضْرَةَ النَّعِيمِ — *al-arāʾiki… naḍrata'n-naʿīm*** · v.23–24
- **Root feelings:** *arāʾik* — **ornate couches/thrones** — ⭐ **you own it from Insān 76:13** (*muttakiʾīna fīhā **ʿala'l-arāʾik***). *naḍrah* — ⭐ the "**fresh glow / bloom**" family — ⭐ **you own it TWICE: Qiyāmah 75:22** (*wujūhun yawmaʾidhin **nāḍirah***) and **Insān 76:11** (*wa laqqāhum **naḍratan** wa surūrā*).
- **Here it means:** "On **couches**, observing… You will recognize in their faces **the radiance of bliss**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **THREE surahs now share this glow-root, and this one adds something new.**
  - **Qiyāmah 75:22** — faces **radiant** (*nāḍirah*), **looking at their Lord**
  - **Insān 76:11** — He **gave them** radiance (*naḍratan*)
  - ⭐ **Muṭaffifīn 83:24** — ***you will RECOGNIZE it in their faces*** (*taʿrifu fī wujūhihim*)
  
  ⭐ **The new element is *taʿrifu* — "YOU will recognize."*** ⭐ **The bliss is VISIBLE from outside.** You don't have to ask them; you can **read it on their faces across the room.** ⭐ **Same idea as ʿAbasa 80:38–39** (*musfirah, ḍāḥikah, mustabshirah* — faces shining, laughing, rejoicing).
  
  ⭐ **And the contrast with v.15 completes:** the wicked are ⭐ **veiled — you can't see their Lord.** The righteous are ⭐ **so unveiled that their joy is legible on their skin.**
  
  ⭐ **Note also *yanẓurūn* ("observing," v.23)** — left **deliberately unspecified**. Watching what? ⭐ **Many read it against v.34–35: they're watching the reversal** (see below). Others: gazing at their Lord, or at the blessings.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***taʿrifu*** is **2nd person singular** — the Qur'an suddenly addresses **YOU**, the reader/listener, as a witness. ⭐ **A small *iltifāt* (turn of address) that pulls you into the scene.**
- **Why this word:** *naḍrah* over "happiness" — it's ⭐ **physical, external, visible bloom**, not an inner state.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **Third surah with this glow: Qiyāmah 75:22, Insān 76:11, here.** ⭐ **New here: "YOU will RECOGNIZE it in their faces" — the joy is readable from outside.** ⭐ **And *arāʾik* = the same couches as Insān 76:13.**

**17. رَّحِيقٍ مَّخْتُومٍ ... خِتَامُهُ مِسْكٌ — *raḥīqin makhtūm… khitāmuhu misk*** · v.25–26
- **Root feelings:** *raḥīq* — ⭐ **pure, unadulterated wine** — the **finest, undiluted** vintage. *makhtūm* — ⭐ the "**sealing**" family — ***khatam*** = **a seal/signet ring**; ⭐ ***khātam an-nabiyyīn*** = **the SEAL of the Prophets**; *khitām* = the seal, or the finish. *misk* — **musk**, the most prized perfume.
- **Here it means:** "They are given to drink **pure sealed wine**, **whose seal is musk**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **Two extraordinary details here.**
  
  ⭐ **First: *makhtūm* (SEALED).** ⭐ **Why does a sealed bottle matter?** Because a seal means: ⭐ **untouched, unopened, reserved.** Nobody has drunk from it. Nobody has diluted it. ⭐ **In a surah about people who ADULTERATE what they sell** — shaving grams, mixing measures — ⭐ **the reward is a drink that has been SEALED against tampering.** **The cheaters get a rusted heart; the honest get an unbroken seal.**
  
  ⭐ **Second: *khitāmuhu misk* — "its seal is MUSK."*** ⭐ **On earth, the seal on a bottle is the throwaway part** — wax, clay, foil. You **break it and discard it**. ⭐ **In Paradise, the part you THROW AWAY is musk** — the most expensive substance they knew. ⭐ **The packaging is more precious than anything on earth.**
  
  ⭐ **And you already own the parallel from Insān 76:5, 17:** drinks mixed with ***kāfūr*** (camphor) and ***zanjabīl*** (ginger). ⭐ **Insān names the FLAVOR; Muṭaffifīn names the SEAL.** **Same reward-vocabulary family.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***khatam* → *khātam an-nabiyyīn*.*** **Recognize the root anywhere.** *Makhtūm* is a **passive participle** ("having been sealed").
- **Why this word:** a **sealed** drink in a surah about **tampering** — the reward is shaped like the antidote to the sin.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***khatam* = a SEAL** → ***khātam an-nabiyyīn*, the Seal of the Prophets.** ⭐ **The cheaters tampered with goods; the righteous get a drink SEALED shut.** ⭐ **And the seal you throw away is MUSK.**

**18. فَلْيَتَنَافَسِ الْمُتَنَافِسُونَ — *fal-yatanāfasi'l-mutanāfisūn*** · v.26
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ **a stunning root** — *n-f-s*: ***nafas*** = **a breath**; ***nafs*** = **a SOUL**; ***nafīs*** = **precious, valuable**; ***munāfasah*** = **competition** — ⭐ **specifically, competing for something so precious you'd give your last breath for it.**
- **Here it means:** "**So for THIS let the competitors compete!**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **This is the surah's one moment of pure encouragement, and the root makes it soar.**
  
  ⭐ ***Munāfasah* is COMPETITION built from the word for BREATH and PRECIOUSNESS.*** It's not ordinary rivalry — it's ⭐ **the striving for something so *nafīs* (precious) that you'd spend your very *nafs* (soul/breath) on it.**
  
  ⭐ **And look at where it sits.** The whole surah has been about people competing for ⭐ **two grams on a scale** — the most worthless thing imaginable. ⭐ **Then, right after describing musk-sealed wine, it says: "THIS is what's worth competing for."** ⭐ **The verse redirects the exact same human drive from the trivial to the eternal.**
  
  ⭐ **It doesn't say "stop competing." It says "compete for the right thing."**
  
  ⭐ **And the root-link you already own:** ⭐ **Takwīr 81:18** — *wa'ṣ-ṣubḥi idhā **tanaffas*** ("the morning when it **breathes**") — ⭐ **and Takwīr 81:14 / Infiṭār 82:5 — *nafs* (soul).** ⭐ **Breath, soul, preciousness, competition: all one root.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form VI (تَفَاعَلَ) = MUTUAL action** — ⭐ **you own it from Nabaʾ 78:1** (*yatasāʾalūn*, asking **one another**). Here: **competing with one another.** ⭐ **And the *lām al-amr*** (**command lām**): *fal-yatanāfas* = "**let them compete**" — ⭐ **same construction as ʿAbasa 80:24** (*fal-yanẓur*).
- **Why this word:** *munāfasah* over a plain word for "strive" — because ⭐ **it validates the competitive instinct while re-aiming it.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***nafas* (breath) + *nafs* (soul) + *nafīs* (precious) + *munāfasah* (competition) — ONE ROOT.*** ⭐ **They competed over 2 grams. "Compete for THIS instead."**

**19. مِزَاجُهُ مِن تَسْنِيمٍ — *mizājuhu min tasnīm*** · v.27–28
- **Root feelings:** *mizāj* — the "**blending**" family — ⭐ **you own it from Insān 76:5** (*kāna **mizājuhā** kāfūrā*) and **76:17** (*kāna **mizājuhā** zanjabīlā*). *tasnīm* — ⭐ the "**raising high**" family — *sanima* = to be elevated; ***sanām*** = **a camel's HUMP** (the highest point of the animal!).
- **Here it means:** "And its **mixture** is from **Tasnīm** — a spring from which **those brought near** drink."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **The name *Tasnīm* is doing something beautiful.**
  
  ⭐ **From *sanām* — a camel's HUMP, the highest part.** So *Tasnīm* is ⭐ **"the elevated one"** — a spring that **flows from above**, the **highest** spring in Paradise.
  
  ⭐ **And it completes the surah's vertical architecture:**
  - *Sijjīn* — ⭐ **the LOW place** (v.7)
  - *ʿIlliyyīn* — ⭐ **the HIGH place** (v.18)
  - *Tasnīm* — ⭐ **the HIGH spring** (v.27)
  
  ⭐ **A surah that began with people LOWERING a scale is organized entirely by ELEVATION.**
  
  ⭐ **And notice the hierarchy in v.28:** the *abrār* get *Tasnīm* **as a MIXTURE** in their drink; ⭐ **the *muqarrabūn* drink it PURE, straight from the spring.** ⭐ **Even in Paradise there are ranks — and nearness is the ranking.**
  
  ⭐ **You now own the full Insān/Muṭaffifīn drink-set:** *kāfūr* (camphor, 76:5), *zanjabīl* (ginger, 76:17), *Salsabīl* (76:18), *raḥīq makhtūm* (83:25), *Tasnīm* (83:27).
- **Grammar bite:** *ʿaynan* (v.28) is **accusative** — a *badal* or specification of *Tasnīm*: "**[namely] a spring**." Note *yashrabu **bihā*** — with **بـ**, an idiom meaning to **drink from/at** it.
- **Why this word:** naming the spring by **height** ties the reward back to *ʿIlliyyīn* and away from *Sijjīn*.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***sanām* = a camel's HUMP (the highest point).*** ⭐ ***Tasnīm* = the high spring.** ⭐ **Sijjīn (low) → ʿIlliyyīn (high) → Tasnīm (highest).** ⭐ **And the *muqarrabūn* drink it PURE; others get it mixed.**

## §4 — The Laughter Reversed (v.29–36)

**20. أَجْرَمُوا ... يَضْحَكُونَ — *ajramū… yaḍḥakūn*** · v.29
- **Root feelings:** *ajrama* — ⭐ the "**crime / cutting off**" family — *jurm* = a crime; ***mujrim*** = a criminal. (The root's older sense relates to **cutting/severing** — a criminal **cuts himself off**.) *yaḍḥakūn* — the "**laughing**" family — ⭐ **you own it from ʿAbasa 80:39** (*ḍāḥikatun mustabshirah*).
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, those who **committed crimes** used to **laugh** at those who believed."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the final movement of the surah is about LAUGHTER — and it's the most humanly recognizable section in these surahs.**
  
  ⭐ **Notice what the mockery consists of:** not violence, not persecution — ⭐ **laughing, winking, gossiping at home, calling them "lost."** ⭐ **It's SOCIAL cruelty: the sneer, the in-joke, the eye-roll.** The surah takes **schoolyard behavior** seriously enough to close on it.
  
  ⭐ **And it fits the opening perfectly:** the surah began with ⭐ **a small sin taken seriously** (two grams). It ends with ⭐ **small cruelty taken seriously** (a smirk). ⭐ **Both are things people dismiss as "nothing."**
- **Grammar bite:** *kānū… yaḍḥakūn* = ⭐ **habitual past** ("they **used to** laugh") — **ongoing behavior**, not one incident.
- **Why this word:** *ajramū* over "disbelieved" — it frames mockery as a ⭐ **CRIME**, not a personality flaw.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **The surah opens with a tiny SIN (2 grams) and closes with a tiny CRUELTY (a smirk).** ⭐ **Both dismissed as "nothing." Both taken seriously here.**

**21. يَتَغَامَزُونَ — *yataghāmazūn*** · v.30
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**winking / signalling with the eye or eyebrow**" family — *ghamaza* = **to wink, to nudge, to signal mockingly**.
- **Here it means:** "And when they passed by them, **they would wink at one another**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **This is one of the most precisely observed verses in the Qur'an — it captures a gesture, not an argument.**
  
  ⭐ ***Ghamz* is the SIDEWAYS glance, the raised eyebrow, the elbow-nudge*** — ⭐ **mockery that leaves no evidence.** You can't be accused of it. You can't be quoted. ⭐ **It's deniable cruelty.**
  
  ⭐ **And Form VI (*tafāʿala*) makes it MUTUAL** — ⭐ **they wink AT EACH OTHER.** It's not one person sneering; it's **a group sharing a private joke** at someone's expense, in front of them.
  
  ⭐ **Anyone who has ever been the target of this recognizes it instantly.** ⭐ **The Qur'an recorded a raised eyebrow.**
  
  ⭐ **And here's the reversal being set up:** in **v.23 and v.35**, the righteous are ***yanẓurūn*** — **LOOKING**. ⭐ **The mockers used their eyes to wink; the believers will use theirs to watch.** ⭐ **Eyes throughout.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form VI = mutual** — **third instance you own**: *yatasāʾalūn* (Nabaʾ 78:1), *yatanāfasūn* (v.26 above), *yataghāmazūn* (here). ⭐ **Recognize the ta- + long ā shape.**
- **Why this word:** *ghamz* over "mocked" — because it names ⭐ **the exact, deniable, wordless gesture** — which is what actually stings.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***ghamz* = the WINK, the eyebrow, the nudge.*** ⭐ **Deniable cruelty — no words, no evidence.** ⭐ **The Qur'an recorded a raised eyebrow.**

**22. انقَلَبُوا ... فَكِهِينَ — *inqalabū… fakihīn*** · v.31
- **Root feelings:** *inqalaba* — ⭐ the "**turning over / returning**" family — *qalaba* = to flip, to overturn; ***qalb*** = **the HEART** (the thing that **turns**!); *inqilāb* = **a coup/revolution** (an overturning). *fakihīn* — ⭐ the "**delighting / joking**" family — related to ***fākihah*** = **FRUIT** — ⭐ **you own it from ʿAbasa 80:31** (*wa **fākihatan** wa abbā*). **Enjoyment as something sweet you consume.**
- **Here it means:** "And when they **returned** to their families, **they returned joking/delighted**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **This verse is a devastating character sketch — and it has an EXACT counterpart one surah later.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Inshiqāq 84:9:** *wa **yanqalibu** ilā ahlihi **masrūrā*** — ⭐ **"and he will RETURN to his family REJOICING"** — describing the man who gets his record in his right hand.
  
  | **Muṭaffifīn 83:31** | **Inshiqāq 84:9** |
  |---|---|
  | *idha'**nqalabū** ilā **ahlihim*** | *wa **yanqalibu** ilā **ahlihi*** |
  | *inqalabū **fakihīn*** | *…**masrūrā*** |
  | **Returned home** amused — **at others' expense** | **Returns home** rejoicing — **having passed** |
  
  ⭐⭐ **SAME VERB. SAME PHRASE ("returned to their family"). Opposite joy.** ⭐ **One surah apart.** ⭐ **The mocker's homecoming and the believer's homecoming, described identically.**
  
  ⭐ **And there's a third layer:** ⭐ **Inshiqāq 84:13** says of the doomed man: *innahu **kāna fī ahlihi masrūrā*** — "**he used to be, among his family, rejoicing**" — ⭐ **the SAME joy, now listed as evidence against him.** ⭐ **Three verses across two surahs, one homecoming, three verdicts.**
  
  ⭐ **And notice the detail:** their mockery ⭐ **wasn't spontaneous — they took it HOME.** It was **dinner-table entertainment.** ⭐ ***Fakihīn* (from *fākihah*, fruit) makes the cruelty into DESSERT** — something enjoyed after the meal.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form VII** (*inqalaba*) — the **ان-** shape you own from **Infiṭār 82:1** (*infaṭarat*), **Takwīr 81:2** (*inkadarat*). *Fakihīn* is a **ḥāl** (state): "returned **while** joking."
- **Why this word:** *fakihīn* (from **fruit**) turns mockery into a **consumed pleasure** — which is exactly how it functions socially.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***qalb* = HEART, the thing that TURNS; *inqilāb* = a coup.*** ⭐⭐ **SAME PHRASE as Inshiqāq 84:9 — "returned to his family."** ⭐ **One returns amused at others; one returns having passed.** ⭐ **And *fakihīn* ← *fākihah* (FRUIT): cruelty as dessert.**

**23. لَضَالُّونَ ... حَافِظِينَ — *la-ḍāllūn… ḥāfiẓīn*** · v.32–33
- **Root feelings:** *ḍāllūn* — the "**straying / being lost**" family (*ḍalāl* = misguidance) — ⭐ **you say *ghayri'l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa la'ḍ-**ḍāllīn*** in **every Fātiḥah**. *ḥāfiẓīn* — ⭐ the "**guarding**" family — **you own it from Infiṭār 82:10** (*wa inna ʿalaykum la-**ḥāfiẓīn***) and **Ṭāriq 86:4**.
- **Here it means:** v.32 — "And when they saw them, they said: **'Indeed, these are astray!'**" v.33 — "**But they had not been sent as guardians over them.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **v.33 is one of the most quietly cutting lines in the Qur'an, and the word *ḥāfiẓīn* is the blade.**
  
  ⭐ **In Infiṭār 82:10**, *ḥāfiẓīn* described ⭐ **the angels appointed over you** — the real, authorized record-keepers. ⭐ **Here, the mockers are told: you were never given that job.**
  
  ⭐ **"They were not SENT over them as guardians"** = ⭐ **"Who appointed you? Nobody. You gave yourself a position you were never assigned."**
  
  ⭐ **The mockers were doing the ANGELS' job without the commission** — assessing, judging, filing verdicts on other people's guidance. ⭐ **And the surah simply notes: that post was already filled, and not by you.**
  
  ⭐ **And notice the irony with v.32's word:** they called the believers ***ḍāllūn* (astray)** — ⭐ **the exact word you ask Allah to protect you from seventeen times a day.** ⭐ **They used the language of the Fātiḥah as an insult.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***mā ursilū*** — **passive** ("they were not **sent**"). ⭐ **The passive is the point: being sent requires a SENDER, and they had none.** Compare *rasūl* (Takwīr 81:19) — **one who IS sent.**
- **Why this word:** *ḥāfiẓīn* over "judges" — because it borrows **the angels' exact title** to expose the overreach.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **Infiṭār 82:10: the ANGELS are *ḥāfiẓīn* over you.** ⭐ **Here: "you were never SENT as guardians."** ⭐ **They took a job that was already filled.** ⭐ **And *ḍāllīn* is the word from your daily Fātiḥah.**

**24. فَالْيَوْمَ ... يَضْحَكُونَ — *fal-yawma… yaḍḥakūn*** · v.34–35
- **Root feelings:** *yaḍḥakūn* — the **laughing** root from v.29. *yanẓurūn* — the "**looking**" family — ⭐ **you own it from ʿAbasa 80:24** (*fal-**yanẓuri**'l-insān*) and **Qiyāmah 75:23** (*ilā rabbihā **nāẓirah***).
- **Here it means:** "**So today**, those who believed are laughing at the disbelievers — **on couches, observing.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **The reversal is exact, verb for verb — this is *muqābala* at its most satisfying.**
  
  | **v.29–31 (then)** | **v.34–35 (now)** |
  |---|---|
  | *al-ladhīna **ajramū*** | *al-ladhīna **āmanū*** |
  | *kānū… **yaḍḥakūn*** | *…**yaḍḥakūn*** |
  | *yata**ghāmaz**ūn* (winking) | *ʿala'l-arāʾiki **yanẓurūn*** (watching) |
  
  ⭐⭐ **The SAME VERB — *yaḍḥakūn* — moves from one group to the other.** ⭐ **The laughter doesn't stop; it changes owners.**
  
  ⭐ **And here's the detail that makes it perfect:** the mockers ⭐ **passed by** the believers (v.30) to sneak their winks — **standing, moving, furtive.** ⭐ **The believers are *ʿala'l-arāʾik* — RECLINING on couches** (v.35), ⭐ **comfortable, seated, in no hurry, openly watching.** ⭐ **The posture is the verdict.**
  
  ⭐ **And *yanẓurūn* appears TWICE (v.23 and v.35)** — ⭐ **framing the whole Paradise section with LOOKING.** They who were **looked down on** now **look**.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***fa'l-yawma*** — "**so TODAY**" — the tense shifts to ⭐ **present/immediate**, as if you're standing in that Day watching it happen. **A sudden shift into the scene.**
- **Why this word:** re-using ***yaḍḥakūn*** rather than a different word for joy is what makes the reversal **land as poetic justice**, not just reward.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **Same verb (*yaḍḥakūn*) in v.29 and v.34 — the laughter changes owners.** ⭐ **They *passed by* winking; the believers *recline* and watch.** ⭐ **Posture = verdict.**

**25. هَلْ ثُوِّبَ — *hal thuwwiba*** · v.36
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ **an extraordinary root choice** — *th-w-b*: ***thawāb*** = **REWARD** (the standard word for the reward of good deeds!); *thawb* = **a garment**; *thāba* = **to return, to come back to a place**.
- **Here it means:** "**Have the disbelievers been REWARDED for what they used to do?**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **The final word of the surah, and it's the sharpest irony in it.**
  
  ⭐ ***Thawāb* is the WORD FOR REWARD.*** It's what you say about **good** deeds — *"may Allah give you thawāb."* ⭐ **And it's used here for the punishment of the disbelievers.**
  
  ⭐ **The tone is unmistakable: "So — have they been well REWARDED for their efforts?"** ⭐ **Said about people in the Fire.** It's the same register as **Nabaʾ 78:30** (*"We will only INCREASE you in punishment"* — the language of a gift, delivering the opposite).
  
  ⭐ **And the root's other meaning makes it richer:** *thāba* = ⭐ **to RETURN / come back**. So *thawāb* is literally ⭐ **"what comes back to you."** ⭐ **They laughed; it came back. They winked; it came back. They shortchanged; it came back.** ⭐ **The final word of a surah about cheating the scales means "what returns to you."**
  
  ⭐ **And it closes the surah's own loop:** they took **more than they gave** (v.2–3). ⭐ **In the end, what came back was exactly what they sent out.** ⭐ **The scale balanced itself.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***hal*** here is a **rhetorical question**, not a real one — the answer is obviously **yes**, and that's the sting. **Form II passive** (*thuwwiba*) — **intensive**: "**thoroughly** rewarded."
- **Why this word:** using the **reward-word** for punishment is ⭐ **the surah's last act of poetic justice** — the vocabulary itself gets flipped, exactly as the laughter did.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***thawāb* = REWARD (the good-deeds word).*** ⭐ **Used sarcastically for the punished.** ⭐ **And *thāba* = "to RETURN" — so *thawāb* = "what comes BACK to you."** ⭐ **A surah about cheating scales ends on "what returns to you."**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐ **v.2 vs. v.3 — the GRAMMAR is the sin.** Taking: **Form VIII + Form X** (elaborate). Giving: **plain Form I** (lazy). ⚠️ **If you read them as the same verb, you miss the entire point.**
2. ⭐⭐ **سِجِّينٍ (v.7) ↔ عِلِّيِّينَ (v.18)** — mirrored structure, **v.7–9 ↔ v.18–20**, word for word. ***Sijjīn* = prison/LOW; *ʿIlliyyīn* = heights/HIGH.** ⚠️ **Learn as a pair.**
3. ⭐⭐ **رَانَ (v.14) → لَّمَحْجُوبُونَ (v.15)** — **the rust becomes the veil.** ⚠️ **Read them as one movement, not two verses.**
4. ⭐⭐ **v.15 (veiled) comes BEFORE v.16 (fire)** — deliberately. ⚠️ **The separation is the greater punishment; the order proves it.**
5. ⭐⭐ **انقَلَبُوا (83:31) and يَنقَلِبُ (84:9)** — ⚠️ **same verb, same phrase ("returned to his family"), one surah apart, opposite joys.** Plus **84:13** uses it a third way.
6. ⭐ **ثُوِّبَ (v.36)** — *thawāb* is the **REWARD** word, used **sarcastically**. ⚠️ **Don't translate it straight.**
7. ⭐ **مُعْتَدٍ (v.12)** — ***manqūṣ***: the final **yāʾ drops**. Same as *rāqin* (75:27), *al-jawāri* (81:16).
8. ⭐ **صَالُو (v.16)** — the **nūn drops** in *iḍāfah* (*ṣālūna* → *ṣālū'l-jaḥīm*). Same rule as *banīh* (80:36).
9. ⚠️ **وَيْلٌ (v.1) ≠ وَلِيّ (ally) ≠ وَالٍ (governor)** — different roots, easy to confuse when reading fast.
10. ⭐ **الْمُطَفِّفِينَ (v.1)** — remember ***ṭaff* = "the negligible amount."** ⚠️ **Their excuse IS their name.**
