# Surah Al-Ghāshiyah (88) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–26)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**It's built as two perfectly mirrored panels — and the mirroring is the argument.**

| | **Panel A (v.2–7)** | **Panel B (v.8–16)** |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | *wujūhun yawmaʾidhin **khāshiʿah*** | *wujūhun yawmaʾidhin **nāʿimah*** |
| | **humbled** faces | **blissful** faces |
| Their work | *ʿāmilatun nāṣibah* — **toiling, exhausted** | *li-saʿyihā **rāḍiyah*** — **pleased with its striving** |
| Drink | a spring at **boiling point** | a spring **flowing** |
| Food | *ḍarīʿ* — thorns that **don't feed** | (no hunger mentioned at all) |
| Sound | — | *lā tasmaʿu fīhā **lāghiyah*** — **no idle talk** |

⭐ **Both groups WORKED.** That's the sting. Panel A's people are *ʿāmilah* (working) and *nāṣibah* (worn out) — ⭐ **they weren't lazy. They toiled.** Panel B's people are *rāḍiyah li-saʿyihā* — **pleased with their striving.**

⭐ **The difference isn't effort. It's direction.** One group exhausted itself and got thorns; the other strove and got a garden.

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐ **This surah does not condemn laziness. It condemns misdirected effort.** ⭐ **Everyone is tired at the end. The question is what you were tired FOR.**

---

## The Big Picture

1. **The Enveloper** (v.1) — *has the report of the Overwhelming reached you?*
2. **Panel A — the exhausted** (v.2–7) — worked hard, got a boiling spring and thorns.
3. **Panel B — the blissful** (v.8–16) — pleased with their striving; the furniture of Paradise, listed piece by piece.
4. **Look at four things** (v.17–20) — ⭐ **the camel, the sky, the mountains, the earth.** The most grounded evidence in the Qurʾān.
5. **Your job** (v.21–22) — *remind. You are not a controller over them.*
6. **The return** (v.23–26) — *to Us is their return; upon Us is their reckoning.*

## §1 — The Enveloper (v.1)

**1. الْغَاشِيَةِ — *al-ghāshiyah*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**covering over / enveloping / overwhelming**" family — *ghashiya* = **to cover something completely, to come over it**. Related: *ghishāwah* = **a covering over the eyes**; *ghāshiyah* also meant **the cover thrown over a saddle**.
- **Here it means:** "Has there come to you the report of **the Overwhelming / the Enveloper**?"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **another name for the Day, and each surah picks a different sense.** Track them:
  - **Nabaʾ 78:2** — *an-Nabaʾ*, **the News** (what you **hear about**)
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:34** — *aṭ-Ṭāmmah*, **the Overwhelming Flood** (what **submerges**)
  - **ʿAbasa 80:33** — *aṣ-Ṣākhkhah*, **the Deafener** (what **destroys hearing**)
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:1** — *al-Ghāshiyah*, **the Enveloper** (what **covers you over**)
  
  ⭐ **Four surahs, four names, four ways of experiencing the same Day.**
  
  ⭐ **And *ghashiya* has a stunning internal echo coming up:** in **Shams 91:4** — *wa'l-layli idhā **yaghshāhā*** ("by the night when it **covers** it"). ⭐ **Same root: night covering the sun, and the Day covering you.** **What night does gently every evening, this Day does permanently.**
- **Grammar bite:** active participle used as a **name**, feminine (the described noun — *as-sāʿah*, the Hour — is **deleted**, standard Qurʾānic practice).
- **Why this word:** *ghāshiyah* implies **being covered from above with no gap** — you don't dodge something that envelops.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***ghashiya* = to COVER completely.** ⭐ **Shams 91:4 uses it for night covering the sun.** ⭐ **Four names for the Day: the News, the Flood, the Deafener, the Enveloper.**

## §2 — Panel A: The Exhausted (v.2–7)

**2. خَاشِعَةٌ — *khāshiʿah*** · v.2
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**humbled, lowered, submissive**" family — ***khushūʿ***, the humility you're meant to have in ṣalāh.
- **Here it means:** "Faces, that Day, will be **humbled / downcast**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **you own this word exactly — it's identical to Nāziʿāt 79:9** (*abṣāruhā **khāshiʿah***, "their eyes downcast"). ⭐ **Same word, two surahs.** Free.
  
  ⭐ **And the bitter irony is worth sitting with:** *khushūʿ* is what you're **asked to bring to prayer voluntarily**. ⭐ **On that Day, everyone has it — involuntarily, and too late.** The posture you could have chosen becomes the posture you're given.
  
  ⭐ **Note it describes *wujūh* (faces)** — the surah is going to spend eleven verses on **faces**, because ⭐ **a face is where an inner state becomes visible.**
- **Grammar bite:** active participle, feminine (agreeing with *wujūh*). ⭐ **Note *wujūhun* is INDEFINITE** — "*some* faces" — which is what makes room for the other kind in v.8.
- **Why this word:** *khāshiʿah* over "sad" — because it names ⭐ **a submission posture**, not just an emotion.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***Khushūʿ* = what you're supposed to have in prayer.** ⭐ **Identical to Nāziʿāt 79:9.** ⭐ **On that Day everyone has it — too late to count.**

**3. عَامِلَةٌ نَّاصِبَةٌ — *ʿāmilatun nāṣibah*** · v.3
- **Root feelings:** *ʿāmilah* — the "**working / doing**" family (*ʿamal* = work, deed; *ʿāmil* = a worker). *nāṣibah* — ⭐ the "**toil to the point of exhaustion**" family — *naṣab* = **weariness, hardship**; ⭐ **also *naṣaba* = to set something upright** (hence *manṣib* = a post/position, and *nuṣibat* in v.19 — "the mountains, how they were **set up**"!).
- **Here it means:** "**working, exhausted**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **THE most important two words in the surah, and most readers skip past them.**
  
  ⭐ **These people are not condemned for being lazy.** They are described as ***ʿāmilah*** (**working**) and ***nāṣibah*** (**worn out from it**). ⭐ **They put in the hours. They're tired. And it earned them nothing.**
  
  ⭐ **Two classical readings, both devastating:**
  - **(a) They toiled in *dunya*** — striving hard at the wrong things, or doing "good" deeds with no faith behind them. ⭐ **Effort without direction.**
  - **(b) They toil *on that Day*** — dragging chains, climbing, laboring endlessly. ⭐ **Effort that never ends and never pays.**
  
  ⭐ **Either way the lesson is identical: being busy is not the same as being on the right road.**
  
  ⭐ **And the contrast in Panel B is exact:** v.9 says they are ***rāḍiyah li-saʿyihā*** — "**pleased with its striving**." ⭐ **Both groups strove. One is exhausted; one is pleased.**
- **Grammar bite:** two participles in a row, both feminine, both describing *wujūh*. ⭐ **Note there's no "and" between them** (asyndeton) — which makes them land as **one compound state**: *working-exhausted*.
- **Why this word:** *nāṣibah* over "tired" — ***naṣab* is the specific word for the weariness that comes FROM labour.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **They WORKED. They're EXHAUSTED. And they got nothing.** ⭐ **The surah condemns misdirected effort, not laziness.** ⭐ **Compare v.9: "pleased with its striving."** ⭐ *(And note *naṣaba* also = to set upright — it reappears in v.19!)*

**4. تَصْلَى نَارًا حَامِيَةً — *taṣlā nāran ḥāmiyah*** · v.4
- **Root feelings:** *taṣlā* — ⭐ the "**being roasted / scorched**" family (*ṣaliya*) — ⚠️ **not the root of *ṣalāh***. *ḥāmiyah* — the "**intense heat**" family — ⭐ **the same root as *ḥamīm* (scalding water) in Nabaʾ 78:25**; also *ḥummā* = a **fever**.
- **Here it means:** "**Roasting** in a **scorching** Fire."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the *ṣaliya* root has now appeared in FIVE consecutive surahs** — this is one of the best runs available to you:
  - **Infiṭār 82:15** — *ya**ṣlawna**hā* (they burn in it)
  - **Muṭaffifīn 83:16** — *la-**ṣālū**'l-jaḥīm* (surely burning in the Blaze)
  - **Inshiqāq 84:12** — *wa ya**ṣlā** saʿīrā* (and he will burn in a Blaze)
  - **Aʿlā 87:12** — *alladhī ya**ṣlā**'n-nāra'l-kubrā* (who burns in the greatest Fire)
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:4** — *ta**ṣlā** nāran ḥāmiyah*
  
  ⭐ **Five surahs, one verb. Learn it once, collect five.**
  
  ⭐ **And *ḥāmiyah* ties to Nabaʾ 78:25's *ḥamīm*** — ⭐ **remember the strange double life of that root: it also gives *ḥamīm* = a close, intimate friend.** Warmth at both extremes.
- **Grammar bite:** *taṣlā* is weak-final, feminine (the subject is *wujūh*); *nāran* is its object; *ḥāmiyah* an adjective. ⭐ **Note the rhyme: *ḥāmiyah, āniyah, nāʿimah, rāḍiyah, ʿāliyah, lāghiyah, jāriyah* — the whole surah rides the "-iyah" ending.**
- **Why this word:** *ḥāmiyah* (actively scorching) rather than just "hot" — the fire is **doing** something.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Ṣaliya* = roasted IN it** — ⭐ **FIVE consecutive surahs: 82:15, 83:16, 84:12, 87:12, 88:4.** ⚠️ **Not the *ṣalāh* root.** ⭐ **And *ḥāmiyah* shares a root with *ḥamīm* (Nabaʾ 78:25).**

**5. عَيْنٍ آنِيَةٍ — *ʿaynin āniyah*** · v.5
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**reaching its full time / reaching peak intensity**" family — *anā* = for something to **reach its moment**; ***ānin* = boiling at maximum** — water that has reached the absolute peak of heat. Related: *al-ān* = **now**, this very moment.
- **Here it means:** "**Given to drink from a spring at boiling point.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **A GENUINE TRAP — and one of the best in the whole juzʾ.**
  
  ⚠️ ⭐ **آنِيَة here (*āniyah*, "boiling") is NOT the same word as آنِيَة in Insān 76:15 (*āniyah*, "vessels")!** ⭐ **Identical spelling. Completely different roots and meanings.**
  - **Insān 76:15** — *bi-**āniyatin** min fiḍḍah* = "with **VESSELS** of silver" (root: *inā'*, a container)
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:5** — *ʿaynin **āniyah*** = "a spring at **BOILING POINT**" (root: *anā*, to reach peak)
  
  ⭐ **Same letters, one is a silver cup in Paradise, one is scalding water in Hell.** ⚠️ **Flag this one hard.**
  
  ⭐ **And the irony of the word:** *ānin* comes from "**reaching its time**" — ⭐ **the water has reached its moment of maximum heat, exactly when they reach theirs.**
- **Grammar bite:** *tusqā* is **passive** ("they are **given** to drink") — ⭐ **no choice in the matter.** *Āniyah* is an active participle, feminine.
- **Why this word:** *āniyah* over "hot" — it means **at the absolute peak**, not merely warm.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ⚠️ **آنِيَة (76:15) = SILVER VESSELS in Paradise. آنِيَة (88:5) = BOILING SPRING in Hell.** ⭐ **Same spelling, opposite worlds.** ⭐ *Al-ān* = **now** — same root: "reached its moment."

**6. ضَرِيعٍ — *ḍarīʿ*** · v.6
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ ***ḍarīʿ* is a specific desert plant** — a dry, thorny bramble (often identified with *shibriq* when dried) that ⭐ **camels won't eat, and which provides no nourishment at all.** The root *ḍ-r-ʿ* also carries **lowliness, abasement** (*taḍarruʿ* = **humble supplication** — ⭐ **the word for begging Allah humbly!**).
- **Here it means:** "They will have **no food except from a bitter thorn-bush**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the plant is chosen because of what it fails to do.**
  
  ⭐ **Every desert Arab knew *ḍarīʿ*: even a starving camel refuses it.** ⭐ **So the "food" of Hell is the one thing that the least fussy animal on earth will not eat.**
  
  ⭐ **And the root connection is haunting:** *ḍ-r-ʿ* also gives ***taḍarruʿ*** — **humbling yourself before Allah in supplication.** ⭐ **The root of *humble begging* names the food of those who refused to humble themselves.** They wouldn't do *taḍarruʿ*; now they eat *ḍarīʿ*.
- **Grammar bite:** genitive after *min*; ⭐ **note the exception structure: *laysa lahum ṭaʿāmun **illā** min ḍarīʿ*** — "no food **except**." **Same cruel technique as Nabaʾ 78:25** (*illā ḥamīman wa ghassāqā*): ⭐ **the "except" raises hope for one syllable, then destroys it.**
- **Why this word:** a **real, recognizable, useless plant** — far more visceral than an abstract description.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Ḍarīʿ* = the thorn even a starving CAMEL refuses.** ⭐ **And its root gives *taḍarruʿ* — humble begging.** ⭐ **They wouldn't beg humbly; now they eat the thorn.**

**7. لَّا يُسْمِنُ وَلَا يُغْنِي مِن جُوعٍ — *lā yusminu wa lā yughnī min jūʿ*** · v.7
- **Root feelings:** *yusminu* — ⭐ the "**fattening**" family — ***samīn* = fat**; *samn* = **clarified butter (ghee)**! ⭐ **Form IV = to make fat/nourish.** *yughnī* — ⭐ the "**sufficiency / richness**" family — you own it from **ʿAbasa 80:5** (*istaghnā*) and **ʿAbasa 80:37** (*yughnīh*). *jūʿ* — **hunger** (*jāʾiʿ* = hungry).
- **Here it means:** "It **neither nourishes nor avails against hunger**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the verse defines food by the two things food does — and denies both.**
  
  ⭐ **Food does two jobs: (a) it builds you up over time (*yusmin*), and (b) it stops the pain right now (*yughnī min jūʿ*).** ⭐ **This "food" does neither.** So it isn't food at all — ⭐ **it's the *experience* of eating with none of the point.**
  
  ⭐ **And the *ghinā* root connection is superb.** In **ʿAbasa 80:5**, the rich chief ***istaghnā*** — "**considered himself in need of nothing**." ⭐ **Here, the thorn *lā **yughnī*** — "**does not suffice**." **The man who felt he needed nothing ends up with food that supplies nothing.** Same root, exact inversion.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **يُغْنِي مِنْ** = "to avail/suffice **against**" — note the preposition. Both verbs negated by *lā*.
- **Why this word:** naming **both** functions of food is what makes the denial total.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***samīn* = fat; *samn* = ghee.** ⭐ **Food does two things: builds you up, and stops the hunger. This does NEITHER.** ⭐ **And *yughnī* ← ʿAbasa 80:5's *istaghnā*: "I need nothing" → "it supplies nothing."**

## §3 — Panel B: The Blissful (v.8–16)

**8. نَّاعِمَةٌ — *nāʿimah*** · v.8
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**blessing / softness / comfort**" family — *niʿmah* = a favour; *naʿīm* = bliss; ⭐ ***nāʿim* also means SOFT/smooth to the touch**; and ⭐ ***naʿam* = "YES."*** All from one root of **agreeable abundance**.
- **Here it means:** "[Other] faces, that Day, will be **blissful / in comfort**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the perfect mirror of v.2.** *Khāshiʿah* (humbled) vs. *nāʿimah* (blissful) — ⭐ **identical grammar (*wujūhun yawmaʾidhin* + participle), opposite content.** ⭐ **Read v.2 and v.8 back to back and you have the whole surah.**
  
  ⭐ **And the root's "softness" sense is doing quiet work:** ⭐ ***nāʿimah* means their faces are literally SMOOTH** — no lines of strain, no tension. ⭐ **Set that against v.3's *nāṣibah* (worn out) and v.40-style exhaustion: one face is creased with toil, the other is smooth.**
  
  ⭐ **Cross-surah:** *naʿīm* you own from **Insān 76:20** and **Infiṭār 82:13** (*inna'l-abrāra la-fī **naʿīm***).
- **Grammar bite:** active participle, feminine; note *wujūhun* is again **indefinite**.
- **Why this word:** *nāʿimah* carries **both** inner bliss **and** visible physical ease — ⭐ **a face you could pick out across a room.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***naʿam* = YES; *nāʿim* = SOFT; *niʿmah* = a blessing.** ⭐ **v.2 face = creased with humiliation. v.8 face = SMOOTH with ease.** ⭐ **And *naʿīm* is in Insān 76:20 and Infiṭār 82:13.**

**9. لِّسَعْيِهَا رَاضِيَةٌ — *li-saʿyihā rāḍiyah*** · v.9
- **Root feelings:** *saʿy* — ⭐ the "**striving / hurrying**" family — ***the Saʿī*** between Ṣafā and Marwah. *rāḍiyah* — the "**contentment / pleasure**" family (*riḍā* = satisfaction; *marḍiyy* = pleasing).
- **Here it means:** "**Pleased with its striving.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **THE VERSE THAT UNLOCKS THE SURAH.**
  
  ⭐ **Look at the pair with v.3 one more time:**
  | v.3 (Panel A) | v.9 (Panel B) |
  |---|---|
  | *ʿāmilatun **nāṣibah*** | *li-**saʿyihā** rāḍiyah* |
  | working — **EXHAUSTED** | striving — **PLEASED** |
  
  ⭐ **Both worked. Both strove. One ended exhausted, one ended satisfied.** ⭐ **The verse says the reward is being *pleased with your own striving* — looking back at what you spent your life on and being glad.**
  
  ⭐ **And *saʿy* is now in FOUR surahs:**
  - **Insān 76:22** — *kāna **saʿyukum** mashkūrā* ("your **striving** has been appreciated")
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:22** — Pharaoh turned his back ***yasʿā*** (**hurrying away**)
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:35** — man remembers ***mā saʿā*** (**what he strove for**)
  - **ʿAbasa 80:8** — the blind man came ***yasʿā*** (**running toward**)
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:9** — ***pleased with its striving***
  
  ⭐ **The Ṣafā–Marwah root, tracking every direction a person can run.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **note *li-saʿyihā* is FRONTED** before *rāḍiyah* — "for **its striving** — pleased." ⭐ **Arabic moves a word forward to spotlight it.** The emphasis is on **what they strove for**, not on the pleasure.
- **Why this word:** *rāḍiyah* (pleased) rather than "rewarded" — ⭐ **because the reward described is a *feeling about your own past*.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **v.3: worked → EXHAUSTED. v.9: strove → PLEASED.** ⭐ **Same effort, different direction.** ⭐ **And *saʿy* = the Ṣafā–Marwah root, now in five verses across four surahs.**

**10. لَاغِيَةً — *lāghiyah*** · v.11
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**empty, void, pointless speech**" family — *laghw* = idle talk; *laghā* = **to be void / to annul**.
- **Here it means:** "**They will hear no idle talk therein.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **you own this exactly — same root as Nabaʾ 78:35** (*lā yasmaʿūna fīhā **laghwan** wa lā kidhdhāban*). ⭐ **Two surahs, same feature of Paradise: no nonsense.**
  
  ⭐ **And think about how strange this is as a *reward*.** Not "no pain," not "no fear" — ⭐ **"no pointless conversation."** ⭐ **The Qurʾān lists the absence of small talk as a luxury.** Which tells you something about how much of your life is spent on words that mean nothing.
  
  ⭐ **Note also *lāghiyah* is a participle** ("a thing that is idle") rather than *laghw* (the abstract noun in Nabaʾ). ⭐ **Slightly more vivid: not even one idle *utterance*.**
- **Grammar bite:** active participle, feminine (the noun *kalimah*, "a word," is deleted); accusative object of *tasmaʿu*.
- **Why this word:** *lāghiyah* names a very specific irritation — ⭐ **words that waste your time** — which most visions of paradise wouldn't think to remove.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Nabaʾ 78:35 = *laghwan*. Ghāshiyah 88:11 = *lāghiyah*.** ⭐ **Same root, same reward: no small talk, ever.**

**11. سُرُرٌ مَّرْفُوعَةٌ — *sururun marfūʿah*** · v.13
- **Root feelings:** *surur* — plural of *sarīr*, **a raised couch/throne**. ⭐ **Related to *surūr* = JOY** (the couch of gladness) — the two words share letters and the connection is often noted. *marfūʿah* — ⭐ the "**raising**" family — you own it from **ʿAbasa 80:14** (*ṣuḥufin… **marfūʿah***); ⭐ **also *marfūʿ* = the NOMINATIVE case in grammar** (the "raised" case!).
- **Here it means:** "Therein are **elevated couches**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **v.13–16 are a furniture catalogue — and that's deliberate.**
  
  ⭐ **Four items, four verses, each with a passive participle describing its state:**
  - **v.13** — couches, ***marfūʿah*** (**raised**)
  - **v.14** — cups, ***mawḍūʿah*** (**set out / placed ready**)
  - **v.15** — cushions, ***maṣfūfah*** (**lined up in rows**)
  - **v.16** — carpets, ***mabthūthah*** (**spread out everywhere**)
  
  ⭐ **Every single one is a PASSIVE participle: raised, placed, arranged, spread.** ⭐ **Someone has already set the room up for you.** **Nothing needs doing.** That's the whole feeling — ⭐ **walking into a room prepared in advance.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Insān 76:13** — *muttakiʾīna fīhā ʿala'l-**arāʾik*** (reclining on **ornate couches**) and **Muṭaffifīn 83:23, 35** (*ʿala'l-**arāʾiki** yanẓurūn*). ⭐ **Three surahs of Paradise furniture.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **passive participle pattern *mafʿūlah*** — recognize the **ma- prefix + -ū-** shape across all four verses. **A single pattern, four words.**
- **Why this word:** "**raised**" couches means ⭐ **a vantage point** — which is why **Muṭaffifīn 83:23, 35** has them *looking* from the couches.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **v.13–16: raised, placed, lined up, spread out — ALL PASSIVE.** ⭐ **The room was prepared before you arrived.** ⭐ **And *marfūʿ* is the grammar term for nominative — the "raised" case.**

**12. أَكْوَابٌ مَّوْضُوعَةٌ — *akwābun mawḍūʿah*** · v.14
- **Root feelings:** *akwāb* — ⭐ plural of *kūb*, **cups without handles** — round, simple, elegant. *mawḍūʿah* — the "**placing / putting down**" family (*waḍaʿa* = to place; *mawḍūʿ* = **a topic/subject** — literally "something placed before you"!).
- **Here it means:** "and **cups placed ready**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **identical word to Insān 76:15** (*wa **akwābin** kānat qawārīrā*). ⭐ **Same handle-less cups, two surahs.** Free vocabulary.
  
  ⭐ **And *mawḍūʿah* is quietly perfect:** the cups are ⭐ **already poured and set out** — you don't ask, you don't wait, you don't pour. ⭐ **Compare Insān 76:16's *qaddarūhā taqdīrā*** ("measured to exact measure") — ⭐ **Insān shows the serving; Ghāshiyah shows the setup.**
- **Grammar bite:** *akwābun* is **indefinite nominative** (subject of an implied "there are"); *mawḍūʿah* is its adjective.
- **Why this word:** *mawḍūʿah* over "full" — the point is ⭐ **readiness**, not quantity.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***akwāb* sounds like "a cup"** — say it fast. ⭐ **Identical to Insān 76:15.** ⭐ ***Mawḍūʿ* = a topic** — literally "**placed before you**."

**13. نَمَارِقُ مَصْفُوفَةٌ — *namāriqu maṣfūfah*** · v.15
- **Root feelings:** *namāriq* — plural of *numruqah*, ⭐ **small cushions / pillows** you lean against. *maṣfūfah* — ⭐ the "**row / rank**" family — ***ṣaff***! ⭐ **The same word as the rows you stand in for prayer** (*sawwū ṣufūfakum*), and **Nabaʾ 78:38** (*aṣ-ṣaffan*), and **Fajr 89:22** (*ṣaffan ṣaffā*).
- **Here it means:** "and **cushions lined up in rows**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the *ṣaff* root turns up in the most unexpected place — Paradise's soft furnishings.**
  
  ⭐ **Track it:** **Nabaʾ 78:38** — the angels and the Spirit standing in ***ṣaff*** (**awe**). **Fajr 89:22** — the angels coming ***ṣaffan ṣaffā*** (**rank upon rank, terror**). ⭐ **Ghāshiyah 88:15 — CUSHIONS in *ṣaff* (comfort).**
  
  ⭐ **Same word: angels in ranks at judgment, and pillows in rows in Paradise.** ⭐ **The universe is orderly in both directions.**
  
  ⭐ **And you stand in *ṣaff* five times a day** — ⭐ **you already know this word with your body.**
- **Grammar bite:** *maṣfūfah* is a **passive participle** — the cushions **were lined up** by someone. ⭐ **Third of the four passives in v.13–16.**
- **Why this word:** *maṣfūfah* conveys ⭐ **arrangement and hospitality** — a room set for guests, not a room with things in it.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Ṣaff* = the prayer row you stand in every day.** ⭐ **Nabaʾ 78:38 = angels in ranks. Fajr 89:22 = angels rank upon rank. Ghāshiyah 88:15 = CUSHIONS in rows.** ⭐ **Same word, terror and comfort.**

**14. زَرَابِيُّ مَبْثُوثَةٌ — *zarābiyyu mabthūthah*** · v.16
- **Root feelings:** *zarābiyy* — ⭐ **rich, luxurious carpets** — plural of *zarbiyyah*, a fine ornamented rug. *mabthūthah* — the "**scattering / spreading widely**" family (*batha* = to spread out, disperse; *inbithāth* = dispersal).
- **Here it means:** "and **carpets spread all around**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the final item, and the verb makes it feel abundant rather than tidy.**
  
  ⭐ ***Mabthūthah* = spread everywhere, scattered generously*** — ⭐ **not laid out in neat squares, but strewn liberally, more than you could use.** ⭐ **Compare *manthūran* in Insān 76:19** (scattered pearls) — **same feeling of casual excess.**
  
  ⭐ **And notice the room is now complete:** ⭐ **raised couches (to sit), cups (to drink), cushions (to lean on), carpets (underfoot).** ⭐ **Every surface of the experience is covered — vertical, horizontal, in hand, underfoot.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **note *zarābiyyu* takes a *ḍammah* without tanwīn** — it's a **diptote** (*mamnūʿ min aṣ-ṣarf*), like *ḥadāʾiq* in Nabaʾ 78:32. **Plural patterns of this shape usually are.**
- **Why this word:** *mabthūthah* over "laid" — ⭐ **generosity is in the scattering.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Couches, cups, cushions, carpets — the room is COMPLETE.** ⭐ ***Mabthūthah* = strewn everywhere**, like *manthūran* (Insān 76:19). ⭐ **More than you could use.**

## §4 — Look At Four Things (v.17–20)

*⭐ The most grounded evidence passage in the Qurʾān. No abstractions — four things any desert Arab could see from where he was standing, in a deliberate order: **from the animal at your feet to the ground under them.***

**15. الْإِبِلِ كَيْفَ خُلِقَتْ — *al-ibili kayfa khuliqat*** · v.17
- **Root feeling:** *ibil* — ⭐ **camels** (a collective noun — the whole category, not one animal).
- **Here it means:** "**Do they not look at the camels — how they were created?**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **one of the most famous verses in the Qurʾān for its choice of subject — and the sequence is the genius.**
  
  ⭐ **Look at the order of the four things:**
  - **v.17** — the **CAMEL** (right beside you)
  - **v.18** — the **SKY** (highest thing you can see)
  - **v.19** — the **MOUNTAINS** (the horizon)
  - **v.20** — the **EARTH** (under your feet)
  
  ⭐ **It starts with the camel, then goes UP to the sky, then DOWN to the mountains, then all the way down to the ground.** ⭐ **A complete 360° sweep — and it begins with the animal you're standing next to.**
  
  ⭐ **Why the camel first?** Because ⭐ **it was the single most important creature in that world** — transport, milk, meat, wool, wealth, survival. **You saw one every day and never once thought about it.** ⭐ **The Qurʾān's method throughout these surahs: the evidence is not exotic. It's the thing you stopped noticing.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare ʿAbasa 80:24** — *fal-yanẓuri'l-insānu ilā **ṭaʿāmih*** ("let man look at his **FOOD**"). ⭐ **ʿAbasa says look at your lunch; Ghāshiyah says look at your camel.** **Same technique, two surahs.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **أَفَلَا يَنظُرُونَ** = *a* (question) + *fa* + *lā* + verb = "**Do they not then look…?**" — a **rebuking rhetorical question**. ⭐ **And *kayfa* ("how") makes it about the MANNER of creation, not the fact of it.**
- **Why this word:** the camel is ⭐ **maximally ordinary and maximally essential** — the perfect blind spot.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **Camel → sky → mountains → earth.** ⭐ **Beside you, then up, then across, then down.** ⭐ **A full 360° — starting with the animal you never look at.** ⭐ **(ʿAbasa 80:24 said: look at your FOOD.)**

**16. رُفِعَتْ / نُصِبَتْ / سُطِحَتْ — *rufiʿat / nuṣibat / suṭiḥat*** · v.18–20
- **Root feelings:** *rufiʿat* — **raised** (the *rafʿ* root, as in *marfūʿah* v.13!). *nuṣibat* — ⭐ **set upright, firmly planted** — ***the same root as *nāṣibah* in v.3!*** (*naṣaba* = to set up; *manṣib* = a post/office). *suṭiḥat* — **spread out flat** (*saṭḥ* = ⭐ **a flat roof/surface** — in modern Arabic *saṭḥ al-maktab* = a **desktop**!).
- **Here it means:** "and at the **sky — how it was raised**? and at the **mountains — how they were set up**? and at the **earth — how it was spread flat**?"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **three passive verbs in a row, and the pattern is the message.**
  
  ⭐ **All three are PASSIVE**: raised, set up, spread. ⭐ **Nobody asks "how did the sky rise?" — the grammar has already answered: it was RAISED. By someone.** ⭐ **Four verses, four passives (*khuliqat, rufiʿat, nuṣibat, suṭiḥat*) — the doer never named, because you already know.**
  
  ⭐ **And the *naṣaba* echo is stunning.** In **v.3**, the wretched faces were ***nāṣibah*** — **worn out from toil**. ⭐ **In v.19, the mountains were *nuṣibat* — SET UP FIRMLY.** ⭐ **Same root: human effort that collapses in exhaustion, and divine setting-up that stands forever.** **One root, two kinds of labour.**
  
  ⭐ **Cross-surah:** mountains as **firmly fixed** you own from **Nabaʾ 78:7** (*awtādan*, **tent pegs**) and **Nāziʿāt 79:32** (*arsāhā*, **anchored them**). ⭐ **Three surahs, three images: pegs, anchors, uprights.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **all Form I passives** (*fuʿilat* shape: *rufiʿat, nuṣibat, suṭiḥat, khuliqat*). ⭐ **Recognize the ـُ ـِ vowel pattern = passive.**
- **Why this word:** *suṭiḥat* ("spread flat") describes the earth ⭐ **as it functions for you** — a usable surface — which is the whole point of the passage.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **ALL FOUR VERBS ARE PASSIVE** — created, raised, set up, spread. ⭐ **The doer is never named because you already know.** ⭐ **And *nuṣibat* (v.19) = same root as *nāṣibah* (v.3): human toil that exhausts vs. divine setting-up that lasts.** ⭐ **Mountains: pegs (78:7), anchors (79:32), uprights (88:19).**

## §5 — Your Job, and the Return (v.21–26)

**17. مُذَكِّرٌ — *mudhakkir*** · v.21
- **Root feeling:** the "**reminding**" family — **Form II active participle** = **one who reminds**.
- **Here it means:** "**So remind — you are only a reminder-giver.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the FIFTH surah to state this job description, and now it's a noun, not just a verb.**
  - **Insān 76:29** — *"this is a **tadhkirah**"*
  - **ʿAbasa 80:11** — *"it is a **tadhkirah**"*
  - **Takwīr 81:27** — *"it is only a **dhikr**"*
  - **Aʿlā 87:9** — *"so **remind**"*
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:21** — *"so **remind** — you are only a ***mudhakkir***"*
  
  ⭐ **And *innamā* ("only") is a restriction particle** — ⭐ **it LIMITS the job.** ⭐ **This is a comfort, not a demotion:** you are responsible for **delivery**, not for **results**.
  
  ⭐ **Compare Nāziʿāt 79:45** — *innamā anta **mundhiru** man yakhshāhā* ("you are only a **warner**"). ⭐ **Same *innamā* + same limitation, two surahs.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **إِنَّمَا = restriction** ("nothing but"). Note the imperative *dhakkir* followed immediately by the participle *mudhakkir* — ⭐ **root repeated for emphasis.**
- **Why this word:** the **participle** makes it an **identity**, not just a task: *you ARE a reminder-giver.*
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***Innamā* = "ONLY."** ⭐ **Five surahs: your job is reminding.** ⭐ **Delivery is yours; results are not.**

**18. بِمُصَيْطِرٍ — *bi-muṣayṭir*** · v.22
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**controlling / dominating / having authority over**" family — *sayṭara* = **to have complete control**; ⭐ **modern Arabic uses *sayṭarah* constantly for "control/domination."** (Originally connected to a *sāṭir*, one who writes/registers, hence one who **keeps the ledger and enforces**.)
- **Here it means:** "**You are not over them a controller.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the sharpest statement in the Qurʾān about the limits of daʿwah.**
  
  ⭐ ***Musayṭir* is a strong word — a dominator, an enforcer, one who compels.** ⭐ **The verse doesn't say "be gentle with them"; it says the role of forcing doesn't exist for you at all.** ⭐ **You remind (v.21). You do not control (v.22).**
  
  ⭐ **And notice the balance with ʿAbasa 80:1–10:** there, the Prophet ﷺ was corrected for **over-prioritizing** someone who seemed strategically valuable. ⭐ **Here he's told he has no power over outcomes anyway.** ⭐ **Both verses remove the burden of managing results.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⚠️ ⭐ **SPELLING TRAP: this word is written with ص (ṣād) — مُصَيْطِر — but pronounced by most readers as *muṣayṭir*, and there's a well-known variant with س (*musayṭir*).** ⭐ **The ص spelling in the muṣḥaf is a known orthographic feature; don't be thrown by it.** Note also the ⭐ ***bāʾ zāʾidah*** (extra *bāʾ*) after the negation *lasta* — **emphasis**, same as **Takwīr 81:22, 24, 25** and **Infiṭār 82:16**.
- **Why this word:** *musayṭir* names ⭐ **exactly the temptation** — the wish to make people believe.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Sayṭarah* = CONTROL** (everyday modern Arabic). ⭐ **"You remind (v.21). You do not control (v.22)."** ⚠️ ⭐ **Note the ص spelling — a known orthographic feature.**

**19. تَوَلَّىٰ وَكَفَرَ — *tawallā wa kafar*** · v.23
- **Root feelings:** *tawallā* — ⭐ **Form V of the "turning" root** = **to turn your back deliberately** — you own it from **Qiyāmah 75:32** and **ʿAbasa 80:1**. *kafara* — ⭐ the "**covering up**" family (**burying a seed**) — you own it from **Insān 76:3**, **ʿAbasa 80:17, 42**, **Infiṭār 82:14**.
- **Here it means:** "**Except for whoever turns away and disbelieves.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the pair covers both directions of rejection.** ⭐ ***Tawallā*** = turning **away** (a movement outward). ⭐ ***Kafara*** = covering **over** (a movement inward). **You leave, and you bury.**
  
  ⭐ **And *tawallā* is now in three surahs — with a remarkable range:**
  - **Qiyāmah 75:32** — the arrogant denier *turned away*
  - **ʿAbasa 80:1** — the Prophet ﷺ *turned away* (a momentary frown — **corrected**)
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:23** — whoever *turns away* and disbelieves
  
  ⭐ **Same verb for a shoulder-turn and for total rejection.** ⭐ **Which tells you how seriously the direction you face is taken.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **إِلَّا مَن** = "except whoever" — ⭐ **this exception connects back to v.22** ("you are not a controller over them — **except** the one who turns away and disbelieves, whom **Allah** will punish"). **The exception clarifies who faces consequence, and from Whom.**
- **Why this word:** two verbs instead of one, because ⭐ **leaving and denying are separate acts.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***Kafara* = to BURY a seed.** ⭐ ***Tawallā* is in three surahs: 75:32 (the denier), 80:1 (a frown — corrected), 88:23 (rejection).** ⭐ **Turn away + cover over.**

**20. إِيَابَهُمْ — *iyābahum*** · v.25
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**returning**" family — *āba* = to return; ⭐ **the same root as *maʾāb* in Nabaʾ 78:22 and 78:39!** ⭐ **And you say it every journey: *āyibūna tāʾibūna ʿābidūna li-rabbinā ḥāmidūn*** — "**returning**, repenting, worshipping, praising our Lord" — the travel duʿāʾ.
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, **to Us is their return**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **this closes a thread that started three surahs ago.**
  
  ⭐ **In Nabaʾ 78:22, Hell was a *maʾāb* (a place of return) for the transgressors. In Nabaʾ 78:39, your Lord was a *maʾāb*.** ⭐ **Two destinations, one word.** ⭐ **Here in Ghāshiyah 88:25, the ambiguity is gone: *ilaynā **iyābahum*** — "**to US is their return.**" ⭐ **No alternative offered. Everyone, one address.**
  
  ⭐ **And the word order is emphatic: *inna **ilaynā** iyābahum*** — ⭐ **"to Us" is FRONTED.** ⭐ **Same move as *ilā rabbika'l-mustaqarr* (Qiyāmah 75:12), *ilā rabbika'l-masāq* (75:30), *ilā rabbika muntahāhā* (Nāziʿāt 79:44).** ⭐ **Five surahs, one closing structure: everything terminates at Him.**
- **Grammar bite:** verbal noun (*maṣdar*) from a **hollow verb** (weak middle letter) — note the **madd** formed by the weak letter: *iyāb*.
- **Why this word:** *iyāb* (**return**) rather than "arrival" — ⭐ **you're going *back*, not forward. You came from Him.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***āyibūna* — the TRAVEL DUʿĀʾ you say every journey.** Same root. ⭐ **Nabaʾ 78:22 & 39: two *maʾāb*s. Ghāshiyah 88:25: only ONE.** ⭐ **And "to US" is fronted — five surahs use this move.**

**21. حِسَابَهُم — *ḥisābahum*** · v.26
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**counting / calculating**" family — *ḥisāb* = arithmetic, an account, **a bill** — you own it from **Qiyāmah 75:3** (*a-yaḥsabu*), **Nabaʾ 78:27, 36**, **Inshiqāq 84:8** (*ḥisāban yasīrā*).
- **Here it means:** "**Then upon Us is their reckoning.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the surah's final verse, and it's structurally beautiful.**
  
  ⭐ **Two clauses, perfectly matched:**
  - **v.25** — *inna **ilaynā** iyābahum* — "**to Us** is their **return**"
  - **v.26** — *thumma inna **ʿalaynā** ḥisābahum* — "**upon Us** is their **reckoning**"
  
  ⭐ **Both fronted, both *inna*-emphasized, both ending in *-hum*.** ⭐ **Return, then reckoning. The two halves of the same arrival.**
  
  ⭐ **And note the reassurance hidden in "*ʿalaynā*" (upon Us):** ⭐ **the reckoning is *Our* responsibility, not yours.** ⭐ **Read it against v.21–22 (remind, don't control): the surah ends by explicitly taking the job you can't do off your hands.**
  
  ⭐ **The *ḥ-s-b* root is now in five surahs** — and remember its sharpest irony from **Qiyāmah 75:3**: ⭐ **the *calculating* creature (*a-yaḥsabu*, "does he reckon?") who never calculated the day of *calculation*.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **note *thumma* here isn't just "then" in time** — it signals **an additional, weightier point**. And ⭐ ***ʿalaynā* (upon Us) = obligation/undertaking** — the same construction as *ʿalaynā jamʿahu wa qurʾānah* in **Qiyāmah 75:17**.
- **Why this word:** ending on ***ḥisāb*** closes the surah on ⭐ **precise accounting** — which is the answer to the two panels of faces it opened with.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **v.25 "TO Us their return" + v.26 "UPON Us their reckoning."** ⭐ **Perfectly matched final pair.** ⭐ **And *ʿalaynā* = it's OUR job — the same construction as Qiyāmah 75:17.** ⭐ **The *ḥisāb* root is now in five surahs.**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐ **آنِيَةٍ (v.5) vs. آنِيَة (Insān 76:15)** — ⚠️ **IDENTICAL SPELLING, completely different words.** *Āniyah* here = **a spring at BOILING POINT** (root: reaching peak). *Āniyah* there = **VESSELS of silver** (root: a container). ⭐ **The single best trap in this surah.**
2. ⭐⭐ **عَامِلَةٌ نَّاصِبَةٌ (v.3)** — ⚠️ **these people WORKED HARD.** The surah condemns **misdirected effort**, not laziness. **Compare v.9: "pleased with its striving."**
3. ⭐ **نَّاصِبَةٌ (v.3) and نُصِبَتْ (v.19)** — **same root**, sixteen verses apart: **human toil that exhausts** vs. **divine setting-up that stands forever.**
4. ⭐ **تَصْلَى (v.4)** — from *ṣaliya* (to be roasted). ⚠️ **NOT the root of *ṣalāh* (prayer).** ⭐ **Now in five consecutive surahs.**
5. ⭐ **مُصَيْطِرٍ (v.22)** — ⚠️ **written with ص in the muṣḥaf**, with a known variant in س. Don't be thrown by the spelling. Meaning: **a controller/enforcer.**
6. ⭐ **بِمُصَيْطِرٍ (v.22)** — the **بـ is EXTRA** (emphasis after negation), not "with." Same as **Takwīr 81:22, 24, 25** and **Infiṭār 82:16**.
7. ⭐ **v.13–16 are all PASSIVE participles** (*marfūʿah, mawḍūʿah, maṣfūfah, mabthūthah*) — ⭐ **the room was prepared before you arrived.** Learn the *mafʿūlah* shape once.
8. ⭐ **v.17–20 are all PASSIVE verbs** (*khuliqat, rufiʿat, nuṣibat, suṭiḥat*) — ⭐ **the doer is never named.** That's the argument.
9. ⭐ **ضَرِيعٍ (v.6)** — ⚠️ **not generic "thorns":** the specific plant **a starving camel refuses.** And its root gives ***taḍarruʿ*** — humble supplication.
10. ⭐ **زَرَابِيُّ (v.16)** — a **diptote**: takes *ḍammah* **without tanwīn**. Same class as *ḥadāʾiq* (Nabaʾ 78:32).
