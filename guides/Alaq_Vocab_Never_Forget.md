# Surah Al-ʿAlaq (96) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–19)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**Its first five verses are the first Qurʾān ever revealed — and they are about READING and WRITING.**

⭐ **The very first command given to humanity through the final Prophet ﷺ was not "pray," not "believe," not "fight."** ⭐ **It was ***IQRAʾ*** — **READ / RECITE**.

⭐ **And look at what the opening five verses contain:**
- ⭐ ***iqraʾ*** (read) — twice
- ⭐ ***ʿallama*** (taught) — twice
- ⭐ ***al-qalam*** (**THE PEN**)
- ⭐ ***mā lam yaʿlam*** (what he did not know)

⭐⭐ **Five verses, and *knowledge* vocabulary appears five times.** ⭐ **The religion opens with a reading lesson.**

⭐ **And then the surah splits in two.** ⭐ **v.1–5 are the first revelation. ⭐ **v.6–19 came later — and they're about a man who tried to stop someone praying.** ⭐ **The surah joins the beginning of knowledge to the first attempt to suppress it.**

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐⭐ **First word ever revealed: READ.** ⭐ **First object mentioned: THE PEN.** ⭐ **Last command in the surah: PROSTRATE AND DRAW NEAR.** ⭐ **From reading to bowing.**

---

## The Big Picture

1. **The first revelation** (v.1–5) — ⭐ **read, from a clot, by the pen, what you didn't know.**
2. **Man overreaches** (v.6–8) — ⭐ *he transgresses because he sees himself as needing nothing.*
3. **The man who forbids** (v.9–14) — ⭐ *have you seen the one who forbids a servant when he prays?*
4. **The threat** (v.15–18) — ⭐ **the forelock, and the guards of Hell.**
5. **The command** (v.19) — ⭐ **prostrate, and draw near.** *(A verse of prostration.)*

## §1 — The First Revelation (v.1–5)

**1. اقْرَأْ — *iqraʾ*** · v.1, 3
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**reading / reciting / gathering together**" family — *qaraʾa* = **to read aloud, to recite**; ⭐ **and the root also carries "to gather/collect"** (⭐ **a recitation *gathers* letters into words**). ⭐ ***QURʾĀN* = "The Recitation" — the same root.***
- **Here it means:** "**Read / Recite** in the name of your Lord who created."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **the first word of revelation, and the choice is the entire message.**
  
  ⭐ **A man who could not read was commanded to ***read***.** ⭐ **Which is precisely why the next words matter: ⭐ ***bi'smi rabbika*** — "**in the name of your Lord**."** ⭐ **The command is impossible by yourself. It becomes possible **through Him**.**
  
  ⭐ **And notice it's repeated in v.3 — *iqraʾ* twice.** ⭐ **First: *"read **in the name of your Lord who created**."* ⭐ **Second: *"read, **and your Lord is the Most Generous**."*** ⭐ **The first grounds reading in **creation**; the second in **generosity**.
  
  ⭐ **And you own the root's promise from Aʿlā 87:6** — *sa-**nuqriʾuka** fa-lā tansā* ("**We will MAKE YOU RECITE**, so you will not forget"). ⭐⭐ **Form IV — the causative.** ⭐ **ʿAlaq says "read"; Aʿlā says "We will make you able to."** ⭐ **The command and its enablement, in two surahs.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **imperative** with a **hamzah as the final root letter** (ق-ر-أ) — ⚠️ **note the sukūn on the hamzah: *iqraʾ*.** ⭐ **And *bi'smi* = *bi* + *ism* (name) — the same construction as *bismillāh*.**
- **Why this word:** ⭐ ***iqraʾ*** over "listen" or "proclaim" — ⭐ **because *reading* implies a **text**, and a text implies **preservation**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***QURʾĀN* = "The Recitation" — SAME ROOT as the first word ever revealed.** ⭐⭐ **A man who couldn't read was told to READ — "in the NAME of your Lord."** ⭐ **And Aʿlā 87:6: "We will MAKE you recite."**

**2. مِنْ عَلَقٍ — *min ʿalaq*** · v.2
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**clinging / hanging / adhering**" family — *ʿaliqa* = **to cling, to stick to**; ⭐ ***ʿalāqah* = a RELATIONSHIP*** (a bond that **clings**!); ***ʿalaq* = a clinging clot*** of congealed blood, and also **a leech** (which clings).
- **Here it means:** "**created man from a clinging clot.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **you own this word exactly from Qiyāmah 75:38** — *thumma kāna **ʿalaqatan** fa-khalaqa fa-sawwā* ("then he was **a clinging clot**, and He created and proportioned"). ⭐ **Same word (singular *ʿalaqah* there, collective *ʿalaq* here).**
  
  ⭐ **And the placement is the whole argument of v.1–2.** ⭐ **Verse 1: **READ** — the highest human activity. ⭐ **Verse 2: **you came from a clinging clot** — the humblest possible origin.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **Reading and a blood-clot, in consecutive verses.** ⭐ **The surah's opening move is: *the being capable of literacy started as something stuck to a wall*.**
  
  ⭐ **And *ʿalāqah* = a RELATIONSHIP is a free hook** — ⭐ **you use it constantly in Arabic.** ⭐ **Both are about **clinging**.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***ʿalaq*** is a **collective noun** (the *ʿalaqah* is its singular). ⭐ **Genitive after *min*.**
- **Why this word:** *ʿalaq* names the stage by ⭐ **its defining physical behaviour — clinging** — which is startlingly accurate for an implanted embryo.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***ʿAlāqah* = A RELATIONSHIP** (a bond that CLINGS). ⭐ **Same root.** ⭐⭐ **v.1 = READ (the highest act). v.2 = a CLINGING CLOT (the humblest origin).** ⭐ **Consecutive verses.** ⭐ **And Qiyāmah 75:38 used the same word.**

**3. الْأَكْرَمُ — *al-akram*** · v.3
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**nobility / generosity / honour**" family — *karam* = generosity; ⭐ **you own it from ʿAbasa 80:13 (*mukarramah*), ʿAbasa 80:16 (*kirām*), Takwīr 81:19 (*karīm*), Infiṭār 82:6 (*al-Karīm*), Infiṭār 82:11 (*kirāman*), Fajr 89:15 & 17 (*akramahu*, *tukrimūna*).**
- **Here it means:** "**Read, and your Lord is the Most Generous.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **it's a SUPERLATIVE — and that's rare and significant.**
  
  ⭐ **Not *al-Karīm* (the Generous) but ***al-Akram*** — **THE MOST GENEROUS**, the superlative form.** ⭐ **This is the only place in the Qurʾān this exact form is used as a description of Allah.**
  
  ⭐ **And why *here*?** ⭐ **Because of what follows: ⭐ **He teaches by the pen, and teaches man what he did not know.** ⭐ **The context of the superlative is ***KNOWLEDGE***.** ⭐ **The surah is saying: the highest expression of His generosity is that **He teaches you.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Infiṭār 82:6** — *mā gharraka bi-rabbika'l-**Karīm*** ("what deceived you about your Lord, **the Generous**?"). ⭐ **There, generosity is what you took advantage of. ⭐ **Here, generosity is what teaches you.** ⭐ **Same root, two surahs, two responses.**
  
  ⭐ **And notice: v.1's *iqraʾ* is grounded in **creation** (*alladhī khalaq*); v.3's *iqraʾ* is grounded in **generosity** (*al-Akram*).** ⭐ **He made you, and He's generous enough to teach you.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***afʿal*** superlative pattern (like *aḥsan*, *asfal*, *akbar*). ⭐ **Note it's definite (*al-akram*) without a following *iḍāfah* — a known usage of the superlative.**
- **Why this word:** the **superlative** signals that ⭐ **what comes next (teaching) is generosity at its peak.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Al-AKRAM* = "the MOST Generous" — a SUPERLATIVE, used here only.** ⭐⭐ **And the context is TEACHING.** ⭐ **The peak of His generosity is that He teaches you.** ⭐ **Compare Infiṭār 82:6's *al-Karīm* — generosity you took advantage of.**

**4. عَلَّمَ بِالْقَلَمِ — *ʿallama bi'l-qalam*** · v.4
- **Root feelings:** *ʿallama* — ⭐ **Form II of the "knowing" root** = **to TEACH** (*ʿilm* = knowledge; *ʿālim* = a scholar; *ʿālam* = a world — ⭐ **that by which He is known!**). *qalam* — ⭐ **A PEN** — originally ⭐ **a reed cut and trimmed for writing** (*qalama* = **to trim/pare** — ⭐ **still used for trimming nails: *taqlīm al-aẓāfir*!**).
- **Here it means:** "**who taught by the pen.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **the first physical object ever mentioned in revelation is A PEN.**
  
  ⭐ **Think about that.** ⭐ **Not a sword, not a throne, not a mountain. ⭐ **A pen.** ⭐ **The tool of **preservation and transmission**.
  
  ⭐ **And *qalam* comes from *qalama* = TO TRIM.** ⭐ **A pen is literally "the trimmed thing" — ⭐ **a reed cut to a point so it can hold ink.** ⭐ **You use the same verb for trimming your nails (*taqlīm*).** ⭐ **Free hook.**
  
  ⭐ **And notice the two teachings in v.4–5:**
  - **v.4** — taught **BY THE PEN** (⭐ **mediated knowledge** — what humans pass to humans, across time)
  - **v.5** — taught man **WHAT HE DID NOT KNOW** (⭐ **direct knowledge** — what only He can give)
  
  ⭐⭐ **Two channels of learning, named in the first revelation.** ⭐ **The pen carries what's known; He supplies what isn't.**
  
  ⭐ **And this answers v.1's problem:** ⭐ **an unlettered man is told to READ — and immediately told that ⭐ **the One commanding him is the One who invented writing itself.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form II** (*ʿallama* = to **cause** to know = to teach). ⭐ **The *bi-* on *bi'l-qalam* = **by means of** (instrumental *bāʾ*).**
- **Why this word:** *qalam* is ⭐ **the humblest possible tool for the grandest possible claim** — that knowledge can outlive the knower.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **The FIRST OBJECT in the FIRST REVELATION is ⭐ A PEN.** ⭐⭐ **And *qalam* ← *qalama* = TO TRIM** (as in *taqlīm al-aẓāfir*, nail-trimming). ⭐ **A pen is "the trimmed reed."** ⭐⭐ **v.4 = knowledge via the PEN (human to human). v.5 = knowledge only HE can give.**

**5. مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ — *mā lam yaʿlam*** · v.5
- **Root feeling:** the "**knowing**" family — *ʿalima* = to know; *ʿilm* = knowledge.
- **Here it means:** "**taught man what he did not know.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the last verse of the first revelation — and it's the one that should humble everyone reading it.**
  
  ⭐ **Every single thing you know, you were **taught**.** ⭐ **You did not generate it.** ⭐ **The verse frames **all human knowledge** as received.
  
  ⭐ **And notice the deliberate contrast with v.6–7, which follow immediately:**
  - **v.5** — man was **TAUGHT what he did not know** (he began ignorant)
  - **v.6–7** — man **TRANSGRESSES** because **he sees himself as needing nothing**
  
  ⭐⭐ **The one who had to be taught everything concludes he needs nothing.** ⭐ **That's the surah's indictment, and it's set up by v.5.**
  
  ⭐ **Also worth noting: the root *ʿ-l-m* appears THREE times in v.4–5** (*ʿallama, ʿallama, yaʿlam*) — ⭐ **hammering the theme of the first revelation.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **لَمْ + jussive = past negation** ("did **not** know"). ⚠️ ⭐ **Note *yaʿlam* has dropped its final vowel** (jussive).
- **Why this word:** stating it as ⭐ **"what he did NOT know"** rather than "knowledge" makes the **prior ignorance** the emphasis.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **"Taught man WHAT HE DID NOT KNOW."** ⭐ **Everything you know was given.** ⭐⭐ **And v.6–7 immediately say: this creature then decides he **needs nothing**.** ⭐ **The taught one claims self-sufficiency.**

## §2 — Man Overreaches (v.6–8)

**6. لَيَطْغَىٰ — *la-yaṭghā*** · v.6
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**overflowing all bounds**" family — original image: ⭐ **water bursting its banks and flooding.**
- **Here it means:** "**No! Indeed, man does transgress all bounds.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the SEVENTH appearance of this root in your study — one of your most-tracked words:**
  - **Nabaʾ 78:22** — *aṭ-**ṭāghīn*** (Hell is the refuge for those who transgress)
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:17** — *innahu **ṭaghā*** (**Pharaoh**)
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:37** — *fa-ammā man **ṭaghā*** (**whoever** — transferable)
  - **Fajr 89:11** — *alladhīna **ṭaghaw*** (ʿĀd, Thamūd, Pharaoh)
  - **Shams 91:11** — *bi-**ṭaghwāhā*** (Thamūd)
  - **ʿAlaq 96:6** — *inna'l-insāna la-**yaṭghā*** (⭐ **MAN, as such**)
  
  ⭐⭐ **And notice the progression across those six.** ⭐ **It started with a category (*the transgressors*), got a face (*Pharaoh*), became transferable (*whoever*), covered civilizations (*ʿĀd, Thamūd*), and ⭐ **now lands on ***al-insān* — MAN HIMSELF**.
  
  ⭐ **The diagnosis has widened to include everyone.**
  
  ⭐ **And the emphasis is heavy: *kallā* (No!) + *inna* + emphatic *lām*** — ⭐ **"No! Indeed, man MOST CERTAINLY overflows."**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **كَلَّا = a particle of rebuke** — you own it from **Qiyāmah 75:11**, **Nabaʾ 78:4**, **ʿAbasa 80:11**. ⭐ **Weak-final verb carrying the *-ā* rhyme.**
- **Why this word:** *ṭaghā* frames the failure as ⭐ **a boundary problem**, not a knowledge problem — which is the point, since v.5 just said he was taught.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Ṭaghā* = WATER BURSTING ITS BANKS.** ⭐ **SEVEN appearances: 78:22, 79:17, 79:37, 89:11, 91:11, 96:6.** ⭐⭐ **And the progression ends here at ***al-insān* — MAN HIMSELF.**

**7. أَن رَّآهُ اسْتَغْنَىٰ — *an raʾāhu'staghnā*** · v.7
- **Root feelings:** *raʾā* — **to see** — ⭐ **here in the sense of "to regard/consider."** *istaghnā* — ⭐ **Form X of the "richness" root** = ⭐ **to CONSIDER yourself free of need.**
- **Here it means:** "**because he sees himself as self-sufficient.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **THE CAUSE. This verse gives the *reason* for v.6 — and it's a word you own deeply.**
  
  ⭐ ***Istaghnā* is now in THREE surahs, and this one names it as the ROOT CAUSE:**
  - **ʿAbasa 80:5** — *ammā mani **staghnā*** — the rich chief the Prophet ﷺ turned toward
  - **Layl 92:8** — *bakhila wa'**staghnā*** — the withholder
  - ⭐ **ʿAlaq 96:7** — *an **raʾāhu'staghnā*** — ⭐ **WHY man transgresses at all**
  
  ⭐⭐ **ʿAbasa and Layl gave you *cases*. ʿAlaq gives you the *diagnosis*.** ⭐ **Every transgression traces back to one sentence: "I don't need anything."**
  
  ⭐ **And ***raʾāhu*** ("he SEES himself") is crucial — ⭐ **it's a *perception*, not a fact.** ⭐ **He isn't self-sufficient; ⭐ **he *sees himself* that way.** ⭐ **Form X (*istaghnā*) already means "to consider yourself rich" — ⭐ **so the verse doubles the illusion: he *sees himself* as one who *considers himself* independent.**
  
  ⭐ **And remember ʿAbasa's payoff:** ⭐ ***istaghnā* (80:5) → *yughnīh* (80:37)** — ⭐ **he wanted to need nothing, and got a concern that left room for nothing.**
  
  ⭐ **And Ḍuḥā 93:8's redemption:** ⭐ ***aghnā*** — **HE enriched you** (Form IV). ⭐ **The problem was never wealth — it was **claiming the sufficiency for yourself.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form X (اسْتَفْعَلَ) = "to CONSIDER yourself X"** — ⭐ **the highest-yield form-pattern in these surahs.** ⭐ **The *an* here is causal ("because").**
- **Why this word:** *istaghnā* moves the fault from **circumstance** to **self-assessment** — ⭐ **which is why anyone can fall into it.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE DIAGNOSIS: man transgresses **because he sees himself as needing nothing**.** ⭐⭐ **THREE surahs: ʿAbasa 80:5 (a case), Layl 92:8 (a case), ʿAlaq 96:7 (⭐ the CAUSE).** ⭐ **And Form X = "to CONSIDER yourself X" — it's a perception, not a fact.**

**8. الرُّجْعَىٰ — *ar-rujʿā*** · v.8
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**returning**" family — *rajaʿa* = to return; ⭐ **you own it from Ṭāriq 86:8** (*ʿalā **rajʿihi** la-qādir*) and **Ṭāriq 86:11** (*wa's-samāʾi dhāti'r-**rajʿ***).
- **Here it means:** "**Indeed, to your Lord is the return.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the answer to *istaghnāʾ*, delivered in four words.**
  
  ⭐ **He thinks he needs nothing (v.7). ⭐ **The reply: **you're going back.** ⭐ **Independence is impossible for something that has to return to its source.**
  
  ⭐ **And notice the FRONTING: *inna **ilā rabbika**'r-rujʿā*** — ⭐ **"to your Lord" comes before the subject.** ⭐ **In Arabic, fronting = RESTRICTION: **to Him ALONE**.**
  
  ⭐ **This is now the SIXTH surah using this exact move:**
  - **Qiyāmah 75:12** — *ilā rabbika yawmaʾidhin **al-mustaqarr***
  - **Qiyāmah 75:30** — *ilā rabbika yawmaʾidhin **al-masāq***
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:44** — *ilā rabbika **muntahāhā***
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:25** — *inna **ilaynā** iyābahum*
  - **Sharḥ 94:8** — *wa **ilā rabbika** fa'rghab*
  - ⭐ **ʿAlaq 96:8** — *inna **ilā rabbika**'r-rujʿā*
  
  ⭐⭐ **Six surahs, one structure: everything terminates at Him.**
  
  ⭐ **And the *rujʿā* form is a *fuʿlā* noun** — ⭐ **same shape as *al-yusrā*, *al-ʿusrā*, *al-ḥusnā*, *al-ūlā*.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***rujʿā*** is a **verbal noun** on the *fuʿlā* pattern, carrying the rhyme.
- **Why this word:** *rujʿā* (**the return**) rather than "judgment" — ⭐ **because "returning" refutes "self-sufficiency" more directly than any threat.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **"I need nothing" (v.7) → **"you're going BACK"** (v.8).** ⭐ **The perfect refutation.** ⭐⭐ **And "to your Lord" is FRONTED = to Him ALONE — ⭐ **SIX surahs use this: 75:12, 75:30, 79:44, 88:25, 94:8, 96:8.**

## §3 — The Man Who Forbids (v.9–14)

**9. أَرَأَيْتَ الَّذِي يَنْهَىٰ — *a-raʾayta'lladhī yanhā*** · v.9
- **Root feelings:** *a-raʾayta* — ⭐ **"have you seen…?"** — an idiom meaning ⭐ **"consider / tell me about…"**, inviting the listener to look at something outrageous. *yanhā* — ⭐ the "**forbidding / bringing to an end**" family — *nahy* = a prohibition; ⭐ ***nihāyah* = the END***; ⭐ **you own it from Nāziʿāt 79:40** (*wa **nahā**'n-nafsa ʿani'l-hawā*) and **79:44** (*muntahāhā*).
- **Here it means:** "**Have you seen the one who forbids…?**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the repetition of *a-raʾayta* three times (v.9, 11, 13) is the surah's rhetorical hammer.**
  
  ⭐ **v.9 — "Have you seen the one who **forbids** a servant when he prays?"**
  ⭐ **v.11 — "Have you seen **if he [the servant] were upon guidance**?"**
  ⭐ **v.13 — "Have you seen **if he [the forbidder] denied and turned away**?"**
  
  ⭐⭐ **Three questions, none answered.** ⭐ **The Qurʾān just keeps asking you to *look*.** ⭐ **The absurdity is left to speak for itself: ⭐ **someone is standing between a worshipper and his Lord.**
  
  ⭐ **And the *nahā* root has a beautiful contrast you own:** ⭐ **Nāziʿāt 79:40 — *wa **nahā**'n-nafsa ʿani'l-hawā*** ("**restrained** the soul from desire") — ⭐ **a PRAISED act of forbidding.** ⭐ **Here, the same verb is used for ⭐ **forbidding someone from prayer.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **Same verb: restrain yourself from desire (noble) / restrain another from worship (monstrous).** ⭐ **The verb is neutral; the object is everything.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **أَرَأَيْتَ** is an idiom ("tell me / consider"), ⚠️ **not a literal question about eyesight.** ⭐ **Weak-final *yanhā* carries the rhyme.**
- **Why this word:** *yanhā* (**to forbid**) rather than a word for persecution — ⭐ **it's the *smallness* of the act that's damning: he's just telling someone to stop.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Nihāyah* = THE END.* ⭐⭐ **Nāziʿāt 79:40: *nahā* = restraining YOURSELF from desire (noble). ʿAlaq 96:9: *yanhā* = restraining ANOTHER from prayer (monstrous).** ⭐ **Same verb, and the object decides everything.** ⭐ **Three *a-raʾayta*'s (v.9, 11, 13), none answered.**

**10. عَبْدًا إِذَا صَلَّىٰ — *ʿabdan idhā ṣallā*** · v.10
- **Root feelings:** *ʿabd* — ⭐ **a servant/slave** (*ʿibādah* = worship — ⭐ **the same root: worship is *servanthood***). *ṣallā* — the "**prayer**" family — ⭐ **you own it from Qiyāmah 75:31** (*fa-lā ṣaddaqa wa lā **ṣallā***) and **Aʿlā 87:15** (*wa dhakara'sma rabbihi **fa-ṣallā***).
- **Here it means:** "**a servant when he prays.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the word *ʿabd* is doing quiet, devastating work.**
  
  ⭐ **The one being forbidden is called ***ʿabdan*** — **A SERVANT** — ⭐ **indefinite, unnamed.** ⭐ **Just: *a servant*.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And that's the horror of it: ⭐ **someone is preventing **a servant** from serving **his Master**.** ⭐ **The word *ʿabd* already establishes the relationship being interfered with.** ⭐ **It's not "a man" or "a believer" — ⭐ **it's *a servant*, and you're standing in his way.**
  
  ⭐ **And *ṣallā* completes a thread across three surahs:**
  - **Qiyāmah 75:31** — the denier *"neither affirmed **nor prayed**"* (⭐ **didn't pray**)
  - **Aʿlā 87:15** — the successful one *"remembered his Lord's name **and prayed**"* (⭐ **did pray**)
  - **ʿAlaq 96:10** — a servant *"**when he prays**"* (⭐ **is stopped from praying**)
  
  ⭐ **Three surahs: not praying, praying, and being **prevented** from praying.**
  
  ⭐ ⚠️ **And remember the Aʿlā trap: *ṣallā* (prayed, v.15) vs *yaṣlā* (roasted, v.12) — three verses apart!**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***ʿabdan*** is **indefinite accusative** — the object of *yanhā*. ⭐ **The indefiniteness generalizes: *any* servant.**
- **Why this word:** *ʿabd* names him by ⭐ **his relationship to Allah**, which makes interference an offence against **Allah**, not just against him.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***ʿAbd* = a SERVANT; *ʿibādah* = worship (SERVANTHOOD).** ⭐⭐ **Someone is stopping **a servant** from serving **his Master**.** ⭐ **The word already names the crime.** ⭐ **Three surahs of *ṣallā*: 75:31 (didn't), 87:15 (did), 96:10 (prevented).**

**11. كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ — *kadhdhaba wa tawallā*** · v.13
- **Root feelings:** *kadhdhaba* — **to call something a lie**. *tawallā* — ⭐ **Form V of the "turning" root** = **to turn your back deliberately.**
- **Here it means:** "**Have you seen if he denied and turned away?**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the THIRD surah to use this exact two-verb phrase — a Qurʾānic signature:**
  - **Qiyāmah 75:32** — *wa lākin **kadhdhaba wa tawallā***
  - **Layl 92:16** — *alladhī **kadhdhaba wa tawallā***
  - **ʿAlaq 96:13** — *in **kadhdhaba wa tawallā***
  
  ⭐ **Identical pairing, three surahs.** ⭐ **Denial with the tongue + turning with the body — ⭐ **both channels of rejection.**
  
  ⭐ **And *tawallā* is now across SIX surahs**, with a range that should stay with you: ⭐ **Qiyāmah 75:32 (the arrogant denier), ⭐ **ʿAbasa 80:1 (the Prophet's ﷺ momentary frown — CORRECTED!)**, Ghāshiyah 88:23, Layl 92:16, ʿAlaq 96:13.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **The same verb for a shoulder-turn and for total rejection.** ⭐ **That's how seriously *direction* is taken.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form V**, weak-final. ⭐ **Note the conditional *in* ("if") in v.11 and v.13 — ⭐ **the surah lays out two possibilities and lets you weigh them.**
- **Why this word:** two verbs because ⭐ **saying no and walking away are separate acts.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Kadhdhaba wa tawallā*** — ⭐ **THREE surahs, identical phrase: 75:32, 92:16, 96:13.** ⭐ **And *tawallā* is in SIX surahs — including ʿAbasa 80:1, where it described a FROWN.**

**12. أَلَمْ يَعْلَم بِأَنَّ اللَّهَ يَرَىٰ — *a-lam yaʿlam bi-anna'llāha yarā*** · v.14
- **Root feelings:** *yaʿlam* — ⭐ **to know** — ⭐ **the same root as *ʿallama* in v.4–5!** *yarā* — **to see**.
- **Here it means:** "**Does he not know that Allah sees?**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **the verse that closes the loop back to the first revelation — and it's brilliant.**
  
  ⭐ **v.5 said: Allah ***taught man what he did not know*** (*mā lam **yaʿlam***).**
  ⭐ **v.14 asks: **does he not KNOW** (*a-lam **yaʿlam***) that Allah sees?**
  
  ⭐⭐ **SAME ROOT, SAME NEGATION-STRUCTURE, nine verses apart.** ⭐ **He was *taught what he didn't know* — ⭐ **and he still doesn't know the most basic thing: that he's being watched.**
  
  ⭐ **And this answers Balad 90:7** — *a-yaḥsabu **an lam yarahu aḥad*** ("does he think **no one saw him**?"). ⭐ **Balad asked the question; ʿAlaq answers it: **Allah sees.**
  
  ⭐ **You own this thread across FOUR surahs:**
  - **Ṭāriq 86:4** — *in kullu nafsin lammā ʿalayhā **ḥāfiẓ*** (over every soul, a **guardian**)
  - **Fajr 89:14** — *inna rabbaka la-**bi'l-mirṣād*** (your Lord is **at the lookout**)
  - **Balad 90:7** — *a-yaḥsabu an **lam yarahu aḥad*** (does he think **no one saw**?)
  - **ʿAlaq 96:14** — *a-lam yaʿlam bi-anna'llāha **yarā*** (**Allah SEES**)
  
  ⭐ **Four surahs, one answer.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **أَلَمْ + jussive** = rhetorical question. ⚠️ ⭐ **Note *yaʿlam* drops its final vowel.** ⭐ **The *bi-* on *bi-anna* is standard after *ʿalima*.**
- **Why this word:** ⭐ **the plainest possible verb (*yarā*, "sees")** — ⭐ **no theology needed, just a fact.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **v.5: taught him ***what he did not know*** (*mā lam yaʿlam*). ⭐ **v.14: ***does he not know*** (*a-lam yaʿlam*) that Allah SEES?** ⭐ **Same root, nine verses apart.** ⭐⭐ **And Balad 90:7 asked "does he think no one saw?" — this is the answer.**

## §4 — The Threat, and the Command (v.15–19)

**13. لَنَسْفَعًا بِالنَّاصِيَةِ — *la-nasfaʿan bi'n-nāṣiyah*** · v.15
- **Root feelings:** *nasfaʿu* — ⭐ the "**seizing violently and dragging**" family — *safaʿa* = **to grab roughly and pull**, ⭐ **also carries a sense of *scorching/blackening*** (the mark left by fire). *nāṣiyah* — ⭐ **THE FORELOCK** — the hair at the very front of the head.
- **Here it means:** "**We will surely drag him by the forelock.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the forelock is chosen with total precision, and it's cultural.**
  
  ⭐ **In Arab culture, the ***nāṣiyah*** (forelock) was the seat of **honour and pride**.** ⭐ **A noble man's forelock was his dignity; ⭐ **to seize someone's forelock was the ultimate gesture of **total domination** — you had taken his honour in your hand.**
  
  ⭐ **So the punishment is precisely fitted to the crime:** ⭐ **the man who ***istaghnā*** — thought himself **above needing anyone** (v.7) — ⭐ **is grabbed by **the very symbol of his self-importance**.
  
  ⭐ **And v.16 doubles it: ⭐ ***nāṣiyatin **kādhibatin khāṭiʾah**** — "a **LYING, SINNING** forelock."** ⭐⭐ **The forelock itself is called lying and sinning** — ⭐ **a *majāz* (metaphorical transfer): the part is described with the character of the whole person.** ⭐ **His head *is* the lie.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⚠️ ⭐ ***la-nasfaʿan*** ends with a ***nūn at-tawkīd al-khafīfah*** — **the light nūn of emphasis**, ⭐ **written in the muṣḥaf as an alif (ـًا) but pronounced as a nūn-sound.** ⭐ **A rare and important orthographic feature — don't misread it as a normal tanwīn.**
- **Why this word:** *nāṣiyah* over "head" — ⭐ **because it's the **honour-symbol**, which is exactly what's being taken.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **The *NĀṢIYAH* (forelock) = ⭐ **THE SEAT OF ARAB HONOUR AND PRIDE**.** ⭐⭐ **The man who thought he needed nothing (v.7) is dragged by ⭐ **the symbol of his pride**.** ⭐ ⚠️ **And *la-nasfaʿan* has the ⭐ **light nūn of emphasis**, written as an alif.**

**14. فَلْيَدْعُ نَادِيَهُ — *fal-yadʿu nādiyah*** · v.17
- **Root feelings:** *yadʿu* — the "**calling**" family (*duʿāʾ* = a call/supplication). *nādī* — ⭐ **an ASSEMBLY / a gathering-place of one's people** — ⭐ **from *nādā* = to call out** (⭐ **you own *nādāhu* from Nāziʿāt 79:16!**); ⭐ **modern Arabic *nādī* = A CLUB** (a sports club, a social club!).
- **Here it means:** "**Then let him call his assembly / his people.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the challenge is dripping with irony, and the modern word makes it stick.**
  
  ⭐ ***Nādī* in modern Arabic is **a CLUB*** — ⭐ **Nādī ar-Riyāḍī, a sports club.** ⭐ **Same word.** ⭐ **It's your **circle, your backers, the people who'd stand behind you**.
  
  ⭐ **So the verse says: ⭐ **"Go ahead — call your crew."** ⭐ **It's a taunt.** ⭐ **He derived his sense of ***istighnāʾ*** (self-sufficiency, v.7) partly from his **social backing** — ⭐ **so he's invited to summon exactly that.**
  
  ⭐ **And v.18 answers instantly: ⭐ ***sa-nadʿu'z-zabāniyah*** — "**WE will call the Zabāniyah**."** ⭐⭐ **The same verb (*daʿā*) on both sides.** ⭐ **You call yours; We'll call Ours.**
  
  ⭐ **The rhetorical shape is *taʿjīz* — challenging someone to do something to expose that they can't.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **لام الأمر** (the *lām* of command) on a third-person verb: *fal-yadʿu* = "**let him call**." ⭐ **You own this from ʿAbasa 80:24** (*fal-yanẓur*). ⚠️ ⭐ **Note *yadʿu* drops its final wāw (jussive).**
- **Why this word:** *nādī* (**his social circle**) rather than "his helpers" — ⭐ **it names exactly what he was relying on.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***NĀDĪ* = A CLUB** in modern Arabic (a sports club!). ⭐ **"Go on — call your CLUB."** ⭐⭐ **And v.18: "WE will call the Zabāniyah."** ⭐ **Same verb, both sides.**

**15. الزَّبَانِيَةَ — *az-zabāniyah*** · v.18
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ **the guards/angels of Hell** — ⭐ **the root carries the sense of *pushing, thrusting, shoving forcefully*** (*zabana* = to push away violently — ⭐ **used of a camel kicking out**).
- **Here it means:** "**We will call the Zabāniyah.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the contrast with v.17 is the entire point, and it's brutal.**
  
  ⭐ **v.17 — *fal-yadʿu **nādiyah*** — "let him call **his club**" (⭐ **human, social, negotiable**)
  ⭐ **v.18 — *sa-**nadʿu'z-zabāniyah*** — "**We** will call **the Zabāniyah**" (⭐ **not human, not social, not negotiable**)
  
  ⭐⭐ **And note the tense shift: ⭐ **his call is a *command* (let him try); Ours is a *promise* (*sa-nadʿu*, "We WILL").** ⭐ **One is hypothetical, one is scheduled.**
  
  ⭐ **The root's meaning — **violent pushing/thrusting** — ⭐ **tells you what they do.** ⭐ **They are the ones who *shove*.**
  
  ⭐ **And the sound is deliberate: ⭐ ***az-zabāniyah*** is a heavy, unfamiliar, slightly frightening word** — ⭐ **it lands harder precisely because you don't hear it often.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **سَ + present = near future** ("We **will** call"). ⭐ **Accusative as the object of *nadʿu*.**
- **Why this word:** an ⭐ **unusual, heavy-sounding name** for the opposing party makes the mismatch feel total.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Zabana* = TO SHOVE VIOLENTLY** (like a camel kicking out). ⭐⭐ **v.17: "call your CLUB" (a dare). v.18: "WE will call the Zabāniyah" (a promise).** ⭐ **Human vs. not-human. Negotiable vs. not.**

**16. وَاسْجُدْ وَاقْتَرِب — *wa'sjud wa'qtarib*** · v.19 ⭐ *(a verse of prostration)*
- **Root feelings:** *usjud* — ⭐ **PROSTRATE** (*sujūd* = prostration; ***masjid* = A MOSQUE*** — ⭐ **literally "the place of prostration"!**). *iqtarib* — ⭐ the "**drawing near**" family — *qarīb* = near; *qurb* = nearness; ⭐ ***al-muqarrabūn* = those brought NEAR*** (⭐ **Muṭaffifīn 83:21, 28!**); ⭐ **and *maqrabah* — Balad 90:15** (an orphan **of near kin**).
- **Here it means:** "**No! Do not obey him. But prostrate and draw near.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **the final verse of the surah — and it is a *sajdah* verse, meaning ⭐ **you actually prostrate when you recite it.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **Think about what that means.** ⭐ **The surah describes a man trying to **stop** someone from praying (v.9–10).** ⭐ **And it ends by commanding ***you*** to **prostrate** — ⭐ **and you physically do it.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **The reader's own body becomes the refutation.** ⭐ **He tried to stop one prostration; the verse generates one every time it's recited, forever.**
  
  ⭐ **And look at the pairing: ⭐ ***usjud*** (go **DOWN**) + ***iqtarib*** (come **NEAR**).** ⭐⭐ **The lowest physical position produces the closest spiritual position.** ⭐ **Down IS near.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare the surah's arc:** ⭐ **v.1 = **IQRAʾ** (read — an act of the mind).** ⭐ **v.19 = **USJUD** (prostrate — an act of the body).** ⭐⭐ **Knowledge to submission. The first revelation begins with reading and ends with your forehead on the ground.**
  
  ⭐ **And *masjid* = "the place of prostration"** is your free hook — ⭐ **you use the word daily.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **two imperatives** (*usjud* Form I, *iqtarib* Form VIII). ⭐ **The *kallā* + *lā tuṭiʿhu* ("do not obey him") is a prohibition with the jussive.**
- **Why this word:** ending on ⭐ **two physical commands** — after all the argument — ⭐ **turns the surah into an action rather than a conclusion.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***MASJID* = "the place of PROSTRATION."** ⭐⭐⭐ **This is a SAJDAH verse — ⭐ **you prostrate when you read it.** ⭐ **He tried to stop one prostration; this verse creates one every time it's recited.** ⭐⭐ **v.1 = READ (mind). v.19 = PROSTRATE (body).** ⭐ **And DOWN (*usjud*) is how you get NEAR (*iqtarib*).**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **لَنَسْفَعًا (v.15)** — ⚠️ **that final alif is the ⭐ *nūn at-tawkīd al-khafīfah* (the LIGHT NŪN OF EMPHASIS)**, ⭐ **written as an alif but pronounced as a nūn-sound.** **Not a normal tanwīn.**
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **اسْتَغْنَىٰ (v.7)** — ⚠️ **Form X = "to CONSIDER yourself rich," a PERCEPTION, not a fact.** ⭐ **And ʿAlaq names it as the CAUSE of all transgression** (ʿAbasa 80:5 and Layl 92:8 gave you the cases).
3. ⭐⭐ **يَعْلَمْ (v.5) and يَعْلَم (v.14)** — ⚠️ **same root, nine verses apart.** ⭐ **"Taught him what he did NOT know" → "does he NOT know that Allah sees?"**
4. ⭐⭐ **النَّاصِيَةِ (v.15–16)** — ⚠️ **the forelock was the ⭐ **SEAT OF ARAB HONOUR**, not just hair.** ⭐ **And in v.16 the *forelock itself* is called "lying, sinning" — a metaphorical transfer.
5. ⭐⭐ **يَنْهَىٰ (v.9)** — ⚠️ **the SAME verb praised in Nāziʿāt 79:40** (*nahā'n-nafsa ʿani'l-hawā*). ⭐ **The verb is neutral; the OBJECT decides everything.**
6. ⭐ **أَرَأَيْتَ (v.9, 11, 13)** — ⚠️ **an IDIOM meaning "tell me / consider,"** not a literal question about seeing. ⭐ **Three times, none answered.**
7. ⭐ **نَادِيَهُ (v.17)** — ⚠️ **means his ASSEMBLY / social circle.** ⭐ **Modern Arabic *nādī* = A CLUB.**
8. ⭐ **صَلَّىٰ (v.10)** — ⚠️ **the *ṣalāh* root.** ⭐ **Remember the Aʿlā trap: *ṣallā* (prayed, 87:15) vs *yaṣlā* (roasted, 87:12), three verses apart.**
9. ⭐ **v.19 is a SAJDAH verse** — ⚠️ **you prostrate when you recite it.** ⭐ **The surah about a man stopping prayer *generates* a prostration every time it's read.**
10. ⭐ **The surah has TWO parts** — ⚠️ **v.1–5 (the first revelation) and v.6–19 (revealed later).** ⭐ **Read them as joined by theme, not by a single occasion.**
