# Surah Al-Qāriʿah (101) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–11)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**It asks "what is it?" THREE TIMES in the first three verses — and one of those verses is just two words.**

⭐ **v.1:** ***al-Qāriʿah*** — one word. A noun standing alone.
⭐ **v.2:** *mā **l-Qāriʿah*** — "**What** is the Striker?"
⭐ **v.3:** *wa mā adrāka mā **l-Qāriʿah*** — "And **what could convey to you** what the Striker is?"

⭐⭐ **Three verses, the same word three times, and no answer yet.** ⭐ **The surah **names** it, **questions** it, then **says the question can't be answered** — ⭐ **and only then describes it.**

⭐ **And then, at the very end, it does it AGAIN:** ⭐ **v.10 — *wa mā adrāka **mā hiyah**?* ("And what could convey to you what **IT** is?") ⭐ **before the final answer in v.11.**

⭐⭐ **Two unanswerable questions bracket the surah.** ⭐ **One at the start about **the Day**; one at the end about **the destination**.

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐⭐ **A surah that repeats its own name three times before explaining it — because the name is a SOUND, and sounds repeat.**

---

## The Big Picture

1. **The name, three times** (v.1–3) — ⭐ **the Striker. What is the Striker? What could tell you?**
2. **Two similes** (v.4–5) — ⭐ **people like scattered moths, mountains like carded wool.**
3. **The scales** (v.6–9) — ⭐ **heavy scales → a pleasing life; light scales → his mother is the Abyss.**
4. **The second question, and the answer** (v.10–11) — ⭐ **a scorching Fire.**

## §1 — The Name, Three Times (v.1–3)

**1. الْقَارِعَةُ — *al-Qāriʿah*** · v.1, 2, 3
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**striking with a loud bang / knocking violently**" family — *qaraʿa* = ⭐ **to strike something so it makes a noise**; ⭐ ***qarʿ al-bāb* = KNOCKING ON A DOOR*** (still everyday Arabic!); *miqraʿah* = a mallet; ⭐ **and the root carries the idea of a blow that **shocks and startles**.
- **Here it means:** "**The Striker / The Crashing Blow.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **another NAME for the Day — and each surah picks a different sense. You now own FIVE:**
  - **Nabaʾ 78:2** — *an-**Nabaʾ*** — ⭐ **THE NEWS** (what you **hear about**)
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:34** — *aṭ-**Ṭāmmah*** — ⭐ **THE OVERWHELMING FLOOD** (what **submerges** you)
  - **ʿAbasa 80:33** — *aṣ-**Ṣākhkhah*** — ⭐ **THE DEAFENER** (what **destroys hearing**)
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:1** — *al-**Ghāshiyah*** — ⭐ **THE ENVELOPER** (what **covers** you)
  - ⭐ **Qāriʿah 101:1** — *al-**Qāriʿah*** — ⭐ **THE STRIKER** (what **hits and startles**)
  
  ⭐⭐ **Five surahs, five names, five sensations.** ⭐ **And *al-Qāriʿah* is the most **sudden** of them: ⭐ **a *qarʿ* is a single shocking blow, not a process.**
  
  ⭐ **And the everyday hook is perfect: ⭐ ***qarʿ al-bāb* = a KNOCK ON THE DOOR***.** ⭐ **You hear one every week.** ⭐ **That's the root — ⭐ **something striking a surface and making a noise you can't ignore.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And now the repetition makes sense:** ⭐ **a *qarʿ* is a SOUND.** ⭐ **Sounds **repeat, echo, knock again**.** ⭐ **Saying *al-Qāriʿah* three times in three verses is the word **knocking**.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **active participle used as a NAME**, feminine (the noun *as-sāʿah* is **deleted**). ⚠️ ⭐ **v.1 is a single-word verse — a *mubtadaʾ* whose predicate arrives later** (a known construction for creating suspense).
- **Why this word:** *qāriʿah* over a word for destruction — ⭐ **because it names the **impact on you**, not the event itself.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Qarʿ al-bāb* = A KNOCK ON THE DOOR.** ⭐ **Same root.** ⭐⭐ **FIVE names for the Day: the News (78:2), the Flood (79:34), the Deafener (80:33), the Enveloper (88:1), the STRIKER (101:1).** ⭐ **And it's repeated 3× because a *qarʿ* is a SOUND — and sounds knock again.**

**2. وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ — *wa mā adrāka*** · v.3, 10
- **Root feeling:** the "**knowing by perception**" family — *darā* = to become aware; ⭐ **Form IV *adrā* = to make someone know.**
- **Here it means:** "**And what could convey to you what the Striker is?**" (v.3) and "**...what it is?**" (v.10)
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **you own this formula across SEVEN surahs now — and Qāriʿah is the only one that uses it TWICE as a frame.**
  - **Infiṭār 82:17–18** (twice, consecutive) · **Muṭaffifīn 83:8, 19** · **Ṭāriq 86:2** · **Balad 90:12** · **Qadr 97:2** · ⭐ **Qāriʿah 101:3, 10**
  
  ⭐ ⚠️ **And the *adrāka* / *yudrīka* distinction stays crucial:**
  - ⭐ ***mā **YUDRĪKA*** (present) — **ʿAbasa 80:3** — ⭐ **"you couldn't know" — NO answer follows.**
  - ⭐ ***mā **ADRĀKA*** (past) — ⭐ **"what could convey it to you?" — an answer DOES follow.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **What makes Qāriʿah unique: ⭐ **it uses *adrāka* at the START (v.3, about the Day) and at the END (v.10, about the destination).** ⭐ **Two brackets.**
  
  ⭐ **And notice what's between them: ⭐ **the entire content of the surah sits inside two admissions that no description is adequate.**
  
  ⭐ ⚠️ **v.10 has an unusual feature: ***mā hiyah*** — ⭐ **with a *hāʾ as-sakt* (a "pause hāʾ") added to *hiya*.** ⭐ **It exists purely to let the voice **land** at the end of the verse.** ⭐ **A rare, beautiful recitation feature.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⚠️ ⭐ **note the TWO *mā*'s** (first interrogative, second introducing the subject-clause). ⭐ **And ***hāʾ as-sakt*** in v.10 — ⭐ **a silent/pausal *hāʾ*, not a pronoun.**
- **Why this word:** the doubled framing ⭐ **makes the whole surah an admission of inadequacy** — which is more effective than any description.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **SEVEN surahs use this formula.** ⭐ ⚠️ ***adrāka* (past) = an answer follows. *yudrīka* (present, ʿAbasa 80:3) = no answer follows.** ⭐⭐ **Qāriʿah uses it TWICE — at the start (the Day) and at the end (the destination).** ⭐ ⚠️ **And *mā hiyah* has a ***hāʾ as-sakt*** — a pause-*hāʾ*, not a pronoun.

## §2 — Two Similes (v.4–5)

**3. كَالْفَرَاشِ الْمَبْثُوثِ — *ka'l-farāshi'l-mabthūth*** · v.4
- **Root feelings:** *farāsh* — ⭐ **MOTHS / small fluttering insects** that swarm toward a flame — ⭐ **from *farasha* = to spread out** (⭐ ***firāsh* = A BED / bedding***, something spread!). *mabthūth* — ⭐ the "**scattering / spreading widely**" family — *batha* = to strew, disperse.
- **Here it means:** "**the Day people will be like scattered moths.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **the simile is chosen for behaviour, not just appearance — and that's what makes it devastating.**
  
  ⭐ **Why *farāsh* (moths) specifically?** ⭐ **Because of what moths **DO**: ⭐ **they swarm **toward the fire**, in chaotic, aimless, colliding motion — ⭐ **each one moving, none of them going anywhere on purpose.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **So the simile says: ⭐ **on that Day people will move like moths around a flame — ⭐ **agitated, numerous, directionless, and heading toward the very thing that destroys them.**
  
  ⭐ **And ***mabthūth*** — you own it EXACTLY from Ghāshiyah 88:16** — *wa **zarābiyyu mabthūthah*** ("and **carpets spread all around**").
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **SAME WORD, two surahs, opposite scenes:** ⭐ **carpets **generously strewn** in Paradise (88:16), ⭐ **and people **chaotically scattered** on Judgment Day (101:4).** ⭐ **The same word for luxurious abundance and for terrified dispersal.**
  
  ⭐ **And the free hook: ⭐ ***firāsh* = A BED*** — ⭐ **something **spread out**. Same root as the moth.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **the *ka-* is *kāf at-tashbīh* (the "like" of simile).** ⭐ ***Mabthūth*** is a **passive participle** — ⭐ **they are **scattered**, not scattering themselves.**
- **Why this word:** *farāsh* names a creature defined by ⭐ **moving urgently toward its own destruction** — ⭐ **the exact human pattern the juzʾ has described.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Farāsh* = MOTHS — which swarm TOWARD THE FLAME.** ⭐ **Agitated, numerous, directionless, heading for what destroys them.** ⭐⭐⭐ **And *mabthūth* is IDENTICAL to Ghāshiyah 88:16** — ⭐ **carpets **generously strewn** in Paradise / people **chaotically scattered** here.** ⭐ **And *firāsh* = A BED (spread out).**

**4. كَالْعِهْنِ الْمَنفُوشِ — *ka'l-ʿihni'l-manfūsh*** · v.5
- **Root feelings:** *ʿihn* — ⭐ **WOOL — specifically DYED, coloured wool.** *manfūsh* — ⭐ the "**teasing apart / carding / fluffing**" family — *nafasha* = **to card wool**, to pull a compact mass apart into loose, floating fibres.
- **Here it means:** "**and the mountains will be like carded wool.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **the most precise destruction-image in the juzʾ — and every detail is doing work.**
  
  ⭐ **THREE things this simile achieves at once:**
  - ⭐ **WEIGHT:** ⭐ **a mountain is the **heaviest** thing you know; carded wool is the **lightest**.** ⭐ **Maximum reversal.**
  - ⭐ **COHESION:** ⭐ **carding means **pulling a solid mass apart into separate fibres**.** ⭐ **The mountain doesn't just get light — ⭐ **it comes apart at the fibre level.**
  - ⭐ **COLOUR:** ⭐ ***ʿihn* means DYED wool*** — ⭐ **which some classical commentators connect to the **multi-coloured** appearance of mountains (rock strata are genuinely banded).** ⭐ **So the image may be visually exact too.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And compare the mountain-fates you now own:**
  - **Nabaʾ 78:20** — *fa-kānat **sarābā*** (⭐ **a MIRAGE** — visible but not there)
  - **Takwīr 81:3** — ***suyyirat*** (⭐ **set MOVING**)
  - **Fajr 89:21** — *dukkat… **dakkan dakkā*** (⭐ **POUNDED flat**)
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:19** — ***nuṣibat*** (⭐ **SET UP firmly** — before the Day)
  - ⭐ **Qāriʿah 101:5** — ***manfūsh*** (⭐ **CARDED into floating fluff**)
  
  ⭐⭐ **Five surahs, five ways mountains end.** ⭐ **This is the gentlest-sounding and the most total.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **second *kāf at-tashbīh*.** ⭐ ***Manfūsh*** is a **passive participle** — ⭐ **the mountains are **carded**; someone does it to them.**
- **Why this word:** *manfūsh* (**carded**) rather than "crushed" — ⭐ **because carding is a **craft** word.** ⭐ **It sounds almost gentle — which makes the totality more unsettling.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Manfūsh* = CARDED WOOL** — ⭐ **a solid mass pulled apart into loose floating fibres.** ⭐⭐ **Heaviest thing → lightest thing, AND it comes apart at the fibre level.** ⭐ ***ʿIhn* = DYED wool** (mountains really are banded!). ⭐⭐ **FIVE mountain-fates: mirage (78:20), moving (81:3), pounded (89:21), carded (101:5).**

## §3 — The Scales (v.6–9)

**5. ثَقُلَتْ / خَفَّتْ مَوَازِينُهُ — *thaqulat / khaffat mawāzīnuh*** · v.6, 8
- **Root feelings:** *thaqulat* — ⭐ the "**heaviness**" family — ⭐ **you own it from Insān 76:27** (*yawman **thaqīlā***) and **Zalzalah 99:2** (***athqālahā***) and **99:7** (***mithqāla*** dharrah). *khaffat* — ⭐ the "**lightness**" family — *khaffa* = to be light; ⭐ ***takhfīf* = making something lighter/easier***. *mawāzīn* — ⭐ **plural of *mīzān* = A SCALE/BALANCE** — ⭐ **from *wazana* = to weigh** (⭐ ***wazn* = weight; *mīzān* = the instrument***).
- **Here it means:** "**As for one whose scales are heavy… and as for one whose scales are light…**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **a perfect *muqābala*, and the connection to the previous surah is exact.**
  
  ⭐ **The two verses are structurally identical:**
  
  | v.6–7 | v.8–9 |
  |---|---|
  | *fa-ammā man ***THAQULAT** mawāzīnuh* | *wa ammā man ***KHAFFAT** mawāzīnuh* |
  | → *fī **ʿīshatin rāḍiyah*** | → *fa-**ummuhu hāwiyah*** |
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **AND HERE'S THE PAYOFF: ⭐ **Zalzalah 99:7 said an atom's weight (*mithqāla dharrah*) of good will be SEEN.** ⭐ **Qāriʿah 101:6 says what happens when those atoms are **WEIGHED**.** ⭐ **Same root (*th-q-l*), consecutive surahs — ⭐ **Zalzalah measures, Qāriʿah weighs.**
  
  ⭐ **And *mawāzīn* is **PLURAL** — ⚠️ ⭐ **"scaleS," not "scale."** ⭐ **Classical readings: many scales, or many *weighed things*.** ⭐ **Either way, the accounting is **itemized**, not lumped.**
  
  ⭐ **The free hook: ⭐ ***mīzān* is the everyday Arabic word for a weighing scale*** — ⭐ **the kitchen scale, the bathroom scale.** ⭐ **And *mīzān* also means **balance/justice** generally.**
  
  ⭐ **Note ⚠️ the *fa-ammā… wa ammā…* structure** — ⭐ **the same two-panel frame you own from Nāziʿāt 79:37–41, Inshiqāq 84:7–12, Layl 92:5–10, Ḍuḥā 93:9–11.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***fa-ammā X fa-Y*** = "as for X, then Y" — ⭐ **a standard two-panel construction.** ⭐ ***Mawāzīnuhu*** is the subject of the verb, which is **feminine singular** (*thaqulat*) — ⚠️ ⭐ **broken plurals take feminine singular agreement.**
- **Why this word:** *mīzān* (**a physical balance**) rather than an abstract word for judgment — ⭐ **it makes accountability **mechanical and visible**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Mīzān* = a WEIGHING SCALE** (the everyday word). ⭐⭐⭐ **Zalzalah 99:7: an atom's weight is SEEN. ⭐ **Qāriʿah 101:6: the atoms are WEIGHED.** ⭐ **Same root, consecutive surahs.** ⭐ ⚠️ **And *mawāzīn* is PLURAL — the accounting is ITEMIZED.**

**6. فِي عِيشَةٍ رَّاضِيَةٍ — *fī ʿīshatin rāḍiyah*** · v.7
- **Root feelings:** *ʿīshah* — ⭐ **a MODE OF LIVING / a way of life** — ⭐ **from *ʿāsha* = to live** (⭐ **you own it from Nabaʾ 78:11**, *wa jaʿalna'n-nahāra ***maʿāshā***, "the day as a **time of living**"; ⭐ **and the name *ʿĀʾishah*!**). *rāḍiyah* — ⭐ the "**contentment**" family (*riḍā*).
- **Here it means:** "**he will be in a pleasing life.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the adjective is doing something grammatically strange and beautiful.**
  
  ⭐ ⚠️ ***Rāḍiyah* is an ACTIVE participle — "a life that is PLEASED."*** ⭐ **Literally: **the life itself is content**.
  
  ⭐⭐ **You'd expect *marḍiyyah* (a life that is **pleasing TO him** — passive).** ⭐ **Instead the Qurʾān says the **life** is pleased.** ⭐ **Classical grammarians call this a ***majāz ʿaqlī*** — attributing to the thing what belongs to its owner.** ⭐ **The effect: ⭐ **the contentment is so total that even the life is happy.**
  
  ⭐ **And *riḍā* is now across SEVEN surahs** — ⭐ **your most consistent reward-word:**
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:9** (*li-saʿyihā **rāḍiyah***) · **Fajr 89:28** (***rāḍiyatan marḍiyyah***) · **Layl 92:21** (*la-sawfa **yarḍā***) · **Ḍuḥā 93:5** (*fa-**tarḍā***) · **Bayyinah 98:8** (*raḍiya… wa **raḍū***) · ⭐ **Qāriʿah 101:7**
  
  ⭐ **And *ʿīshah* (from *ʿāsha*, to live) ties back to Nabaʾ 78:11's *maʿāsh*** — ⭐ **"the day as a time of LIVING."** ⭐ **Two surahs, one root: dunya's working-life, and the life that finally pleases.**
  
  ⭐ **The free hook: ⭐ **the name *ʿĀʾishah* means "one who lives."**
- **Grammar bite:** ⚠️ ⭐ ***rāḍiyah* is ACTIVE, not passive*** — ⭐ **a *majāz ʿaqlī* (attributing to the life what belongs to the liver).**
- **Why this word:** *ʿīshah* (**a mode of living**) rather than *jannah* — ⭐ **because the reward described is **how you live**, not just where.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ⚠️ ***Rāḍiyah* is ACTIVE — "a life that is PLEASED."** ⭐ **Not "pleasing to him" — the LIFE ITSELF is content.** ⭐⭐ **And *ʿĀʾishah* = "one who lives"; *maʿāsh* (Nabaʾ 78:11) = "the time of living."** ⭐ **And *riḍā* is now in SEVEN surahs.**

**7. فَأُمُّهُ هَاوِيَةٌ — *fa-ummuhu hāwiyah*** · v.9
- **Root feelings:** *umm* — ⭐ **A MOTHER** — ⭐ **and also "the source/origin" of something** (⭐ ***umm al-kitāb* = the mother of the Book; *umm al-qurā* = the mother of cities***). *hāwiyah* — ⭐ the "**falling / plunging down**" family — *hawā* = **to fall headlong**; ⭐ ***hāwiyah* = AN ABYSS, a bottomless pit***; ⭐⭐ **and the SAME ROOT as *al-hawā* = DESIRE** (⭐ **Nāziʿāt 79:40!**) and ⭐ ***hawāʾ* = AIR** (⭐ **the empty space you fall through!**).
- **Here it means:** "**his mother is the Abyss.**" ⭐ *(or: his refuge/shelter is the Abyss)*
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **THE MOST HAUNTING PHRASE IN THE SURAH — and the root-payoff is the best in this batch.**
  
  ⭐ **Why "his MOTHER"?** ⭐ **Two classical readings, both extraordinary:**
  - ⭐ **(a) *Umm* = **where you take refuge**.** ⭐ **A child runs to his mother when terrified.** ⭐ **So: **the only shelter he has left is a bottomless pit.**
  - ⭐ **(b) *Umm* = **the source/origin you return to**.** ⭐ **Like *umm al-kitāb*, the **root** of a thing.** ⭐ **So: **the Abyss is where he came from and goes back to.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **Either way it is unbearable: ⭐ **the word for the safest place a human knows is attached to the word for a bottomless fall.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE ROOT-PAYOFF: ⭐ ***Hāwiyah* and *al-hawā* (DESIRE) are THE SAME ROOT.***
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:40** — *wa **nahā**'n-nafsa ʿani'l-***HAWĀ*** (⭐ **"restrained the soul from DESIRE"**)
  - ⭐ **Qāriʿah 101:9** — *fa-ummuhu ***HĀWIYAH*** (⭐ **"his mother is the ABYSS"**)
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **DESIRE is named after FALLING. And where does following it land you? ⭐ **In the thing named after falling.** ⭐ **Two surahs, one root, and the second is the destination of the first.**
  
  ⭐ **And *hawāʾ* = AIR** — ⭐ **empty space, nothing to catch you.** ⭐ **Free hook.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **a nominal sentence: *ummuhu* (subject) + *hāwiyah* (predicate).** ⭐ ***Hāwiyah*** is an active participle used as a **name** for the Fire.
- **Why this word:** ***umm*** — ⭐ **the single warmest word in the language — used for the coldest destination.** ⭐ **Maximum dissonance.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Al-HAWĀ* (DESIRE, Nāziʿāt 79:40) and *HĀWIYAH* (THE ABYSS) are THE SAME ROOT — "to FALL."** ⭐⭐⭐ **Desire is named after falling; the Abyss is where the falling ends.** ⭐⭐ **And ***umm*** = MOTHER = **where you run for refuge**.** ⭐ **His refuge is a bottomless pit.** ⭐ **(And *hawāʾ* = AIR — nothing to catch you.)**

## §4 — The Answer (v.10–11)

**8. نَارٌ حَامِيَةٌ — *nārun ḥāmiyah*** · v.11
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**intense heat**" family — *ḥamiya* = **to become scorching hot**; ⭐ ***ḥammām* = A BATHHOUSE*** (⭐ **the hot room!**); ⭐ ***ḥummā* = A FEVER***; ⭐ **and *ḥamīm*** = ⭐ **BOTH scalding water AND a close intimate friend** (⭐ **Nabaʾ 78:25!**).
- **Here it means:** "**A scorching Fire.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **you own this exact phrase from Ghāshiyah 88:4 — and the surah's ending is a masterclass in restraint.**
  
  ⭐ **Ghāshiyah 88:4 — *ta**ṣlā nāran ḥāmiyah*** ("roasting in a **scorching Fire**").
  ⭐ **Qāriʿah 101:11 — ***nārun ḥāmiyah*** ("**a scorching Fire**").
  
  ⭐⭐ **Identical phrase, two surahs.** ⭐ **Free vocabulary.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **But look at HOW the surah ends.** ⭐ **After *al-Qāriʿah* (the Striker), scattered moths, carded mountains, the scales, and ⭐ ***ummuhu hāwiyah*** (his mother is the Abyss) — ⭐ **the final answer is just **two plain words**.**
  
  ⭐ **No elaboration. No adjectives piled up. ⭐ **Just: "a hot fire."**
  
  ⭐⭐ **That plainness is deliberate.** ⭐ **v.10 asked "**what could convey to you what it is?**" — ⭐ **and the answer is deliberately **under**-whelming in vocabulary, because the point is that nothing *could* convey it.** ⭐ **The simplest possible words, after the biggest possible question.**
  
  ⭐ **And the root's double life stays haunting: ⭐ ***ḥamīm*** is both **boiling water** and **a dear friend**.** ⭐ **Warmth at both extremes.**
  
  ⭐ **You own this root from **Nabaʾ 78:25** (*ḥamīman*) and **Ghāshiyah 88:4** (*ḥāmiyah*).**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **a nominal sentence with a **deleted subject**: "[It is] a scorching Fire."** ⭐ ***Ḥāmiyah*** is an **active participle** — ⭐ **the fire is actively scorching, not passively hot.**
- **Why this word:** ⭐ **two plain words** after two unanswerable questions — ⭐ **the restraint is the rhetoric.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Ḥammām* = A BATHHOUSE (the HOT room); *ḥummā* = A FEVER.** ⭐ **Same root.** ⭐⭐⭐ **IDENTICAL phrase to Ghāshiyah 88:4.** ⭐⭐ **And the surah ends with TWO PLAIN WORDS after asking "what could convey it to you?"** ⭐ **The plainness IS the point.** ⭐ **(And *ḥamīm* = boiling water AND a dear friend — Nabaʾ 78:25.)**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **هَاوِيَةٌ (v.9)** — ⚠️ **SAME ROOT as *al-hawā* (DESIRE) in Nāziʿāt 79:40.** ⭐ **Both mean "to FALL."** ⭐ **Desire is named after falling; the Abyss is where the fall ends.**
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **رَّاضِيَةٍ (v.7)** — ⚠️ **it's an ACTIVE participle: "a life that is PLEASED,"** ⭐ **not "pleasing to him."** ⭐ **A *majāz ʿaqlī* — the life itself is content.**
3. ⭐⭐ **أُمُّهُ (v.9)** — ⚠️ **means "his MOTHER"**, read as **where you run for refuge** or **the source you return to**. ⭐ **Not a copying error — the dissonance is deliberate.**
4. ⭐⭐ **مَوَازِينُهُ (v.6, 8)** — ⚠️ **PLURAL ("scaleS").** ⭐ **The accounting is itemized, not lumped.** ⭐ **And note it takes **feminine singular** agreement (*thaqulat*, *khaffat*) — the broken-plural rule.**
5. ⭐⭐ **مَا هِيَهْ (v.10)** — ⚠️ **the final *hāʾ* is a ***hāʾ as-sakt*** (a PAUSE-*hāʾ*), not a pronoun.** ⭐ **It exists purely for the voice to land.**
6. ⭐⭐ **الْمَبْثُوثِ (v.4)** — ⚠️ **IDENTICAL to Ghāshiyah 88:16's *mabthūthah*** — ⭐ **luxurious carpets there, terrified people here.** **Same word, opposite scenes.**
7. ⭐ **فَرَاشِ (v.4)** — ⚠️ **MOTHS, chosen for BEHAVIOUR** (swarming toward flame), not appearance. ⭐ **And *firāsh* = A BED** (same root, "spread out").
8. ⭐ **الْمَنفُوشِ (v.5)** — ⚠️ **CARDED wool** — ⭐ **a solid mass pulled apart into fibres**, not merely "crushed."
9. ⭐ **الْقَارِعَةُ (v.1)** — ⚠️ **v.1 is a ONE-WORD verse** whose predicate arrives later. ⭐ **A suspense construction.**
10. ⭐ **v.11 is deliberately PLAIN** — ⚠️ **after asking "what could convey it to you?", the answer is two simple words.** ⭐ **The restraint is the rhetoric.**
