# Surah Al-Burūj (85) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–22)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**It tells the story of believers who lost — and calls it a victory.**

The *Aṣḥāb al-Ukhdūd* ("the People of the Trench") were believers thrown into a burning ditch for refusing to abandon their faith. ⭐ **They were not rescued.** There is no last-minute miracle in this surah. **They burned, and their persecutors sat on the edge watching.**

⭐ **And the Qur'an calls that the great success** (*al-fawz al-kabīr*, v.11).

⭐ **This surah exists to redefine winning.** For a Muslim community in Makkah being tortured and getting no rescue, it says: ⭐ **the people in the fire won; the people on the chairs lost.**

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐ **Every other surah so far has shown the wicked burning. This one shows the BELIEVERS burning — and still calls them the winners.**

---

## The Big Picture

1. **Four Oaths** (v.1–3) — the sky's constellations, the promised Day, ⭐ **and "a witness and a witnessed."**
2. **The Trench** (v.4–9) — the fire, the spectators, and ⭐ **the one line that explains all persecution ever.**
3. **The Two Verdicts** (v.10–11) — for the burners, **the Burning**; for the burned, **gardens**.
4. **Who Your Lord Is** (v.12–16) — ⭐ **His grip is severe** … **and He is the Loving.** Both, in four verses.
5. **You've Seen This Before** (v.17–20) — Pharaoh, Thamūd. ⭐ **Armies that lost.**
6. **The Book That Can't Burn** (v.21–22) — ⭐ **they burned believers; the message is on a PRESERVED tablet.**

## §1 — Four Oaths (v.1–3)

**1. الْبُرُوجِ — *al-burūj*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**tower / fortress / prominent structure**" family — ***burj*** = **a TOWER** (modern Arabic: *Burj Khalīfa*, *burj* = a tower/high-rise!); *tabarruj* = to display oneself prominently. Applied to the sky: ⭐ **the great constellations / the mansions the sun and moon pass through** — the sky's **towers**.
- **Here it means:** "By the sky **containing the great constellations**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **The choice of "TOWERS" for the opening oath is not decoration — it's the surah's first argument.**
  
  ⭐ **A *burj* is a FORTRESS TOWER — a place of strength, height, and protection.** ⭐ **And the surah is about to describe believers being burned in a DITCH — the lowest possible place.**
  
  ⭐ **Towers above; a trench below.** ⭐ **The first word sets the vertical frame, and the whole surah works inside it.**
  
  ⭐ **And the constellations are the sky's most ORDERED feature** — fixed, mapped, returning on schedule for millennia. ⭐ **Swearing by cosmic order, right before describing an event that looks like chaos.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare the sky-oaths you own:** **Ṭāriq 86:1** (*wa's-samāʾi wa'ṭ-ṭāriq*), **Takwīr 81:15–16** (*al-khunnas, al-jawāri'l-kunnas*). ⭐ **Three consecutive surahs swear by the night sky.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***dhāt*** = "**possessor of / the one having**" (feminine of *dhū*). ⭐ **You own *dhū/dhī* from Takwīr 81:20** (*dhī quwwatin*). ⭐ **Note it appears THREE times in this surah**: *dhāti'l-burūj* (v.1), *dhāti'l-waqūd* (v.5), *dhu'l-ʿArsh* (v.15).
- **Why this word:** *burūj* (towers) over "stars" — because ⭐ **the surah is about who really holds the high ground.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***burj* = a TOWER** (Burj Khalīfa!). ⭐ **The surah opens with TOWERS and is about a TRENCH.** ⭐ **High and low, from the first word.**

**2. الْيَوْمِ الْمَوْعُودِ — *al-yawmi'l-mawʿūd*** · v.2
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**promising**" family — *waʿada* = **to promise**; ***waʿd*** = a promise; ***mīʿād*** = an appointment/appointed time; *mawʿid* = a rendezvous.
- **Here it means:** "And by **the PROMISED Day**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **notice it's not called "the Day of Judgment" or "the Day of Resurrection" here — it's called the day that was PROMISED.**
  
  ⭐ **To a community being tortured with no rescue coming, the operative word is *promise*.** ⭐ **The Day is named by the fact that ⭐ someone gave their word.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Nabaʾ 78:17** — *inna yawma'l-faṣli kāna **mīqātā*** ("an **appointed time**"). ⭐ **Same root-family idea: this is SCHEDULED.** ⭐ **Nabaʾ says it has an appointment; Burūj says it was promised.**
- **Grammar bite:** **passive participle** (*mawʿūd*) — "**that which has been promised**." ⭐ **The promiser is unnamed because obvious.**
- **Why this word:** *mawʿūd* over any other title — because ⭐ **the people who need this surah need a PROMISE, not a description.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***waʿd* = a PROMISE; *mīʿād* = an appointment.*** ⭐ **The Day is named after the fact that someone GAVE THEIR WORD.**

**3. شَاهِدٍ وَمَشْهُودٍ — *shāhidin wa mashhūd*** · v.3
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**witnessing / being present**" family — *shahida* = to witness, to be present; ***shahādah*** = **testimony** (and **the SHAHĀDAH you declare!**); ***shahīd*** = ⭐ **a martyr** (one who bears witness with his life) *and* **a witness**; *mashhad* = a scene.
- **Here it means:** "And by **a WITNESS and that which is WITNESSED**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **This is the surah's thesis statement, hidden in an oath — and once you see it, the whole surah reorganizes around it.**
  
  ⭐ **The active/passive pair — *shāhid* (one who witnesses) and *mashhūd* (that which is witnessed) — is deliberately LEFT UNSPECIFIED.** ⭐ **Commentators offer many readings** (Friday and the Day of ʿArafah; the Prophet ﷺ and his ummah; the witness and the scene).
  
  ⭐ **But watch what the surah does with the root immediately afterward:**
  - ⭐ **v.7** — *wa hum ʿalā mā yafʿalūna bi'l-muʾminīna **SHUHŪD*** ("and they were **WITNESSES** to what they were doing to the believers")
  - ⭐ **v.9** — *wa'llāhu ʿalā kulli shayʾin **SHAHĪD*** ("and Allah is **WITNESS** over everything")
  
  ⭐⭐ **THE ROOT APPEARS FOUR TIMES IN NINE VERSES.** ⭐ **The persecutors sat and WATCHED (v.7) — they made themselves witnesses.** ⭐ **And v.9 answers: there was Someone ELSE watching.**
  
  ⭐ **The whole surah is about who was watching whom.** ⭐ **And *shahīd* also means MARTYR — so the believers in the trench are, by the root itself, witnesses too.** ⭐ **Three sets of eyes on that ditch: the persecutors, the martyrs, and Allah.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **active participle (*shāhid*, the doer) vs. passive participle (*mashhūd*, the receiver)** — ⭐ **the cleanest example of this pair in the Qur'an.** Learn the shapes side by side: *fāʿil* / *mafʿūl*.
- **Why this word:** leaving it **unspecified** lets the root do its work across the surah rather than pinning it to one reference.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***shahādah* = testimony (and your SHAHĀDAH); *shahīd* = a MARTYR *and* a witness.*** ⭐ **The root appears 4× in 9 verses.** ⭐ **They watched (v.7). Allah watched (v.9). The whole surah is about WHO WAS WATCHING.**

## §2 — The Trench (v.4–9)

**4. قُتِلَ أَصْحَابُ الْأُخْدُودِ — *qutila aṣḥābu'l-ukhdūd*** · v.4
- **Root feelings:** *qutila* — ⭐ **the imprecation-idiom you own from ʿAbasa 80:17** (*qutila'l-insānu mā akfarah* — "**Confound man!**") — not literal killing but **"may they perish / cursed be."** *ukhdūd* — ⭐ the "**furrow / groove / long cut in the ground**" family — *khadd* = ⭐ **the CHEEK** (the furrowed side of the face!); *ukhdūd* = **a long trench dug in the earth**.
- **Here it means:** "**Cursed were the people of the TRENCH.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **Two things worth catching.**
  
  ⭐ **First: *ukhdūd* shares its root with *khadd*, the CHEEK.*** ⭐ **A trench is a furrow in the earth's face** — the same shape as the lines on a face. ⭐ **The word makes the ditch feel like a **scar on the ground.**
  
  ⭐ **Second, and more important: notice WHO is cursed.** ⭐ **"*Aṣḥāb al-ukhdūd*" — the COMPANIONS/PEOPLE of the trench — refers to the PERSECUTORS**, the ones who dug it and lit it. ⭐ **Not the victims.**
  
  ⭐ **And the same imprecation-word (*qutila*) that ʿAbasa 80:17 aimed at ungrateful humanity is aimed here at torturers.** ⭐ **Second instance of this idiom.**
  
  ⭐ **The historical background** (as related in reports about the boy and the king, and connected by many commentators to Najrān): ⭐ **believers were given a choice — renounce your faith or burn. They chose the fire.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***qutila*** is grammatically **passive past**, used **idiomatically as a curse**. ⚠️ **Don't translate literally.** *Aṣḥāb* = "companions/people of" — from *ṣāḥib*, which you own from **ʿAbasa 80:36** and **Takwīr 81:22**.
- **Why this word:** *ukhdūd* (a dug furrow) over "fire" or "pit" — because ⭐ **it emphasizes that this was PREPARED**, dug in advance, premeditated.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***khadd* = the CHEEK; *ukhdūd* = a FURROW in the earth** — a scar in the ground. ⭐ **And "the people of the trench" = the PERSECUTORS, not the victims.** ⭐ **Same curse-word as ʿAbasa 80:17.**

**5. ذَاتِ الْوَقُودِ — *dhāti'l-waqūd*** · v.5
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**kindling / fuel**" family — *waqada* = to burn/kindle; ***waqūd*** = ⭐ **FUEL** (modern Arabic: *waqūd* = petrol/fuel!); *mawqid* = a stove/hearth.
- **Here it means:** "**the fire full of FUEL**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **The word *waqūd* is chosen to show PREMEDITATION, and it's chilling.**
  
  ⭐ **A fire "possessing fuel" means someone GATHERED the fuel.** ⭐ **Somebody chopped the wood. Somebody carried it. Somebody stacked it and kept it burning.** ⭐ **This wasn't a fire that happened; it was a fire that was **maintained**.
  
  ⭐ **Combined with *ukhdūd* (a DUG trench) in v.4, you get:** ⭐ **they dug it, they fueled it, they kept it going.** ⭐ **Two words establishing full intent.**
  
  ⭐ **And ⭐ *dhāt* appears again** (v.1 *dhāti'l-burūj*, v.5 *dhāti'l-waqūd*) — ⭐ **the same construction for the sky's TOWERS and the trench's FUEL.** **Same grammar, opposite worlds.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***dhāt* + genitive** = "the one possessing X." **Second of three instances in this surah.**
- **Why this word:** *waqūd* over "burning" — because ⭐ **fuel implies a supplier.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***waqūd* = FUEL** (petrol, in modern Arabic). ⭐ **Someone GATHERED it.** ⭐ **Dug (v.4) + fueled (v.5) = premeditated.**

**6. قُعُودٌ ... شُهُودٌ — *quʿūd… shuhūd*** · v.6–7
- **Root feelings:** *quʿūd* — the "**sitting**" family — *qaʿada* = to sit; ⭐ ***qāʿidah*** = **a base/foundation** (something that sits); *maqʿad* = a seat. *shuhūd* — ⭐ **the WITNESSING root from v.3** — plural of *shāhid*.
- **Here it means:** v.6 — "**when they were SITTING by it**"; v.7 — "**and they were WITNESSES to what they were doing to the believers**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **These two verses are, in my view, the most disturbing pair in this juzʾ — and the horror is entirely in the vocabulary.**
  
  ⭐ **The Qur'an does NOT describe the burning.** ⭐ **No screams, no flames, no bodies.** ⭐ **It describes the SEATING ARRANGEMENT.**
  
  ⭐ ***Quʿūd* — they were SITTING.*** ⭐ **Sitting means: comfortable. Settled. Not in a hurry. Watching an event.** ⭐ **They pulled up chairs.**
  
  ⭐ ***Shuhūd* — they were WITNESSES.*** ⭐ **They didn't just kill; they made themselves an AUDIENCE.** ⭐ **The word turns the torture into a SPECTACLE with attendance.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And this is the surah's genius:** ⭐ **by using *shuhūd*, the persecutors are placed in the SAME ROOT as the oath in v.3 and as Allah's own attribute in v.9 (*shahīd*).** ⭐ **They appointed themselves witnesses — and v.9 names the real One.**
  
  ⭐ **One more layer:** ⭐ **the believers in the trench are, by the root, *shuhadāʾ* — MARTYRS/witnesses.** ⭐ **So in one scene you have: the seated witnesses (persecutors), the burning witnesses (martyrs), and the Witness over all (v.9).**
  
  ⭐ **And note the horrific detail:** ⭐ **"what they were doing to the believers"** — *yafʿalūn*, **present-tense**, ⭐ **making it ONGOING while they sat.** **They watched it happen in real time.**
- **Grammar bite:** *quʿūd* and *shuhūd* are both **broken plurals** on the same *fuʿūl* pattern — ⭐ **and they RHYME**, which makes the pairing unforgettable. ⭐ **The rhyme yokes "sitting" to "witnessing."**
- **Why this word:** describing **the chairs instead of the fire** is ⭐ **far more disturbing than describing the fire** — it indicts the casualness.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **The Qur'an doesn't describe the burning. It describes the SEATING.** ⭐ ***quʿūd* = SITTING; *shuhūd* = WATCHING.** ⭐ **They pulled up chairs.** ⭐ **And *shuhūd* puts them in the same root as v.3's oath and v.9's *shahīd*.**

**7. وَمَا نَقَمُوا مِنْهُمْ إِلَّا أَن يُؤْمِنُوا — *wa mā naqamū minhum illā an yuʾminū*** · v.8
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**resenting / holding a grievance / taking vengeance for**" family — *naqama* = **to find fault with, to resent, to avenge oneself for something**; ***intiqām*** = **vengeance**; *al-Muntaqim* = the Avenger.
- **Here it means:** "**And they RESENTED them for nothing except that they believed in Allah, the Almighty, the Praiseworthy.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **This is one of the most quietly devastating verses in the Qur'an, and the structure is what does it.**
  
  ⭐ ***Naqama* means "to hold something AGAINST someone" — to have a grievance.*** ⭐ **So the verse asks: what was their grievance? What was the charge?**
  
  ⭐ **And the answer, with *mā… illā* (nothing… except):** ⭐ **"that they believed in Allah."** ⭐ **That's it. That's the entire indictment.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **Look at what this does: it strips away every possible justification.** ⭐ **Not "they rebelled." Not "they threatened us." Not "they broke the law."** ⭐ **The only crime was faith.**
  
  ⭐ **And then the two names chosen are perfect:** ⭐ ***al-ʿAzīz* (the Almighty/Invincible) and *al-Ḥamīd* (the Praiseworthy).***
  - ⭐ ***al-ʿAzīz*** — **the ALMIGHTY.** ⭐ **Said about the God of people burning in a ditch.** The verse insists on His invincibility **at the exact moment it looks least true.**
  - ⭐ ***al-Ḥamīd*** — **the PRAISEWORTHY.** ⭐ **He deserves praise even here.** ⭐ **Even from inside the trench.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **This verse is the template for understanding all persecution:** ⭐ **the "reason" is never the reason.** ⭐ **The grievance is the faith itself.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***mā … illā*** = **the restriction structure** ("nothing… except") — ⭐ **you own it from Takwīr 81:27** (*in huwa illā dhikr*) and **Muṭaffifīn 83:12** (*wa mā yukadhdhibu bihi illā…*). ⭐ **It EXCLUDES everything else.**
- **Why this word:** *naqamū* (resented/held against) over "punished" — because ⭐ **it frames the persecution as a GRIEVANCE**, which forces the question: *a grievance about what?*
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***intiqām* = VENGEANCE; *naqama* = to hold a grievance.*** ⭐ **"They held NOTHING against them EXCEPT that they believed."** ⭐ **The only charge was faith.** ⭐ **And He's called *al-ʿAzīz* (Almighty) — while His believers burn.**

## §3 — The Two Verdicts (v.10–11)

**8. فَتَنُوا ... عَذَابُ الْحَرِيقِ — *fatanū… ʿadhābu'l-ḥarīq*** · v.10
- **Root feelings:** *fatana* — ⭐ **an extraordinary root**: the original meaning is ⭐ **to put GOLD IN FIRE to test its purity**. From that: ***fitnah*** = **a trial, a test, a temptation, persecution, civil strife** — all senses of **being put through fire**. *ḥarīq* — the "**burning**" family — *ḥaraqa* = to burn; ***ḥarīq*** = **a conflagration, a fire**.
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, those who **PERSECUTED / put to trial** the believing men and women, then did not repent — for them is the punishment of Hell, and for them is **the punishment of the BURNING**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **The root *f-t-n* is the single most important word in this surah, and the metallurgy is the whole point.**
  
  ⭐ ***Fatana* = putting GOLD IN FIRE to see if it's real.*** ⭐ **Impure metal is destroyed by the assay; pure gold comes out unchanged.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **So the very word used for what the persecutors DID to the believers means "TESTING FOR PURITY BY FIRE."** ⭐ **They thought they were destroying. The vocabulary says they were ASSAYING.** ⭐ **And the gold passed.**
  
  ⭐ **This single root reframes the entire event.** ⭐ **The trench wasn't a defeat — it was a furnace, and what came out was proven.**
  
  ⭐ **And then the justice is exact (*wifāq*-style, like Nabaʾ 78:26):** ⭐ **they used FIRE — they get *ʿadhāb al-ḥarīq*, the punishment of BURNING.** ⭐ **Note it's listed SEPARATELY from "the punishment of Hell"** — ⭐ **two punishments named, and the second one names their own method.**
  
  ⭐ **One more mercy hidden here:** ⭐ ***thumma lam yatūbū* — "THEN they did not repent."*** ⭐ **The door was open even for them.** ⭐ **Even for people who burned believers alive, the verse pauses to note: they could have repented.** **That clause did not have to be there.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **note *al-muʾminīna wa'l-muʾmināt*** — ⭐ **both masculine AND feminine explicitly named.** ⭐ **The women are not folded into the masculine plural; they are stated.**
- **Why this word:** *fatanū* over "tortured" — because ⭐ **the root turns the persecution into a purity test they passed.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***fitnah* comes from PUTTING GOLD IN FIRE to test it.*** ⭐ **They thought they were destroying; the word says they were ASSAYING.** ⭐ **And note: "then they did not REPENT" — the door was open even for them.**

**9. الْفَوْزُ الْكَبِيرُ — *al-fawzu'l-kabīr*** · v.11
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**triumph / attainment / escape to safety**" family — *fāza* = to win, to attain; ***fawz*** = **victory** — ⭐ **you own *mafāzan* from Nabaʾ 78:31** (*inna li'l-muttaqīna **mafāzā*** — a place of triumph/escape).
- **Here it means:** "**That is THE GREAT SUCCESS.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **This is the verse the whole surah exists for.**
  
  ⭐ **Read the sequence:** believers are thrown into a fire (v.4–7) → their only crime was faith (v.8) → they are **not rescued** → and then: ⭐ **"THAT is the great success."**
  
  ⭐⭐ **The Qur'an looks at people who were burned alive and calls it WINNING.**
  
  ⭐ **This is a total redefinition of victory** — and it was revealed to a community in Makkah being tortured, watching no rescue arrive. ⭐ **The message: your outcome in this world is not the scoreboard.**
  
  ⭐ **And *fawz* carries BOTH senses (as in Nabaʾ 78:31):** ⭐ **a great WIN and a safe ESCAPE.** ⭐ **They didn't escape the fire; they escaped everything the fire was threatening them with.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare the "success" language you own:**
  - **Nabaʾ 78:31** — *mafāzan* (a place of triumph)
  - ⭐ **Burūj 85:11** — *al-fawzu'l-kabīr* (**the GREAT** success)
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***dhālika*** ("**THAT**") is a **far demonstrative** — used for something **grand and elevated**, not merely distant. ⭐ **"THAT — [that magnificent thing] — is the great success."**
- **Why this word:** *al-kabīr* ("the GREAT") added — because ⭐ **the world's verdict on that trench was "total defeat," and the correction has to be emphatic.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **They burned. They weren't rescued. And the Qur'an says: "THAT is the GREAT SUCCESS."** ⭐ ***fawz* = a WIN and an ESCAPE (Nabaʾ 78:31).** ⭐ **The scoreboard is not in this world.**

## §4 — Who Your Lord Is (v.12–16)

**10. بَطْشَ رَبِّكَ لَشَدِيدٌ — *baṭsha rabbika la-shadīd*** · v.12
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**seizing violently / gripping with force**" family — *baṭasha* = **to grab hold of forcefully, to strike, to seize with the hand**. ***Baṭsh*** = a violent, powerful grip.
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, **the GRIP of your Lord is SEVERE**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **The word is physical, and deliberately so.**
  
  ⭐ ***Baṭsh* is HAND vocabulary — a seizing, a grabbing, a strike.*** ⭐ **Not abstract power (*qudrah*), not authority (*sulṭān*) — ⭐ **a GRIP.**
  
  ⭐ **And think about who's being addressed.** ⭐ **The persecutors used their hands: they dug the trench, gathered the fuel, threw people in.** ⭐ **The answer is described as a HAND too.** ⭐ **Their grip vs. His.**
  
  ⭐ **And *shadīd* is a root you own** — **Nabaʾ 78:12** (*sabʿan **shidādā*** — seven **firm** heavens) and **Insān 76:28** (*shadadnā asrahum* — We **tightened** their frame). ⭐ **The same root that BUILDS firmly also GRIPS firmly.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***inna* + accusative + *la-*** — **the double-emphasis frame** you own from **Infiṭār 82:10** and **Muṭaffifīn 83:7**. ⭐ **Note the *iḍāfah*: *baṭsha rabbika* = "the grip OF your Lord."**
- **Why this word:** *baṭsh* over a word for "punishment" — because ⭐ **a grip is personal and close**, not distant.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***baṭsh* = a violent GRIP of the hand.*** ⭐ **They used their hands on the believers.** ⭐ **And *shadīd* = the same root that built the firm heavens (78:12) and knit your frame (76:28).**

**11. يُبْدِئُ وَيُعِيدُ — *yubdiʾu wa yuʿīd*** · v.13
- **Root feelings:** *yubdiʾu* — the "**beginning / originating**" family — ***badaʾa*** = to begin; ***bidāyah*** = a beginning; *mubtadiʾ* = a beginner. *yuʿīd* — ⭐ the "**returning / repeating**" family — *ʿāda* = to return; ***iʿādah*** = repetition; ⭐ ***ʿĪd*** = **a festival that RETURNS** each year!; *muʿīd* = one who repeats.
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, it is He who **ORIGINATES and REPEATS**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **A perfect *ṭibāq* (opposite-pair), and it's the resurrection argument in three words.**
  
  ⭐ **He STARTS things and He BRINGS THEM BACK.** ⭐ **That's the entire case for resurrection: the One who did it once can do it again.**
  
  ⭐ **And placed HERE, after the trench, it means something specific:** ⭐ **those believers were "ended." He originates and repeats. They're coming back.**
  
  ⭐ **And the *ʿĪd* hook is perfect:** ⭐ ***ʿĪd* is literally "the RETURNING one"*** — the festival that **comes back** every year. ⭐ **You celebrate this root twice a year.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***innahu HUWA yubdiʾu*** — the extra pronoun ***huwa*** is *ḍamīr al-faṣl* (**the separating pronoun**), adding **restriction**: ⭐ **"it is HE [and no other] who originates."** ⭐ **You own this from Nāziʿāt 79:39** (*fa-inna'l-jaḥīma **hiya**'l-maʾwā*).
- **Why this word:** the **pair** rather than one verb — because ⭐ **the argument needs both halves.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***ʿĪd* = "the RETURNING one" — a festival that comes back.*** ⭐ **He ORIGINATES and REPEATS.** ⭐ **Said right after the trench: those believers are coming back.**

**12. الْغَفُورُ الْوَدُودُ — *al-Ghafūru'l-Wadūd*** · v.14
- **Root feelings:** *ghafūr* — ⭐ the "**covering / concealing in order to forgive**" family — ***ghafara*** = to cover over; ⭐ ***mighfar*** = **a HELMET** (what covers the head in battle!); *maghfirah* = forgiveness. *wadūd* — ⭐ the "**love / affection**" family — ***wudd*** = **loving affection, tender attachment**; *mawaddah* = love; ⭐ ***Wadd*** was even an old idol-name, and *widād* = affection.
- **Here it means:** "And He is **the Forgiving, the LOVING**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **This is the most surprising word in the surah, and its placement is everything.**
  
  ⭐ **Two verses ago: "the GRIP of your Lord is SEVERE" (v.12).** ⭐ **Now: "He is the Forgiving, the LOVING."**
  
  ⭐⭐ **In a surah about believers being burned alive, Allah names Himself *al-Wadūd* — THE LOVING.**
  
  ⭐ **And *wudd* is a specific kind of love: TENDER, AFFECTIONATE ATTACHMENT.** ⭐ **Not abstract benevolence — ⭐ **the warm, personal fondness you have for someone you cherish.** ⭐ **The *faʿūl* pattern makes it INTENSE and CONSTANT** (same intensive shape as *ghafūr*, *ṣabūr*, *shakūr*).
  
  ⭐⭐ **Think about why this is here.** ⭐ **A community watching its members tortured could easily conclude: "He doesn't care."** ⭐ **The surah answers directly: *al-Wadūd*.** ⭐ **Not "He is powerful" (already said in v.12), not "He is just" — ⭐ **"He LOVES you."**
  
  ⭐ **And *al-Ghafūr* first is deliberate too:** ⭐ **from *ghafara*, to COVER** — like ***mighfar*, a helmet.*** ⭐ **Forgiveness as PROTECTIVE COVERING.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare the endings you've collected:** ⭐ **Takwīr closed on *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* (the Nurturer); Inshiqāq closed on *ghayru mamnūn* (a reward with no reproach); here, mid-surah, *al-Wadūd*.** ⭐ **These surahs keep inserting tenderness at the hardest moments.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***faʿūl* is an INTENSIVE adjective pattern*** — ⭐ **you own it from Nabaʾ 78:13** (*wahhāj* is *faʿʿāl*; *ghafūr/wadūd* are *faʿūl*) and **Insān 76:21** (*ṭahūrā* — actively **purifying**). ⭐ **Both patterns mean "does it a LOT."**
- **Why this word:** *al-Wadūd* over *ar-Raḥīm* — because ⭐ ***wudd* is warmer and more personal**, and that's exactly what a persecuted believer needs to hear.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***mighfar* = a HELMET** (forgiveness = protective covering). ⭐ ***wudd* = TENDER, WARM affection.*** ⭐ **Two verses after "His GRIP is SEVERE," He calls Himself THE LOVING.** ⭐ **Said to people whose friends were burned alive.**

**13. ذُو الْعَرْشِ الْمَجِيدُ — *dhu'l-ʿArshi'l-Majīd*** · v.15
- **Root feelings:** *ʿarsh* — **the Throne**. *majīd* — ⭐ the "**glory / nobility / abundance**" family — ***majd*** = **glory, honour, magnificence**; ⭐ **the name *Majīd/Mājid***. The root also carries a sense of **abundance and generosity**.
- **Here it means:** "**Owner of the Throne, the Glorious.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the third and final *dhū/dhāt* in the surah** (v.1 the sky's towers, v.5 the fire's fuel, v.15 the Throne). ⭐ **Three "possessors": a sky, a fire, and Him.**
  
  ⭐ **And note *al-Majīd* appears TWICE in this surah:** ⭐ **v.15 for the Owner of the Throne, and v.21 for the QUR'AN itself** (*bal huwa **qurʾānun majīd***). ⭐ **The same adjective for Allah and for His speech.**
  
  ⭐ **Placement matters:** ⭐ **the persecutors had a throne too** (a king ordered this). ⭐ **The surah quietly names the real Throne-owner.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***dhū* (nominative) vs. *dhī* (genitive) vs. *dhā* (accusative)*** — ⭐ **one of the FIVE SPECIAL NOUNS (الأسماء الخمسة)** that show case with a **long vowel**. ⭐ **You own this from Takwīr 81:20** (*dhī quwwatin ʿinda dhi'l-ʿArsh*). ⭐ **Note Takwīr also said *dhi'l-ʿArsh* — same phrase!**
- **Why this word:** *majīd* over "great" — because the root carries ⭐ **glory AND generosity together.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***majd* = GLORY.*** ⭐ **Used TWICE: for Allah (v.15) and for the QUR'AN (v.21).** ⭐ **And *dhi'l-ʿArsh* is the same phrase as Takwīr 81:20.**

**14. فَعَّالٌ لِّمَا يُرِيدُ — *faʿʿālun li-mā yurīd*** · v.16
- **Root feelings:** *faʿʿāl* — ⭐ **the INTENSIVE pattern you own from Nabaʾ 78:13** (*sirājan **wahhājā***), **78:14** (*mā'an **thajjājā***), **78:25** (*ghassāq*), **78:28** (*kidhdhābā*) — from *faʿala*, **to do**. *yurīd* — the "**willing/wanting**" family (*irādah* = will).
- **Here it means:** "**Doer of whatever He wills.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **You already own this word-SHAPE — now watch it applied to Allah Himself.**
  
  ⭐ **The *faʿʿāl* pattern means "does it INTENSELY, CONSTANTLY, ABUNDANTLY."** ⭐ **You met it four times in Nabaʾ describing a blazing lamp, pouring rain, foul discharge, and relentless lying.** ⭐ **Here it's built on the root *faʿala* — "to DO" itself.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **So *faʿʿāl* literally means "THE ONE WHO DOES, INTENSELY."*** ⭐ **The intensive form of the verb "to do" — the ultimate Doer.**
  
  ⭐ **And *li-mā yurīd* ("of whatever He wills") removes every limit.** ⭐ **Placed after the trench, it means: what happened was not outside His will, and what comes next is not outside it either.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Takwīr 81:29** — *wa mā tashāʾūna illā an **yashāʾa'llāh*** ("you do not will except that Allah wills"). ⭐ **Same doctrine, two surahs.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **the *faʿʿāl* intensive** — ⭐ **FIFTH instance you own.** **Learn the shape once, collect it everywhere.**
- **Why this word:** the **intensive of the verb "to do"** is the most economical way to say **absolute agency**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***faʿʿāl* = the INTENSIVE pattern** (*wahhāj, thajjāj, ghassāq, kidhdhāb* — all from Nabaʾ). ⭐ **Built on "to DO" = THE Doer.** ⭐ **"Doer of whatever He wills" — after the trench, this is the answer.**

## §5 — You've Seen This Before (v.17–22)

**15. حَدِيثُ الْجُنُودِ — *ḥadīthu'l-junūd*** · v.17
- **Root feelings:** *ḥadīth* — ⭐ **"a narration / account / something newly told"** — from *ḥadatha* = **to happen, to be new** (***ḥadīth*** = **new**, and also **a report** — the same word as the Prophetic **ḥadīth**!). *junūd* — plural of *jund*, ⭐ **an ARMY/troops** — related to *jundī* = **a soldier**.
- **Here it means:** "**Has the story of the ARMIES reached you?**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **The word *junūd* (ARMIES) is the point, and it's a devastating choice.**
  
  ⭐ **Pharaoh and Thamūd are introduced not as "peoples" or "nations" but as ⭐ **ARMIES** — military forces, organized power, troops.
  
  ⭐ **Why?** Because ⭐ **the believers in the trench had NO army.** ⭐ **The surah says: you're worried about being outmatched? Let me tell you about some ARMIES.** ⭐ **And then names two that no longer exist.**
  
  ⭐ **And ⭐ *hal atāka* ("has it reached you?") is a construction you own from Nāziʿāt 79:15** (*hal atāka **ḥadīthu Mūsā*** — "has the **story of Mūsā** reached you?"). ⭐ **Same opening, same word *ḥadīth*, same purpose: a history lesson mid-surah.**
  
  ⭐ **Nāziʿāt told the Pharaoh story at LENGTH (12 verses). Burūj compresses it to a NAME (v.18).** ⭐ **Because you already know it — Nāziʿāt told you.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***hal atāka…*** = a **rhetorical question** introducing a known lesson. ⭐ **Note *Thamūda* in v.18 — it's a DIPTOTE** (*mamnūʿ min aṣ-ṣarf*), so it takes **fatḥah without tanwīn.**
- **Why this word:** *junūd* (armies) over "peoples" — because ⭐ **it directly answers the fear of being outnumbered.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***jundī* = a SOLDIER; *junūd* = ARMIES.*** ⭐ **The believers had no army. "Let me tell you about some armies."** ⭐ **And *hal atāka ḥadīth…* is the same opening as Nāziʿāt 79:15.**

**16. مِن وَرَائِهِم مُّحِيطٌ — *min warāʾihim muḥīṭ*** · v.20
- **Root feelings:** *warāʾ* — **behind** — ⭐ **you own it from Inshiqāq 84:10** (*warāʾa ẓahrih* — the record **behind his back**). *muḥīṭ* — ⭐ the "**encompassing / surrounding**" family — *aḥāṭa* = to encircle completely; ⭐ ***muḥīṭ*** = **the OCEAN** in modern Arabic (*al-Muḥīṭ al-Hindī* = the Indian Ocean — "the encompassing one")! Also *ḥāʾiṭ* = **a wall**.
- **Here it means:** "**But Allah encompasses them from behind.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **A stunning image, and the two words work together perfectly.**
  
  ⭐ ***Muḥīṭ* means SURROUNDING ON ALL SIDES — like an OCEAN around an island.*** ⭐ **There's no edge you can reach and step past.**
  
  ⭐ **And ⭐ *min warāʾihim* — "from BEHIND them" — is the killer detail.*** ⭐ **Behind is the direction you can't watch.** ⭐ **They're facing forward, denying (v.19), confident — ⭐ **and He has them from the one angle they never check.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Inshiqāq 84:10:** ⭐ **the condemned man's record is handed to him *warāʾa ẓahrih* — BEHIND HIS BACK, where he can't see.** ⭐ **Same blind spot, two surahs.**
  
  ⭐ **And it answers v.7 exactly:** ⭐ **they sat WATCHING (*shuhūd*) the believers burn — thinking they had the best view.** ⭐ **v.20: someone was behind them the whole time.** ⭐ **The watchers were being watched from a direction they couldn't turn toward.**
- **Grammar bite:** **active participle** (*muḥīṭ*) = a **continuous, present state** — "**is [always] encompassing.**"
- **Why this word:** *muḥīṭ* over "knows" or "sees" — because ⭐ **encompassment means there is no OUTSIDE.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***muḥīṭ* = the OCEAN** (*al-Muḥīṭ al-Hindī*) — **surrounding on all sides.** ⭐ **"From BEHIND them" — the one direction they never check.** ⭐ **They sat WATCHING (v.7); Someone was behind them all along.**

**17. لَوْحٍ مَّحْفُوظٍ — *lawḥin maḥfūẓ*** · v.22
- **Root feelings:** *lawḥ* — ⭐ **a TABLET / flat board** — the surface you write on; also *lawḥah* = **a painting/panel** in modern Arabic. *maḥfūẓ* — ⭐ the "**guarding / preserving**" family — **you own it richly**: **Infiṭār 82:10** (*la-**ḥāfiẓīn***), **Muṭaffifīn 83:33** (*ḥāfiẓīn*), **Ṭāriq 86:4** (*ḥāfiẓ*), and ⭐ ***ḥāfiẓ*** = **one who has memorized the Qur'an.**
- **Here it means:** "**In a PRESERVED TABLET.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **The final verse of the surah, and it is the perfect answer to the trench.**
  
  ⭐ **Line up the surah's images:**
  - ⭐ **They dug a trench** (v.4) — a thing in the ground
  - ⭐ **They gathered fuel** (v.5) — a thing that burns
  - ⭐ **They burned believers** (v.7) — flesh, destructible
  - ⭐⭐ **And the message is on *al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ*** — ⭐ **a PRESERVED TABLET.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **You can burn a person. You cannot burn this.** ⭐ **The surah ends by naming the one thing their fire could never reach.**
  
  ⭐ **And *maḥfūẓ* is the passive of the root you've now seen FOUR times across these surahs** — the guarding angels, the guardians they weren't appointed as, the guardian over every soul. ⭐ **Here the root reaches its highest object: the Book itself.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare ʿAbasa 80:13–14:** *fī **ṣuḥufin mukarramah · marfūʿatin muṭahharah*** — ⭐ **"honoured, exalted, purified scrolls."** ⭐ **Same idea: the message lives somewhere untouchable.** ⭐ **ʿAbasa said it to a Prophet ﷺ tempted by worldly endorsement; Burūj says it to a community facing fire.** ⭐ **Same reassurance, two crises.**
  
  ⭐ **And the last two verses pair beautifully:** ⭐ **v.21 — "it is a GLORIOUS Qur'an" (*majīd*, the same word used for Allah in v.15).** ⭐ **v.22 — "in a PRESERVED tablet."** ⭐ **Glorious and safe. Neither diminished nor destructible.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **both indefinite** (*lawḥin maḥfūẓin*) — **and note the passive participle** *maḥfūẓ* ("**that which HAS BEEN preserved**"). ⭐ **Someone is doing the preserving.**
- **Why this word:** ending on **a tablet** rather than a threat — because ⭐ **the surah's real answer to fire is PERMANENCE.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***lawḥ* = a TABLET; *maḥfūẓ* = PRESERVED** (same root as ***ḥāfiẓ***, one who memorizes the Qur'an). ⭐ **They burned people. The message is on a tablet fire can't touch.** ⭐ **Compare ʿAbasa 80:13–14 — same reassurance, different crisis.**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **أَصْحَابُ الْأُخْدُودِ (v.4) = the PERSECUTORS, not the victims.** ⚠️ **The curse is on the people who DUG the trench.**
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **فَتَنُوا (v.10)** — ***fitnah* comes from PUTTING GOLD IN FIRE to test purity.** ⚠️ **Translating it as merely "persecuted" loses the entire reframe.**
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **الْوَدُودُ (v.14)** — ⚠️ **"THE LOVING," two verses after "His GRIP is SEVERE," in a surah about believers being burned.** **Don't skim past it.**
4. ⭐⭐ **The *sh-h-d* root appears 4× in 9 verses** (v.3 twice, v.7, v.9). ⚠️ **Track it — the surah is about who was watching.**
5. ⭐⭐ **قُعُودٌ / شُهُودٌ (v.6–7)** — the Qur'an describes **the SEATING**, not the burning. ⚠️ **The horror is in the casualness.**
6. ⭐ **قُتِلَ (v.4)** — an **imprecation-idiom** ("cursed be"), ⚠️ **NOT literal killing.** Same as ʿAbasa 80:17.
7. ⭐ **فَعَّالٌ (v.16)** — ⚠️ **the intensive of the verb "to DO"** — the same pattern as Nabaʾ's *wahhāj/thajjāj*.
8. ⭐ **الْمَجِيدُ appears TWICE** — v.15 (**Allah**) and v.21 (**the Qur'an**). ⚠️ **Same adjective, two subjects.**
9. ⭐ **ثَمُودَ (v.18)** — a **DIPTOTE**: fatḥah, **no tanwīn**. Same rule as *ḥadāʾiqa* (ʿAbasa 80:30), *kawāʿiba* (Nabaʾ 78:33).
10. ⭐ **مُّحِيطٌ (v.20)** — ***muḥīṭ* = the OCEAN** in modern Arabic. ⚠️ **Encompassing means there is no outside.**
