# Surah Ash-Sharḥ (94) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–8) · *(also called Al-Inshirāḥ)*

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**One sentence is repeated twice in a row — and the repetition is a mathematical argument.**

⭐ **v.5:** *fa-inna maʿa'l-**ʿusri** yusrā* — "So indeed, with the hardship is ease."
⭐ **v.6:** *inna maʿa'l-**ʿusri** yusrā* — "Indeed, with the hardship is ease."

⭐ **Classical scholars noticed something in the Arabic that English cannot show:**

- ⭐ ***al-ʿusr*** (hardship) is **DEFINITE** — "**THE** hardship" — ⭐ **so both verses refer to *the same one*.**
- ⭐ ***yusran*** (ease) is **INDEFINITE** — "**a** ease" — ⭐ **so each mention is *a different one*.**

⭐⭐ **The result: ONE hardship, TWO eases.** ⭐ **A famous saying attributed to the early generations captures it: *"one hardship will never overcome two eases."***

⭐ **And note the preposition: *maʿa* = "WITH," not "after."** ⭐ **The ease isn't waiting on the far side of the hardship — ⭐ **it's travelling alongside it.**

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐⭐ **THE hardship (one, definite). A ease… a ease (two, indefinite).** ⭐ **And they arrive *together*, not in sequence.**

---

## The Big Picture

*⭐ This surah is the direct continuation of Aḍ-Ḍuḥā (93) — same audience, same comfort, same moment. ⭐ **Ḍuḥā asked three rhetorical questions about the past; Sharḥ asks one and then lists three more gifts.***

1. **Three gifts** (v.1–4) — ⭐ **the chest expanded, the burden removed, the mention raised.**
2. **The law** (v.5–6) — ⭐ **with hardship comes ease. Twice.**
3. **Two commands** (v.7–8) — ⭐ **when you finish, exert yourself; and to your Lord, turn with longing.**

## §1 — Three Gifts (v.1–4)

**1. أَلَمْ نَشْرَحْ لَكَ صَدْرَكَ — *a-lam nashraḥ laka ṣadrak*** · v.1
- **Root feelings:** *sharaḥa* — ⭐ the "**opening out / expanding / laying open**" family — ⭐ **originally *to slice meat open and spread it flat*; from that: **to expand**, and ⭐ ***sharḥ* = AN EXPLANATION/commentary*** (⭐ **you "open up" a text so people can see inside it!**). *ṣadr* — ⭐ **the CHEST** — ⭐ **and by extension the *front* of anything** (*ṣadr al-majlis* = the head of the gathering; ⭐ ***ṣadara* = to issue forth**).
- **Here it means:** "**Did We not expand for you your chest?**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the root gives you a free everyday word and a perfect image.**
  
  ⭐ ***Sharḥ*** is what a scholar writes to **explain** a difficult book — ⭐ **a commentary "opens the text out" so you can see what's inside.** ⭐ **Same verb here: **your chest was opened out**.
  
  ⭐ **And why the CHEST specifically?** ⭐ **In Arabic the *ṣadr* is where you carry **anxiety, grief, and constriction** — ⭐ **the opposite state is *ḍayq aṣ-ṣadr* (tightness of chest), the standard idiom for distress.** ⭐ **So *sharḥ aṣ-ṣadr* = **the removal of that tightness**: capacity, calm, room to breathe.**
  
  ⭐ **Notice the sequence in v.1–4: three gifts, and they're all about **weight and space**:** ⭐ **the chest EXPANDED (room made), the burden REMOVED (weight lifted), the mention RAISED (elevation given).** ⭐ **Open, unload, lift.**
  
  ⭐ **And *a-lam* is a question expecting "yes"** — ⭐ **the same technique as Ḍuḥā 93:6–8** (*a-lam yajidka…*). ⭐ **Two consecutive surahs comforting by asking him to remember.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **أَلَمْ + jussive** = rhetorical question expecting agreement. ⭐ **Note *nashraḥ* has dropped its final vowel.** ⭐ **And *laka* ("for you") is fronted before *ṣadrak* — ⭐ **spotlighting that it was done FOR HIM.**
- **Why this word:** *sharaḥa* over "calmed" — ⭐ **it's a word of **making space**, not of soothing.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Sharḥ* = A COMMENTARY** — ⭐ **"opening a text out" so you can see inside.** ⭐ **Same verb: your chest was OPENED OUT.** ⭐ **And *ḍayq aṣ-ṣadr* (tight chest) = the standard Arabic idiom for distress.**

**2. وَوَضَعْنَا عَنكَ وِزْرَكَ — *wa waḍaʿnā ʿanka wizrak*** · v.2
- **Root feelings:** *waḍaʿa* — ⭐ the "**putting down / setting aside**" family — ⭐ **you own it from Ghāshiyah 88:14** (*akwābun **mawḍūʿah***, cups **set out**); ⭐ ***mawḍūʿ* = a TOPIC*** (something "placed before you"). *wizr* — ⭐ the "**heavy burden / load**" family — *wizr* = a crushing load and, by extension, **a sin**; ⭐ ***wazīr* = a MINISTER*** (one who **carries the ruler's load**!) — ⭐ **and you own the root from Qiyāmah 75:11** (*lā **wazar***, "no refuge/mountain-shelter").
- **Here it means:** "**And We removed from you your burden.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the *wazīr* connection is one of the best free hooks in Arabic.**
  
  ⭐ **A *wazīr* (minister/vizier) is literally ⭐ **"the one who bears the weight"** — the person who carries the burden of governing **so the ruler doesn't have to**.** ⭐ **Same root as the *wizr* being lifted here.**
  
  ⭐ **And you already met the root in Qiyāmah 75:11** — *kallā **lā wazar*** ("No! There is **no refuge**"). ⭐ **There, *wazar* meant a **mountain stronghold** — a place that **bears** you.** ⭐ **Two surahs, one root: a load that crushes, and a shelter that carries.**
  
  ⭐ **Note the verb: *waḍaʿnā ʿanka* — "We PUT [it] DOWN, OFF you."** ⭐ **Not "We forgave" or "We lightened" — ⭐ **it was **physically taken off your back**.** ⭐ **And the *ʿan* ("off/away from") makes the removal directional.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***waḍaʿa ʿan*** = "to remove **from**" — note the preposition. ⭐ ***ʿanka* is fronted before *wizrak*** — the same emphatic fronting as v.1's *laka*.
- **Why this word:** *wizr* (a crushing physical load) rather than an abstract word for sin or worry — ⭐ **it makes the relief **bodily**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***WAZĪR* = A MINISTER — "the one who CARRIES THE LOAD."** ⭐ **Same root as this burden.** ⭐ **And Qiyāmah 75:11 used *wazar* = a MOUNTAIN SHELTER.** ⭐ **One root: the load, and the thing that bears it.**

**3. الَّذِي أَنقَضَ ظَهْرَكَ — *alladhī anqaḍa ẓahrak*** · v.3
- **Root feelings:** *anqaḍa* — ⭐ **an extraordinary word**: from *naqaḍa* = **to undo, unravel, break apart**; ⭐ ***anqaḍa* specifically = to make something CREAK/CRACK under weight*** — ⭐ **the sound a loaded structure makes just before it breaks.** *ẓahr* — ⭐ **the BACK** (⭐ also *ẓahara* = to appear/become visible — what's on your back is what shows).
- **Here it means:** "**which had weighed down / made your back creak.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **this is the most physically vivid word in the surah, and it's about SOUND.**
  
  ⭐ ***Anqaḍa* describes the AUDIBLE CRACK of something under unbearable load*** — ⭐ **like a wooden beam groaning, or a saddle creaking on an overloaded camel.** ⭐ **The burden wasn't just heavy: ⭐ **you could HEAR it.**
  
  ⭐ **And *naqaḍa*'s core meaning — **to unravel / undo a rope's twist** — ⭐ **adds another layer: the load was *coming apart at the seams*.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Qiyāmah 75:29** — *wa'ltaffati's-sāqu bi's-**sāq*** (legs **twisting** together in agony) — ⭐ **and Ghāshiyah 88:3** — *ʿāmilatun **nāṣibah*** (working, **worn out**). ⭐ **The Qurʾān keeps describing distress through the **body's mechanics**, not through emotion words.
  
  ⭐ **And note the tenderness of the sequence:** ⭐ **v.2 says the burden was removed. ⭐ **v.3 pauses to acknowledge how heavy it actually was.** ⭐ **He doesn't just fix it — He names how bad it was.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form IV** (*anqaḍa* = to **cause** to creak). ⭐ ***Alladhī*** is a relative pronoun describing *wizrak* in v.2.
- **Why this word:** *anqaḍa* (**made it creak**) over "was heavy" — ⭐ **because a sound is more real than an adjective.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Anqaḍa* = TO MAKE SOMETHING CREAK/CRACK under weight.** ⭐ **The sound a beam makes before it breaks.** ⭐ **You could HEAR the burden.** ⭐ **And v.2 removes it; v.3 acknowledges how heavy it was.**

**4. وَرَفَعْنَا لَكَ ذِكْرَكَ — *wa rafaʿnā laka dhikrak*** · v.4
- **Root feelings:** *rafaʿa* — ⭐ the "**raising / elevating**" family — ⭐ you own it from **ʿAbasa 80:14** (*ṣuḥufin **marfūʿah***), **Ghāshiyah 88:13** (*sururun **marfūʿah***), **Ghāshiyah 88:18** (*as-samāʾi kayfa **rufiʿat***); ⭐ **and *marfūʿ* = the NOMINATIVE case** in grammar (the "raised" case!). *dhikr* — ⭐ the "**mention / remembrance**" family — ⭐ **you own it across SIX surahs now** (76:29, 80:11, 81:27, 87:9, 88:21, 89:23).
- **Here it means:** "**And We raised high for you your mention.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the third gift, and it completes the pattern perfectly.**
  
  ⭐ **v.1: chest **OPENED** (space made inside).**
  ⭐ **v.2–3: burden **REMOVED** (weight taken off).**
  ⭐ **v.4: mention **RAISED** (elevation given).**
  
  ⭐⭐ **Notice the physics: you can only be *raised* once the *weight is off*.** ⭐ **The three gifts are in causal order — empty, unload, lift.**
  
  ⭐ **And the *dhikr* root is the one that has defined the Prophet's ﷺ mission for six surahs — ⭐ **he was told to *remind* (*dhakkir*) again and again.** ⭐ **Here the same root turns around: ⭐ **the reminder's own *mention* is raised.** ⭐ **He spent his life mentioning; his mention was elevated.**
  
  ⭐ **And there's a reality every Muslim lives:** ⭐ **his name is joined to Allah's in the *shahādah*, in the *adhān*, in every *tashahhud*.** ⭐ **You enact verse 4 several times a day.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***laka* fronted again** — third time in four verses (*laka* v.1, *ʿanka* v.2, *laka* v.4). ⭐ **The surah keeps putting "for you / from you" ahead of the object.** ⭐ **Everything is personalized.**
- **Why this word:** *rafaʿnā* (**We** raised) with the divine subject — ⭐ **the elevation wasn't earned by reputation-building; it was **granted**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **Three gifts in causal order: ⭐ **chest OPENED → burden REMOVED → mention RAISED.** ⭐ **You can only be lifted once the weight is off.** ⭐ **And you enact v.4 in every *adhān* and *tashahhud*.**

## §2 — The Law (v.5–6)

**5. فَإِنَّ مَعَ الْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا — *fa-inna maʿa'l-ʿusri yusrā*** · v.5, **repeated** v.6
- **Root feelings:** *ʿusr* — ⭐ the "**hardship / difficulty**" family (*ʿasīr* = difficult; *muʿsir* = in financial straits). *yusr* — ⭐ the "**ease / smoothness**" family (*yassara* = to make easy; ⭐ **and *al-yusrā*, *yasīr*** — you own these from **ʿAbasa 80:20**, **Inshiqāq 84:8**, **Aʿlā 87:8**, **Layl 92:7 & 92:10**).
- **Here it means:** "**So indeed, with the hardship is ease. Indeed, with the hardship is ease.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **THE MOST QUOTED VERSE IN THIS JUZʾ — and there are THREE things in the Arabic that translation hides.**
  
  ⭐ **ONE — THE DEFINITE/INDEFINITE ASYMMETRY.**
  ⭐ ***al-ʿusr*** = **THE hardship** (definite, so **the same one** both times).
  ⭐ ***yusran*** = **A ease** (indefinite, so **a different one** each time).
  ⭐⭐ **Result: ONE hardship, TWO eases.** ⭐ **The classical maxim: *"one hardship will never defeat two eases."***
  
  ⭐ **TWO — THE PREPOSITION.**
  ⭐ **It says ***maʿa*** = "**WITH**," ⚠️ ⭐ **NOT *baʿda* ("after").** ⭐ **The ease is not on the far side of the hardship — ⭐ **it is *accompanying* it, right now, inside it.**
  
  ⭐ **THREE — THE REPETITION ITSELF.**
  ⭐ **Saying the identical sentence twice is *takrār* — ⭐ **and the second one drops the *fa-*, making it stand alone as a bare, absolute law.** ⭐ **v.5 is a *consequence* ("SO indeed…"); v.6 is a *principle* ("Indeed…").**
  
  ⭐ **And the placement is everything:** ⭐ **it comes right after three verses describing a burden so heavy it made his back CREAK.** ⭐ **This isn't abstract encouragement — ⭐ **it's said to someone who was just described as breaking.**
  
  ⭐ **The *y-s-r* root is now across SIX surahs:** ⭐ **ʿAbasa 80:20, Inshiqāq 84:8, Aʿlā 87:8, Layl 92:7, Layl 92:10, Sharḥ 94:5–6.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐⭐ ***maʿa*** = "with/alongside" (an adverb of accompaniment). ⚠️ ⭐ **The definite *al-* vs. the *tanwīn* on *yusran* is the whole argument — ⭐ **learn to *see* definiteness in Arabic and this verse opens up.**
- **Why this word:** *maʿa* over *baʿda* — ⭐ **because a person in the middle of hardship needs to know the ease is **already here**, not scheduled.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **THE hardship (definite = ONE). A ease… a ease (indefinite = TWO).** ⭐⭐ **"One hardship will never defeat two eases."** ⭐⭐⭐ **And *maʿa* = "WITH," not "after."** ⭐ **The ease travels alongside.**

## §3 — Two Commands (v.7–8)

**6. فَإِذَا فَرَغْتَ فَانصَبْ — *fa-idhā faraghta fa'nṣab*** · v.7
- **Root feelings:** *faragha* — ⭐ the "**emptying / becoming free / finishing**" family — ***farāgh* = EMPTINESS / free time*** (⭐ **modern Arabic: *waqt al-farāgh* = leisure time!**); *fārigh* = empty. *inṣab* — ⭐⭐ the "**setting upright / toiling to exhaustion**" family — ⭐ **THE SAME ROOT as *nāṣibah* (Ghāshiyah 88:3, "exhausted") and *nuṣibat* (Ghāshiyah 88:19, "the mountains were SET UP")!**
- **Here it means:** "**So when you have finished, then exert yourself / stand up [to the next task].**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **THE BEST ROOT-PAYOFF IN THIS SURAH — and it connects straight back to Ghāshiyah.**
  
  ⭐ **The root *n-ṣ-b* has two faces you already own:**
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:3** — *ʿāmilatun ***nāṣibah*** — faces **WORN OUT** from toil ⭐ (**effort that exhausts and earns nothing**)
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:19** — *wa ila'l-jibāli kayfa ***nuṣibat*** — the mountains **SET UP FIRMLY** ⭐ (**effort that stands forever**)
  - ⭐ **Sharḥ 94:7 — *fa'**nṣab*** — **YOU exert yourself / stand up**
  
  ⭐⭐ **The command is: be the *mountain* kind of *naṣab*, not the *exhausted-faces* kind.** ⭐ **Same root, and you choose which one you are.**
  
  ⭐ **And *faragha* is beautiful: ⭐ ***farāgh* = EMPTY TIME.*** ⭐ **So the verse literally says: ⭐ **"when you become EMPTY — FILL UP."** ⭐ **The moment a task is done, the vacancy is to be occupied.**
  
  ⭐ **Classical readings of what to move on to:** ⭐ **from worldly affairs to worship; from one act of worship to another; from daʿwah to prayer.** ⭐ **All share one idea: ⭐ **rest is not the reward for finishing. The next thing is.**
  
  ⭐ **Note the placement — right after "with hardship comes ease."** ⭐ **The surah refuses to let "ease" mean "stop."**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **imperative** (*inṣab*, with the connecting hamzah). ⭐ ***Idhā*** + past tense = "**when** you have [finished]" — a definite future condition.
- **Why this word:** *inṣab* (a **toil** word) rather than a word for worship — ⭐ **because the point is *effort*, whatever it's directed at.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Naṣab*: Ghāshiyah 88:3 = faces WORN OUT (wasted effort). Ghāshiyah 88:19 = mountains SET UP (lasting effort). Sharḥ 94:7 = YOU — choose which.*** ⭐⭐ **And *farāgh* = FREE TIME.** ⭐ **"When you become EMPTY — FILL UP."**

**7. وَإِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ فَارْغَب — *wa ilā rabbika fa'rghab*** · v.8
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**desiring / yearning / turning toward with longing**" family — *raghiba fī* = **to want something eagerly**; ⚠️ ⭐ **but *raghiba ʿAN* = to turn AWAY from in distaste!** ⭐ **The preposition flips the meaning completely.** ⭐ **Also *raghbah* = a strong desire.**
- **Here it means:** "**And to your Lord turn [all] longing.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the final verse, and the grammar carries the whole meaning.**
  
  ⭐ **Look at the word order: ***wa **ilā rabbika*** fa'rghab*** — ⭐ **"and TO YOUR LORD — [then] direct your longing."** ⭐ **The phrase "to your Lord" is **FRONTED before the verb**.
  
  ⭐⭐ **In Arabic, fronting = RESTRICTION.** ⭐ **So it means: **to your Lord ALONE** direct your longing.** ⭐ **Not "also want Allah" — ⭐ **"want Him, and specifically Him."**
  
  ⭐ **You've seen this exact fronting move across FIVE surahs now:**
  - **Qiyāmah 75:12** — *ilā rabbika yawmaʾidhin **al-mustaqarr***
  - **Qiyāmah 75:30** — *ilā rabbika yawmaʾidhin **al-masāq***
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:44** — *ilā rabbika **muntahāhā***
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:25** — *inna **ilaynā** iyābahum*
  - **Sharḥ 94:8** — *wa **ilā rabbika** fa'rghab*
  
  ⭐ **Four of them are about where you END UP. This one is about where you *want* to end up.** ⭐ **The others describe the destination; this one commands the desire.**
  
  ⭐ **And the ⚠️ preposition trap is important:** ⭐ ***raghiba FĪ* = to desire something; *raghiba ʿAN* = to shun it.*** ⭐ **Here it's *ilā* — **direct your longing TOWARD**.
  
  ⭐⭐ **Beautiful pairing with v.7:** ⭐ **v.7 = **WORK** (*inṣab* — exert your body). ⭐ **v.8 = **WANT** (*irghab* — direct your heart).** ⭐ **Effort and longing, action and intention, in two final commands.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **imperative.** ⚠️ ⭐ **Note the ***fa-*** on *fa'rghab* even though *ilā rabbika* comes first — ⭐ **this is a known construction where the fronted phrase is emphasized and the *fa* keeps the sentence connected.**
- **Why this word:** *raghiba* (**yearning**) rather than a word for worship or obedience — ⭐ **because the last command is about what you *want*, which is upstream of everything you do.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **"TO YOUR LORD" is FRONTED = **to Him ALONE**.** ⭐ **FIVE surahs use this move: 75:12, 75:30, 79:44, 88:25, 94:8.** ⭐⭐ ⚠️ ***raghiba FĪ* = desire it; *raghiba ʿAN* = shun it.** ⭐ **The preposition flips it.** ⭐⭐ **v.7 = WORK (body). v.8 = WANT (heart).**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **الْعُسْرِ is DEFINITE; يُسْرًا is INDEFINITE (v.5–6)** — ⚠️ **this is the entire argument.** ⭐ **ONE hardship, TWO eases.** **If you don't see the *al-* vs. the *tanwīn*, you miss the verse.**
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **مَعَ (v.5–6) means "WITH," not "AFTER."** ⚠️ **The ease accompanies the hardship — it isn't waiting on the other side.**
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **فَانصَبْ (v.7)** — ⚠️ **same root as *nāṣibah* (Ghāshiyah 88:3, EXHAUSTED faces) and *nuṣibat* (88:19, mountains SET UP).** ⭐ **Two kinds of toil — the command is to be the second kind.**
4. ⭐⭐ **فَارْغَب (v.8)** — ⚠️ ***raghiba FĪ* = to DESIRE. *raghiba ʿAN* = to SHUN.** ⭐ **The preposition reverses the meaning entirely.**
5. ⭐⭐ **وَإِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ is FRONTED (v.8)** — ⚠️ **fronting = RESTRICTION.** ⭐ **"To your Lord **alone**," not merely "also to your Lord."**
6. ⭐ **وِزْرَكَ (v.2)** — from the root of ***wazīr* (a minister — "the load-bearer")** and ***wazar*** (Qiyāmah 75:11, a mountain shelter). ⚠️ **Not just "sin" — a **physical crushing load**.
7. ⭐ **أَنقَضَ (v.3)** — ⚠️ **means "made it CREAK/CRACK,"** the audible groan of something under unbearable weight. **Not merely "was heavy."**
8. ⭐ **فَرَغْتَ (v.7)** — from ***farāgh*, EMPTINESS / free time.** ⚠️ **"When you become EMPTY," not just "when you finish."**
9. ⭐ **نَشْرَحْ / نَجْعَل-type jussives** — ⚠️ **after *lam*, the verb DROPS its final vowel.** Same rule as Ḍuḥā 93:6 (*yajidka*).
10. ⭐ **The three gifts (v.1–4) are in CAUSAL order** — ⚠️ **opened → unloaded → raised.** **You can only be lifted once the weight is off.**
