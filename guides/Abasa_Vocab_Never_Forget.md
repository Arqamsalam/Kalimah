# Surah ʿAbasa (80) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–42)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**It opens on one face — and closes on two kinds of faces.**

Verse 1: *ʿabasa* — "**he frowned**." A single face, for a single moment, turned away from a blind man who came asking to learn.

Verses 38–42: ***wujūhun yawmaʾidhin*** — "**faces, on that Day**…" — some **shining, laughing, rejoicing at good news**; others **dust-covered, overtaken by darkness**. The surah that began by correcting one expression ends by showing the only expressions that will finally matter.

And look at **how** the correction is made. Verses 1–2 speak of the Prophet ﷺ in the **third person** — "*he* frowned and turned away, because the blind man came to *him*" — as if too gentle to say "you" at first. Only in v.3 does the surah turn and speak to him directly: "And what would make **you** know…?" The reproach arrives **with its courtesy already inside it**.

The man was ʿAbdullāh ibn Umm Maktūm. The narrations of the occasion (in Tirmidhī and the Muwaṭṭaʾ) describe him arriving while the Prophet ﷺ was speaking with the chiefs of Quraysh, hoping to win them. The surah's verdict is simple: **the one who comes seeking outranks the one who thinks he needs nothing.**

🧠 **The hook:** one face frowned at the start; on that Day, every face is either **lit** or **dust**.

## The Big Picture

1. **The Frown** (v.1–10) — the eager blind man and the self-sufficient chief. A correction, in the gentlest grammar.
2. **Where the Message Lives** (v.11–16) — it needs no one's endorsement: honoured scrolls, raised and pure, in the hands of noble scribes.
3. **Man, From a Drop to a Grave** (v.17–23) — how ungrateful! Created, measured, eased, buried, raised — and still hasn't done what he was told.
4. **Look at Your Plate** (v.24–32) — water poured, earth split, and eight foods for you and your animals.
5. **The Deafener** (v.33–37) — the Day a person flees his brother, mother, father, wife and sons.
6. **Two Kinds of Faces** (v.38–42) — lit with good news, or dust and darkness.

**Memory spine:** one frown (v.1) → the message's dignity (v.13) → your origin (v.19) → your food (v.24) → your family fled (v.34) → your face (v.38).

## §1 — The Frown (v.1–10)

**1. عَبَسَ — *ʿabasa*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** the "**frowning, scowling**" family — *ʿabūs* = a scowling face (Insān 76:10 — *yawman ʿabūsan*, "a **frowning** Day"); and ⭐ the name ***ʿAbbās*** — "one who frowns a lot," a name for a **lion** (the stern face).
- **Here it means:** "**He frowned**…"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the surah opens with a single, very human facial movement — not a sin of words or deeds, but a **look**. And the Qur'an notices it. That is the first lesson: **the expression on your face, when someone weak comes to you, is recorded**. The surah is named after this word.
- **Grammar bite:** Form I past, third person masculine — and ⭐ ***third person*** is the point: *he* frowned, not *you* frowned. The courtesy is in the pronoun.
- **Why this exact word:** *ʿabasa* is the smallest unit of rejection — a face tightening. Allah corrects even **that**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ʿAbbās* = the lion, the one who frowns.** The surah begins with **one frown** — and ends (v.38–41) with **faces**.

**2. تَوَلَّىٰ — *tawallā*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** the "**turning / facing / being near**" family — *wallā* = to turn; *tawallā* (Form V) = to **turn away deliberately**, to turn your back; and the same root gives *walī* (a close friend — the one you turn **toward**).
- **Here it means:** "…and **turned away**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ two verbs, two stages: the **face** tightened (*ʿabasa*), then the **body** turned (*tawallā*). And remember this verb from Qiyāmah 75:32 — *kadhdhaba wa tawallā* — used there for the arrogant **denier**. Here the same verb describes a **momentary** turning by the best of creation, which is **corrected at once**. The word is the same; the person, and the outcome, could not be more different.
- **Grammar bite:** Form V (تَفَعَّلَ) past; weak-final root (w-l-y), so it ends in *alif maqṣūrah* — carrying the **-ā rhyme** of v.1–10.
- **Why this exact word:** *tawallā* names a **deliberate** turning — attention moved elsewhere on purpose.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***walī* = the friend you turn TOWARD; *tawallā* = turning AWAY.** Same root, opposite directions.

**3. الْأَعْمَىٰ — *al-aʿmā*** · v.2
- **Root feeling:** the "**blindness**" family — *ʿamā* = blindness; *aʿmā* = a blind person; *ʿamiya* = to be blind.
- **Here it means:** "…because **the blind man** came to him."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ he is not named — he is described by his **disability**, and that is exactly the point: the man who **couldn't see** who else was in the room, couldn't read the social situation, simply **came** — because he wanted to learn. The surah will honour his eagerness above every visible status in the gathering.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***afʿal* is the pattern for physical traits and colours** — *aʿmā* (blind), *aṣamm* (deaf), *abkam* (mute), *aḥmar* (red), *abtar* (cut off, Kawthar 108:3). *An jāʾahu* = "**because** he came to him."
- **Why this exact word:** naming him by blindness makes the moral **visible**: worth is not in what people can see.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***afʿal* = traits & colours** (*aʿmā, aṣamm, aḥmar*). The **blind** man saw what mattered — he came to learn.

**4. وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ — *wa mā yudrīka*** · v.3
- **Root feeling:** the "**knowing by discovery**" family — *darā* = to come to know; *adrā* = to **make** someone know (Form IV); *dirāyah* = **know-how**, practical understanding.
- **Here it means:** "And **what would make you know**…?"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ ⭐ **a rule you will use for the rest of the juzʾ.** With the **past** tense — *wa mā **adrāka*** — the Qur'an asks and then **answers** (Infiṭār 82:17–19, Qāriʿah 101:10–11). With the **present** — *wa mā **yudrīka*** — it asks and **gives no answer**: the thing is **unknowable to you**. Here: you could not know whether this blind man would be purified — **so don't judge by appearances.**
- **Grammar bite:** Form IV present (*yudrī*) + *-ka* (you). And ⭐ here the address **switches to the second person** — *iltifāt*: after v.1–2's "he," the surah now turns and speaks to him directly.
- **Why this exact word:** the present tense keeps the question **open** — humility before the unseen.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***mā ADRĀKA* (past) → an answer follows. *mā YUDRĪKA* (present) → no answer.** One vowel and a letter apart.

**5. لَعَلَّهُ — *laʿallahu*** · v.3
- **Root feeling:** a particle of **hope** — "perhaps," "it may be that" (for something **possible**).
- **Here it means:** "…**perhaps he** would purify himself."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ *laʿalla* opens a **door of possibility**: this man, whom you turned from, **might** be the one who grows. The whole correction lives in this one word — **you never know who is about to bloom**.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***laʿalla* (possible hope) vs. *layta* (impossible wish)** — compare Nabaʾ 78:40, *yā **laytanī** kuntu turābā*. Both "take" a noun or pronoun in the accusative (*laʿalla-hu*).
- **Why this exact word:** *laʿalla* sets the tone of the whole passage: **hope for the seeker**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***laʿalla* = "maybe" (possible). *layta* = "if only" (impossible).** The blind man got a ***laʿalla***; the denier in Nabaʾ got a ***layta***.

**6. يَزَّكَّىٰ — *yazzakkā*** · v.3 (and v.7)
- **Root feeling:** the "**purifying and growing**" family — *zakāh* = the purifying charity; *zakiyy* = pure. ⭐ This root means **purity and growth at once**: removing what's rotten lets a thing **grow**.
- **Here it means:** "…perhaps he would **purify himself**." (v.7: "And it is not on you if he does **not** purify himself.")
- **Word magic:** ⭐ remember Nāziʿāt 79:18 — Mūsā was sent to ask **Pharaoh**, gently, *hal laka ilā an **tazakkā*** — "would you like to purify yourself?" Pharaoh was **offered** purification and refused. Here a blind man **comes looking for it**. **The tyrant was invited and declined; the blind man arrived uninvited and eager.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form V with assimilation:** *yatazakkā* → ***yazzakkā*** — the *tāʾ* melts into the *zāy* and **doubles** it. ⚠️ Don't look for a root *z-z-k*. (Same trick: Muzzammil 73:1, Muddaththir 74:1.)
- **Why this exact word:** the reflexive form means he would **purify himself** — his own effort, his own growth.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***yatazakkā → yazzakkā.*** The ت **melts** and doubles the ز. **Pharaoh was offered it (79:18); the blind man came for it.**

**7. يَذَّكَّرُ / الذِّكْرَىٰ — *yadhdhakkaru / adh-dhikrā*** · v.4
- **Root feeling:** the "**remembering / being reminded**" family — *dhakara* = to remember, to mention; *dhikr* = remembrance; *dhikrā* = a **reminder**; *tadhkirah* = a **ticket**, a reminder note (modern Arabic!).
- **Here it means:** "**or be reminded**, and **the reminder** would benefit him."
- **Word magic:** two hopes for the blind man: **purification** (v.3) or, at the very least, **a reminder that helps**. Even the smaller outcome is worth your attention. And the *dhikr* root now starts its run through this surah: *adh-dhikrā* (v.4) → *tadhkirah* (v.11) → *dhakarah* (v.12).
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ another **assimilated Form V**: *yatadhakkaru* → ***yadhdhakkaru*** — the ت melts into the ذ. *Fa-tanfaʿahu* = subjunctive after *fa-* answering the hope of *laʿalla*.
- **Why this exact word:** *dhikrā* means the benefit is **remembering what you already half-know** — the message lands in a ready heart.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***tadhkirah* = a ticket** — a little note that **reminds** you where you're going. ***yatadhakkaru → yadhdhakkaru***: the same melting rule as v.3.

**8. اسْتَغْنَىٰ — *istaghnā*** · v.5
- **Root feeling:** the "**sufficiency / richness**" family — *ghaniyy* = rich, self-sufficient; *ghinā* = wealth; *aghnā* = to make sufficient.
- **Here it means:** "As for **the one who considered himself free of need**…"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ ⭐ **Form X turns wealth into an attitude.** He wasn't just rich — he ***istaghnā***: he **regarded himself** as needing nothing, not even guidance. That is the root of all arrogance (ʿAlaq 96:7: *an raʾāhu'staghnā* — man transgresses "because he sees himself **self-sufficient**"). And the surah **answers this word at the end**: v.37 — *shaʾnun **yughnīh*** — on that Day everyone will have an affair that **suffices** him, leaving room for nobody else. **The man who felt he needed no one will find no one has room for him.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form X (اِسْتَفْعَلَ) = seeing oneself as / seeking** — *istaghnā* = to consider oneself rich. Contrast *istaghfara* = to **seek** forgiveness: same form, opposite souls.
- **Why this exact word:** *istaghnā* names the chief's real problem: not wealth, but **self-sufficiency**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***istaghnā* = "I need nothing."** It's the disease; v.37's ***yughnīh*** is its echo on the Day. Same root.

**9. تَصَدَّىٰ — *taṣaddā*** · v.6
- **Root feeling:** the "**turning to face / attending to**" family — *taṣaddā lahu* = to turn toward someone and **give him your full attention**; *ṣadā* = an **echo** (the sound that turns back to face you); modern *taṣaddī* = to **take on**, to confront.
- **Here it means:** "…**to him you give your attention**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ a precise reversal of v.1. To the blind man: *tawallā* — turned **away**. To the self-sufficient chief: *taṣaddā* — turned **toward**, eagerly. **The same body, two directions, decided by status.** The surah makes you see it.
- **Grammar bite:** Form V past/present (*taṣaddā* ← *tataṣaddā*, one tāʾ dropped); *fa-anta lahu* — "**you**, to **him**" — both pronouns fronted for emphasis.
- **Why this exact word:** *taṣaddā* describes **eager attending** — the energy that should have gone to the seeker.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ṣadā* = an echo** — a sound that **turns to face you**. *Taṣaddā* = turning to face someone. v.1 turned **away** from the blind man; v.6 turned **toward** the chief.

**10. يَسْعَىٰ — *yasʿā*** · v.8
- **Root feeling:** the "**striving / hurrying**" family — ⭐ the **same root as *Saʿī***, the walk between Ṣafā and Marwah; *saʿy* = effort.
- **Here it means:** "And as for the one who **came to you striving**…"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ remember Nāziʿāt 79:22 — Pharaoh *adbara **yasʿā*** — "turned his back, **hurrying**" away from the truth. Here the blind man came ***yasʿā*** — hurrying **toward** it, without even being able to see the road. **Same verb, opposite directions** — and a blind man's *saʿy* toward guidance outweighs a king's *saʿy* away from it.
- **Grammar bite:** *ḥāl* (state): "came **while striving**." Weak-final present, carrying the -ā rhyme.
- **Why this exact word:** *saʿy* is **effort** — he didn't just arrive; he **made the trip**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***Saʿī* root.** Pharaoh **ran from** the truth (79:22, *yasʿā*); the blind man **ran to** it (80:8, *yasʿā*).

**11. يَخْشَىٰ — *yakhshā*** · v.9
- **Root feeling:** the "**awe-filled fear**" family — *khashyah* = fear that comes from **knowing** something's greatness (compare *khawf*, plain fear).
- **Here it means:** "…**while he is in awe** [of Allah]."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ this is the **filter** you met three times in Nāziʿāt (79:19, 26, 45): the lesson, the warning, the guidance — all reach **whoever has *khashyah***. The blind man **had it**. That is why he came. The chief, *istaghnā*, had none.
- **Grammar bite:** *wa huwa yakhshā* — a **ḥāl clause** introduced by *wa*: "**and he** [was] fearing." Nominal sentence describing his state on arrival.
- **Why this exact word:** *khashyah* explains his eagerness: he came **because he knew what was at stake**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***khashyah* = awe from knowing.** It's the ticket the message checks at the door — **the blind man had it**.

**12. تَلَهَّىٰ — *talahhā*** · v.10
- **Root feeling:** the "**distraction / amusement**" family — *lahw* = amusement, idle play; *alhā* = to distract (Takāthur 102:1 — *alhākumu't-takāthur*, "rivalry **distracted** you"); *malhā* = an **amusement hall**.
- **Here it means:** "…**from him you were distracted**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the strongest word in the correction — and still gentle. Even an attempt to bring **chiefs** to Islam became, in that moment, a ***lahw*** — a distraction — because it pulled attention from the man **in front of you who wanted to learn**. **Even good work is a distraction when it pushes aside the seeker.**
- **Grammar bite:** Form V (*talahhā* ← *tatalahhā*, one tāʾ dropped); *fa-anta ʿanhu* — "**you**, **from** him" — the preposition *ʿan* (away from) completes the picture: v.6 *lahu* (toward him), v.10 *ʿanhu* (away from him).
- **Why this exact word:** *lahw* reframes "busy with important people" as **distracted**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***malhā* = an amusement hall.** v.6 ***lahu taṣaddā*** (toward the chief) ↔ v.10 ***ʿanhu talahhā*** (away from the seeker). Two prepositions, one lesson.

## §2 — Where the Message Lives (v.11–16)

**13. كَلَّا إِنَّهَا تَذْكِرَةٌ — *kallā innahā tadhkirah*** · v.11
- **Root feelings:** *kallā* — "**No! Not so!**" — the stern particle of rebuke. *tadhkirah* — a **reminder** (modern: a **ticket**).
- **Here it means:** "**No! Indeed, it is a reminder.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ *kallā* closes the whole episode: **not like that.** Then the surah zooms out: this message is a ***tadhkirah*** — a reminder, offered to everyone, chased by no one. It does not need to court the powerful; **it is not a sales pitch**. Compare Insān 76:29 — *inna hādhihi tadhkirah* — the same declaration.
- **Grammar bite:** *innahā* — the *-hā* refers to the verses/the message (feminine). *Kallā* here is **rebuke** of what preceded.
- **Why this exact word:** *tadhkirah* — it reminds; it doesn't **beg**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **The message is a *tadhkirah* — a ticket, a reminder.** You don't chase people to hand them a ticket. **Kallā** — not like that.

**14. فَمَن شَاءَ ذَكَرَهُ — *fa-man shāʾa dhakarah*** · v.12
- **Root feelings:** *shāʾa* — the "**willing**" family (*mashīʾah* = will; *in shāʾ Allāh*). *dhakara* — the remembering root again.
- **Here it means:** "**So whoever wills will remember it.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the open door** — and the responsibility moves to **the listener**. The Prophet ﷺ is not to bend himself around the chief's mood; whoever **wants** it will take it. This phrase-shape returns across the juzʾ (Nabaʾ 78:39, Insān 76:29, Takwīr 81:28). **The door is open; nobody is dragged through it.**
- **Grammar bite:** *man* = "whoever" (conditional); *dhakarahu* — the *-hu* (it) refers to the reminder. Past tense for a general rule: "whoever wills, remembers."
- **Why this exact word:** *shāʾa* — guidance answers **willing**, not status.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***fa-man shāʾa*** = the **open door** — Nabaʾ 78:39, Insān 76:29, here. **Whoever wills.**

**15. صُحُفٍ — *ṣuḥuf*** · v.13
- **Root feeling:** the "**written page**" family — *ṣaḥīfah* = a page, a scroll; ⭐ modern Arabic: ***ṣaḥīfah* = a newspaper**, ***ṣaḥāfah* = journalism**, *ṣuḥufī* = a journalist; *muṣḥaf* = a bound copy of the Qur'an.
- **Here it means:** "[It is recorded] **in scrolls**…"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the message the chief ignored is already **written** — in scrolls far above any human gathering. Its dignity does not depend on who accepts it in Makkah. And *ṣuḥuf* returns across the juzʾ in three senses: revelation here, **your deeds** spread open (Takwīr 81:10), the **earliest scriptures** (Aʿlā 87:18–19).
- **Grammar bite:** genitive after *fī*; broken plural of *ṣaḥīfah*. Indefinite — "**scrolls** [beyond description]."
- **Why this exact word:** *ṣuḥuf* makes the message **documented** — permanent, independent of any audience.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ṣaḥīfah* = a newspaper; *muṣḥaf* = the bound Qur'an.** The message is **already printed** — in scrolls above the heavens.

**16. مُكَرَّمَةٍ — *mukarramah*** · v.13
- **Root feeling:** the "**honour / nobility / generosity**" family — *karam* = generosity; *karīm* = noble; ⭐ ***Makkah al-Mukarramah*** — "Makkah the **Honoured**."
- **Here it means:** "…scrolls **honoured**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the irony is perfect: the Prophet ﷺ turned toward the chiefs **to honour them**, hoping they'd honour the message. But the message is **already** *mukarramah* — honoured by Allah. **It needs no one's honour; it bestows it.** You'll see this root grow: *kirām* (v.16), *karīm* (Takwīr 81:19), *al-Karīm* (Infiṭār 82:6).
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form II passive participle (*mufaʿʿal*)** — *mukarramah* = "**made** noble, honoured." Feminine to agree with *ṣuḥuf*.
- **Why this exact word:** *mukarramah* — the scrolls are honoured **from above**, not by their readers.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Makkah al-Mukarramah** — same word. The Qur'an's scrolls are **mukarramah**: honoured already.

**17. مَرْفُوعَةٍ مُطَهَّرَةٍ — *marfūʿatin muṭahharah*** · v.14
- **Root feelings:** *marfūʿ* — the "**raising**" family (*rafaʿa* = to raise; ⭐ in grammar, ***marfūʿ*** = the **nominative** — the "raised" case!). *muṭahhar* — the "**purity**" family (*ṭahārah* = ritual purity; *ṭahūr* = purifying, Insān 76:21).
- **Here it means:** "**exalted, purified**."
- **Word magic:** three adjectives in two verses — **honoured, raised, purified**. ⭐ Raised **above** any human gathering; purified **from** any tampering, any devil's whisper. The chief's approval would have added **nothing**.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ two different shapes: ***marfūʿah* = Form I passive participle (*mafʿūl*)**; ***muṭahharah* = Form II passive participle (*mufaʿʿal*)**, like *mukarramah*. ⚠️ Don't lump all three into one pattern.
- **Why this exact word:** *marfūʿah* (height) + *muṭahharah* (cleanliness) — **out of reach, and untouched**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***marfūʿ* = the nominative case** — the "**raised**" word. The scrolls are **raised** and **purified** — beyond anyone's reach.

**18. سَفَرَةٍ — *safarah*** · v.15
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**uncovering / making clear**" family — *safara* = to uncover; *sāfir* = a **scribe** (one who makes things clear in writing), or an **envoy**; *sifr* = a book (*asfār* = great books, Jumuʿah 62:5); *safar* = **travel** (it uncovers a person's character); ⭐ modern ***safīr* = an ambassador**.
- **Here it means:** "**in the hands of scribes** / envoys" — the angels who carry and record revelation.
- **Word magic:** ⭐ one root, three jobs that all fit: **writing** (scribes), **carrying** (envoys between Allah and His messengers), **uncovering** (making the message plain). And hold onto it — the **same root** returns in **v.38**: *wujūhun yawmaʾidhin **musfirah*** — faces **shining**, uncovered like dawn. **The scribes uncover the message; the believers' faces are uncovered in light.**
- **Grammar bite:** *bi-aydī* = "by the **hands** of" — *aydī* is plural of *yad*; genitive after *bi-*. *Safarah* is a broken plural of *sāfir*.
- **Why this exact word:** *safarah* — the message travels through **ambassadors**, not salesmen.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***safīr* = an ambassador.** The angels are revelation's **ambassadors** — and the same root lights the faces in **v.38** (*musfirah*).

**19. كِرَامٍ بَرَرَةٍ — *kirāmin bararah*** · v.16
- **Root feelings:** *kirām* — plural of *karīm*: **noble**, generous. *bararah* — plural of *bārr*: **righteous, devoted** (*birr* = goodness; *birr al-wālidayn* = **devotion to parents**; *al-abrār* = the righteous, Insān 76:5).
- **Here it means:** "**noble and righteous**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the carriers are as worthy as the scrolls. **Noble** in rank, **righteous** in conduct. You'll meet the same adjective for the **recording** angels in Infiṭār 82:11 — *kirāman kātibīn*. **Those who bring the message and those who record your response are both noble.**
- **Grammar bite:** two adjectives of *safarah*, genitive, broken plurals (*fiʿāl*, *faʿalah*).
- **Why this exact word:** *birr* — devoted goodness — is the quality the chiefs lacked; the angels **are made of it**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***birr al-wālidayn* = devotion to parents.** The scribes are ***kirām*** and ***bararah*** — **noble and devoted**.

## §3 — Man, From a Drop to a Grave (v.17–23)

**20. قُتِلَ الْإِنسَانُ — *qutila'l-insān*** · v.17
- **Root feeling:** the "**killing**" family — *qatala* = to kill — but here used as an **idiom**.
- **Here it means:** "**Confound man!**" — "may man perish!"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ ⚠️ **not a news report.** *Qutila* is an Arabic **imprecation**, an exclamation of outrage — like "**damn him!**" in English, which is not a theological statement. After the honoured scrolls (v.13–16), the surah turns to the ungrateful human and **explodes**. You'll meet the same idiom in Muddaththir 74:19–20 and Burūj 85:4.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ Form I **passive past** used as a **curse** — "may he be killed." *Al-insān* = mankind in general, not one person.
- **Why this exact word:** the most forceful exclamation available — for the most baffling thing: **ingratitude**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***qutila* = "Confound him!" — an idiom, NOT "he was killed."** ⚠️ Translate the feeling, not the letters.

**21. مَا أَكْفَرَهُ — *mā akfarah*** · v.17
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**covering up**" family — *kafara* = to **cover** (a farmer is a *kāfir* because he **covers** the seed with soil); *kufr* = covering the truth, and **ingratitude** (covering a favour); *kaffārah* = an **expiation** (it covers a sin).
- **Here it means:** "**How ungrateful he is!**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ ingratitude as **covering**: every gift listed in v.18–32 is **covered over** — buried under complaints and forgetfulness. And the root brackets the surah: *mā **akfarah*** (v.17) → ***al-kafarah*** (v.42, the very last verse). **The ingratitude of v.17 becomes the identity of v.42.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***mā afʿalahu* = the exclamation pattern** ("how … he is!") — *mā ajmalahu* = how beautiful he is! Some read it as a question — "what made him ungrateful?" — both are valid; the exclamation is the strongest.
- **Why this exact word:** *kufr* in its root sense — **covering a gift** — is the exact charge.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***kaffārah* = expiation (covering a sin).** *Mā akfarah* = "how he **covers over** every gift!" — and v.42 calls them ***al-kafarah***.

**22. نُطْفَةٍ — *nuṭfah*** · v.19
- **Root feeling:** the "**dripping**" family — *naṭafa* = to drip; *nuṭfah* = a **drop**, a tiny amount of fluid.
- **Here it means:** "From **a drop** He created him…"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ v.18 asks the question — *min ayyi shayʾin khalaqah?* ("From **what** did He create him?") — and v.19 answers with the smallest, humblest thing: **a drop**. The arrogant man of v.17 (and the self-sufficient chief of v.5) began as **a drip**. You'll find this origin across the juzʾ: Qiyāmah 75:37, Insān 76:2, Mursalāt 77:20.
- **Grammar bite:** genitive after *min*; indefinite — "**a** drop," just a drop.
- **Why this exact word:** *nuṭfah* — the most deflating answer to "what are you made of?"
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **"From WHAT? — From a DROP."** Question in v.18, answer in v.19. *Istighnāʾ* begins as a **drip**.

**23. فَقَدَّرَهُ — *fa-qaddarah*** · v.19
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**precise measuring / decreeing**" family — *qadr* = measure, worth; *qadar* = decree; *qaddara* = to **measure out exactly**; *miqdār* = a quantity.
- **Here it means:** "…**then proportioned him** precisely."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ from a drop to a **measured design**: every limb, every stage, every capacity **apportioned**. And the *fa-* is immediate — the measuring **begins at once**. You met this root in Qiyāmah 75:4 (*qādirīn*), Insān 76:16 (*qaddarūhā*), Mursalāt 77:23 (*fa-qadarnā*). **The One who measured you from a drop can measure your deeds to the atom.**
- **Grammar bite:** Form II past + *-hu*; *fa-* = **sequence without delay**.
- **Why this exact word:** *qaddara* turns "creation" into **engineering** — exact specifications.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***miqdār* = a quantity.** A drop, then **measured to the exact quantity** — *fa-qaddarah*.

**24. السَّبِيلَ يَسَّرَهُ — *as-sabīla yassarah*** · v.20
- **Root feelings:** *sabīl* — a **way, a path** (*fī sabīli'llāh* = in Allah's way; *ibn as-sabīl* = the traveller). *yassara* — the "**ease**" family: *yusr* = ease; *yasīr* = easy; *taysīr* = **facilitation**.
- **Here it means:** "**Then the way — He made it easy** for him."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ two classical readings, both true: (a) He **eased his way out of the womb** — birth; (b) He **made the path of guidance easy** — the choice between good and evil laid open. And the object ***as-sabīla* is fronted** — "**the way**, He eased" — the path itself is the gift. This root runs through the juzʾ: Aʿlā 87:8, Layl 92:7 & 10, Sharḥ 94:5–6.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **fronted object** (*as-sabīla* before its verb) = emphasis. Form II past + *-hu*.
- **Why this exact word:** *yassara* — every stage from here is **made easy**; ingratitude has no excuse.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***taysīr* = facilitation.** He made **the way out (birth)** and **the way forward (guidance)** easy.

**25. أَمَاتَهُ فَأَقْبَرَهُ — *amātahu fa-aqbarah*** · v.21
- **Root feelings:** *amāta* — Form IV of *māta* (to die): to **cause to die**. *aqbara* — Form IV of the "**grave**" family (*qabr* = a grave; *maqbarah* = a cemetery): to **give someone a grave**.
- **Here it means:** "**Then He caused him to die, and gave him a grave.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ ⭐ **even burial is listed as a blessing.** Not left on the ground for scavengers — **given** a grave, covered, honoured. The verb is not *qabara* (to bury) but ***aqbara*** — "He **provided** him with a grave." Death is in the middle of a **list of favours** (v.19–22). And keep this word: Infiṭār 82:4 shows the graves **overturned**, ʿĀdiyāt 100:9 shows them **emptied**, Takāthur 102:2 shows you **visiting** them.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form IV (أَفْعَلَ) = to cause / to provide** — *amāta* (made to die), *aqbara* (provided a grave), *ansharah* (v.22, raised up). Three Form IVs in two verses.
- **Why this exact word:** *aqbara* reframes the grave as **care**, not abandonment.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***aqbara* = He GAVE him a grave** — a blessing in the list. Infiṭār (82:4) will **tip that grave over**.

**26. أَنشَرَهُ — *ansharah*** · v.22
- **Root feeling:** the "**spreading out / unfolding**" family — *nashara* = to spread, to unroll; ⭐ ***nashr* = publishing**; *nashrah* = a bulletin; *nushūr* = the **Resurrection** (people "spread out" from the graves).
- **Here it means:** "**Then, when He wills, He will raise him up.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the life-cycle closes: drop → measured → eased → died → buried → **unfolded** again. The root's picture is a **scroll being unrolled**: the person folded into the grave is **opened back out**. And *idhā shāʾa* — "**when He wills**": the timing is His alone. (Compare Mursalāt 77:3 — *an-nāshirāt nashrā*, those that spread out.)
- **Grammar bite:** Form IV past + *-hu*; *idhā shāʾa* — a conditional of time with the verb of **will**.
- **Why this exact word:** *ansharah* — resurrection as **unfolding**, the reverse of the burial just described.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***nashr* = publishing.** You'll be **published again** — unfolded from the grave like an unrolled scroll.

**27. لَمَّا يَقْضِ — *lammā yaqḍi*** · v.23
- **Root feeling:** the "**finishing / fulfilling / deciding**" family — *qaḍā* = to complete, to fulfil, to decide; *qāḍī* = a **judge** (one who settles); *qaḍāʾ* = making up a missed prayer.
- **Here it means:** "**No! He has not yet fulfilled** what He commanded him."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ after the whole list of favours (v.17–22), the verdict: *kallā* — **No!** — he **still** hasn't done what he was told. And *lammā* (not yet) holds a sliver of hope: **not yet** means the deadline hasn't passed. The door from v.12 is still open — but time is running.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***lammā* + jussive = "not yet"** (stronger than *lam*: it implies the expectation **still stands**). *Yaqḍi* is jussive — the long ī **shortens**. ⚠️ Contrast *lammā* + **past** = "when."
- **Why this exact word:** *qaḍā* = to **complete** — he started, perhaps, but never **finished** his duty.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***lammā* + jussive = NOT YET** (hope still open). ***lammā* + past = WHEN.** Same word, two jobs.

## §4 — Look at Your Plate (v.24–32)

**28. فَلْيَنظُرِ — *fal-yanẓur*** · v.24
- **Root feeling:** the "**looking / considering**" family — *naẓara* = to look; *naẓar* = **eyesight**; *naẓariyyah* = a **theory** (a way of looking!); *manẓar* = a view.
- **Here it means:** "**So let man look** at his food."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the argument moves from the womb to the **dinner plate**. Not "think about the cosmos" — just **look at your lunch**. Every meal is a chain of miracles (v.25–31) that you eat without noticing. You'll meet this exact opening again: Ṭāriq 86:5 — *fal-yanẓuri'l-insānu mimma khuliq* ("let man look at what he was **created** from").
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***lām al-amr* (the lām of command)** on a third-person verb: *fal-yanẓur* = "**let him** look." The verb goes **jussive**; it takes a kasra (*fal-yanẓuri*) to join the next word.
- **Why this exact word:** *naẓar* means **attentive looking** — study it, don't just see it.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***fal-yanẓur* = "LET him look"** (lām of command). ʿAbasa: look at your **food**. Ṭāriq: look at your **origin**.

**29. طَعَامِهِ — *ṭaʿāmih*** · v.24
- **Root feeling:** the "**food / tasting**" family — *ṭaʿām* = food; *ṭaʿm* = **taste**, flavour; *maṭʿam* = a **restaurant** (everyday Arabic!).
- **Here it means:** "…at **his food**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the ungrateful man (v.17) is invited to look at the thing he **touches three times a day** and never thinks about. The surah will now walk backwards through its production: water → soil → grain → fruit — **farm to table in eight verses**.
- **Grammar bite:** genitive after *ilā*, with *-hi* (his). *Ṭaʿāmihi* ends the verse — rhyming with the surah's sound pattern here.
- **Why this exact word:** *ṭaʿām* — the most universal thing a human shares: everyone eats.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***maṭʿam* = a restaurant.** The proof of resurrection is **on your plate**.

**30. صَبَبْنَا… صَبًّا — *ṣababnā… ṣabbā*** · v.25
- **Root feeling:** the "**pouring**" family — *ṣabba* = to pour; *maṣabb* = a **river mouth** (where water pours out); *inṣibāb* = pouring, gushing.
- **Here it means:** "**that We poured down the water in torrents**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the verb **and** its own noun — *ṣababnā… ṣabbā* — "We poured, a **pouring**!" Arabic's underline key. And the same verb returns, terrifyingly, in Fajr 89:13: *fa-ṣabba ʿalayhim rabbuka sawṭa ʿadhāb* — your Lord **poured** on them a whip of punishment. **Same verb: rain that gives life, a lash that ends it.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***annā*** (that We) explains *ṭaʿāmih* — "look at his food: [namely] **that We** poured…" ⭐ ***ṣabban*** = *mafʿūl muṭlaq* (cognate accusative). You saw four in Nāziʿāt 79:1–4.
- **Why this exact word:** *ṣabb* is **heavy**, deliberate pouring — rain as an act, not weather.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ṣababnā… ṣabbā* = "We POURED — a pouring!"** And Fajr 89:13: the same verb pours a **whip**.

**31. شَقَقْنَا… شَقًّا — *shaqaqnā… shaqqā*** · v.26
- **Root feeling:** the "**splitting / cleaving**" family — *shaqqa* = to split; *shiqq* = a half; ⭐ ***shaqqah* = an apartment** (a "split-off" section of a building!); *inshiqāq* = splitting apart (Inshiqāq 84:1).
- **Here it means:** "**then We split the earth** — a splitting."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ second cognate accusative in two verses — **pour, then split**. The picture is a seedling **cracking the soil open** from below. The same root splits the **sky** at the end (Inshiqāq 84:1 — *idha's-samāʾu'nshaqqat*). **The verb that brings up your food will one day bring down the heavens.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **a doubled verb** (*sh-q-q*): *shaqqa* normally merges its two qāfs, but with *-nā* (We) they **separate**: *shaqaqnā*. *Shaqqan* = cognate accusative.
- **Why this exact word:** *shaqq* — **force from below**: a tender shoot splits hard ground.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***shaqqah* = an apartment.** A seedling **splits** the earth like a key opening a flat — *shaqaqnā… shaqqā*.

**32. فَأَنبَتْنَا / حَبًّا — *fa-anbatnā / ḥabbā*** · v.27
- **Root feelings:** *anbata* — Form IV of the "**sprouting**" family: to **make grow** (*nabāt* = plants; *nabātī* = **vegetarian**). *ḥabb* — **grain**, seeds (*ḥabbah* = a single grain).
- **Here it means:** "**and caused grain to grow** in it."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the list of eight foods begins here — and every one is **caused** by Him (*fa-anbatnā*: We made it grow). You met this pair in Nabaʾ 78:15 — *li-nukhrija bihi ḥabban wa nabātā*. **Grain comes first because it is the base of every meal.**
- **Grammar bite:** Form IV past; ***fa-*** = immediately after the splitting. *Ḥabban* = object; *fīhā* = in it (the earth).
- **Why this exact word:** *anbata* — He doesn't just let things grow; He **grows** them.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***nabātī* = vegetarian.** Food item #1: ***ḥabb*** — the grain on which every plate is built.

**33. عِنَبًا / قَضْبًا — *ʿinaban / qaḍbā*** · v.28
- **Root feelings:** *ʿinab* — **grapes**. *qaḍb* — the "**cutting**" family: *qaḍaba* = to cut; *qaḍb* = **fresh green fodder** that is **cut again and again** and grows back (like alfalfa); *qaḍīb* = a rod, a cut branch.
- **Here it means:** "**and grapes, and fresh fodder**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ *qaḍb* is a gift with a built-in miracle: you **cut it, and it grows back** — again and again. A plant that rehearses **resurrection** every season. Grapes for you; *qaḍb* for your animals — the list keeps both in mind (v.32).
- **Grammar bite:** both accusative, joined by *wa* to *ḥabban* — all objects of *anbatnā*.
- **Why this exact word:** *qaḍb* names the fodder by its **regrowth** — cut, and back again.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***qaḍīb* = a rod (cut).** *Qaḍb* = fodder you **cut and cut** — and it keeps coming back.

**34. زَيْتُونًا / نَخْلًا — *zaytūnan / nakhlā*** · v.29
- **Root feelings:** *zaytūn* — the **olive** (⭐ ***zayt* = oil** — the olive is literally "the oil-thing"). *nakhl* — **date palms** (*nakhlah* = one palm).
- **Here it means:** "**and olives and date palms**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the two trees that fed the Arabs for millennia: **oil** and **dates** — fat and sugar, the energy of a whole civilisation. You'll meet the olive again under oath in Tīn 95:1 — *wa't-tīni wa'z-zaytūn*.
- **Grammar bite:** accusatives, continuing the list after *anbatnā*.
- **Why this exact word:** two **long-lived trees** after the seasonal crops — the list moves from annual to **ancient**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***zayt* = oil → *zaytūn* = the oil-fruit.** Olives and palms: the **two pillars** of the Arab table.

**35. حَدَائِقَ غُلْبًا — *ḥadāʾiqa ghulbā*** · v.30
- **Root feelings:** *ḥadāʾiq* — plural of *ḥadīqah*: a **walled garden** (*ḥadaqah* = the **pupil** of the eye, ringed by white). *ghulb* — plural of *ghalbāʾ*: **dense, thick-trunked** (*aghlab* = thick-necked).
- **Here it means:** "**and gardens dense with trees**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ remember Nabaʾ 78:32 — *ḥadāʾiqa wa aʿnābā*: walled gardens and grapes **in Paradise**. Here the **same word** describes gardens **in this world**, given to the ungrateful man. **The dunya's gardens are samples of the next one** — and he ate from them without noticing.
- **Grammar bite:** *ḥadāʾiqa* is a **diptote** (no tanwīn); *ghulban* its adjective.
- **Why this exact word:** *ghulb* — trees so **thick** they look like strong necks: abundance you can see.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ḥadāʾiq* in ʿAbasa 80:30 = this world. *ḥadāʾiq* in Nabaʾ 78:32 = Paradise.** Same word — a sample and the real thing.

**36. فَاكِهَةً / أَبًّا — *fākihatan / abbā*** · v.31
- **Root feelings:** *fākihah* — **fruit** (and *fukāhah* = **joking** — the same root of **delight**). *abb* — **pasture, grazing plants** for animals.
- **Here it means:** "**and fruit, and pasture**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the last pair, again split: **fruit for you, pasture for your animals**. A famous report has ʿUmar reciting this verse and wondering aloud what exactly *abb* is — then telling himself not to **over-strain** over a word whose purpose is clear. A lovely lesson for a vocabulary student: **know the point even when a detail is uncertain**.
- **Grammar bite:** accusatives; the eight foods are complete: *ḥabb, ʿinab, qaḍb, zaytūn, nakhl, ḥadāʾiq, fākihah, abb*.
- **Why this exact word:** *fākihah* — food for **pleasure**, not just survival; even delight is a gift.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⚠️ ***abb* (pasture) ≠ *ab* (father)** — and *abīh* (his **father**) is in this very surah (v.35)! ***Fākihah* = fruit; *fukāhah* = joking** — both are **delight**.

**37. مَتَاعًا — *matāʿan*** · v.32
- **Root feeling:** the "**temporary enjoyment / provision**" family — *matāʿ* = goods you use **for a while**; ⭐ modern ***amtiʿah* = luggage** — things you carry **temporarily**.
- **Here it means:** "**as provision** for you and for your livestock."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ ⭐ **this whole verse is word-for-word identical to Nāziʿāt 79:33** — *matāʿan lakum wa li-anʿāmikum*. Two consecutive surahs close their creation passages with **the same sentence**. And *matāʿ* carries an **expiry date**: even while listing gifts, it whispers **this doesn't last** — which leads straight into v.33.
- **Grammar bite:** *mafʿūl li-ajlih* (accusative of purpose): "[all this] **as** provision for you."
- **Why this exact word:** *matāʿ* — temporary goods; the next verse arrives to **end** them.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***amtiʿah* = luggage.** And this verse **repeats Nāziʿāt 79:33 exactly**. Same luggage, next surah.

## §5 — The Deafener (v.33–37)

**38. الصَّاخَّةُ — *aṣ-ṣākhkhah*** · v.33
- **Root feeling:** the "**deafening blast**" family — *ṣakhkha* = to strike the ear with a sound so piercing it **deafens**.
- **Here it means:** "**But when the Deafening Blast comes**…"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the Day is named by **what it does to your ears**. After eight verses of quiet harvest, the surah **shatters** the calm with one word. And compare its twin in Nāziʿāt 79:34 — ***aṭ-ṭāmmah*** (the Overwhelming): **both are doubled roots, both are active participles used as names**. Nāziʿāt's Day **drowns** everything; ʿAbasa's Day **deafens** everyone.
- **Grammar bite:** *fa-idhā jāʾat* — "**then when** it comes"; *ṣākhkhah* is a *fāʿilah* active participle from a **doubled** root (ṣ-kh-kh), used as a **name**.
- **Why this exact word:** *ṣākhkhah* — so loud that a person **hears nothing else** — and so **listens to no one** (v.34).
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***aṭ-ṭāmmah* (79:34) drowns; *aṣ-ṣākhkhah* (80:33) deafens.** ⚠️ Twin names — don't swap them.

**39. يَفِرُّ الْمَرْءُ — *yafirru'l-marʾ*** · v.34
- **Root feelings:** *farra* — the "**fleeing in panic**" family (*firār* = escape, desertion; *mafarr* = an escape route — Qiyāmah 75:10: *ayna'l-mafarr?*). *al-marʾ* — **the person** (*imraʾah* = a woman; *muruwwah* = decency).
- **Here it means:** "**the Day a person flees** from his brother…"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the deafened person (v.33) now **runs** — and runs **from** the people he loved most. And Qiyāmah 75:10 already asked *ayna'l-mafarr?* — "where is the **escape**?" Here is the answer: people will flee, but **from each other**, not from the Day.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **a doubled verb** (*f-r-r*): *yafirru*, not *yafriru* — the two rāʾs merge with a shaddah. *Al-marʾ* = "the person" — one unnamed human (as in Nabaʾ 78:40).
- **Why this exact word:** *farra* — **panicked** flight, the opposite of the blind man's *yasʿā* (v.8) **toward** guidance.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***firār* = escape.** v.8: a blind man **hurried toward** the truth. v.34: everyone **flees** — from family.

**40. صَاحِبَتِهِ وَبَنِيهِ — *ṣāḥibatihi wa banīh*** · v.36
- **Root feelings:** *ṣāḥibah* — feminine of *ṣāḥib*, a **companion** — here: **his wife**. *banīn* — **sons** (*ibn* = a son).
- **Here it means:** "…and from **his wife and his sons**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ ⭐ **read the order of v.34–36: brother → mother → father → wife → sons.** Classical scholars note it **climbs** toward the dearest: a brother, then the parents who raised you, then the spouse who shares your life, and finally **your children** — the ones you'd give your life for. **The list ends on the one you'd never imagine running from.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***banīh* = *banīna* + *-hi*** — the **nūn drops** in iḍāfah. And *ṣāḥibah* for "wife" — the wife as **companion**, the one always at your side.
- **Why this exact word:** *ṣāḥibah* stresses **companionship** — even the one beside you every day is left behind.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **brother → mother → father → wife → SONS** — climbing toward the dearest. ***banīh***: the nūn **drops** before a pronoun.

**41. شَأْنٌ يُغْنِيهِ — *shaʾnun yughnīh*** · v.37
- **Root feelings:** *shaʾn* — an **affair, a matter** (*fī shaʾnī* = my own business; *bi-shaʾn* = **regarding**). *yughnī* — the "**sufficiency**" family of *istaghnā* (v.5): *aghnā* = to **suffice**, to make someone need nothing else.
- **Here it means:** "every person among them, that Day, will have **an affair that occupies him completely**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ ⭐ **the surah's bookend.** In v.5, the chief ***istaghnā*** — felt he **needed no one**. In v.37, every person has a *shaʾn* that ***yughnīh*** — **suffices** him so completely that he has **no room for anyone else**. **The self-sufficiency he chose in this life becomes the loneliness he gets in the next.** Same root, exact inversion.
- **Grammar bite:** *li-kulli'mriʾin* = "**for every** person" — fronted for emphasis; *shaʾnun yughnīhi* = noun + descriptive verb clause.
- **Why this exact word:** *yughnīh* — the affair is **enough** — enough to fill a person entirely.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **v.5 *istaghnā* ("I need no one") → v.37 *yughnīh* (no one has room for you).** The root of v.5 comes back to collect.

## §6 — Two Kinds of Faces (v.38–42)

**42. مُسْفِرَةٌ — *musfirah*** · v.38
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**uncovering / shining forth**" family — *asfara* = to **shine**, to become bright (dawn unveiling itself — Muddaththir 74:34: *wa'ṣ-ṣubḥi idhā asfar*); the root of ***safarah*** (v.15, the scribes).
- **Here it means:** "**Faces, that Day, will be shining.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the surah began with **one face frowning** (v.1). Now: ***wujūhun*** — **faces**, plural, **shining** like dawn breaking. And the root links back to v.15: the ***safarah*** (scribes) **uncovered** the message; the believer's face is now **uncovered in light**. **The message they received shows on their faces.**
- **Grammar bite:** Form IV active participle, feminine (agreeing with *wujūh*, a broken plural treated as feminine). *Wujūhun* is **indefinite** — "**some** faces."
- **Why this exact word:** *musfirah* — light that comes **from within**, like dawn, not a lamp shone on it.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***asfara aṣ-ṣubḥ* = dawn broke.** v.1 one face **frowned**; v.38 faces **dawn**. And it's the root of ***safarah*** (v.15).

**43. ضَاحِكَةٌ مُسْتَبْشِرَةٌ — *ḍāḥikatun mustabshirah*** · v.39
- **Root feelings:** *ḍāḥik* — the "**laughing**" family (*ḍaḥika* = to laugh; *ḍiḥkah* = a laugh). *mustabshir* — the "**good news**" family: *bushrā* = glad tidings; and ⭐ ***bashrah* = the skin** — because **good news shows on your face** (*bashar* = a human, named for bare skin).
- **Here it means:** "**laughing, rejoicing at good news**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ three adjectives for one face: **shining** (v.38), **laughing**, **rejoicing**. And *mustabshirah* is the root of skin — the joy is **literally visible on the skin**. The frown of v.1 was a face **closing**; these are faces **opening**.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***mustabshir* = Form X** participle — *istabshara* = to **receive** good news with joy. Same form as *istaghnā* (v.5): Form X can be **arrogance** — or **joy**.
- **Why this exact word:** *istibshār* — the joy of **receiving** news, not producing it: they were **told** something wonderful.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***bashrah* = skin → *bushrā* = good news** (it shows on your skin). **Form X twice in this surah: *istaghnā* (v.5, arrogance) and *mustabshirah* (v.39, joy).**

**44. غَبَرَةٌ — *ghabarah*** · v.40
- **Root feeling:** the "**dust**" family — *ghubār* = **dust** (everyday Arabic: the dust on your car!); *aghbar* = dust-coloured.
- **Here it means:** "**And faces, that Day, will have dust upon them**…"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the mirror image of v.38–39: instead of light, **dust** — a dull layer **settled** on the face, like a road no one has walked in years. The faces that turned away from the message now look like **neglected ground**.
- **Grammar bite:** *ʿalayhā ghabarah* = "**upon them** [is] dust" — a nominal sentence with the prepositional phrase **fronted**.
- **Why this exact word:** *ghabarah* — dust is what **covers** what has been abandoned.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ghubār* = dust.** v.38 faces of **dawn**; v.40 faces of **dust**.

**45. تَرْهَقُهَا قَتَرَةٌ — *tarhaquhā qatarah*** · v.41
- **Root feelings:** *rahiqa* — the "**overtaking / covering with a burden**" family (*murhaq* = **exhausted**; *murāhiq* = an **adolescent** — on the edge of being "overtaken" by adulthood). *qatarah* — the "**smoky darkness**" family: *qatar* = the dark haze of smoke and soot.
- **Here it means:** "**darkness overtakes them**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ two layers: **dust** settles (v.40), then **darkness** rises over it (v.41) — like soot on a dusty wall. Dust is dullness; *qatarah* is **blackness**. And *tarhaqu* means it **covers them as a burden** — the face carries its own darkness. You'll meet the *rahaq* root again: Jinn 72:6, 13 and Muddaththir 74:17.
- **Grammar bite:** present tense (*tarhaqu*) + *-hā* (them, the faces); *qatarah* is the **subject** — placed **after** its verb and object.
- **Why this exact word:** *qatar* — not just dark, but **smoky** dark: the colour of something burned.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***murhaq* = exhausted.** **Dust (v.40), then soot (v.41)** — the darkness **overtakes** them.

**46. الْكَفَرَةُ الْفَجَرَةُ — *al-kafaratu'l-fajarah*** · v.42
- **Root feelings:** *kafarah* — plural of *kāfir*: **those who covered up** the truth (the root of v.17's *mā akfarah*). *fajarah* — plural of *fājir*: ⭐ the "**bursting open**" family — *fajr* = the **dawn** that bursts through the night; *fājir* = one who **bursts past all limits**.
- **Here it means:** "**Those are the disbelievers, the wicked.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ ⭐ a two-word diagnosis — **inner and outer**. *Kafarah*: they **covered** the truth **inside** (belief). *Fajarah*: they **burst** every boundary **outside** (conduct). And the surah ends where it began its argument: v.17's ***mā akfarah*** (how ungrateful!) has become, by v.42, their **name**. ⚠️ And notice: ***fajr*** (dawn) shares this root — the same "bursting" that gives the world its morning gives the wicked their name.
- **Grammar bite:** *ulāʾika humu…* — demonstrative + **separating pronoun** (*hum*) + definite noun = "**those — they are** THE…" Total restriction. Both plurals are the ***faʿalah*** pattern (*kafarah, fajarah, bararah* — v.16!).
- **Why this exact word:** *faʿalah* plurals rhyme with ***bararah*** (v.16) — **the righteous scribes** and **the wicked** sound alike and mean opposites.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***kafarah* (covering inside) + *fajarah* (bursting outside).** And they rhyme with ***bararah*** (v.16): same shape, opposite people.

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐ **وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ (present, v.3)** → **no answer follows** — unknowable to you. ⚠️ Contrast **وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ (past)** → an answer **does** follow. One letter and a vowel apart.
2. ⭐⭐ **يَزَّكَّىٰ (v.3) and يَذَّكَّرُ (v.4)** are **assimilated Form V** — *yatazakkā*, *yatadhakkaru*. ⚠️ There is no root *z-z-k* or *dh-dh-k*.
3. ⭐ **The address switches at v.3** — *ʿabasa… jāʾahu* (**he**) → *yudrīka* (**you**). ⚠️ Don't miss the *iltifāt*: the courtesy is in the grammar.
4. ⭐ **لَعَلَّ (v.3) = a possible hope; لَيْتَ (Nabaʾ 78:40) = an impossible wish.** ⚠️ Don't translate both as "if only."
5. ⭐ **قُتِلَ (v.17) is an imprecation — "Confound him!"** ⚠️ Not "he was killed."
6. ⭐ **لَمَّا يَقْضِ (v.23) — *lammā* + jussive = "not yet."** ⚠️ *Lammā* + past = "when."
7. ⭐ **أَبًّا (v.31, pasture) ≠ أَبِيهِ (v.35, his father).** Same surah, one shaddah apart.
8. ⭐ **سَفَرَةٍ (v.15, scribes) and مُسْفِرَةٌ (v.38, shining)** — same root *s-f-r*. ⚠️ Don't read *safarah* as "travellers."
9. ⭐ **اسْتَغْنَىٰ (v.5) ↔ يُغْنِيهِ (v.37)** — same root: the self-sufficient man, and the Day when everyone is too occupied for anyone else.
10. ⭐ **الصَّاخَّةُ (80:33, deafens) vs. الطَّامَّةُ (79:34, overwhelms)** — twin doubled-root names for the Day. ⚠️ Easy to swap.
11. ⭐ **مَرْفُوعَةٍ is *mafʿūl* (Form I); مُكَرَّمَةٍ and مُطَهَّرَةٍ are *mufaʿʿal* (Form II).** ⚠️ Three adjectives, two patterns.
