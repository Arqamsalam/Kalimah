# Surah Aḍ-Ḍuḥā (93) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–11)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**Three gifts are listed — then three commands, and they map onto each other in exact order.**

| **What He did for you (v.6–8)** | **What you must now do (v.9–11)** |
|---|---|
| *a-lam yajidka **yatīman** fa-**āwā*** | *fa-ammā'l-**yatīma** fa-lā **taqhar*** |
| Found you **an orphan** → **sheltered** you | The **orphan** → do not **crush** him |
| *wa wajadaka **ḍāllan** fa-**hadā*** | *wa ammā's-**sāʾila** fa-lā **tanhar*** |
| Found you **lost** → **guided** you | The **asker** → do not **rebuff** him |
| *wa wajadaka **ʿāʾilan** fa-**aghnā*** | *wa ammā bi-**niʿmati** rabbika fa-**ḥaddith*** |
| Found you **needy** → **enriched** you | His **favour** → **speak of it** |

⭐ **Three finds, three fixes, three duties.** ⭐ **You were an orphan — so don't crush one. You were lost and asked — so don't rebuff an asker. You were poor and were enriched — so talk about the favour.**

⭐ **Every command is generated by a corresponding gift.** ⭐ **The ethics aren't arbitrary; they're your own biography, handed back to you as instructions.**

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐⭐ **What was done FOR you becomes what you must do FOR others.** ⭐ **Three for three. That's the whole surah.**

---

## The Big Picture

*⭐ Context worth knowing: this surah came after a pause in revelation, when the Prophet ﷺ was distressed and mockers claimed his Lord had abandoned him. ⭐ **The surah's entire job is comfort — and it comforts by pointing at his own past.***

1. **Two oaths** (v.1–2) — ⭐ **the morning brightness and the night when it settles.**
2. **The denial** (v.3) — ⭐ *He has not forsaken you, nor does He detest you.*
3. **The promise** (v.4–5) — ⭐ *the last is better than the first… and you will be pleased.*
4. **Three finds** (v.6–8) — ⭐ **orphan, lost, needy.**
5. **Three commands** (v.9–11).

## §1 — Two Oaths, and a Denial (v.1–3)

**1. وَالضُّحَىٰ — *wa'ḍ-ḍuḥā*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**forenoon brightness**" family — ***ḍuḥā*** = **mid-morning**, when the sun is well up and everything is fully, warmly lit. ⭐ **You know it as *Ṣalāt aḍ-Ḍuḥā*.**
- **Here it means:** "**By the morning brightness.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **FOURTH appearance of this word in your study — and now it's a surah title:**
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:29** — *wa akhraja **ḍuḥāhā*** — creation's **daily gift of light**
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:46** — *illā ʿashiyyatan aw **ḍuḥāhā*** — ⭐ **your ENTIRE LIFE, measured as one morning**
  - **Shams 91:1** — *wa'sh-shamsi wa **ḍuḥāhā*** — the sun's brightness
  - **Ḍuḥā 93:1** — the surah's name
  
  ⭐ **And the choice is perfectly targeted at the situation.** ⭐ **The Prophet ﷺ was being told his Lord had abandoned him — so the surah swears by ***the moment the light is fullest***.** ⭐ **Not dawn (which is a beginning), not noon — ⭐ ***ḍuḥā*, the settled, warm, unmistakable brightness.**
  
  ⭐ **The oath IS the argument: look at the light. Does that look like abandonment?**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **the *wāw* is *wāw al-qasam*** (the "by" of oaths), so the noun is **genitive**. ⭐ **Note: every verse in this surah ends in *-ā* — the same sustained rhyme as Aʿlā (87) and Layl (92).**
- **Why this word:** *ḍuḥā* is ⭐ **light at its most reassuring** — exactly what a distressed person needs sworn to them.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **FOURTH appearance: 79:29 (daily gift), 79:46 (your whole life!), 91:1, 93:1.** ⭐ **He swears by the FULLEST light to a man who felt left in the dark.**

**2. إِذَا سَجَىٰ — *idhā sajā*** · v.2
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**becoming still, calm, settled**" family — *sajā* = **to grow quiet and motionless**; ⭐ **used of a **calm, windless sea** and of ⭐ **an eye that becomes still and languid**. Related: *sājin* = tranquil.
- **Here it means:** "**and by the night when it settles into stillness.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the word choice here is a masterpiece of emotional precision, and it's easy to miss.**
  
  ⭐ **Every other surah you've studied describes night as something ACTIVE:**
  - **Takwīr 81:17** — *idhā **ʿasʿas*** — the night **CREEPING**
  - **Fajr 89:4** — *idhā **yasr*** — the night **TRAVELLING/departing**
  - **Shams 91:4** — *idhā **yaghshāhā*** — the night **COVERING**
  - **Layl 92:1** — *idhā **yaghshā*** — the night **COVERING**
  - ⭐ **Ḍuḥā 93:2 — *idhā **sajā*** — the night **GOING STILL**
  
  ⭐⭐ **This is the ONLY one where the night just... stops.** ⭐ **No creeping, no covering, no journeying. **Stillness.**
  
  ⭐ **And that's the entire point.** ⭐ **The Prophet ﷺ was distressed by a *silence* — revelation had paused.** ⭐ **So the oath is by ***a stillness that is not abandonment***. ⭐ **The night going quiet doesn't mean the morning left. ⭐ **Silence is not absence.**
  
  ⭐ **Two oaths: LIGHT (v.1) and QUIET (v.2).** ⭐ **Both are things the Prophet ﷺ needed reassuring about.**
- **Grammar bite:** weak-final past verb (س-ج-و), carrying the rhyme. ⭐ ***Idhā* + past = a habitual, recurring state.**
- **Why this word:** *sajā* over any of night's other verbs — ⭐ **because the complaint was about **silence**, and this word makes silence peaceful.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Sajā* = a CALM, WINDLESS SEA — total stillness.** ⭐ **Five surahs describe night; this is the ONLY one where it just goes QUIET.** ⭐⭐ **He was upset by a silence — so the oath is by a silence that isn't abandonment.**

**3. مَا وَدَّعَكَ ... وَمَا قَلَىٰ — *mā waddaʿaka… wa mā qalā*** · v.3
- **Root feelings:** *waddaʿa* — ⭐ the "**leaving / bidding farewell**" family — ***tawdīʿ* = A FAREWELL*** (⭐ ***Ḥajjat al-Wadāʿ* — the Farewell Pilgrimage!**); Form II = to take leave of someone. *qalā* — ⭐ the "**intense dislike / detesting**" family — *qilā* = severe hatred, ⭐ **the kind of loathing that makes you cast someone off.**
- **Here it means:** "**Your Lord has not taken leave of you, nor does He detest [you].**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **two extraordinary things happen in this short verse.**
  
  ⭐ **FIRST — *waddaʿa* is a *FAREWELL* word.** ⭐ **You know it from ***Ḥajjat al-Wadāʿ***, the Farewell Pilgrimage, and ***ṭawāf al-wadāʿ***, the Farewell Circuit.** ⭐ **A *wadāʿ* is a **deliberate, ceremonial goodbye** — you don't *waddaʿ* someone by accident.** ⭐ **So the denial is precise: **He didn't say goodbye to you.** The silence wasn't a departure.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **SECOND — and this is exquisite — look at what's MISSING.** ⭐ **The verse says *mā waddaʿa-**KA*** ("has not forsaken **YOU**") — ⭐ **but then just *wa mā **qalā*** — ⭐ **"nor does He detest" — WITH NO "YOU" ATTACHED.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **The pronoun is deliberately deleted.** ⭐ **Classical scholars note this as a mercy of the highest order: ⭐ **the word "detest" is not allowed to sit next to "you" in the same breath.** ⭐ **Even grammatically, He won't place that word against His Prophet ﷺ.**
  
  ⭐ **(The rhyme also required it — and that's the beauty: the sound and the mercy arrive together.)**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **مَا + past tense = past negation.** ⚠️ ⭐ **Note the dropped object pronoun on *qalā*** — ⭐ **a known and much-discussed omission.**
- **Why this word:** *waddaʿa* (a ceremonial farewell) rather than *taraka* (left) — ⭐ **because a farewell is *intentional*, and that's what he feared.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Ḥajjat al-Wadāʿ* = the FAREWELL Pilgrimage.** Same root. ⭐ **"He didn't say goodbye to you."** ⭐⭐ **And "detest" has NO "you" attached — the pronoun is dropped so the word never touches him.**

## §2 — The Promise (v.4–5)

**4. وَلَلْآخِرَةُ خَيْرٌ لَّكَ مِنَ الْأُولَىٰ — *wa la'l-ākhiratu khayrun laka mina'l-ūlā*** · v.4
- **Root feelings:** *ākhirah* — **the last / the Hereafter**. *ūlā* — **the first / this world**.
- **Here it means:** "**And the last is better for you than the first.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **you own this pairing from TWO surahs already:**
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:25** — *nakāla'l-**ākhirati wa'l-ūlā***
  - **Layl 92:13** — *wa inna lanā la'l-**ākhirata wa'l-ūlā***
  - **Aʿlā 87:17** — *wa'l-**ākhiratu khayrun** wa abqā* ⭐ **("better and more lasting")**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And Aʿlā 87:17 is nearly the same sentence** — ⭐ **but with a crucial addition here: *khayrun **LAKA*** — "better **FOR YOU**."**
  
  ⭐ **Aʿlā stated a general truth: the Hereafter is better.** ⭐ **Ḍuḥā makes it personal: **better for YOU, specifically.**
  
  ⭐ **And there's a second, gentler reading widely held:** ⭐ ***al-ākhirah* can mean "what comes later" and *al-ūlā* "what came before"** — ⭐ **i.e. *every later stage of your life will be better than the earlier one*.** ⭐ **To a man in the middle of a hard season, that's the exact comfort needed: **it gets better from here.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***wa* + emphatic *lām*** on *al-ākhiratu*. ⭐ ***khayrun*** is a **comparative** used without *afʿal* form (like *khayr* and *sharr* — ⚠️ **irregular comparatives, worth knowing**).
- **Why this word:** adding ***laka*** ("for you") turns a doctrine into a ⭐ **personal promise.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **Aʿlā 87:17 said "better and more lasting." Ḍuḥā 93:4 adds *LAKA* — "better FOR YOU."** ⭐ **And a second reading: **every later part of your life beats the earlier part.**

**5. وَلَسَوْفَ يُعْطِيكَ رَبُّكَ فَتَرْضَىٰ — *wa la-sawfa yuʿṭīka rabbuka fa-tarḍā*** · v.5
- **Root feelings:** *yuʿṭī* — the "**giving**" family (*ʿaṭāʾ* = a gift — ⭐ **Nabaʾ 78:36**; *aʿṭā* — ⭐ **Layl 92:5**). *tarḍā* — the "**contentment**" family (*riḍā*).
- **Here it means:** "**And your Lord is going to give you, and you will be pleased.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **THE BEST CONSECUTIVE-SURAH LINK IN THE WHOLE JUZʾ.**
  
  ⭐ **Layl 92:21 (the final verse of the previous surah) — *wa **la-sawfa yarḍā*** ("and he **will surely be pleased**").
  ⭐ **Ḍuḥā 93:5 — *wa **la-sawfa** yuʿṭīka rabbuka fa-**tarḍā*** ("and your Lord will surely give you, **and you will be pleased**").
  
  ⭐⭐ **IDENTICAL construction (*wa la-sawfa*), IDENTICAL verb (*r-ḍ-ā*), BACK-TO-BACK SURAHS.**
  
  ⭐ **Layl ended by promising *the generous giver* he'd be pleased.** ⭐ **Ḍuḥā opens the same promise to *the Prophet ﷺ*.** ⭐ **Read them in sequence and it's one continuous sentence across a surah boundary.**
  
  ⭐ **And *yuʿṭī* connects to Layl 92:5** — *fa-ammā man ***aʿṭā*** ("as for he who **GAVE**"). ⭐⭐ **Layl: the man who GIVES. Ḍuḥā: the Lord who GIVES to him.** ⭐ **Same root, consecutive surahs, both directions.**
  
  ⭐ **And note ***tarḍā*** has **no object** — pleased with *what*? ⭐ **Not said. Same deliberate deletion as Layl 92:21.** ⭐ **As big as you can imagine.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***la-sawfa*** = emphatic *lām* + future particle = ⭐ **the strongest future affirmation in Arabic.** ⭐ **The *fa-* on *fa-tarḍā* = "**with the result that**."
- **Why this word:** leaving *tarḍā* **unspecified** is ⭐ **the most generous possible promise.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **Layl 92:21 ends *wa la-sawfa YARḌĀ*. Ḍuḥā 93:5 says *wa la-sawfa… fa-TARḌĀ*.** ⭐ **Back-to-back surahs, same construction.** ⭐ **And *aʿṭā* (Layl 92:5, the man gives) → *yuʿṭīka* (Ḍuḥā 93:5, the Lord gives).**

## §3 — Three Finds (v.6–8)

**6. أَلَمْ يَجِدْكَ يَتِيمًا فَآوَىٰ — *a-lam yajidka yatīman fa-āwā*** · v.6
- **Root feelings:** *yajid* — **to find**. *yatīm* — **an orphan** — ⭐ you own it from **Insān 76:8**, **Fajr 89:17**, **Balad 90:15**. *āwā* — ⭐ the "**taking shelter / giving refuge**" family — ***maʾwā* = a SHELTER/abode*** (⭐ **Nāziʿāt 79:39, 41!**); *awā* = to take shelter; *āwā* (Form IV) = **to GIVE shelter**.
- **Here it means:** "**Did He not find you an orphan and give you shelter?**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the *āwā* connection is the gem here, and it's a genuine payoff.**
  
  ⭐ **Nāziʿāt 79:39 — *fa-inna'l-jaḥīma hiya'l-**maʾwā*** ("Hellfire is **the shelter**").
  ⭐ **Nāziʿāt 79:41 — *fa-inna'l-jannata hiya'l-**maʾwā*** ("Paradise is **the shelter**").
  ⭐ **Ḍuḥā 93:6 — *fa-**āwā*** ("**He gave [you] shelter**").
  
  ⭐ **Nāziʿāt named the two eternal shelters. Ḍuḥā shows Him *personally sheltering* one orphan boy.** ⭐ **Same root: the cosmic destination, and the roof over a child's head.**
  
  ⭐ **And notice the structure of v.6–8: three rhetorical questions, all beginning with "did He not FIND you…"** ⭐ **The verb is *wajada* (to FIND) — ⭐ **which implies He came looking.**
  
  ⭐ **And *a-lam* is a question expecting "yes."** ⭐ **He's not informing the Prophet ﷺ of anything new — ⭐ **He's asking him to *remember his own life*.** ⭐ **The comfort is: your entire biography is the evidence.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **أَلَمْ + jussive** = a rhetorical question expecting agreement. ⭐ **Note *yajid*** has **dropped its final vowel** (jussive after *lam*). ⭐ ***Fa-*** = "**and so / and then**," showing the response was immediate.
- **Why this word:** *āwā* (Form IV, **to give shelter**) — ⭐ **the causative makes God the active shelterer**, not a passive provider.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Maʾwā* = SHELTER** — ⭐ **Nāziʿāt 79:39 & 41 used it for HELL and PARADISE.** ⭐ **Here the same root shelters one orphan.** ⭐ **And "did He not FIND you" — ⭐ **He came looking.**

**7. وَوَجَدَكَ ضَالًّا فَهَدَىٰ — *wa wajadaka ḍāllan fa-hadā*** · v.7
- **Root feelings:** *ḍāll* — ⭐ the "**straying / being lost / not knowing the way**" family — *ḍalla* = to lose one's way; ⭐ ***ḍāllah* = A LOST CAMEL*** (an animal that has wandered off and needs finding!). *hadā* — the "**guiding**" family (*hudā*).
- **Here it means:** "**And He found you lost/unaware, and guided you.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **an important clarification, because this word is often misread.**
  
  ⭐ ***Ḍāll* here does NOT mean "misguided" in the sense of being on a false religion.*** ⭐ **The root's core image is ⭐ ***a lost camel* (*ḍāllah*)*** — **something wandering, looking for the way, not yet arrived.** ⭐ **Classical scholars explain it as **not yet knowing the details of the revelation and Sharīʿah** before prophethood — searching without a map.
  
  ⭐ **And the beautiful implication: ⭐ **a *ḍāllah* is something *valuable* that got separated — ⭐ **you don't search for worthless things.** ⭐ **He was sought because he mattered.**
  
  ⭐ **The *hudā* root you own from Aʿlā 87:3** (*qaddara **fa-hadā*** — "measured, then guided") and **Balad 90:10** (*wa **hadaynāhu**'n-najdayn* — "guided him to the two heights").
  
  ⭐ **Note the pattern across all three verses (v.6–8): a LACK, then *fa-* + the CURE.** ⭐ **Orphan → sheltered. Lost → guided. Needy → enriched.** ⭐ **Three problems, three immediate solutions.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***ḍāllan*** is an active participle (accusative, as *ḥāl* — the state he was found in). ⭐ **Note the doubled *lām* (*ḍ-l-l*) — a **doubled root**.**
- **Why this word:** *ḍāll* (a searching wanderer) rather than a word for error — ⭐ **it describes someone *looking*, which is what he was.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Ḍāllah* = A LOST CAMEL** — ⭐ **something VALUABLE that wandered off.** ⭐ **You don't search for worthless things.** ⭐ **Not "misguided" — **searching without a map yet.**

**8. وَوَجَدَكَ عَائِلًا فَأَغْنَىٰ — *wa wajadaka ʿāʾilan fa-aghnā*** · v.8
- **Root feelings:** *ʿāʾil* — ⭐ the "**having dependents / being in need**" family — *ʿāla* = **to have a family to support**, and hence **to be in need**; ⭐ ***ʿāʾilah* = A FAMILY*** in modern Arabic! *aghnā* — ⭐ **Form IV of the "richness" root** = **to make rich / suffice**.
- **Here it means:** "**And He found you in need, and enriched you.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **two hooks, and one of them completes a thread you've been tracking for surahs.**
  
  ⭐ **FIRST: *ʿāʾil* shares its root with ***ʿāʾilah*, a FAMILY**.** ⭐ **Because having dependents is what makes you *need*.** ⭐ **Poverty is named after having people to feed.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **SECOND — and this is the big one — ⭐ *aghnā* is the root of *istaghnā*, and this is its REDEMPTION.**
  
  ⭐ **Track this root across FOUR surahs:**
  - **ʿAbasa 80:5** — *mani **staghnā*** — the rich chief who **thought he needed nothing** (bad)
  - **ʿAbasa 80:37** — *shaʾnun **yughnīh*** — a concern that **leaves room for nothing** (his punishment)
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:7** — *lā **yughnī** min jūʿ* — food that **doesn't suffice** (bad)
  - **Layl 92:8** — *bakhila wa'**staghnā*** — the withholder who **needed nothing** (bad)
  - ⭐ **Ḍuḥā 93:8 — *fa-**aghnā*** — **HE enriched you** (good!)
  
  ⭐⭐ **Every previous appearance was negative — a delusion of self-sufficiency.** ⭐ **Here, for the first time, the root is used correctly: ⭐ **God is the one who makes rich; you are the one who is made rich.** ⭐ **The problem was never *ghinā* — it was **claiming it for yourself.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form IV** (*aghnā* = to **make** rich) — ⭐ **compare Form X *istaghnā* = to **consider yourself** rich.** ⭐ **Same root, and the form is the entire moral difference.**
- **Why this word:** *aghnā* with **God as the subject** ⭐ **corrects everything *istaghnā* got wrong.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***ʿĀʾilah* = A FAMILY** — poverty named after having mouths to feed. ⭐⭐ **And *aghnā* REDEEMS the root: ⭐ ʿAbasa 80:5 & Layl 92:8 = *istaghnā* ("I need nothing" — Form X, a delusion). ⭐ Ḍuḥā 93:8 = *aghnā* ("HE enriched you" — Form IV, the truth).** ⭐ **The form is the whole difference.**

## §4 — Three Commands (v.9–11)

**9. فَلَا تَقْهَرْ — *fa-lā taqhar*** · v.9
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**overpowering / subduing by force**" family — *qahara* = **to dominate, to crush, to overwhelm someone weaker**; ⭐ ***al-Qahhār* — "The Subduer," a Name of Allah***; *qahr* = coercion.
- **Here it means:** "**So as for the orphan — do not oppress [him].**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the verb is chosen with terrifying precision.**
  
  ⭐ ***Qahara* means to overpower someone *because you can*** — ⭐ **using your strength on someone who has none.** ⭐ **It's not "don't be unkind" — it's **don't use your power against the powerless**.
  
  ⭐ **And an orphan is defined by having no protector.** ⭐ **He's the easiest person in society to *qahar*.** ⭐ **That's exactly why the word is used.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And here's the weight of it: ⭐ ***al-Qahhār* is one of Allah's own Names.*** ⭐ **Overpowering-by-might is **His** attribute — ⭐ **and the Prophet ﷺ is told not to exercise it on an orphan.** ⭐ **The verse quietly says: that's not your role.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Fajr 89:17** — *bal lā **tukrimūna**'l-yatīm* ("you do not **HONOUR** the orphan"). ⭐ **Fajr: don't fail to honour him. Ḍuḥā: don't crush him.** ⭐ **Two surahs, floor and ceiling.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **لا الناهية (*lā* of prohibition) + jussive** — ⭐ **note *taqhar* has dropped its final vowel.** ⭐ **The *fa-ammā… fa-* structure is a "as for X… then Y" frame, used three times in v.9–11.**
- **Why this word:** *qahara* names ⭐ **the specific abuse a powerless person is vulnerable to.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Al-Qahhār* — "The Subduer," a NAME OF ALLAH.** ⭐ **Same root.** ⭐ **"Don't use your power on the powerless" — that's His role, not yours.** ⭐ **Compare Fajr 89:17: don't fail to HONOUR him.**

**10. فَلَا تَنْهَرْ — *fa-lā tanhar*** · v.10
- **Root feelings:** *tanhar* — ⭐ the "**scolding harshly / rebuffing / driving away with a shout**" family — *nahara* = **to snap at someone, to repel them roughly**. *sāʾil* — ⭐ **one who ASKS** (from *saʾala* — ⭐ **the same root as *yatasāʾalūn*, Nabaʾ 78:1!**) — ⭐ **means both "a beggar" AND "a questioner."**
- **Here it means:** "**And as for the one who asks — do not repel [him].**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the ambiguity of *sāʾil* is a feature, and both readings are classical.**
  
  ⭐ ***Sāʾil* = someone asking for MONEY (a beggar) OR someone asking for KNOWLEDGE (a questioner).*** ⭐ **The Qurʾān doesn't disambiguate — ⭐ **so the command covers both.** ⭐ **Don't snap at the beggar; don't snap at the student.**
  
  ⭐ **And it maps back onto v.7:** ⭐ **the Prophet ﷺ was *ḍāll* — **searching, not knowing** — ⭐ **and he was guided, not rebuffed.** ⭐ **So: do the same for the next person who's searching.**
  
  ⭐ **Note the *nahara* image: it's the verb for ⭐ **shooing someone away with a raised voice.** ⭐ **Not refusing — **refusing rudely**.** ⭐ **You may not have money to give. You must still not snap.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***lā* of prohibition + jussive.** ⭐ **Note the deliberate rhyme with v.9: *taqhar* / *tanhar* — ⭐ **two prohibitions, matched sounds.**
- **Why this word:** *nahara* targets ⭐ **the manner**, not the outcome — you're being told **how to say no**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Sāʾil* = a BEGGAR **and** a QUESTIONER** (same root as *yatasāʾalūn*, Nabaʾ 78:1). ⭐ **Both covered.** ⭐ **And *nahara* = **shooing someone away with a raised voice** — you may refuse, but never snap.** ⭐ **Maps to v.7: he was searching, and was guided, not rebuffed.**

**11. فَحَدِّثْ — *fa-ḥaddith*** · v.11
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**newness / relating news**" family — *ḥadatha* = to happen/be new; ⭐ ***ḥadīth* = (a) speech/narration and (b) NEW*** — ⭐ **and of course *al-Ḥadīth*, the Prophet's ﷺ narrations!** ⭐ **Form II *ḥaddatha* = to narrate, to tell about.**
- **Here it means:** "**And as for the favour of your Lord — speak of it.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the third command, and it's the one nobody expects.**
  
  ⭐ **You'd expect: "be grateful," or "don't boast."** ⭐ **Instead: ⭐ ***TALK ABOUT IT***.
  
  ⭐ **The verb is ***ḥaddith*** — ⭐ **the very root of ***ḥadīth*, a narration.** ⭐ **So: **narrate** the favour. Make it into speech others hear.**
  
  ⭐ **And why is that a *command*, not vanity?** ⭐ **Because of the two verses before it.** ⭐ **v.9: don't crush the orphan. v.10: don't rebuff the asker. ⭐ **v.11: TELL people what was done for you** — ⭐ **so that the next orphan and the next asker know that rescue is possible.**
  
  ⭐ **Your testimony is someone else's evidence.**
  
  ⭐ **And note the root also means NEW** (*ḥadīth* = new) — ⭐ **a favour spoken about stays **fresh**; a favour kept silent goes stale.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Insān 76:9** — *lā nurīdu minkum jazāʾan wa lā **shukūrā*** (they want **no thanks**). ⭐ **Insān: don't seek thanks from people. Ḍuḥā: do speak of what Allah gave.** ⭐ **Not a contradiction — ⭐ **one is about *taking credit*, the other about *giving evidence*.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form II imperative.** ⭐ **Note *bi-niʿmati rabbika* is FRONTED before the verb** — ⭐ **"as for **the favour of your Lord** — speak!"** ⭐ **The fronting spotlights *whose* favour it is: not yours.**
- **Why this word:** *ḥaddith* (**narrate**) rather than *ushkur* (be grateful) — ⭐ **because gratitude is internal, and this command is about **making it audible.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Ḥadīth* = a NARRATION **and** "NEW."** ⭐ **Same root.** ⭐ **The command isn't "be grateful" — it's ⭐ **"NARRATE IT."** ⭐ **Because v.9 and v.10 are about orphans and askers — ⭐ **and your story is their evidence that rescue happens.**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **وَمَا قَلَىٰ (v.3)** — ⚠️ **the "you" pronoun is DELETED.** ⭐ **"Has not forsaken YOU… nor does He detest —"** ⭐ **The word "detest" is never allowed to touch him. Don't read a *-ka* that isn't there.**
2. ⭐⭐ **ضَالًّا (v.7)** — ⚠️ **does NOT mean "misguided" in the sense of false belief.** ⭐ **Root image = ***ḍāllah*, a LOST CAMEL** — something valuable, searching for the way.
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **فَأَغْنَىٰ (v.8)** — ⚠️ **Form IV (He MADE you rich)**, not Form X ***istaghnā*** (you CONSIDERED yourself rich). ⭐ **Same root, opposite morality. The form is everything.**
4. ⭐⭐ **سَجَىٰ (v.2)** — ⚠️ **the ONLY night-verb in this juzʾ that means STILLNESS.** All others (*ʿasʿas*, *yasr*, *yaghshā*) are motion. ⭐ **The stillness is the whole point.**
5. ⭐ **وَدَّعَكَ (v.3)** — from ***wadāʿ*, a CEREMONIAL FAREWELL** (*Ḥajjat al-Wadāʿ*). ⚠️ **Not just "left" — *said goodbye*, deliberately.**
6. ⭐ **السَّائِلَ (v.10)** — ⚠️ **means BOTH a beggar AND a questioner.** Don't narrow it to one.
7. ⭐ **تَقْهَرْ (v.9)** — from ***al-Qahhār*, a NAME OF ALLAH.** ⚠️ **Means "overpower by might," not merely "be unkind."**
8. ⭐ **فَحَدِّثْ (v.11)** — ⚠️ **the command is "NARRATE IT," not "be grateful."** ⭐ **Same root as *ḥadīth*.**
9. ⭐ **خَيْرٌ (v.4)** — ⚠️ **an IRREGULAR comparative** (no *afʿal* form). Same for *sharr*. **Worth memorizing as an exception.**
10. ⭐ **The v.6–8 / v.9–11 mapping** — ⚠️ **read them as PAIRS, not two separate lists.** ⭐ **Orphan→sheltered/don't crush; lost→guided/don't rebuff; needy→enriched/speak of it.**
