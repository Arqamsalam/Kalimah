# Surah At-Takwīr (81) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–29)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**Twelve verses, twelve passive verbs, one sentence.**

Verses 1–13 repeat "When… when… when…" without ever completing the sentence. Only verse 14 gives the answer: *"Then a soul will know what it has brought."* Twelve cosmic catastrophes grammatically prepare one quiet clause about you.

The twelve hammers, in order: *kuwwirat* (sun wound up), *inkadarat* (stars dim and fall), *suyyirat* (mountains moved), *ʿuṭṭilat* (camels abandoned), *ḥushirat* (beasts gathered), *sujjirat* (seas stoked), *zuwwijat* (souls paired), *suʾilat* (the girl questioned), *nushirat* (scrolls spread), *kushiṭat* (sky stripped), *suʿʿirat* (Hell kindled), *uzlifat* (Paradise brought near).

Nothing acts; everything is acted upon. The doer is unnamed — because you already know Who.

Then verse 14 lands: عَلِمَتْ نَفْسٌ مَّا أَحْضَرَتْ — "A soul will know what it has brought." The universe is dismantled; the camera settles on one soul.

🧠 **The hook:** a twelve-hammer drumroll — then one sentence about YOU.

## The Big Picture

1. **The Great Undoing** (v.1–13) — sky → earth → creatures → records → destinations.
2. **The Point** (v.14) — a soul knows what it brought.
3. **Three Oaths** (v.15–18) — hiding stars, departing night, breathing dawn.
4. **Defense of the Messenger** (v.19–25) — not madness, greed, or a devil's whisper.
5. **The Open Door** (v.26–29) — where are you going? Straightness remains open.

---

**1. كُوِّرَتْ — *kuwwirat*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** the winding / coiling / wrapping family; *kāraʾl-ʿimāmah* = to wind a turban.
- **Here it means:** the sun is wound up / folded in.
- **Word magic:** an everyday turban-motion is applied to the sun: its light is rolled away like cloth. Cosmic destruction is described with a household verb.
- **Grammar bite:** Form II passive: the pattern adds intensity and leaves the doer unnamed.
- **Why this exact word:** more physical and memorable than simply saying the sun went dark.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** picture the sun rolled up like a turban and wrapped away.

**2. انكَدَرَتْ — *inkadarat*** · v.2
- **Root feeling:** the murkiness / turbidity family; *kadiraʾl-māʾ* = clear water becoming cloudy.
- **Here it means:** the stars fall scattered and become dim.
- **Word magic:** the stars lose two things together: their clarity and their place. Light is being unmade in a second way after the sun.
- **Grammar bite:** Form VII (*infaʿala*): something happens to the subject, with no doer stated.
- **Why this exact word:** losing clarity is more unsettling than merely falling.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** picture bright stars turning like clear water stirred into mud — dimmed and falling.

**3. سُيِّرَتْ — *suyyirat*** · v.3
- **Root feeling:** the traveling / moving family; *sayr* = journey and *sayyārah* = car.
- **Here it means:** the mountains are set moving.
- **Word magic:** it is identical to Nabaʾ 78:20. Nabaʾ tells what they become; Takwīr moves on immediately, making the pace faster.
- **Grammar bite:** Form II passive: not drifting, but forcibly driven away.
- **Why this exact word:** the identical wording makes this a fixed image of the Hour.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *sayyārah* = car: picture mountains driving away.

**4. الْعِشَارُ عُطِّلَتْ — *al-ʿishāru ʿuṭṭilat*** · v.4
- **Root feeling:** *ʿishār* = ten-month pregnant she-camels; *ʿuṭṭilat* = left idle or out of service.
- **Here it means:** the most valuable pregnant camels are left untended.
- **Word magic:** the Day is measured by what it does to priorities: a prized investment is still present and valuable, yet suddenly no longer matters.
- **Grammar bite:** Form II passive; the scene suddenly shifts from the cosmos to one treasured possession.
- **Why this exact word:** the first audience instantly understood this as unthinkable to abandon.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *ʿashr* = ten: imagine your life savings about to pay out — and you simply walk away.

**5. الْوُحُوشُ حُشِرَتْ — *al-wuḥūshu ḥushirat*** · v.5
- **Root feeling:** *wuḥūsh* = wild beasts, linked with *waḥshah* (loneliness); *ḥushirat* = gathered.
- **Here it means:** the wild beasts are gathered.
- **Word magic:** it is a deliberate contradiction: the scattered, solitary creatures become "the loners assembled." Even the ungatherable is gathered.
- **Grammar bite:** simple Form I passive, creating a small rhythmic break from Form II.
- **Why this exact word:** *ḥashara* is the Judgment-Day gathering verb, extending the Gathering to all creation.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** wild animals never gather — yet every one of them is rounded up.

**6. الْبِحَارُ سُجِّرَتْ — *al-biḥāru sujjirat*** · v.6
- **Root feeling:** the stoking-a-furnace family; *sajjaraʾt-tannūr* = to fuel and fire an oven.
- **Here it means:** the seas are set ablaze or made to boil over.
- **Word magic:** water — the thing that defeats fire — becomes the furnace. A basic rule of reality collapses; both classical meanings also show limits bursting.
- **Grammar bite:** Form II passive: intensely stoked, not merely lit.
- **Why this exact word:** oven vocabulary makes the cosmic event tactile and impossible to ignore.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** picture the ocean as a furnace: the thing that kills fire becomes fire.

**7. النُّفُوسُ زُوِّجَتْ — *an-nufūsu zuwwijat*** · v.7
- **Root feeling:** the pairing / mating family; *zawj* = one of a pair or a spouse.
- **Here it means:** the souls are paired up.
- **Word magic:** pairing was a blessing in Nabaʾ 78:8; here it becomes sorting — souls matched with their type, or reunited with their bodies.
- **Grammar bite:** Form II passive; *nufūs* is the plural of *nafs*.
- **Why this exact word:** pairing implies likeness, not merely sorting into piles.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** Nabaʾ: pairing is a blessing. Takwīr: pairing is a verdict — you meet your own kind.

**8. الْمَوْءُودَةُ سُئِلَتْ — *al-mawʾūdatu suʾilat*** · v.8 (and v.9)
- **Root feeling:** *waʾd* = burying an infant alive; *mawʾūdah* = the buried-alive baby girl.
- **Here it means:** the murdered girl is asked: "For what sin was she killed?"
- **Word magic:** the victim — not her killer — is given the floor. Because she committed no sin, the question itself becomes the verdict. One child is weighed beside cosmic events.
- **Grammar bite:** *mawʾūdah* is a passive participle ("the one acted upon"); *suʾilat* then makes her the one addressed.
- **Why this exact word:** naming the crime and placing the victim as subject makes the condemnation unavoidable.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** the universe is ending, yet it stops for the girl nobody thought counted.

**9. الصُّحُفُ نُشِرَتْ — *aṣ-ṣuḥufu nushirat*** · v.10
- **Root feeling:** *ṣuḥuf* = scrolls / pages; *nushirat* = spread open or unfolded.
- **Here it means:** the records are spread open.
- **Word magic:** ʿAbasa 80:22 used the same root for you being unfolded from the grave. Here your record unfolds: you and your file open together.
- **Grammar bite:** simple Form I passive.
- **Why this exact word:** "spread open" emphasizes public unrolling before anything is even read.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** you are unfolded; your record is unfolded — both open at once.

**10. السَّمَاءُ كُشِطَتْ — *as-samāʾu kushiṭat*** · v.11
- **Root feeling:** *kashaṭa* = to flay or skin an animal, stripping its hide away.
- **Here it means:** the sky is stripped away.
- **Word magic:** a butcher's verb treats the sky as a covering peeled off a body, exposing what lay beneath. Nabaʾ had doors in the sky; here the covering disappears.
- **Grammar bite:** Form I passive.
- **Why this exact word:** the raw physical verb conveys total removal, not merely opening.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** picture the sky peeled off like an animal's hide.

**11. الْجَحِيمُ سُعِّرَتْ — *al-jaḥīmu suʿʿirat*** · v.12
- **Root feeling:** *jaḥīm* = fiercely blazing fire; *suʿʿirat* = kindled and stoked to a blaze.
- **Here it means:** the Blaze is kindled fiercely.
- **Word magic:** the seas were stoked in v.6; now Hell is stoked. It is immediately paired with Paradise being brought near — both destinations prepared together.
- **Grammar bite:** Form II passive, matching the surah's repeated intensive passive pattern.
- **Why this exact word:** "kindled" describes deliberate preparation for those arriving.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** Insān 76:4 has *saʿīran* from the same root: Hell is being stoked.

**12. الْجَنَّةُ أُزْلِفَتْ — *al-jannatu uzlifat*** · v.13
- **Root feeling:** the drawing-near family; *zulfah* = closeness, and Muzdalifah shares the root.
- **Here it means:** Paradise is brought near.
- **Word magic:** the final hammer is kindness: after everything else is undone or taken away, one thing moves toward you. The passive comforts — you do not have to travel to it.
- **Grammar bite:** Form IV passive; the twelfth verb and the only one with a positive object.
- **Why this exact word:** nearness is the reward for those who spent life reaching toward it.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** eleven things are torn away; one thing comes closer — remember Muzdalifah.

**13. عَلِمَتْ نَفْسٌ مَّا أَحْضَرَتْ — *ʿalimat nafsun mā aḥḍarat*** · v.14
- **Root feeling:** *ʿalimat* = knew; *nafs* = soul; *aḥḍarat* = brought forward or made present.
- **Here it means:** a soul will know what it brought.
- **Word magic:** this is the payoff to twelve "when" clauses. One unnamed soul knows rather than being told, and faces the deeds it packed and carried in.
- **Grammar bite:** the *jawāb* (answer) to all twelve *idhā* clauses; past tense describes the future with certainty.
- **Why this exact word:** *aḥḍarat* turns deeds into luggage you personally brought.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** the universe ends, the camera finds one soul, and you open your own bag.

**14. فَلَا أُقْسِمُ — *fa-lā uqsimu*** · v.15
- **Root feeling:** the dividing / apportioning family; an oath settles a disputed matter.
- **Here it means:** So I swear by…
- **Word magic:** the construction is identical to Qiyāmah 75:1. *Lā* can rebut the objection before the oath or intensify it; the present tense makes the oath living and renewed.
- **Grammar bite:** the following *bāʾ* is *bāʾ al-qasam* — the "by" used for oaths.
- **Why this exact word:** the repeated Qiyāmah construction signals another contested truth being affirmed.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** you already own this opening from Qiyāmah 75:1.

**15. الْخُنَّسِ — *al-khunnas*** · v.15
- **Root feeling:** the shrinking-back / withdrawing / hiding family; the same root as *al-khannās* in Sūrat an-Nās.
- **Here it means:** the retreating or hiding stars.
- **Word magic:** stars are real but invisible by day, preparing the argument that an unseen angel is not imaginary. The familiar whisperer also withdraws when Allah is remembered.
- **Grammar bite:** an intensive *fuʿʿal* plural, deliberately rhyming with *al-kunnas*.
- **Why this exact word:** the stars are named by their disappearing, not their shining.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *al-khannās* withdraws; these stars hide — real, even when unseen.

**16. الْجَوَارِ الْكُنَّسِ — *al-jawāriʾl-kunnas*** · v.16
- **Root feeling:** *jawārī* = running / flowing; *kunnas* = sweeping, or entering a gazelle's lair. *Miknasah* means broom.
- **Here it means:** the running stars that sweep along or retire into hiding.
- **Word magic:** both classical readings join motion with concealment: they sweep across the sky like a broom, or run like gazelles into their dens.
- **Grammar bite:** *al-jawāri* is a *manqūṣ* noun and drops its final *yāʾ* here.
- **Why this exact word:** the gazelle-den image makes celestial motion feel alive and shy.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** broom = sweep; gazelle's den = hide. They run, then disappear.

**17. عَسْعَسَ — *ʿasʿasa*** · v.17
- **Root feeling:** a reduplicated sound-root for the night's slow creeping; it can mean approaching or departing.
- **Here it means:** the night as it comes on or moves away.
- **Word magic:** the ambiguity perfectly captures the seam between night and day. The doubled sound itself performs slow, repeated movement.
- **Grammar bite:** reduplicated roots such as *waswasa* and *zalzala* often express repeated or gradual action.
- **Why this exact word:** one word meaning both directions fits a threshold where arrival and departure blur.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** hear the doubling — *waswasa, zalzala, ʿasʿasa* — the night creeps either way.

**18. الصُّبْحِ إِذَا تَنَفَّسَ — *aṣ-ṣubḥi idhā tanaffas*** · v.18
- **Root feeling:** the breath / soul family; *nafas* = breath, *tanaffasa* = breathe, and *nafs* = soul.
- **Here it means:** the morning when it breathes.
- **Word magic:** dawn becomes a living thing exhaling light into darkness. *Tanaffasa* here and *nafs* in v.14 share one root: the soul knows; the morning breathes.
- **Grammar bite:** Form V, with a reflexive sense: morning breathes itself into being.
- **Why this exact word:** breathing means life, fitting a surah arguing for resurrection.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** v.14: a *nafs* knows. v.18: dawn breathes. Picture morning exhaling light.

**19. رَسُولٍ كَرِيمٍ — *rasūlin karīm*** · v.19
- **Root feeling:** *rasūl* = messenger / envoy; *karīm* belongs to the nobility and generosity family.
- **Here it means:** it is the word carried by a noble messenger — understood here as Jibrīl.
- **Word magic:** the message's chain of custody is spotless. Calling it the messenger's *qawl* means carried and delivered, not composed by him.
- **Grammar bite:** *la-qawlu* contains the *lām* of emphasis with *inna*: double emphasis.
- **Why this exact word:** *rasūl* stresses his function as carrier rather than simply identifying him as an angel.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** a messenger delivers another's words; he does not author them.

**20. ذِي قُوَّةٍ … مَكِينٍ — *dhī quwwatin… makīn*** · v.20
- **Root feeling:** *quwwah* = power; *makīn* = firmly placed and high in rank, from *makān* (place) and *makānah* (status).
- **Here it means:** possessing strength and firmly established in rank near the Owner of the Throne.
- **Word magic:** place and status share one root: where he stands and how highly he ranks become one idea. Nearness to the Throne is itself honor.
- **Grammar bite:** *dhū / dhā / dhī* is one of the five special nouns; *dhī* is genitive here.
- **Why this exact word:** *makīn* makes the honor positional, firm, and unshakeable.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *makān* = place; *makānah* = status. Where you stand becomes your rank.

**21. مُطَاعٍ ثَمَّ أَمِينٍ — *muṭāʿin thamma amīn*** · v.21
- **Root feeling:** *muṭāʿ* = obeyed; *thamma* = there; *amīn* = trustworthy, from the safety / trust family.
- **Here it means:** obeyed there in the heavens, and trustworthy.
- **Word magic:** authority and trust complete the argument: he can deliver the message and will not alter it. The heavenly carrier is *amīn*, as the earthly carrier was known as al-Amīn.
- **Grammar bite:** a trap worth marking: ثَمَّ *thamma* = "there," while ثُمَّ *thumma* = "then."
- **Why this exact word:** *amīn* exactly describes someone trusted with property that is not his own.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** THAMMA = THERE, not then. Obeyed + trustworthy.

**22. صَاحِبُكُم … بِمَجْنُونٍ — *ṣāḥibukum… bi-majnūn*** · v.22
- **Root feeling:** *ṣāḥib* = companion; *majnūn* comes from "covering," like *jannah*, *jinn*, and *janīn*.
- **Here it means:** your lifelong companion is not mad.
- **Word magic:** "Your companion" appeals to their own decades of knowing him. *Majnūn* means a mind covered over, while the oaths showed hidden things can still be real and ordered.
- **Grammar bite:** the *bāʾ* in *bi-majnūn* is extra after negation, strengthening it: "not at all mad."
- **Why this exact word:** *ṣāḥibukum* makes the defense personal and relational, not merely titular.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** garden, jinn, fetus, and *majnūn* all share the picture of being covered.

**23. بِالْأُفُقِ الْمُبِينِ — *biʾl-ufuqiʾl-mubīn*** · v.23
- **Root feeling:** *ufuq* = horizon; *mubīn* belongs to the clarity family, like *bayān*.
- **Here it means:** he certainly saw him on the clear horizon.
- **Word magic:** the answer to "madness" is a clear sighting at the furthest visible point. The horizon is far but still seen: unseen does not mean imaginary.
- **Grammar bite:** *laqad* + past tense gives strong affirmation; *mubīn* is a Form IV active participle.
- **Why this exact word:** *mubīn* means unmistakable and self-evidencing, not merely bright.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** the horizon is the farthest visible edge — far, but not unseen.

**24. بِضَنِينٍ — *bi-ḍanīn*** · v.24
- **Root feeling:** the miserliness / withholding family; *ḍanna bi-* means to hoard something precious.
- **Here it means:** he does not withhold knowledge of the unseen.
- **Word magic:** false soothsayers rationed hidden claims for payment; he delivered the message fully and freely. The established variant *ẓanīn* means "suspect" — both readings defend him.
- **Grammar bite:** another extra *bāʾ* after negation, matching v.22 and v.25.
- **Why this exact word:** *ḍanīn* targets the profit model of false prophecy, not just its claim.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** fortune-tellers hoard and charge; he gave everything. Variant: *ẓanīn* = suspect.

**25. شَيْطَانٍ رَّجِيمٍ — *shayṭānin rajīm*** · v.25
- **Root feeling:** *shayṭān* carries remoteness from mercy; *rajīm* is from stoning / pelting, like *rajm* at Jamarāt.
- **Here it means:** it is not the word of an accursed, expelled devil.
- **Word magic:** three attacks are closed: not a defect in his mind, motive, or source. The true messenger is near and obeyed; the devil is pelted and shut out.
- **Grammar bite:** the third extra *bāʾ* in the sequence of emphatic denials.
- **Why this exact word:** *rajīm* emphasizes exclusion and denied access, the opposite of a trusted courier.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** one messenger is welcomed near the Throne; the devil is stoned out.

**26. فَأَيْنَ تَذْهَبُونَ — *fa-ayna tadhhabūn*** · v.26
- **Root feeling:** *ayna* = where?; *dhahaba* = to go.
- **Here it means:** So where are you going?
- **Word magic:** after the argument, the surah places a hand on the listener's shoulder. It asks rather than condemns: where else can you go? Qiyāmah asks "where is escape?" in panic; here it is calm and early.
- **Grammar bite:** *ayna* is an indeclinable place-question; *fa-* makes the question the conclusion to all that preceded.
- **Why this exact word:** a question leaves room to turn around; a verdict would not.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** four words: "So where are you going?" You can still change direction.

**27. ذِكْرٌ لِّلْعَالَمِينَ — *dhikrun liʾl-ʿālamīn*** · v.27
- **Root feeling:** *dhikr* = remembrance / reminder; *ʿālamīn* = all worlds and peoples.
- **Here it means:** it is nothing but a reminder to all the worlds.
- **Word magic:** Insān, ʿAbasa and Takwīr repeat the same reminder-root. Here it expands to everyone, answering the rank-based priorities corrected in ʿAbasa.
- **Grammar bite:** إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا is restriction: *in* means "not," so the phrase means "It is nothing but…"
- **Why this exact word:** *dhikr* keeps revelation's role as reminding, not coercing.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** three surahs, one declaration — and remember: *in* here means NOT, not "if."

**28. لِمَن شَاءَ مِنكُمْ أَن يَسْتَقِيمَ — *li-man shāʾa minkum an yastaqīm*** · v.28
- **Root feeling:** *shāʾa* = to will; *yastaqīm* comes from standing upright — like *aṣ-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*.
- **Here it means:** for whoever among you wills to go straight.
- **Word magic:** four nearby surahs repeat "whoever wills": the door is open, but you must walk through it. Form X suggests seeking and maintaining straightness.
- **Grammar bite:** *yastaqīm* is Form X; after *an* it is accusative / subjunctive: *yastaqīma*.
- **Why this exact word:** going straight is a posture maintained, not a single moment of belief.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** you ask for this root in every rakʿah: *aṣ-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*.

**29. وَمَا تَشَاءُونَ إِلَّا أَن يَشَاءَ اللَّهُ — *wa mā tashāʾūna illā an yashāʾaʾllāh*** · v.29
- **Root feeling:** *shāʾa* = to will, repeated from v.28.
- **Here it means:** you do not will except that Allah wills — the Lord of all worlds.
- **Word magic:** v.28 affirms your will; v.29 places it inside His. The same verb holds both truths together. *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* describes the greater will through nurturing care, and echoes v.27.
- **Grammar bite:** *an yashāʾa* is an exception construction; *yashāʾa* is accusative after *an*.
- **Why this exact word:** ending on *Rabb al-ʿālamīn* closes on care, not sheer power, and echoes al-Fātiḥah.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** v.28: your will. v.29: His will. Same verb, both true — the sun-folding surah ends with the Nurturer.
