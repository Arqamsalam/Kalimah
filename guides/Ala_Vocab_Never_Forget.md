# Surah Al-Aʿlā (87) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–19)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**Every single verse ends in the same long "-ā" sound — all nineteen of them.**

*al-Aʿlā, sawwā, hadā, al-marʿā, aḥwā, tansā, yakhfā, al-yusrā, adh-dhikrā, yakhshā, al-ashqā, al-kubrā, yaḥyā, tazakkā, fa-ṣallā, ad-dunyā, abqā, al-ūlā, wa Mūsā.*

⭐ **Nineteen verses, one vowel.** It's the most sustained single rhyme in this whole section of the Qurʾān. Say it aloud and it feels like **a long exhale that never breaks** — which is exactly right for a surah whose command is *sabbiḥ* (glorify).

⭐ **And here's the gift for you specifically:** almost every one of those endings is a **word ending in alif maqṣūrah (ى)** — the exact category of verse-ending noun/verb you said trips you up. **This surah is nineteen of them in a row.**

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐ **This is the surah the Prophet ﷺ is reported to have loved reciting in Witr and in ʿĪd and Jumuʿah prayers.** You have almost certainly heard it hundreds of times. **The rhyme is already in your ear — now attach the meanings to it.**

---

## The Big Picture

1. **Glorify the Highest** (v.1–5) — He created, proportioned, measured, guided, and grows the pasture… **then turns it to dark debris.**
2. **A promise to the Prophet ﷺ** (v.6–8) — *We will make you recite; you will not forget. We will ease you toward ease.*
3. **So remind** (v.9–13) — the one who is in awe **will** take heed; the wretched **avoids** it.
4. **Who succeeds** (v.14–15) — the one who **purifies**, **remembers the Name**, and **prays**.
5. **The real problem** (v.16–19) — *but you prefer this world.* **And this was in the earliest scriptures too.**

## §1 — Glorify the Highest (v.1–5)

**1. سَبِّحِ — *sabbiḥ*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**swimming / gliding swiftly**" family — *sabaḥa* = to swim. ⭐ **You met this exact root in Nāziʿāt 79:3** (*as-sābiḥāt* — those who **glide**). From that motion comes ***tasbīḥ***: **declaring Allah free of every flaw** — the idea of *moving swiftly away* from any imperfection attributed to Him.
- **Here it means:** "**Glorify** the name of your Lord, the Most High."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **understanding the root changes what *subḥānallāh* means to you.** It's not simply "praise" (*ḥamd*) — ⭐ ***tasbīḥ* is DECLARING HIM FAR ABOVE any defect.** It's a word of **distance and elevation**, not just appreciation.
  
  ⭐ **Which is why it's paired with *al-Aʿlā* (the Most High).** *Sabbiḥ* = *push away all flaws*; *al-Aʿlā* = *He is highest*. **The verb and the name are doing the same work.**
  
  ⭐ **And note it's the name that's glorified:** *sabbiḥ **isma** rabbik* — "glorify **the NAME** of your Lord." Some read this as: even His **name** is beyond flaw, so how much more His essence.
- **Grammar bite:** **Form II imperative** (*sabbiḥ*). ⭐ **Form II often = doing something intensively or causing it.** Note the **kasrah** on the ḥāʾ when joined to the next word (*sabbiḥi'sma*) — that's just to avoid two sukūns colliding.
- **Why this word:** *sabbiḥ* over *iḥmad* (praise) — because the surah is about **His height above everything**, not about thanking Him for a favor.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***sabaḥa* = to SWIM** (Nāziʿāt 79:3, *as-sābiḥāt*). ⭐ ***Tasbīḥ* = swimming AWAY from every flaw** — declaring Him far above defect. **You say it dozens of times a day.**

**2. فَسَوَّى — *fa-sawwā*** · v.2
- **Root feeling:** the "**making even / proportioning / perfecting**" family.
- **Here it means:** "who created **and proportioned**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE FIFTH APPEARANCE of *sawwā* in your collection — it is officially the most important cross-surah root you own.** Track it:
  - **Qiyāmah 75:4** — He can re-proportion your **fingertips**
  - **Qiyāmah 75:38** — He proportioned you from **a clot**
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:28** — He proportioned **the sky**
  - **Infiṭār 82:7** — He proportioned **you**
  - **Aʿlā 87:2** — He created **and proportioned** (everything)
  - ⭐ *(and twice more in Shams 91:7 and 91:14 — coming up)*
  
  ⭐ **Seven appearances across seven surahs.** ⭐ **If you learn one root perfectly, make it *s-w-y*.**
  
  Note the compression here: **Qiyāmah 75:38** said *"khalaqa **fa**-sawwā"* — ⭐ **the exact same two words as here.** Aʿlā 87:2 is a **verbatim echo** of Qiyāmah 75:38.
- **Grammar bite:** Form II past, weak-final. ⭐ **The فَـ shows immediate sequence: created → *then straight away* proportioned.** Same "fa" chain you saw in Infiṭār 82:7.
- **Why this word:** *sawwā* frames creation as **precise craftsmanship**, not just bringing-into-being.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***khalaqa fa-sawwā*** here = **the exact same two words as Qiyāmah 75:38.** ⭐ **And this root appears in SEVEN surahs.** Fingertips, clot, sky, you, everything, the soul, a flattened town.

**3. قَدَّرَ فَهَدَى — *qaddara fa-hadā*** · v.3
- **Root feelings:** *qaddara* — ⭐ the "**precise measuring / decreeing**" family (*qadar* = measure/decree) — you own it from **Qiyāmah 75:4** (*qādirīn*), **Insān 76:16** (*qaddarūhā*), **ʿAbasa 80:19** (*fa-qaddarah*). *hadā* — the "**guiding**" family (*hudā* = guidance; *hādī* = a guide).
- **Here it means:** "and who **measured out** and then **guided**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the pairing is a complete theology in three words.**
  
  ⭐ ***Qaddara*** = He gave every created thing its **exact specification** — its size, capacity, limits, purpose. ⭐ ***Hadā*** = He then **showed it how to operate within that specification.** Measuring without guidance would be cruel (you'd have a design with no instructions); guidance without measuring would be useless (instructions for nothing in particular).
  
  ⭐ **And it covers everything, not just humans.** The bee is measured *and* guided. The seed is measured *and* guided. **You are too.**
  
  ⭐ **Note the four *fa*-chains in v.2–5:** *khalaqa **fa**-sawwā*, *qaddara **fa**-hadā*, *akhraja… **fa**-jaʿalahu*. ⭐ **Three matched pairs, each with an instant consequence.** The rhythm is deliberate.
- **Grammar bite:** both weak-final past verbs; **الَّذِي** (who) links them as descriptions of *rabbik*.
- **Why this word:** *qaddara* over "created" — because **measuring implies a spec sheet**, which implies a purpose.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***Qadar* = the exact measurement. *Hudā* = the operating instructions.** ⭐ **He specced you, then told you how to run.** Both, or neither works.

**4. الْمَرْعَى — *al-marʿā*** · v.4
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**shepherding / grazing / tending**" family — *raʿā* = to graze **or to tend**; ***rāʿī* = a SHEPHERD**; ***riʿāyah* = care, guardianship.** The famous hadith: *"each of you is a **rāʿī** and responsible for his flock."*
- **Here it means:** "and who **brings out the pasture**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **you own this word exactly** — it's **identical to Nāziʿāt 79:31** (*akhraja minhā māʾahā wa **marʿāhā***). ⭐ **Same word, same verb (*akhraja*), two surahs.** Free vocabulary.
  
  ⭐ **And the root's double life matters:** *marʿā* isn't just "grass" — because the root also means **to take care of**, ⭐ **the pasture is literally "the care-place."** Grazing land is named after **guardianship**.
  
  ⭐ **Notice the ascent in v.1–4:** *al-Aʿlā* (the Most High) → created → proportioned → measured → guided → **grass**. **From the highest name to a field of green.** The Qurʾān keeps doing this: cosmic scale, then something you can touch.
- **Grammar bite:** noun of place (*mafʿal*) from a weak-final root; the *alif maqṣūrah* carries the surah's rhyme.
- **Why this word:** *marʿā* over a plain word for plants — it names them **by their function for living creatures**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***rāʿī* = shepherd; *riʿāyah* = CARE.** The pasture is the "**care-place**." ⭐ **Identical to Nāziʿāt 79:31.**

**5. غُثَاءً أَحْوَى — *ghuthāʾan aḥwā*** · v.5
- **Root feelings:** *ghuthāʾ* — ⭐ **the scum, froth, and debris a flood carries** — the dried, tangled rubbish left on a riverbank after the water drops. *aḥwā* — the "**dark colour**" family — **blackish-brown**, the colour of dried, rotted vegetation. (Interestingly *aḥwā* can also describe the **deep green-black** of very lush growth, which some read as describing the pasture *before* it dries.)
- **Here it means:** "then makes it **dark, withered debris**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the most sobering two words in the surah, and they arrive without warning.**
  
  ⭐ **Look at the sequence: v.4 = He brings out the pasture (fresh, green, alive). v.5 = He turns it into dried black scum.** ⭐ **One verse apart.** No transition, no explanation. **Growth, then rot.**
  
  ⭐ **And *ghuthāʾ* is precisely the right word** — it's not "dead grass," it's **flood-debris**: the stuff that gets swept along, tangled, and dumped worthless on a bank. **Something that was alive, now just *matter in the way*.**
  
  ⭐ **Why put this in a surah about glorifying the Most High?** Because it's the **argument**: the same hand that grows also withers. ⭐ **And v.16–17 will say the quiet part out loud: "you prefer this life — but the Hereafter is better and more lasting."** *Al-marʿā* is dunya. *Ghuthāʾ aḥwā* is what dunya becomes.
- **Grammar bite:** *ghuthāʾan* is the second object of *jaʿalahu* ("made it [into] debris"); *aḥwā* is an adjective on the *afʿal* colour-pattern (like *aḥmar* red, *aswad* black).
- **Why this word:** *ghuthāʾ* carries **worthlessness and displacement** — not just death, but **being swept aside as rubbish**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Ghuthāʾ* = the dried scum a flood leaves on the bank.** ⭐ **v.4: fresh green pasture. v.5: black worthless debris.** ⭐ **That's dunya, in two verses.**

## §2 — A Promise to the Prophet ﷺ (v.6–8)

**6. سَنُقْرِئُكَ فَلَا تَنسَى — *sa-nuqriʾuka fa-lā tansā*** · v.6
- **Root feelings:** *nuqriʾu* — ⭐ **Form IV of *qaraʾa* (to recite/read)** = **to make someone recite**. ⭐ **Same root as *Qurʾān*** (the Recitation) and *qirāʾah* (recitation). *tansā* — the "**forgetting**" family (*nisyān* = forgetfulness).
- **Here it means:** "**We will make you recite, so you will not forget.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **this is the same reassurance you already met in Qiyāmah 75:16–19** — where the Prophet ﷺ was told **not to move his tongue rushing to catch the revelation**, because *"upon Us is its collection and its recital."*
  
  ⭐ **Qiyāmah said: don't rush, We'll gather it for you. Aʿlā says: We'll make you recite, and you won't forget.** ⭐ **Two surahs, one promise: the preservation is Ours, not yours.**
  
  ⭐ **And *nuqriʾuka* is Form IV — CAUSATIVE.** Not "you will recite" but ⭐ **"We will MAKE you recite."** The ability itself is being given, not just the content.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **سَ + present tense = near future** ("We *will* soon…"). Note *tansā* is weak-final, carrying the rhyme. The **فَـ** = "**so / with the result that**."
- **Why this word:** *nuqriʾuka* (Form IV) places **all the effort on the divine side** — which is exactly the comfort intended.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***Qurʾān* = "The Recitation."** Same root. ⭐ **Qiyāmah 75:16–19 said "don't rush." Aʿlā 87:6 says "you won't forget."** Same promise, twice.

**7. الْجَهْرَ وَمَا يَخْفَى — *al-jahra wa mā yakhfā*** · v.7
- **Root feelings:** *jahr* — ⭐ the "**loud, open, out-in-the-open**" family — ⭐ **you know it from prayer: *ṣalāh jahriyyah*** = the **audible** prayers (Fajr, Maghrib, ʿIshāʾ), where the imam recites **aloud**. *yakhfā* — the "**hiding**" family (*khafiyy* = hidden; *ikhfāʾ* = concealment — ⭐ **also a tajwīd rule!**).
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, He knows **what is open** and **what is hidden**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **two free hooks from things you already do.**
  - ⭐ ***Jahr*** — you distinguish *jahrī* from *sirrī* prayers **every single day**. Same word.
  - ⭐ ***Ikhfāʾ*** — if you've studied tajwīd, you know *ikhfāʾ* is the rule where the **nūn is hidden**. Same root.
  
  ⭐ **And it's a perfect *ṭibāq* (opposite-pair)** covering **everything**: what's broadcast and what's buried. ⭐ **Placed right after the promise about memory (v.6), it quietly says: We know what you retain and what slips — nothing is unaccounted for.**
- **Grammar bite:** *al-jahra* is a **noun**; *mā yakhfā* is a **relative clause** ("what is hidden"). ⭐ **Note the mismatch — Arabic happily pairs a noun with a clause.**
- **Why this word:** naming the **audible** first, then the **hidden**, moves from easy to hard — He knows both equally.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***Ṣalāh jahriyyah*** = the **out-loud** prayers. ⭐ ***Ikhfāʾ*** = the **hiding** rule in tajwīd. **Both from these two words.**

**8. نُيَسِّرُكَ لِلْيُسْرَى — *nuyassiruka li'l-yusrā*** · v.8
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**ease / smoothness**" family — ***yusr*** = ease (the opposite of *ʿusr*, hardship). ⭐ **You know the verse: *inna maʿa'l-ʿusri yusrā*** ("with hardship comes ease").
- **Here it means:** "And **We will ease you toward the easiest way**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the phrasing is unusual and beautiful — notice it's the PERSON being eased, not the path.**
  
  It doesn't say "We will make the way easy for you." It says ⭐ **"We will ease YOU toward ease."** The change happens **in you**. **You get made into someone for whom this is easy.**
  
  ⭐ **And the root repeats within the verse** (*nu-**yassir**-uka li'l-**yusrā***) — verb and object from the same root, which is Arabic's way of hammering an idea.
  
  ⭐ **Cross-surah gold — you own this root three times over:**
  - **ʿAbasa 80:20** — *thumma's-sabīla **yassarah*** ("then the way — He **made it easy**")
  - **Inshiqāq 84:8** — *ḥisāban **yasīrā*** ("an **easy** reckoning")
  - **Aʿlā 87:8** — ***nuyassiruka li'l-yusrā***
  
  ⭐ **Three surahs: the way is eased, the reckoning is easy, and YOU are eased.**
- **Grammar bite:** Form II (*yassara* = to make easy). ⭐ **Note *al-yusrā* is a superlative feminine** (*fuʿlā* pattern, like *al-kubrā*, *al-ūlā*) — "**the easiest** [way/state]."
- **Why this word:** *nuyassiruka* frames divine help as **changing the person**, not just the circumstances.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **Not "the path is eased" — "*YOU* are eased."** ⭐ ***Yusr* vs *ʿusr***, from *"inna maʿa'l-ʿusri yusrā."* ⭐ **Three surahs: 80:20, 84:8, 87:8.**

## §3 — So Remind (v.9–13)

**9. فَذَكِّرْ ... الذِّكْرَى — *fa-dhakkir… adh-dhikrā*** · v.9
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**remembering / mentioning**" family — you own it from **Insān 76:1** (*madhkūran*), **Insān 76:29** (*tadhkirah*), **ʿAbasa 80:11** (*tadhkirah*), **Takwīr 81:27** (*dhikr*).
- **Here it means:** "**So remind**, if the **reminder** benefits."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the surah's job description for the Prophet ﷺ — and it's the same one every time.**
  
  ⭐ **Track this across five surahs now:**
  - **Insān 76:29** — *"this is a **tadhkirah**"*
  - **ʿAbasa 80:11** — *"indeed it is a **tadhkirah**"*
  - **Takwīr 81:27** — *"it is only a **dhikr** for all the worlds"*
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:21** — *"so **remind** — you are only a **reminder**"* *(coming next)*
  - **Aʿlā 87:9** — *"so **remind**, if the **reminder** benefits"*
  
  ⭐ **Five surahs, one root, one message: reminding, never forcing.**
  
  ⭐ **And "*in nafaʿati'dh-dhikrā*" ("if the reminder benefits") is subtle** — it isn't permission to give up on people. Most read it as: **remind regardless, since reminding is what benefits** — or as guidance on **wisdom in timing**, not on abandoning anyone. ⭐ **The very next verse (v.10) shows the reminder *does* land — on whoever has awe.**
- **Grammar bite:** Form II imperative (*dhakkir* = **cause** to remember). Note the root repeats in verb and object (*dhakkir… dhikrā*).
- **Why this word:** *dhakkir* (remind) rather than *ʿallim* (teach) — because ⭐ **the truth is already in them; it needs waking, not installing.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **FIVE surahs, same root: 76:29, 80:11, 81:27, 87:9, 88:21.** ⭐ **The job is always *reminding*.**

**10. سَيَذَّكَّرُ مَن يَخْشَى — *sa-yadhdhakkaru man yakhshā*** · v.10
- **Root feelings:** *yadhdhakkaru* — same reminding root, **Form V**. *yakhshā* — ⭐ the "**awe-filled fear**" family — fear that comes from **knowing** something's greatness (compare *khawf*, plain fear).
- **Here it means:** "**The one who is in awe will take heed.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **two things you already own, side by side.**
  - ⭐ ***Yadhdhakkaru* is the same assimilated Form V you learned in ʿAbasa 80:4** — originally **يَتَذَكَّر**, with the **ت melting into the ذ** and doubling it. ⚠️ **Recognize it or you'll misparse it.**
  - ⭐ ***Yakhshā* completes the *khashyah* thread from Nāziʿāt** — v.19 (Pharaoh could have had it), v.26 (the lesson is for whoever has it), v.45 (you warn whoever has it). ⭐ **Now a fourth: whoever has it *will* take heed.**
  
  ⭐ **And note the certainty: *sa-yadhdhakkaru* — "he WILL take heed."** Not "might." ⭐ **Awe is presented as a reliable filter: put the reminder in front of someone who has it, and it lands.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form V assimilation:** when Form V's **ت** meets **ذ، ز، ص، ض، ط، ظ، د، ث**, it commonly **merges and doubles**. (يتذكر→يذّكر، يتزكى→يزّكى.)
- **Why this word:** *khashyah* over *khawf* — **reverence from knowledge**, which is why it reliably produces heed.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **يَتَذَكَّر → يَذَّكَّر** (the ت melts). ⭐ **And *khashyah* is now in FOUR verses: Nāziʿāt 79:19, 26, 45 + here.** **Awe is the filter.**

**11. وَيَتَجَنَّبُهَا الْأَشْقَى — *wa yatajannabuhā'l-ashqā*** · v.11
- **Root feelings:** *yatajannabu* — ⭐ the "**side**" family — ***janb* = a SIDE/flank**; *tajannaba* = **to step to the side of something, to give it a wide berth**. *ashqā* — the "**wretchedness / misery**" family (*shaqiyy* = wretched; *shaqāwah* = misery — the opposite of *saʿādah*, happiness).
- **Here it means:** "But **the most wretched will avoid it**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ ***tajannaba* is a beautifully physical word.** It literally means **to turn your side to something and go around it** — like crossing the street to avoid someone. ⭐ **Not "rejects it" (an argument) but "SIDESTEPS it" (a body movement).** He doesn't debate the reminder; **he swerves.**
  
  ⭐ **And the contrast with v.10 is exact:**
  | v.10 | v.11 |
  |---|---|
  | *man **yakhshā*** (whoever is in awe) | *al-**ashqā*** (the most wretched) |
  | **takes heed** (moves toward) | **sidesteps** (moves away) |
  
  ⭐ **Same reminder, two body-languages.**
  
  ⭐ **Cross-surah:** *al-ashqā* returns in **Shams 91:12** — *idhi'nbaʿatha **ashqāhā*** ("when **the most wretched of them** was sent forth") — describing the man who hamstrung the she-camel of Thamūd. ⭐ **Same superlative, and Shams gives it a face.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form V (*tafaʿʿala*)** here = **doing something for/to yourself** — *putting yourself* to the side of it. *Ashqā* is a **superlative** (*afʿal*) carrying the rhyme.
- **Why this word:** *tajannaba* makes avoidance **bodily and deliberate**, not intellectual.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***janb* = a SIDE.** *Tajannaba* = ⭐ **to turn your side and walk around it** — like crossing the road to dodge someone. ⭐ **And *al-ashqā* gets a face in Shams 91:12.**

**12. لَا يَمُوتُ فِيهَا وَلَا يَحْيَى — *lā yamūtu fīhā wa lā yaḥyā*** · v.13
- **Root feelings:** *yamūtu* — the "**death**" family (*mawt*). *yaḥyā* — the "**life**" family (*ḥayāh*; *al-Ḥayy* = the Ever-Living).
- **Here it means:** "Then he will **neither die therein nor live**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **one of the most chilling lines in the Qurʾān, and it works by *removing an option you didn't know you were counting on*.**
  
  ⭐ **A perfect *ṭibāq* (opposite-pair) — but BOTH sides are negated.** Normally life and death are the only two possibilities; ⭐ **here both are denied at once**, leaving a state with no name. **You can't even use death as an escape.**
  
  ⭐ **And notice the contrast with Qiyāmah 75:40:** *"Is not that [Creator] able to **give life to the dead**?"* — where *yuḥyī* was **the great mercy**. ⭐ **Here, not-living is the punishment.** Same root, opposite function.
  
  ⭐ **Compare also Nabaʾ 78:30** — *"We will never increase you except in punishment"* — ⭐ **same technique: closing the last exit.**
- **Grammar bite:** two negated present-tense verbs; *yaḥyā* is weak-final (ح-ي-ي), carrying the rhyme.
- **Why this word:** naming **both** poles and denying both is stronger than any description of pain.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **"Neither dies nor lives."** ⭐ **Both exits sealed.** Compare **Qiyāmah 75:40**, where *giving life* was the mercy.

## §4 — Who Succeeds (v.14–15)

**13. قَدْ أَفْلَحَ — *qad aflaḥa*** · v.14
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ **a farming root!** *falaḥa* = **to split/plough the earth**; ***fallāḥ* = a FARMER** (still the everyday word in Arabic). ⭐ **From ploughing comes *falāḥ* = success** — because the farmer who **breaks open the soil** is the one who **gets the harvest**.
- **Here it means:** "**He has certainly succeeded** who purifies himself."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the etymology is a whole lesson.** Arabic's word for **success** comes from ⭐ **splitting hard ground open to plant something.** Success isn't luck or arrival — ⭐ **it's the farmer's kind: break the surface, put something in, wait.**
  
  ⭐ **And you say this root every day**: ***ḥayya ʿala'l-falāḥ*** — "come to **success**" — in **every adhān**. ⭐ **The call to prayer is literally calling you to "the harvest."**
  
  ⭐ **Cross-surah:** *qad aflaḥa man tazakkā* here ↔ **Shams 91:9** — *qad **aflaḥa** man **zakkāhā*** ("he has succeeded who purifies it"). ⭐ **Nearly identical verses, four surahs apart.** Free vocabulary again.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **قَدْ + past tense = certainty/completion** ("has *certainly* succeeded"). Compare **لَقَدْ** in Takwīr 81:23 — same emphasis, with an added *lām*.
- **Why this word:** *falāḥ* over a generic word for winning — it builds **effort and cultivation** into the concept.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***fallāḥ* = A FARMER** (from splitting soil). ⭐ ***Ḥayya ʿala'l-falāḥ*** — you hear it in **every adhān**. ⭐ **Success = the farmer's kind.** ⭐ **And Shams 91:9 says it almost identically.**

**14. تَزَكَّى — *tazakkā*** · v.14
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**purify + grow**" family — ***zakāh***! ⭐ **Key insight: this root means cleansing AND increase at once** — removing what's rotten is what lets a thing **grow**.
- **Here it means:** "who **purifies himself**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the fourth surah in a row for this root — and it has a remarkable range.**
  - **Nāziʿāt 79:18** — the gentle offer **made to Pharaoh**: *"would you like to **purify yourself**?"*
  - **ʿAbasa 80:3, 7** — the blind man who came seeking to ***purify himself***
  - **Aʿlā 87:14** — *"he has succeeded who **purifies himself**"*
  - **Shams 91:9** — *"he has succeeded who **purifies it** [the soul]"* *(coming up)*
  
  ⭐ **From the worst tyrant to the sincerest seeker to the definition of success — one root.**
  
  ⭐ **And note the FORM V reflexive:** *tazakkā* = ⭐ **purify YOURSELF.** Nobody does it for you. Compare **Shams 91:9** (*zakkāhā*, **Form II** — "purified **it**") — ⭐ **slightly different grammar, same demand.**
  
  ⭐ **Beautiful placement:** *falāḥ* (v.14) is a **farming** word and *zakāh* is a **growth** word. ⭐ **Two agricultural roots in one verse: plough the ground, and the thing grows.**
- **Grammar bite:** **Form V**, weak-final, carrying the rhyme.
- **Why this word:** *tazakkā* over "was good" — it makes success **an active, self-directed cultivation**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***Zakāh* = purify AND grow.** ⭐ **Four surahs: offered to Pharaoh (79:18), sought by the blind man (80:3), defines success (87:14, 91:9).**

**15. وَذَكَرَ اسْمَ رَبِّهِ فَصَلَّى — *wa dhakara'sma rabbihi fa-ṣallā*** · v.15
- **Root feelings:** *dhakara* — the **remembering/mentioning** root again. *ṣallā* — the "**prayer**" family (*ṣalāh*). ⚠️ **Note: different root from *ṣaliya* (to be roasted)** in v.12 — ⭐ **the surah uses both, three verses apart!**
- **Here it means:** "and **mentions the name of his Lord** and **prays**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **look at the three-step recipe for success in v.14–15**: ⭐ **purify yourself → remember His name → pray.** **Inner, verbal, physical.** Nothing missing.
  
  ⭐ **And notice the *inclusio* (ring) with verse 1.** The surah **opened**: *"**Glorify the NAME** of your Lord."* It now **closes its instruction**: *"and he **mentioned the NAME** of his Lord and prayed."* ⭐ **Verse 1 gives the command; verse 15 shows the man who obeyed it.**
  
  ⭐ **The exact same pair appears in Qiyāmah 75:31** — *fa-lā **ṣaddaqa** wa lā **ṣallā*** ("he neither affirmed the truth **nor prayed**"). ⭐ **Qiyāmah shows the man who did neither; Aʿlā shows the man who did both.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⚠️ ⭐ **TRAP: صَلَّى (*ṣallā*, prayed) vs. يَصْلَى (*yaṣlā*, is roasted) in v.12.** **Same three letters ص-ل-ي, but different roots and meanings.** Context decides.
- **Why this word:** ending on ***ṣallā*** grounds all the abstraction (purify, remember) in ⭐ **one concrete action you can do right now**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Purify → remember the Name → pray.** ⭐ **v.1 commanded "glorify the NAME"; v.15 shows the man who did.** ⚠️ ⭐ **And watch: *yaṣlā* (v.12, roasted) vs *ṣallā* (v.15, prayed) — three verses apart.**

## §5 — The Real Problem (v.16–19)

**16. بَلْ تُؤْثِرُونَ الْحَيَاةَ الدُّنْيَا — *bal tuʾthirūna'l-ḥayāta'd-dunyā*** · v.16
- **Root feelings:** *tuʾthirūna* — ⭐ the "**trace / footprint**" family — ***athar* = a mark left behind** (*āthār* = **archaeological remains**); from it, *āthara* = **to prefer**, and ⭐ ***īthār* = the virtue of preferring OTHERS over yourself** (selflessness). *dunyā* — ⭐ from *danā* = **to be near/low** — ⭐ **"the nearer life."**
- **Here it means:** "**But you prefer the life of this world.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **you own this exact move from Nāziʿāt 79:38** — *wa **āthara'l-ḥayāta'd-dunyā*** ("and **preferred** the life of this world"). ⭐ **Same verb, same object, two surahs.** Only the person changes: **Nāziʿāt said "he"; Aʿlā says "YOU."**
  
  ⭐ **And the root's noble meaning makes it cut:** ***īthār*** — putting others first — is one of the **highest virtues** in Arabic. ⭐ **Here the same machinery of preference is aimed at the dunya.** You have the capacity for *īthār*. **You pointed it at the wrong thing.**
  
  ⭐ **Bonus: *dunyā* means "the NEARER one"** (from *adnā*, nearer/lower) — ⭐ **it's named for being close, not for being good.** Compare **Qiyāmah 75:20** and **Insān 76:27**, which call it ***al-ʿājilah*** — "**the hurrying one**." ⭐ **Three different nicknames, all critical: the near one, the quick one, the debris (v.5).**
- **Grammar bite:** **Form IV** present, plural. ⭐ **Note the sudden shift to "you"** — after fifteen verses of "he/they," ⭐ **the surah turns and points.**
- **Why this word:** *tuʾthirūna* frames it as a **deliberate ranking**, not ignorance. **You knew. You chose.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***athar* = a footprint; *īthār* = putting OTHERS first.** ⭐ **Nāziʿāt 79:38: "HE preferred." Aʿlā 87:16: "YOU prefer."** ⭐ **And *dunyā* = "the NEARER one."**

**17. خَيْرٌ وَأَبْقَى — *khayrun wa abqā*** · v.17
- **Root feeling:** *abqā* — ⭐ the "**remaining / lasting**" family — *baqiya* = to remain; ***bāqī* = remaining**; *al-Bāqī* = the Everlasting; ⭐ ***bāqiyāt ṣāliḥāt*** = "the enduring good deeds."
- **Here it means:** "while the Hereafter is **better and more lasting**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the two-word answer to v.16, and each word does separate work.**
  
  ⭐ ***Khayr* (better)** answers **quality**. ⭐ ***Abqā* (more lasting)** answers **duration**. ⭐ **Together they close both possible defences of choosing dunya:** *"but it's good"* — no, that's better; *"but it's here now"* — yes, and it ends.
  
  ⭐ **And *abqā* is a comparative built from the root of *bāqī*** — set that against **v.5's *ghuthāʾan aḥwā*** (dark withered debris). ⭐ **The surah has already SHOWN you what dunya becomes; now it names the alternative "more-lasting."** **v.5 is the evidence; v.17 is the verdict.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Nāziʿāt 79:46** — your whole life was *"an evening or its morning."* ⭐ **Same argument, different image.**
- **Grammar bite:** both are **comparatives** (*afʿal* pattern); *abqā* is weak-final, carrying the rhyme.
- **Why this word:** naming **duration** explicitly is the killer argument, because ⭐ **nobody disputes that dunya ends.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***Bāqī* = remaining** (*al-Bāqī*, *bāqiyāt ṣāliḥāt*). ⭐ **"Better AND longer-lasting" — closes both excuses at once.** ⭐ **v.5 already showed you the debris.**

**18. الصُّحُفِ الْأُولَى — *aṣ-ṣuḥufi'l-ūlā*** · v.18
- **Root feelings:** *ṣuḥuf* — ⭐ **scrolls/pages** (*ṣaḥīfah*; modern *ṣaḥāfah* = **journalism**, *ṣaḥīfah* = a **newspaper**) — you own it from **ʿAbasa 80:13** and **Takwīr 81:10**. *al-ūlā* — "**the first / the earliest**" (feminine superlative of *awwal*).
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, this is in **the earliest scrolls** — the scrolls of **Ibrāhīm and Mūsā**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the most surprising ending in the surah, and it's an argument about age.**
  
  ⭐ **Quraysh's standard objection was that this was something new** — an innovation, a break from the ancestors. ⭐ **The surah's closing move: "this isn't new. It was in the oldest scriptures there are."**
  
  ⭐ **And notice WHICH message is being called ancient.** Not a ritual, not a law — ⭐ **the message of v.14–17: purify yourself, remember His name, pray, and don't prefer the fleeting over the lasting.** ⭐ **That's what Ibrāhīm and Mūsā were given too.**
  
  ⭐ **Cross-surah:** *ṣuḥuf* has now appeared three ways: **ʿAbasa 80:13** (honored scrolls of **revelation**), **Takwīr 81:10** (the scrolls of **your deeds**, spread open), **Aʿlā 87:18** (the **earliest** scrolls of previous prophets). ⭐ **Same word, three archives.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **note v.19 (*ṣuḥufi Ibrāhīma wa Mūsā*) is a *badal*** — an **appositive** explaining v.18. ⭐ **Arabic often states something, then immediately specifies it.** (Same technique as Takwīr 81:15–16.)
- **Why this word:** *al-ūlā* (the earliest) is chosen precisely to answer **the charge of novelty**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ṣaḥīfah* = a newspaper page.** ⭐ **Three archives, one word: revelation's scrolls (80:13), YOUR scrolls (81:10), the ancient scrolls (87:18).** ⭐ **And the surah ends by saying: "this message is the OLDEST thing there is."**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐ **يَصْلَى (v.12, "is roasted") vs. صَلَّى (v.15, "prayed")** — ⚠️ **THREE VERSES APART, same three letters ص-ل-ي, different roots.** *Ṣaliya* = burn; *ṣallā* = pray. **The single sharpest trap in this surah.**
2. ⭐ **يَذَّكَّرُ (v.10)** — this is **Form V with the ت assimilated** (يَتَذَكَّر → يَذَّكَّر). ⚠️ **Don't misread it as Form II.** Same rule as **ʿAbasa 80:4**.
3. ⭐ **سَوَّى (v.2)** — ⚠️ **if you learn one root in this whole juzʾ, make it *s-w-y*.** It appears in **seven surahs**.
4. ⭐ **أَفْلَحَ (v.14)** — from ***fallāḥ*, a FARMER** (splitting soil). ⚠️ **Not a generic "win" word** — it carries **cultivation and effort.**
5. ⭐ **غُثَاءً أَحْوَى (v.5)** — ⚠️ **not just "dry grass":** *ghuthāʾ* = **the worthless scum a flood dumps on a bank.** The worthlessness is the point.
6. ⭐ **الْيُسْرَى / الْكُبْرَى / الْأُولَى** — all **feminine superlatives** (*fuʿlā* pattern). ⚠️ **Recognize the shape**; it recurs constantly in these surahs.
7. ⭐ **يَتَجَنَّبُهَا (v.11)** — from ***janb*, a SIDE.** It means **physically sidestepping**, not intellectually rejecting.
8. ⭐ **نُقْرِئُكَ (v.6)** — **Form IV = "make you recite,"** not "you recite." ⚠️ **The causative is the whole comfort of the verse.**
9. **الدِّينِ-style note:** ⚠️ **دُنْيَا comes from *adnā* (nearer/lower)** — it's named for **proximity**, not quality.
10. ⭐ **Every verse ends in alif maqṣūrah (ى)** — ⚠️ **nineteen in a row.** Use the rhyme as your recall hook: if a word doesn't end in "-ā," it isn't a verse-ending here.
