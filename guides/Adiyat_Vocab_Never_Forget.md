# Surah Al-ʿĀdiyāt (100) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–11)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**Five oaths in five verses — and every single one is a WAR HORSE in motion, described by a different sense.**

⭐ **v.1** — *wa'l-ʿādiyāti **ḍabḥā*** — ⭐ **SOUND**: you *hear* them panting.
⭐ **v.2** — *fa'l-mūriyāti **qadḥā*** — ⭐ **SIGHT**: you *see* sparks strike from their hooves.
⭐ **v.3** — *fa'l-mughīrāti **ṣubḥā*** — ⭐ **TIME**: they raid at *dawn*.
⭐ **v.4** — *fa-atharna bihi **naqʿā*** — ⭐ **DUST**: they kick up a cloud.
⭐ **v.5** — *fa-wasaṭna bihi **jamʿā*** — ⭐ **POSITION**: they plunge into the middle of the enemy.

⭐⭐ **Five verses, and every one ends in a *cognate accusative*** — ⭐ ***ḍabḥan, qadḥan, ṣubḥan, naqʿan, jamʿan*** — ⭐ **Arabic's underline key, five times in a row.**

⭐ **And then v.6 lands: ⭐ ***inna'l-insāna li-rabbihi la-KANŪD*** — "**indeed, man is ungrateful to his Lord.**"**

⭐⭐ **The horse gives everything — breath, hooves, life — for a master it can't even understand.** ⭐ **The human, who *can* understand, gives nothing.** ⭐ **The oath IS the argument.**

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐⭐ **Five verses of a horse's total devotion, then one verse about human ingratitude.** ⭐ **The animal shames the man.**

---

## The Big Picture

1. **Five oaths** (v.1–5) — ⭐ **a charging war horse, sense by sense.**
2. **Three charges** (v.6–8) — ⭐ **ungrateful · a witness against himself · intensely loving wealth.**
3. **The reversal** (v.9–11) — ⭐ **the graves emptied, the chests extracted, and their Lord fully informed.**

## §1 — Five Oaths (v.1–5)

**1. الْعَادِيَاتِ ضَبْحًا — *al-ʿādiyāti ḍabḥā*** · v.1
- **Root feelings:** *ʿādiyāt* — ⭐ the "**running fast / exceeding**" family — *ʿadā* = **to run, to gallop**; ⭐ **and *ʿadwān* = transgression** (⭐ **running past your limits!**); ⭐ ***ʿadū* = an ENEMY*** (one who runs against you); ⭐ **modern *ʿadw* = running/jogging.** *ḍabḥ* — ⭐ **the SOUND a horse makes when galloping hard** — a heavy, rasping pant through the nostrils.
- **Here it means:** "**By the chargers that pant.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **the surah opens with a SOUND, and it's a very specific one.**
  
  ⭐ ***Ḍabḥ* is not "breathing."*** ⭐ **It's the particular **rasping, forced panting** of an animal running beyond comfort.** ⭐ **The word exists for almost nothing else.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **Which means the first thing the surah does is put you close enough to **hear the horse's lungs**.** ⭐ **Not "horses that run" — ⭐ **horses running so hard you can hear them.**
  
  ⭐ **And the *ʿ-d-w* root's other meanings are rich:** ⭐ ***ʿadū* = an ENEMY*** (⭐ **the one who runs at you**) and ⭐ ***ʿudwān* = aggression*** (⭐ **running past the boundary**). ⭐ **Running, hostility, and transgression share three letters.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare the oath-structure to Nāziʿāt 79:1–5** — ⭐ **five feminine plural participles, five cognate accusatives, no subject named.** ⭐⭐ **The two surahs use **the identical opening architecture**.** ⭐ **Nāziʿāt's five were angels; ʿĀdiyāt's five are horses.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **active participle, feminine plural** (*fāʿilāt* pattern) — ⭐ **the described noun (*al-khayl*, horses) is **DELETED**, as in Nāziʿāt.** ⭐ ***Ḍabḥan*** is a ***maf'ūl muṭlaq*** (cognate accusative).
- **Why this word:** *ḍabḥ* is chosen because ⭐ **it can only describe extreme exertion** — the horse is already giving everything in verse one.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Ḍabḥ* = the RASPING PANT of a horse running beyond comfort.** ⭐ **You *hear* it.** ⭐⭐ **And *ʿadū* = AN ENEMY; *ʿudwān* = aggression** — ⭐ **all from "running."** ⭐ **Same five-oath architecture as Nāziʿāt 79:1–5.**

**2. الْمُورِيَاتِ قَدْحًا — *al-mūriyāti qadḥā*** · v.2
- **Root feelings:** *mūriyāt* — ⭐ **Form IV of *warā*** = **to strike fire, to produce a spark**; ⭐ ***wariyah* = the tinder or flint used to start fire.*** *qadḥ* — ⭐ **the ACT of striking flint** to produce a spark; *qadaḥa* = to strike sparks.
- **Here it means:** "**and those that strike sparks [with their hooves].**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **from sound (v.1) to SIGHT (v.2) — and the image is night-time.**
  
  ⭐ **Iron-shod hooves striking rock at full gallop **throw sparks**.** ⭐ **You only see that in **darkness**.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **Which sets up v.3 perfectly: ⭐ **v.2 is night (you can see the sparks), ⭐ **v.3 is dawn (*ṣubḥan*).** ⭐ **The horses have been running **all night** and arrive at daybreak.** ⭐ **The chronology is embedded in the imagery.**
  
  ⭐ **And ***qadḥ*** — the flint-strike — ⭐ **is the sound-and-light of effort against stone.** ⭐ **The horse is striking fire out of the ground with its feet.**
  
  ⭐ ⚠️ **Note the particle shift: ⭐ **v.1 begins with *wa* (the oath-*wāw*); ⭐ **v.2–5 all begin with *fa*.** ⭐ **The *fa* means "**and then immediately**" — ⭐ **so the five oaths are **a sequence**, not a list.** ⭐ **Run → spark → raid → dust → plunge.** ⭐⭐ **Same technique as Nāziʿāt 79:4–5.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form IV participle, feminine plural.** ⭐ **Second cognate accusative in two verses.**
- **Why this word:** *qadḥ* (**flint-striking**) rather than a word for fire — ⭐ **it names the **friction**, which is what effort looks like.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **Hooves striking rock throw SPARKS — ⭐ **which you can only see at NIGHT.** ⭐⭐ **So v.2 = night, v.3 = dawn.** ⭐ **They ran all night.** ⭐ **And the *fa*'s in v.2–5 make it a SEQUENCE, not a list.**

**3. الْمُغِيرَاتِ صُبْحًا — *al-mughīrāti ṣubḥā*** · v.3
- **Root feelings:** *mughīrāt* — ⭐ **Form IV of *ghāra*** = **to raid, to make a sudden attack**; ⭐ ***ghārah* = A RAID***; ⭐ **and the related *ghayr* = other/different** (⭐ **a raid comes from *outside*, from the other**). *ṣubḥ* — ⭐ **early morning / daybreak** — ⭐ ***Ṣalāt aṣ-Ṣubḥ***! ⭐ **And *aṣbaḥa* = to become/to enter the morning.**
- **Here it means:** "**and those that raid at dawn.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the timing is historically exact, and you pray at that hour.**
  
  ⭐ **Dawn raids were **standard Arab warfare** — ⭐ **you travel at night and strike when there's just enough light to see, before the camp is awake.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And ***ṣubḥ*** is the word you use every day for **Fajr prayer time**.** ⭐ **Free hook.**
  
  ⭐ **Note what the surah has done in three verses: ⭐ **it has built a complete military operation — ⭐ **the exhausting night ride (v.1), the sparks in the dark (v.2), the dawn strike (v.3)** — ⭐ **without narrating anything.** ⭐ **Just three oaths.**
  
  ⭐ **And compare: ⭐ **Takwīr 81:18** — *wa'ṣ-**ṣubḥi** idhā tanaffas* ("and by the morning when it **BREATHES**"). ⭐⭐ **Two surahs swear by the dawn: ⭐ **one where it *breathes* gently, one where it's the hour of attack.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form IV participle, feminine plural.** ⚠️ ⭐ ***Ṣubḥan*** here is an **accusative adverb of time** ("at dawn") — ⭐ **NOT a cognate accusative like the others**, even though it matches the rhyme. **A subtle variation in an otherwise uniform pattern.**
- **Why this word:** *ṣubḥ* (**the everyday word for morning**) rather than a poetic term — ⭐ **it grounds an epic scene in a familiar hour.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Ṣalāt aṣ-ṢUBḤ*** — you pray at this exact hour. ⭐ **Dawn raids were **standard Arab warfare**.** ⭐⭐ **Three verses build a whole battle without narrating anything.** ⭐ ⚠️ **And *ṣubḥan* is an **adverb of time**, not a cognate accusative — a subtle break in the pattern.**

**4. فَأَثَرْنَ بِهِ نَقْعًا — *fa-atharna bihi naqʿā*** · v.4
- **Root feelings:** *atharna* — ⭐ **Form IV of *thāra*** = **to stir up, to raise, to kick up**; ⚠️ ⭐ **DIFFERENT ROOT from *athar* (a trace) — this is *th-w-r*, the root of *thawrah* = A REVOLUTION!** (something **stirred up**). *naqʿ* — ⭐ **DUST raised in the air** — a cloud of it.
- **Here it means:** "**and stir up thereby a cloud of dust.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the root is one you know from the news, and the image is what a whole army looks like from a distance.**
  
  ⭐ ***Thāra* → *thawrah* = A REVOLUTION*** — ⭐ **literally "an uprising, a stirring-up."** ⭐ **Modern Arabic uses it constantly.** ⭐ **Free hook.**
  
  ⭐ ⚠️ **CAREFUL — this is NOT the *athar* (trace/footprint) root** you own from **Nāziʿāt 79:38** (*wa **āthara**'l-ḥayāta'd-dunyā*) and **Aʿlā 87:16** (***tuʾthirūna***). ⭐ **Those are *ʾ-th-r*; this is *th-w-r*.** ⭐⭐ **They look similar and mean completely different things. A real trap.**
  
  ⭐ **And *naqʿ* (a raised dust-cloud) is ⭐ **what you see before you see the army**.** ⭐ **From a distance, a cavalry charge is a **cloud on the horizon**.**
  
  ⭐ **Note the *bihi* ("thereby / with it") in v.4 and v.5** — ⚠️ ⭐ **the "it" is unstated: the place, the time, or the raid itself.** ⭐ **Classical readings differ; the ambiguity keeps the scene fast-moving.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ⚠️ **NOTE THE SHIFT**: v.1–3 were **participles** (*al-ʿādiyāt*, *al-mūriyāt*, *al-mughīrāt*); ⭐ **v.4–5 switch to **finite verbs** (*atharna*, *wasaṭna*) with the **feminine plural *nūn***.** ⭐⭐ **The grammar accelerates — from describing them to watching them act.**
- **Why this word:** *naqʿ* is ⭐ **the *visible* consequence** of everything in v.1–3 — the proof from far away.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Thawrah* = A REVOLUTION** (a "stirring up"). ⭐ **Same root as *atharna*.** ⭐⭐ ⚠️ **NOT the same root as *āthara* (to prefer, Nāziʿāt 79:38) — that's *ʾ-th-r*, this is *th-w-r*.** ⭐ **A real trap.** ⭐ **And v.4–5 switch from PARTICIPLES to VERBS — the scene speeds up.**

**5. فَوَسَطْنَ بِهِ جَمْعًا — *fa-wasaṭna bihi jamʿā*** · v.5
- **Root feelings:** *wasaṭna* — ⭐ the "**middle**" family — *wasaṭ* = **the middle/centre**; ⭐ ***ummatan wasaṭā* = a middle/balanced community***; ⭐ **and *wasaṭa* = to penetrate into the middle of something.** *jamʿ* — ⭐ **a gathering, a massed group** — ⭐ **the same root as *yawm al-jamʿ*, and *jāmiʿ* (a congregational mosque!), and *jumuʿah*.**
- **Here it means:** "**and penetrate thereby into the midst of a gathering [of the enemy].**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the fifth and final oath, and it's the **climax of the charge**.**
  
  ⭐ ***Wasaṭna*** = they reached ⭐ **the very CENTRE** — ⭐ **not the edge, not the flank.** ⭐ **The deepest, most dangerous point of the enemy formation.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And that completes the horse's devotion:** ⭐ **it ran all night (v.1), struck sparks (v.2), raided at dawn (v.3), raised the dust (v.4), ⭐ **and plunged into the middle of the enemy (v.5).**
  
  ⭐ **The horse doesn't understand the cause. It doesn't get the spoils. It won't be thanked.** ⭐⭐ **And it goes to the centre anyway.** ⭐ **Then v.6 says: *man is ungrateful to his Lord*.**
  
  ⭐ **And *jamʿ* is your everyday word: ⭐ ***jumuʿah*** (the day of **gathering**), ⭐ ***masjid jāmiʿ*** (the mosque where people **gather**), ⭐ ***jamīʿ*** (all/altogether).** ⭐ **Free hook.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Qiyāmah 75:3** — *allan **najmaʿa** ʿiẓāmah* ("that We will not **gather** his bones"). ⭐ **Same root: gathering enemies, gathering bones.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **finite verb with **feminine plural nūn** (*wasaṭna*), matching *atharna*.** ⭐ **Fifth cognate accusative (*jamʿan*).**
- **Why this word:** *wasaṭ* (**the centre**) rather than a word for "attacked" — ⭐ **because *how deep it went* is the measure of the devotion.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Jumuʿah* = the day of GATHERING; *masjid jāmiʿ* = where people GATHER.** ⭐ **Same root.** ⭐⭐ **And *wasaṭna* = they reached **the CENTRE** — the most dangerous point.** ⭐ **The horse understands nothing, gets nothing, and goes to the middle anyway.** ⭐ **Then v.6.**

## §2 — Three Charges (v.6–8)

**6. لَكَنُودٌ — *la-kanūd*** · v.6
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**withholding good / being stubbornly ungrateful**" family — *kanada* = **to deny a favour, to be miserly with thanks**; ⭐ **classical lexicographers connect *kanūd* to ⭐ **barren ground that grows nothing*** — ⭐ **land that takes the rain and gives back nothing.**
- **Here it means:** "**Indeed, man is ungrateful to his Lord.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **the word is an INTENSIVE, and the barren-ground image is the key.**
  
  ⭐ ***Kanūd* is on the *faʿūl* intensive pattern*** — ⭐ **the same shape as *kafūr* (Insān 76:3), *ghafūr*, *shakūr*.** ⭐ **It means **habitually, characteristically** ungrateful — ⭐ **not a lapse but a disposition.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And the *barren-land* connection makes it unforgettable:** ⭐ **a *kanūd* is ground that **receives rain and yields nothing**.** ⭐ **It's not hostile — it's just **unproductive**.
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **NOW PUT IT AGAINST THE HORSE.** ⭐ **v.1–5: an animal that gives **everything** — its breath, its hooves, its life — for a master whose purposes it cannot comprehend.** ⭐ **v.6: a human who **receives everything** and returns nothing, to a Lord he *can* comprehend.**
  
  ⭐ **The five oaths weren't scene-setting. ⭐ **They were the **prosecution's exhibit**.**
  
  ⭐ **And you own the sibling word from Insān 76:3** — *immā **shākiran** wa immā **kafūrā*** ("either **grateful** or **utterly ungrateful**"). ⭐⭐ **Insān offered two roads; ʿĀdiyāt says which one man takes.**
  
  ⭐ **Note *li-rabbihi* is FRONTED: *inna'l-insāna **LI-RABBIHI** la-kanūd*** — ⭐ **"**TO HIS LORD** — ungrateful."** ⭐ **The one he's ungrateful *to* is spotlighted first.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***inna* + emphatic *lām*** = double emphasis. ⭐ ***Faʿūl* intensive pattern.** ⭐ **Fronting of *li-rabbihi* = emphasis.**
- **Why this word:** *kanūd* over *kāfir* — ⭐ **it names **unproductiveness**, not hostility, which is a more accurate and more damning charge.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Kanūd* = BARREN GROUND that takes rain and gives NOTHING BACK.** ⭐⭐⭐ **The horse gives everything for a master it can't understand; ⭐ **man receives everything and returns nothing to a Lord he CAN understand.** ⭐ **That's why the five oaths are there.** ⭐ **And Insān 76:3 offered the two roads: *shākir* or *kafūr*.**

**7. لَشَهِيدٌ — *la-shahīd*** · v.7
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**witnessing / being present**" family — *shahida* = **to witness, to be present**; ⭐ ***shahādah* = TESTIMONY*** (⭐ **and the *shahādah* you declare!**); ⭐ ***shahīd* = a witness AND a martyr***; ⭐ ***mashhad* = a scene***; ⭐ **and *shahr* = a month** (⭐ **the sighted, witnessed crescent!**).
- **Here it means:** "**And indeed, he is a witness to that.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **"a witness to *that*" — to WHAT? And WHO is the witness? Both are debated, and both readings are devastating.**
  
  ⭐ **READING ONE (most common): ⭐ ***man* is a witness against **himself***.** ⭐ **He KNOWS he's ungrateful.** ⭐ **It isn't ignorance — he can testify to his own ingratitude if asked.**
  
  ⭐ **READING TWO: ⭐ ***Allah* is witness** to man's ingratitude.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **The first reading connects to a thread you own:**
  - **Qiyāmah 75:14** — *bali'l-insānu ʿalā nafsihi ***BAṢĪRAH*** (⭐ **"man is a clear witness against himself"**)
  - ⭐ **ʿĀdiyāt 100:7** — *wa innahu ʿalā dhālika **la-SHAHĪD*** (⭐ **"he is a witness to that"**)
  
  ⭐⭐ **Two surahs, two words (*baṣīrah* = insight-witness; *shahīd* = present-witness), one claim: ⭐ **you don't need a prosecutor. You already know.**
  
  ⭐ **And you own *shahīd* from Burūj 85:3, 9** (*wa **shāhidin wa mashhūd***, and *wa'llāhu ʿalā kulli shayʾin **shahīd***).
  
  ⭐ **The free hook: ⭐ **you say the ***shahādah*** — the **testimony** — every day.** ⭐ **Same root.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***inna* + emphatic *lām*** again — ⭐ **three verses in a row (v.6, 7, 8) all use *inna* + *lām*.** ⭐ **Triple hammer.**
- **Why this word:** *shahīd* (**one present at the scene**) rather than *ʿālim* (one who knows) — ⭐ **presence is harder to deny than knowledge.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **You say the *SHAHĀDAH* — the TESTIMONY — daily.** Same root. ⭐⭐ **Qiyāmah 75:14: man is a *BAṢĪRAH* against himself. ⭐ **ʿĀdiyāt 100:7: he is a *SHAHĪD* to it.** ⭐ **Two surahs: you're your own witness.** ⭐ **And v.6, 7, 8 ALL use *inna* + emphatic *lām*.**

**8. لِحُبِّ الْخَيْرِ لَشَدِيدٌ — *li-ḥubbi'l-khayri la-shadīd*** · v.8
- **Root feelings:** *ḥubb* — **love** (⭐ **you own it from Fajr 89:20**, *wa **tuḥibbūna**'l-māla **ḥubban jammā***). *khayr* — ⭐ **here it means WEALTH/good things** ⚠️ **(a recognized Qurʾānic usage — "the good" as a euphemism for money!)**. *shadīd* — ⭐ the "**firm / intense / severe**" family — ⭐ **you own it from Nabaʾ 78:12** (*sabʿan **shidādā***), **Insān 76:28** (***shadadnā** asrahum*), **Burūj 85:12** (*inna baṭsha rabbika la-**shadīd***).
- **Here it means:** "**And indeed, he is intense in his love of wealth.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **two things: the euphemism, and the choice of *shadīd*.**
  
  ⭐ ⚠️ ***Al-khayr* here means WEALTH, not "good."*** ⭐ **A recognized Qurʾānic usage — ⭐ **money is called "**the good**" because that's what people *call* it.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And that's the quiet sting: ⭐ **the surah uses **his own word** for it.** ⭐ **He calls money "the good" — so the verse does too, and lets the irony sit.**
  
  ⭐ **And ***shadīd*** is the stunning part.** ⭐ **You own this root from **three** places, and look where:
  - **Nabaʾ 78:12** — seven ***shidād*** heavens (⭐ **FIRMLY built**)
  - **Insān 76:28** — *we ***shadadnā*** their frame* (⭐ **your skeleton, tightly knitted**)
  - **Burūj 85:12** — *your Lord's grip is ***shadīd*** (⭐ **severe**)
  - ⭐ **ʿĀdiyāt 100:8** — *he is ***shadīd*** in loving wealth*
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **The same word describes: **the firmness of the sky**, **the firmness of your bones**, **the severity of divine grip** — ⭐ **and **the intensity of your grip on money**.** ⭐ **Four firmnesses. Three are His. One is yours, and it's aimed at cash.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Fajr 89:20** — *tuḥibbūna'l-māla **ḥubban jammā*** ("you love wealth with an **overflowing** love"). ⭐ **Fajr says *how much*; ʿĀdiyāt says *how hard*.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***li-ḥubbi'l-khayr*** is **fronted** before *la-shadīd* — ⭐ **spotlighting the object of the intensity.** ⭐ **Third *inna* + *lām* in three verses.**
- **Why this word:** *shadīd* (**firm/intense**) rather than *kathīr* (much) — ⭐ **it describes the **grip**, not the quantity.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ⚠️ ***Al-khayr* HERE = WEALTH**, not "good." ⭐ **The verse uses **his own word** for money.** ⭐⭐⭐ **And *shadīd*: the SKY is firm (78:12), your BONES are firm (76:28), His GRIP is firm (85:12) — ⭐ **and your grip on MONEY is firm (100:8).** ⭐ **Four firmnesses; only one is yours.**

## §3 — The Reversal (v.9–11)

**9. بُعْثِرَ مَا فِي الْقُبُورِ — *buʿthira mā fi'l-qubūr*** · v.9
- **Root feelings:** *buʿthira* — ⭐ **a QUADRILITERAL root** (ب-ع-ث-ر) meaning ⭐ **to overturn, to turn inside out, to scatter the contents** — ⭐ **like tipping a container upside down and shaking it.** Related to *baʿatha* = **to raise up / resurrect**. *qubūr* — ⭐ **graves** (⭐ **you own *aqbarah* from ʿAbasa 80:21** — "He **GAVE him a grave**").
- **Here it means:** "**when what is in the graves is overturned.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **you own this verse ALMOST VERBATIM from Infiṭār 82:4.**
  
  ⭐ **Infiṭār 82:4 — *wa idha'l-qubūru ***BUʿTHIRAT*** ("when the **graves are overturned**").
  ⭐ **ʿĀdiyāt 100:9 — *idhā ***BUʿTHIRA** mā fi'l-**QUBŪR*** ("when **what is IN the graves** is overturned").
  
  ⭐⭐ **Same rare quadriliteral verb, same noun, two surahs.** ⭐ **⚠️ **But note the difference: ⭐ **Infiṭār overturns **the graves**; ʿĀdiyāt overturns **what is in them**.** ⭐ **ʿĀdiyāt goes one layer deeper.**
  
  ⭐ **And *buʿthira* is precisely the verb: ⭐ **not "opened" but **tipped over and emptied**, like a bag shaken out.** ⭐ **Nothing left behind, nothing neat.**
  
  ⭐ **And the ʿAbasa payoff still applies: ⭐ **ʿAbasa 80:21 listed **being given a grave** among Allah's blessings.** ⭐ **Here that gift is turned upside down.**
  
  ⭐ ⚠️ **Four-letter roots in Arabic often describe **messy, tumbling, repeated** actions** — ⭐ **compare *zalzala* (99:1), *damdama* (91:14), *waswasa*.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **quadriliteral passive.** ⭐ ***Mā fi'l-qubūr*** ("what is in the graves") is the subject-substitute — ⚠️ **note it says "**what**," not "**who**," which widens it.**
- **Why this word:** *buʿthira* conveys ⭐ **total emptying and disorder** — the opposite of a dignified resting place.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **Infiṭār 82:4: the **GRAVES** are overturned. ⭐ **ʿĀdiyāt 100:9: **WHAT IS IN** the graves is overturned.** ⭐ **Same rare verb, one layer deeper.** ⭐⭐ **And ʿAbasa 80:21 listed being GIVEN a grave as a BLESSING.** ⭐ **Picture a bag tipped over and shaken empty.**

**10. وَحُصِّلَ مَا فِي الصُّدُورِ — *wa ḥuṣṣila mā fi'ṣ-ṣudūr*** · v.10
- **Root feelings:** *ḥuṣṣila* — ⭐ the "**extracting the essence / obtaining the final result**" family — *ḥaṣala* = **to result, to be obtained**; ⭐ ***taḥṣīl* = ACQUISITION / attainment*** (⭐ **modern Arabic: *taḥṣīl ʿilmī* = educational attainment; *taḥṣīl* = collecting/extracting**); ⭐ **the root originally refers to **separating the grain from the chaff** — extracting what's valuable from what's mixed in.** *ṣudūr* — ⭐ **CHESTS** (⭐ **you own *ṣadr* from Sharḥ 94:1**, *a-lam **nashraḥ** laka **ṣadrak***).
- **Here it means:** "**and what is in the breasts is extracted / brought out.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **the grain-and-chaff image is the whole verse, and the pairing with v.9 is perfect.**
  
  ⭐ **v.9 — the **GRAVES** are overturned → ⭐ **what's in the GROUND comes out** (bodies).
  ⭐ **v.10 — the **CHESTS** are extracted → ⭐ **what's in the HEART comes out** (intentions).
  
  ⭐⭐ **Two containers, two excavations.** ⭐ **The earth gives up its dead; the chest gives up its secrets.** ⭐ **And neither is voluntary.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **And *ḥaṣṣala* is far more precise than "revealed."** ⭐ **The root means ⭐ **separating out the essence** — ⭐ **threshing grain, so the valuable kernel is separated from the husk.**
  
  ⭐ **So it isn't that your chest is *opened* — ⭐ **it's that your chest is **THRESHED**, and what was actually in there is **separated out from everything you pretended**.
  
  ⭐ **And *taḥṣīl* = ATTAINMENT/acquisition in modern Arabic** — ⭐ **your free hook.** ⭐ **What you *actually acquired* gets extracted.**
  
  ⭐ **And the Sharḥ connection is lovely: ⭐ **Sharḥ 94:1 — your *ṣadr* was **EXPANDED** as a gift.** ⭐ **ʿĀdiyāt 100:10 — every *ṣadr* is **EXTRACTED**.** ⭐ **Same word, one opened in mercy, one opened in judgment.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form II passive** (*ḥuṣṣila*) — ⭐ **intensive + no doer named.** ⭐ **Note the deliberate parallel with *buʿthira*: two passives, two *mā fi'l-…* phrases.**
- **Why this word:** *ḥaṣṣala* (**thresh out the essence**) rather than *ubdiya* (revealed) — ⭐ **because the issue is **separating the real from the performed**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Ḥaṣṣala* = TO THRESH — separate the GRAIN from the CHAFF.** ⭐ **And *taḥṣīl* = ATTAINMENT** in modern Arabic. ⭐⭐⭐ **v.9: the GROUND gives up bodies. v.10: the CHEST gives up intentions.** ⭐ **Both involuntary.** ⭐ **And Sharḥ 94:1 EXPANDED the *ṣadr* as a gift; here every *ṣadr* is EXTRACTED.**

**11. لَّخَبِيرٌ — *la-khabīr*** · v.11
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**deep, inside knowledge**" family — *khabara* = **to know something by testing and experiencing it from within**; ⭐ ***khabar* = news/a report*** (⭐ **you own *akhbārahā* from Zalzalah 99:4 — the surah right before!**); ⭐ ***khibrah* = EXPERTISE / practical experience***; ⭐ **and *al-Khabīr* — a NAME OF ALLAH.**
- **Here it means:** "**Indeed, their Lord, that Day, is fully Acquainted with them.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **the final word, and the root is precisely chosen.**
  
  ⭐ ***Khabīr* is NOT the same as *ʿalīm*.*** ⭐ ***ʿAlīm* = knowing (all knowledge).*** ⭐ ***Khabīr* = ⭐ **knowing the INSIDE of a thing — its hidden reality, learned by penetration.**
  
  ⭐ **And *khibrah* in modern Arabic = **EXPERTISE**, the knowledge you only get from **being inside** a field.** ⭐ **Free hook.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **So why *khabīr* here?** ⭐ **Because v.10 just said **the chests are threshed open**.** ⭐ **The verse pairs the **extraction of the inside** with the One who **knows insides**.** ⭐ **Perfect fit.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **And the consecutive-surah link is beautiful:**
  ⭐ **Zalzalah 99:4 — *tuḥaddithu ***AKHBĀRAHĀ*** (⭐ **the earth narrates its NEWS**)
  ⭐ **ʿĀdiyāt 100:11 — *inna rabbahum bihim yawmaʾidhin la-***KHABĪR*** (⭐ **their Lord is fully ACQUAINTED**)
  
  ⭐⭐ **SAME ROOT, back-to-back surahs.** ⭐ **The earth gives its report (*khabar*) — ⭐ **and the One receiving it already has full inside-knowledge (*khabīr*).** ⭐ **The testimony confirms what He already knew.**
  
  ⭐ **And note *bihim* is FRONTED: *inna rabbahum **BIHIM** yawmaʾidhin la-khabīr*** — ⭐ **"**about THEM** — fully acquainted."**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***inna* + emphatic *lām*** — ⭐ **the FOURTH in this surah (v.6, 7, 8, 11).** ⭐ ***Faʿīl* intensive adjective pattern.**
- **Why this word:** *khabīr* (**inside-knowledge**) rather than *ʿalīm* — ⭐ **because the verse before it opened people's insides.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Khibrah* = EXPERTISE** — knowledge from being **INSIDE** something. ⭐ ***Khabīr* ≠ *ʿalīm***: ⭐ **He knows the **inside**, not just the facts.** ⭐⭐⭐ **And Zalzalah 99:4 (*akhbārahā*, the earth's NEWS) → ʿĀdiyāt 100:11 (*khabīr*, fully ACQUAINTED).** ⭐ **Same root, back-to-back surahs.**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **فَأَثَرْنَ (v.4)** — ⚠️ **root *th-w-r* (to STIR UP — as in *thawrah*, a REVOLUTION).** ⭐ **NOT *ʾ-th-r* (*athar*, a trace / *āthara*, to prefer — Nāziʿāt 79:38, Aʿlā 87:16).** **Two different roots that look alike.**
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **الْخَيْرِ (v.8)** — ⚠️ **means WEALTH here**, not "good." ⭐ **A recognized Qurʾānic euphemism — the verse uses *his own* word for money.**
3. ⭐⭐ **صُبْحًا (v.3)** — ⚠️ **an ADVERB OF TIME** ("at dawn"), ⭐ **NOT a cognate accusative** like the other four, even though it matches the rhyme. **A subtle break in the pattern.**
4. ⭐⭐ **كَنُودٌ (v.6)** — ⚠️ **from BARREN GROUND that takes rain and gives nothing back.** ⭐ ***Faʿūl* intensive** — a **disposition**, not a lapse.
5. ⭐⭐ **حُصِّلَ (v.10)** — ⚠️ **means THRESHED — grain separated from chaff**, not merely "revealed." ⭐ **The real is separated from the performed.**
6. ⭐⭐ **لَّخَبِيرٌ (v.11)** — ⚠️ ***khabīr* ≠ *ʿalīm*.** ⭐ ***Khabīr* = knowing the INSIDE** (as in *khibrah*, expertise).
7. ⭐ **v.1–3 are PARTICIPLES; v.4–5 switch to FINITE VERBS** — ⚠️ **the grammar accelerates deliberately.**
8. ⭐ **The *fa*'s in v.2–5** — ⚠️ **make the five oaths a **SEQUENCE**, not a list. ⭐ **Run → spark → raid → dust → plunge.**
9. ⭐ **بُعْثِرَ (v.9)** — ⚠️ **a QUADRILITERAL root**, like *zalzala* (99:1) and *damdama* (91:14). ⭐ **Four-letter roots = messy, tumbling action.**
10. ⭐ **مَا فِي الْقُبُورِ (v.9)** — ⚠️ **"WHAT is in the graves," not "who."** ⭐ **The wider word is deliberate.**
