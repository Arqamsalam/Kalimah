# Surah An-Nabaʾ (78) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–40)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**Hell and Paradise are described with the SAME words — mirrored, half a surah apart.**

Read v.21–30 (the Fire) and v.31–36 (the Garden) side by side, and the vocabulary repeats with its meaning flipped.

**The frame:** *lā **yadhūqūna fīhā** bardan wa lā sharābā* — "they **taste** no coolness **therein**, nor drink" (v.24) → *lā **yasmaʿūna fīhā** laghwan wa lā kidhdhābā* — "they **hear** no idle talk **therein**, nor lying" (v.35).

**jazāʾ:** *jazāʾan **wifāqā*** — a recompense that **exactly fits** (v.26) → *jazāʾan min rabbika **ʿaṭāʾan*** — a recompense, and a **gift** on top (v.36).

**ḥisāb:** *lā yarjūna **ḥisābā*** — they never expected a **reckoning** (v.27) → *ʿaṭāʾan **ḥisābā*** — a gift **amply reckoned** (v.36).

**kidhdhāb:** *kadhdhabū… **kidhdhābā*** — they **denied** utterly (v.28) → *wa lā **kidhdhābā*** — **no lying** is heard there (v.35).

**The drink:** *illā ḥamīman wa ghassāqā* — **scalding and foul** (v.25) → *kaʾsan dihāqā* — a cup **full to the brim** (v.34).

And around them, one more pair: *maʾāb* — "a place of return" — is **Hell** for the transgressors (v.22) and **your Lord** for whoever chooses (v.39).

Almost every verse ends on the same long **-ā**: *mihādā, awtādā, azwājā, subātā, libāsā, maʿāshā…* — forty verses on one note, like a drum that never changes beat.

🧠 **The hook:** two destinations, one vocabulary — the words you hear in the Fire come back in the Garden, turned inside out.

## The Big Picture

1. **The Question** (v.1–5) — *"What are they asking each other about?"* The Great News. *No — they will know.* Then again: *no — they will know.*
2. **Nine Gifts, One Argument** (v.6–16) — earth, mountains, pairs, sleep, night, day, sky, sun, rain. **Whoever built all this can rebuild you.**
3. **The Appointment** (v.17–20) — the Day of Sorting has a fixed time: the horn, the crowds, the sky's gates, the mountains turned to mirage.
4. **The Ambush** (v.21–30) — Hell lies in wait. Every deed was counted. *"So taste."*
5. **The Escape** (v.31–36) — for those who shielded themselves: a place of triumph, and a gift on top of the reward.
6. **The Silence** (v.37–40) — the Spirit and the angels stand in rows; nobody speaks without permission. And the last line: *"I wish I were dust."*

**Memory spine:** asked about (v.1) → proven (v.6) → scheduled (v.17) → two exits (v.21, 31) → silence (v.38) → dust (v.40).

## §1 — The Question (v.1–5)

**1. عَمَّ — *ʿamma*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** not a root at all — a **contraction**: عَنْ (*ʿan*, "about") + مَا (*mā*, "what"). The nūn melts into the mīm, and the alif of *mā* **drops**.
- **Here it means:** "**About what** are they asking one another?"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **this one tiny word names a whole juzʾ.** Juzʾ ʿAmma — the thirtieth part of the Qur'an, the one you probably learned first — is called after the first word of this surah. And the question is **rhetorical**: Allah knows what they ask about. Asking it out loud makes the subject feel **enormous** before it is even named.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **interrogative مَا loses its alif after a preposition** — *ʿamma, fīma, bima, lima, mimma*. The single highest-yield small rule in this juzʾ.
- **Why this exact word:** a question opens the surah because the whole surah is the **answer**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⚠️ ***ʿamma* is NOT *ʿamm* (uncle).** It is ʿan + mā = "about what?" — and it gives Juzʾ ʿAmma its name.

**2. يَتَسَاءَلُونَ — *yatasāʾalūn*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** the "**asking**" family — *saʾala* = to ask; *suʾāl* = a question; *sāʾil* = one who asks (and a beggar — one who asks for help).
- **Here it means:** "they are **asking one another**."
- **Word magic:** Form VI makes it **mutual**: not "they ask the Prophet ﷺ" but "they ask **each other**" — a crowd buzzing among itself, unsettled, comparing doubts. It is the sound of people who cannot leave a question alone **and** cannot accept its answer.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form VI (تَفَاعَلَ) = doing it to each other** — *yatasāʾalūn* = they ask one another. Recognise the shape: ta- + long ā after the first root letter.
- **Why this exact word:** the mutual form exposes a society talking **to itself** about the one thing it refuses to settle.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *suʾāl* = a **question**. Picture a room where everyone turns to the person next to them and whispers: *"Do you really think…?"*

**3. النَّبَإِ الْعَظِيمِ — *an-nabaʾi'l-ʿaẓīm*** · v.2
- **Root feeling:** the "**momentous news**" family — *nabaʾ* is not ordinary news (*khabar*); it is news of **great weight**, the kind that changes things. And ⭐ the same root gives ***nabiyy*** — a **prophet**: one who brings the *nabaʾ*.
- **Here it means:** "about **the Great News**" — the Resurrection and the Day.
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the surah is named after this word — **An-Nabaʾ, the News**. A *nabaʾ* has three marks in classical usage: it is **important**, it brings **knowledge**, and it is **certain**. So the deniers are "asking each other" about something that is, by its very label, *certain* — the word wins the argument before it begins. Then *al-ʿaẓīm* (the Great) seals it.
- **Grammar bite:** genitive after *ʿan*; *al-ʿaẓīm* is its adjective. Notice v.2 **repeats the preposition** *ʿan* from v.1 — the question is answered by **reusing its own word**.
- **Why this exact word:** *nabaʾ* over *khabar* — because this is news **you must act on**, not trivia.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***nabaʾ* = momentous news; *nabiyy* = the one who brings it.** The surah of the News is about what the prophets came to announce.

**4. مُخْتَلِفُونَ — *mukhtalifūn*** · v.3
- **Root feeling:** the "**behind / after / differing**" family — *khalf* = behind; *khalīfah* = a successor (one who comes after); *ikhtilāf* = **disagreement** (each one going off behind his own view).
- **Here it means:** "the one about which they **differ**."
- **Word magic:** the deniers do not even agree **with each other** — some deny the Resurrection outright, some doubt it, some mock it. Error has many versions; that is why they *differ*. And it confirms v.1: people asking each other are people who have not settled.
- **Grammar bite:** Form VIII active participle (*muftaʿil*), plural; *fīhi* (about it) is **fronted** before it — "about it, specifically, they differ."
- **Why this exact word:** *ikhtilāf* shows the confusion is **internal** to them — not truth vs. falsehood, but falsehood vs. falsehood.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *ikhtilāf* = **disagreement**; *khalf* = **behind**. Picture everyone walking off **behind** their own opinion, in different directions.

**5. كَلَّا سَيَعْلَمُونَ — *kallā sa-yaʿlamūn*** · v.4–5
- **Root feelings:** *kallā* — a particle of **stern rebuke**: "No! Not at all!" *yaʿlamūn* — the "**knowing**" family (*ʿilm* = knowledge; *ʿālim* = a scholar).
- **Here it means:** "**No! They will come to know.** Then — **no! They will come to know.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the repetition is the threat.** Say it once: a warning. Say it again with *thumma* (then): heavier, slower, closer. No details are given — *what* they will know is left blank, and the blank is more frightening than any description. You will meet this exact escalation again (Takāthur 102:3–4).
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***sa-* + present = "will" (near future)** — it is a separate particle, not part of the root. *Thumma* here marks **escalation**, not just sequence.
- **Why this exact word:** *ʿilm* (knowing) — because their problem was never lack of information; it was refusing to **know** it.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***kallā sa-yaʿlamūn — thumma kallā sa-yaʿlamūn.*** Same sentence twice; the second lands harder. ⚠️ ***sa-* = will** — don't look for it in the root.

## §2 — Nine Gifts, One Argument (v.6–16)

**6. مِهَادًا — *mihādā*** · v.6
- **Root feeling:** the "**smoothing / preparing a bed**" family — *mahd* = a **cradle**; *mahhada* = to smooth, to pave; *tamhīd* = **preparation, a preface** (modern Arabic: an introduction, *mumahhad* = a paved road!).
- **Here it means:** "**Did We not make the earth a cradle**?"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ a cradle is **prepared before the baby arrives** — smoothed, softened, made safe. That is the picture of the earth: laid out for you **before you came**. And the argument begins here: nine gifts follow, each one in the same pattern, each one answering v.1–5. **The One who made your cradle can wake you from your sleep.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***a-lam* + jussive = "Did… not?"** — a question expecting **"Yes, You did."** *Najʿal* is jussive after *lam*; it takes a kasra (*najʿali*) to join the next word.
- **Why this exact word:** *mihād* over "floor" or "ground" — it carries **care**, not just flatness.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *mahd* = **a cradle**; *tamhīd* = **a preface**. The earth is the cradle — and this verse is the **preface** to the argument.

**7. أَوْتَادًا — *awtādā*** · v.7
- **Root feeling:** the "**peg / stake**" family — *watad* = a **tent peg**, the stake hammered into the ground that keeps the tent from blowing away.
- **Here it means:** "and the mountains as **pegs**?"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the image is a desert tent: the cloth is the earth's surface, and the mountains are the **pegs driven deep** that hold it steady. You see only the head of a peg; most of it is **below ground**. Gift #2 builds on gift #1 — a cradle that **does not rock**.
- **Grammar bite:** second object of *najʿal* (understood from v.6) — "made [the mountains] **as** pegs." Broken plural of *watad*.
- **Why this exact word:** *awtād* turns mountains from obstacles into **fasteners** — stability, not size.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *watad* = **a tent peg**. The world is a tent; the mountains are its **pegs**.

**8. أَزْوَاجًا — *azwājā*** · v.8
- **Root feeling:** the "**pairing**" family — *zawj* = one of a pair, a **spouse**; *zawāj* = **marriage**; *zawjān* = a couple.
- **Here it means:** "and We created you in **pairs**."
- **Word magic:** after the earth and the mountains, the camera turns to **you** — and the first thing said is that you were made as **halves of pairs**: male and female, each completed by the other. Some also read it as **kinds** (tall and short, rich and poor). Either way: you were **designed** to need another.
- **Grammar bite:** *ḥāl* (state) or second object: "created you **as** pairs." Note the verb switches from *jaʿalnā* (We made) to ***khalaqnā* (We created)** — for humans, the stronger verb.
- **Why this exact word:** *azwāj* frames human pairing as **part of creation**, not an afterthought.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *zawāj* = **marriage**. You were created already **half of a pair**.

**9. سُبَاتًا — *subātā*** · v.9
- **Root feeling:** the "**cutting off / stopping**" family — *sabata* = to cut off, to cease; ⭐ ***as-sabt*** = **Saturday**, the Sabbath — the day work was **cut off**.
- **Here it means:** "and made your sleep **a rest** — a cutting-off."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ sleep is described as a **switch that cuts you off** from the day — work, worry, movement, all stopped. It is a small daily death: you lie still, unaware, and you **wake up again**. The surah is arguing for resurrection, and it hands you **the proof you rehearse every night**.
- **Grammar bite:** second object of *jaʿalnā* — "made your sleep [**as**] *subāt*."
- **Why this exact word:** *subāt* is not "sleep" (that's *nawm*, already in the verse); it names sleep's **function** — a complete stop.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***as-Sabt* = Saturday, the day of stopping.** Every night you are **switched off — and switched back on.** That is the resurrection in miniature.

**10. لِبَاسًا — *libāsā*** · v.10
- **Root feeling:** the "**clothing / covering**" family — *libās* = a garment; *labisa* = to wear; *malābis* = **clothes** (everyday Arabic!).
- **Here it means:** "and made the night **a garment**."
- **Word magic:** the night **wraps** the world like clothing — it covers, hides, gives privacy and rest. The same word is used for spouses: *hunna libāsun lakum wa antum libāsun lahunna* (2:187) — "they are a **garment** for you and you for them." **A garment is the thing closest to you that covers you.** Night does that for the whole earth.
- **Grammar bite:** second object of *jaʿalnā*; indefinite, carrying the -ā rhyme.
- **Why this exact word:** *libās* gives darkness a **kind** job — covering, not frightening.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *malābis* = **clothes**. Every night, the world **puts on its pyjamas.**

**11. مَعَاشًا — *maʿāshā*** · v.11
- **Root feeling:** the "**living**" family — *ʿāsha* = to live; *ʿaysh* = life (and in Egyptian Arabic, *ʿēsh* = **bread** — the stuff of life!); *maʿāsh* = **livelihood** (modern: a salary, a pension).
- **Here it means:** "and made the day **a time for earning a living**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ verses 9–11 are a perfect **cycle**: sleep (*subāt*) → night (*libās*) → day (*maʿāsh*). Stop, cover, work. **Your whole daily rhythm is a gift on a schedule** — and you never asked for it.
- **Grammar bite:** *maʿāsh* is a *mafʿal* noun — of **time** here: "a time of living."
- **Why this exact word:** *maʿāsh* names the day by what you **do** in it, just as *subāt* named sleep by what it **does**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *maʿāsh* = **a salary / livelihood**; *ʿēsh* = **bread**. The day is for **earning your bread**.

**12. سَبْعًا شِدَادًا — *sabʿan shidādā*** · v.12
- **Root feeling:** the "**tightness / strength**" family — *shadda* = to tighten, to bind firmly; *shadīd* = strong, severe; and ⭐ ***shaddah*** — the doubling mark (ـّ) on a letter, named because it **strengthens** the letter.
- **Here it means:** "and **built** above you **seven strong** [heavens]."
- **Word magic:** the verb is ***banaynā* — We BUILT**: construction language, as if the sky had a **builder's specification**. And *shidād* means more than "strong" — it means **tightly knit, nothing loose**: millennia pass and there is no crack, no sag, no repair.
- **Grammar bite:** *sabʿan* stands for "seven [heavens]" — the noun is **dropped** because it is obvious; *shidādan* is its adjective (broken plural of *shadīd*).
- **Why this exact word:** *shidād* stresses **durability** — exactly the quality the Day will undo (the sky "opened into gates," v.19).
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ the **shaddah** on a letter = **strengthening** it. The heavens are **seven shaddahs** above you — tight, firm, unbroken.

**13. سِرَاجًا وَهَّاجًا — *sirājan wahhājā*** · v.13
- **Root feelings:** *sirāj* — a **lamp that burns** and makes its own light. *wahhāj* — the "**blazing glow**" family: *wahaj* = the intense **heat and glare** of fire.
- **Here it means:** "and placed [in it] **a blazing lamp**" — the sun.
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the sun is not named — it is **described**: a *lamp* (useful, given for you) that *blazes* (powerful, beyond you). And the Qur'an keeps this distinction: the sun is a *sirāj* (it **burns**), the moon is a *nūr* (it **reflects**) — see Nūḥ 71:16.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***faʿʿāl* = the intensive pattern** — *wahhāj* = blazing **again and again, intensely**. You'll meet its brothers in this surah: *thajjāj* (v.14), *ghassāq* (v.25).
- **Why this exact word:** *wahhāj* captures **heat and light together** — both of which you need.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **sun = *sirāj* (burns its own light), moon = *nūr* (borrows it).** And *wahhāj* is the first of three ***faʿʿāl*** words in this surah.

**14. الْمُعْصِرَاتِ — *al-muʿṣirāt*** · v.14
- **Root feeling:** the "**squeezing / pressing**" family — *ʿaṣara* = to squeeze; *ʿaṣīr* = **juice** (squeezed fruit!); *muʿṣirāt* = the **rain-clouds**, ready to be wrung out.
- **Here it means:** "and We sent down from **the rain-clouds**…"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the clouds are named by what they are about to do — **squeeze themselves out**, like a wet towel being wrung. (Some early scholars read *muʿṣirāt* as the **winds** that press the clouds; both readings keep the squeezing.) And the same letters give ***al-ʿAṣr*** (103) — the late afternoon, when the day is **pressed** toward its end.
- **Grammar bite:** Form IV active participle, feminine plural (*afʿala* → *mufʿilāt*) — "those that have **reached** the point of squeezing."
- **Why this exact word:** it names rain by its **mechanism** — water does not just fall; it is **pressed out**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ʿaṣīr* = juice.** Rain-clouds are the sky's **juicers**. ⚠️ Don't translate *muʿṣirāt* as "times" because of al-ʿAṣr.

**15. ثَجَّاجًا — *thajjājā*** · v.14
- **Root feeling:** the "**pouring in torrents**" family — *thajja* = to pour out copiously. ⭐ The Prophet ﷺ described the best Hajj as ***al-ʿajj wa'th-thajj*** — raising the voice in the *talbiyah*, and the **flowing** of the sacrifice.
- **Here it means:** "…water **pouring in torrents**."
- **Word magic:** after the squeezing comes the **gush**. *Thajjāj* is the second *faʿʿāl* intensive in two verses — the sun **blazes**, the rain **pours**. Both are gifts at **full strength**.
- **Grammar bite:** *faʿʿāl* intensive, adjective of *māʾan* (water).
- **Why this exact word:** *thajjāj* over "plentiful" — you can **hear** a downpour in the doubled *j*.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***al-ʿajj wa'th-thajj*** = the voice raised and the sacrifice **flowing** in Hajj. *Thajjāj* rain = water that **flows like that** — in torrents.

**16. حَبًّا وَنَبَاتًا — *ḥabban wa nabātā*** · v.15
- **Root feelings:** *ḥabb* — **grain**, seeds (*ḥabbah* = a single grain; also a pill!). *nabāt* — the "**sprouting**" family: *nabata* = to sprout; *nabāt* = **plants**; *nabātī* = **vegetarian** / vegetable (*zayt nabātī* = vegetable oil).
- **Here it means:** "so that We bring forth by it **grain and plants**."
- **Word magic:** the rain has a **purpose** — the *li-* in *li-nukhrija* means "**in order that**." Squeeze → pour → bring out: three verbs, one supply chain. And the verb *nukhrija* (We bring OUT) is the same idea as resurrection: **what is buried comes out**.
- **Grammar bite:** ***li-* + subjunctive = "in order that"** (*lām at-taʿlīl*): *li-nukhrija* — the final vowel turns to *-a*.
- **Why this exact word:** *ḥabb* (what you eat) and *nabāt* (what your animals eat) — the full food supply in two words.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *ḥabbah* = **a grain (or a pill)**; *nabātī* = **vegetarian**. The seed in the soil is a **rehearsal of the grave**: buried, then brought out.

**17. أَلْفَافًا — *alfāfā*** · v.16
- **Root feeling:** the "**wrapping / winding together**" family — *laffa* = to wrap; *lafīf* = intertwined; (Levantine Arabic: a ***laffeh*** is a **wrap sandwich**!).
- **Here it means:** "and gardens **dense with intertwined trees**."
- **Word magic:** the last gift is a garden so thick that the branches **wrap around each other**. The nine gifts end in **abundance** — and gardens will return in v.32 as the reward. **The world's garden is a preview of the next one.**
- **Grammar bite:** adjective of *jannātin*; a plural that some grammarians say has **no singular** in use (others give *lifīf*).
- **Why this exact word:** *alfāf* conveys **density** — not a few trees, but a tangle of them.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *laffa* = **to wrap**. Picture trees so close they've **wrapped themselves together** — gardens like a *laffeh*.

## §3 — The Appointment (v.17–20)

**18. يَوْمَ الْفَصْلِ — *yawma'l-faṣl*** · v.17
- **Root feeling:** the "**cutting apart / separating / deciding**" family — *faṣala* = to separate; *faṣl* = a school **term**, a **chapter**, a **season** (*fuṣūl* = seasons); *fāṣilah* = a **comma** — and the technical term for a **verse-ending** in the Qur'an.
- **Here it means:** "Indeed **the Day of Sorting** is an appointed time."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the Day is named by its **job**: it **separates** — truth from falsehood, the believer from the denier, the deed from the excuse. And it answers v.3: they *differed* about it (*mukhtalifūn*). **The Day of Separation is the end of their disagreement.**
- **Grammar bite:** *inna* + *kāna*: "**has been**, indeed, an appointment" — *kāna* here means "it was **always** so," fixed from the beginning.
- **Why this exact word:** *faṣl* over "judgment" — because the Day's first act is **sorting**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***fāṣilah* = a verse-ending** (and a comma). *Yawm al-faṣl* = the Day of **Separation** — where everything mixed is finally **separated**.

**19. مِيقَاتًا — *mīqātā*** · v.17
- **Root feeling:** the "**time / appointment**" family — *waqt* = time; *mīqāt* = an **appointed time or place**; ⭐ the ***mawāqīt*** of Hajj = the **boundary points** where pilgrims enter iḥrām.
- **Here it means:** "…**an appointed time**."
- **Word magic:** they asked *when* (in other surahs: *ayyāna*); the answer here is not a date but a **fact**: it **has** a date. Like a flight that is **already booked** — you don't know the hour, but the seat exists.
- **Grammar bite:** *mifʿāl* pattern (*mīqāt* ← *miwqāt*; the w turns to ī after kasra) — a word for **an instrument or appointed thing**. *Khabar* of *kāna*.
- **Why this exact word:** *mīqāt* carries **appointment**, not just "time" — someone **set** it.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ pilgrims stop at the **mīqāt** before entering Makkah. The Day has a *mīqāt* too: **fixed, scheduled, waiting**.

**20. الصُّورِ — *aṣ-ṣūr*** · v.18
- **Root feeling:** a **horn / trumpet** — the instrument blown to announce the end and the rising.
- **Here it means:** "the Day **the Horn** is blown…"
- **Word magic:** one blast is enough. The verb is **passive** — *yunfakhu* (it is blown) — no blower named; the event is too vast for a name. The sound goes out, and **everyone** responds (*fa-taʾtūna*, "and you come") — the *fa-* means **immediately**.
- **Grammar bite:** *yunfakhu* = passive present; *fī* = "into" the horn. *Yawma* is accusative of time, explaining v.17's "appointed time."
- **Why this exact word:** *ṣūr* is concrete — a **real instrument**, not a metaphor for "the end."
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⚠️ ***ṣūr* (horn) ≠ *ṣūrah* (picture/form) ≠ *sūrah* (chapter, with sīn).** One blast into the *ṣūr*, and everyone stands.

**21. أَفْوَاجًا — *afwājā*** · v.18
- **Root feeling:** the "**crowd / troop / wave of people**" family — *fawj* = a group moving together (modern Arabic: a **regiment** or **battalion**).
- **Here it means:** "…and you come forth **in crowds**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ you will meet this **exact word** again in Naṣr 110:2 — *yadkhulūna fī dīni'llāhi **afwājā*** — people entering Allah's religion **in crowds**. Same word, two crowds: here, rising from the graves toward judgment; there, entering faith in joy. **Which crowd you belong to there depends on which crowd you joined here.**
- **Grammar bite:** *ḥāl* (state): "you come **as** crowds." Broken plural of *fawj*.
- **Why this exact word:** *afwāj* shows **waves** of people, group after group — not one blob.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***afwāj* here (78:18) = crowds to judgment; *afwāj* in Naṣr (110:2) = crowds into Islam.** Same word. Choose your crowd.

**22. أَبْوَابًا — *abwābā*** · v.19
- **Root feeling:** the "**door / gate**" family — *bāb* = a door; *bawwāb* = a **doorman** (everyday Arabic!).
- **Here it means:** "and the sky is opened, and **becomes gateways**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ remember v.12: the sky was built ***shidād*** — tight, sealed, without a crack. Now it **opens** and becomes **nothing but doors**. The solid ceiling turns into openings. **Strength reversed into gaps** — that is what the Day does to everything you thought was permanent.
- **Grammar bite:** *fa-kānat abwābā* — "and it **became** doors"; *kāna* here means **became**, not "was."
- **Why this exact word:** *abwāb* over "split" — a door is an opening **with a purpose**: angels descend through them.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **v.12 sealed (*shidād*) → v.19 all doors (*abwāb*).** The ceiling you trusted becomes a hallway.

**23. سَرَابًا — *sarābā*** · v.20
- **Root feeling:** a **mirage** — the shimmer on hot sand that looks like water and is not there.
- **Here it means:** "and the mountains are set moving, and **become a mirage**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the pegs of v.7 — the most solid things on earth — end as **something you can see but cannot touch**. A mirage is the perfect picture: **appearance without reality**. And it is a quiet warning: so much of what you lean on is already, in a sense, a mirage.
- **Grammar bite:** *suyyirat* = Form II passive: "were **made to travel**" (*sāra* = to travel; *sayyārah* = a **car**!). *Fa-kānat* = and **became**.
- **Why this exact word:** *sarāb* is the most **dissolving** image possible — not broken, not scattered, **unreal**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***sayyārah* = a car — and the mountains will "drive away"** (*suyyirat*), until they're just a **mirage**. v.7 pegs → v.20 shimmer.

## §4 — The Ambush (v.21–30)

**24. مِرْصَادًا — *mirṣādā*** · v.21
- **Root feeling:** the "**watching / lying in wait**" family — *raṣada* = to watch, to lie in ambush; *marṣad* = an **observatory**; *raṣīd* = a **balance** in a bank account (what's being watched!).
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, **Hell lies in wait** — a lookout."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ Hell is not a place you **stumble** into — it is described as **waiting for you**, watching the road. The *mifʿāl* pattern makes it a **purpose-built** lookout. Chilling inversion: the people who wouldn't watch for the Day are **being watched** by its consequence.
- **Grammar bite:** *mifʿāl* pattern — a place or instrument **built for** an action; *khabar* of *kānat*.
- **Why this exact word:** *mirṣād* turns punishment into **ambush** — the danger of the unseen.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *marṣad* = **an observatory**. Hell is **an observatory pointed at the road** of those who transgress.

**25. الطَّاغِينَ — *aṭ-ṭāghīn*** · v.22
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**overflowing past all limits**" family — the original image is **water bursting its banks** and flooding everything; *ṭughyān* = tyranny.
- **Here it means:** "for **those who transgress**…"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ this is your **first meeting** with the root that runs through the whole juzʾ — Pharaoh *ṭaghā* (Nāziʿāt 79:17), Thamūd's *ṭaghwā* (Shams 91:11), man *yaṭghā* (ʿAlaq 96:6). Every time, hear the **river over its banks**: a person who would not stay inside his limits.
- **Grammar bite:** active participle, masculine plural, genitive after *li-* (for). From ṭ-gh-y, a weak-final root.
- **Why this exact word:** *ṭāghīn* names the sin as **excess** — not ignorance, but crossing every line.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ṭaghā* = water bursting its banks.** A *ṭāghī* is a **flood in human form**. You'll meet this root again and again.

**26. مَآبًا — *maʾābā*** · v.22 (and v.39)
- **Root feeling:** the "**returning home**" family — *āba* = to return; *awwāb* = one who **keeps returning** to Allah; and the travel duʿāʾ: ***āyibūna tāʾibūna*** — "**returning**, repenting…"
- **Here it means:** v.22 — "[Hell is] **a place of return** for the transgressors." v.39 — "whoever wills, let him take **a way of return** to his Lord."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **one word, two destinations, seventeen verses apart.** Everyone is "returning" somewhere — the question is only **where**. For the transgressor, the place he returns to is the Fire; for the one who chooses, the place he returns to is **his Lord**. A *maʾāb* is where you end up when you **come home** — and you choose your home.
- **Grammar bite:** *mafʿal* noun of place from a hollow root (ʾ-w-b): "a **place** of returning."
- **Why this exact word:** *maʾāb* over "destination" — it implies you **belong** there, that it was where you were always heading.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***maʾāb* in v.22 = Hell. *maʾāb* in v.39 = your Lord.** Same word. Every journey ends at a home — **pick yours.**

**27. لَابِثِينَ / أَحْقَابًا — *lābithīna / aḥqābā*** · v.23
- **Root feelings:** *lābith* — the "**staying / lingering**" family (*labitha* = to remain, to linger). *aḥqāb* — plural of *ḥuqb*: a **long age**, an era (classical estimates vary — some said eighty years each; the point is length).
- **Here it means:** "**remaining** therein for **ages upon ages**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the plural *aḥqāb* is given **no number** — ages, and ages, and ages, with no end in sight. And remember this root: in Nāziʿāt 79:46 the same verb measures their whole **earthly** life as *lam yalbathū illā ʿashiyyatan aw ḍuḥāhā* — "as if they lingered only an evening or a morning." **Same verb: here, ages; there, an afternoon. That is the exchange rate.**
- **Grammar bite:** *lābithīna* is a *ḥāl* (state) — "while **remaining**"; *aḥqāban* is an accusative of **time**.
- **Why this exact word:** *aḥqāb* is the longest time-word available — and left uncounted.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***labitha* = to linger.** Your life: **an afternoon.** Their stay: ***aḥqāb***, ages without number.

**28. بَرْدًا وَلَا شَرَابًا — *bardan wa lā sharābā*** · v.24
- **Root feelings:** *bard* — **coolness, cold** (*barrād* = a fridge; *bārid* = cold). *sharāb* — a **drink** (*shariba* = to drink; *sharāb* = a beverage).
- **Here it means:** "they will not taste therein any **coolness**, nor any **drink**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ this verse is the **first half of the surah's mirror**: *lā yadhūqūna fīhā…* — "they will not **taste** therein…" Hold it in your mind until v.35, where Paradise is described with the **same frame**: *lā yasmaʿūna fīhā…* — "they will not **hear** therein…" Two lists of what is **absent**: here, relief is absent; there, pain is absent.
- **Grammar bite:** *lā yadhūqūna* = present negated; *bardan* and *sharāban* are **objects** of "taste" — note you "taste" **coolness**: the senses are mixed, making the lack physical.
- **Why this exact word:** *bard* (coolness) is the very thing a burning person craves — denied by name.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***barrād* = a fridge.** No *bard*, no *sharāb* — and remember the frame **"lā ya…ūna fīhā"**: it returns in **v.35** for Paradise.

**29. حَمِيمًا وَغَسَّاقًا — *ḥamīman wa ghassāqā*** · v.25
- **Root feelings:** *ḥamīm* — the "**intense heat**" family (*ḥammām* = a **bathhouse**; *ḥummā* = a **fever**) — and ⭐ the same word means **a close, intimate friend** (Ḥāqqah 69:35). *ghassāq* — the "**darkness / dripping**" family: *ghasaq* = deep night (Isrāʾ 17:78); *ghassāq* = a **dark, foul, freezing** fluid.
- **Here it means:** "**except scalding water and a dark, foul discharge**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the cruellest grammar in the surah: ***illā*** — "**except**." After "no coolness, no drink," the word *except* raises hope for **one syllable** — and then delivers the opposite: drink that **scalds** and drink that is **filthy and freezing**. The two extremes, heat and cold, both used as punishment.
- **Grammar bite:** *istithnāʾ* (exception) after a negative; ⭐ *ghassāq* is the third ***faʿʿāl*** intensive in the surah (after *wahhāj*, *thajjāj*).
- **Why this exact word:** *ghassāq* packs **darkness, dripping, and stench** into one word — horror by accumulation.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ḥammām* = bathhouse; *ḥummā* = fever.** And the trap of ***illā***: "no drink — **except**…" — hope for one syllable, then the worst.

**30. جَزَاءً وِفَاقًا — *jazāʾan wifāqā*** · v.26
- **Root feelings:** *jazāʾ* — **recompense**, what you are paid back (*jazāka'llāhu khayran* = may Allah **repay** you with good). *wifāq* — the "**agreement / exact fit**" family: *muwāfaqah* = **approval**, agreement; ⭐ ***tawfīq*** = success that Allah **makes fit** your effort.
- **Here it means:** "**a fitting recompense**" — matching their deeds exactly.
- **Word magic:** ⭐ two words, one verse, and it is the surah's **justice principle**: nothing more than they did, nothing different from what they did. The punishment **fits like a key in a lock**. And hold *jazāʾ* in mind: in **v.36** it returns for Paradise — with something added that this verse does not have.
- **Grammar bite:** *mafʿūl muṭlaq*-style accusative: "**as** a fitting repayment." *Wifāq* is a *fiʿāl* verbal noun of Form III (*wāfaqa*).
- **Why this exact word:** *wifāq* insists on **precision** — Allah's justice is exact, not excessive.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***muwāfaqah* = agreement; *tawfīq* = Allah-given success.** *Jazāʾan wifāqā* = a payback that **agrees exactly** with what you did.

**31. لَا يَرْجُونَ حِسَابًا — *lā yarjūna ḥisābā*** · v.27
- **Root feelings:** *rajā* — the "**hope / expecting**" family (*rajāʾ* = hope; *arjūka* = **please**, literally "I hope of you"). *ḥisāb* — the "**counting / reckoning**" family (*ḥisāb* = arithmetic, an account, a **bill**; *ḥāsūb* = a **computer**!).
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, they **were not expecting a reckoning**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ here is the **reason** for everything in v.21–26: they lived as though no bill would ever come. And *ḥisābā* is the second word of the mirror — in **v.36**, the same word describes Paradise's gift. **The reckoning they didn't expect** becomes, for others, **a gift beyond reckoning**.
- **Grammar bite:** *kānū lā yarjūna* = "they **used to** not expect" — *kāna* + present = a **habit**, a lifestyle.
- **Why this exact word:** *rajā* (hope/expect) — they didn't just doubt the reckoning; they never even **anticipated** it.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ḥāsūb* = a computer; *ḥisāb* = a bill.** They lived as if **the bill would never come**.

**32. كِذَّابًا — *kidhdhābā*** · v.28 (and v.35)
- **Root feeling:** the "**lying / calling something a lie**" family — *kadhaba* = to lie; *kadhdhaba* (Form II) = to **call** something a lie, to deny; *kadhdhāb* = a habitual liar.
- **Here it means:** v.28 — "and they **denied** Our signs with **utter denial**." v.35 — "nor any **lying**" in Paradise.
- **Word magic:** ⭐ third word of the mirror: the denial they **practised** here is the very thing that is **never heard** there. They filled their lives with *kidhdhāb*; Paradise is defined, partly, by its **absence**. And the verb + its own noun (*kadhdhabū… kidhdhābā*) is Arabic's **underline key**: "they denied — and I mean **denied**."
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ *kidhdhāb* (with **kasra**) is a **verbal noun** of Form II — an old, heavy alternative to *takdhīb* — used here as a *mafʿūl muṭlaq* (cognate accusative). ⚠️ It sounds like the *faʿʿāl* intensives (*wahhāj*, *ghassāq*), but strictly it is the **fiʿʿāl** maṣdar.
- **Why this exact word:** the rare, heavy form makes the denial sound as **heavy as it was**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***kidhdhāb* in v.28 = what they did. *kidhdhāb* in v.35 = what is never heard in Paradise.** Same word, mirrored.

**33. أَحْصَيْنَاهُ — *aḥṣaynāhu*** · v.29
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**pebble / counting**" family — *ḥaṣā* = **pebbles**; *iḥṣāʾ* = **statistics** (counting by pebbles!); *ḥaṣāh* = a **kidney stone**.
- **Here it means:** "and **everything** We have **counted**, in a record."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the oldest counting method in the desert was **pebbles** — one stone per sheep. So *aḥṣā* means to count **one by one, missing nothing**. They expected no reckoning (v.27); Allah had been counting **everything** all along — and *kitābā* (in writing) means it is **recorded**, not just remembered.
- **Grammar bite:** Form IV past + pronoun; *kulla shayʾin* is **fronted** before the verb — "**every thing** — We counted it." The object first, for emphasis.
- **Why this exact word:** *aḥṣā* over "knew" — it is **itemised**: a pebble per deed.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***iḥṣāʾ* = statistics; *ḥaṣā* = pebbles.** **A pebble for every single deed** — and every pebble is written down.

**34. فَلَن نَّزِيدَكُمْ — *fa-lan nazīdakum*** · v.30
- **Root feeling:** the "**increasing / adding**" family — *zāda* = to increase; *ziyādah* = an increase; *mazīd* = **more** ("*hal min mazīd*" — "is there more?").
- **Here it means:** "**So taste! We will never increase you** except in punishment."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the language of a **gift**, delivering the opposite. *Dhūqū* (taste!) is what you say to a guest at a feast; *nazīdakum* (We'll give you more) is what a generous host promises. Here both are turned into punishment — the exit door closing. Some early scholars called this **the hardest verse on the people of the Fire**.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***lan* + subjunctive = "will never"** (emphatic future negation) — *nazīda* takes fatḥa. *Illā* = "except": **the only increase is more of the same.**
- **Why this exact word:** *zāda* — because they asked for **more** of everything in the dunya, and now they get **more**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ "***Taste!*** — and **We'll only give you more**." Host-language, turned inside out. ***lan* = never.**

## §5 — The Escape (v.31–36)

**35. الْمُتَّقِينَ — *al-muttaqīn*** · v.31
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**shielding / guarding**" family — *waqā* = to protect; *wiqāyah* = **protection** (modern: preventive medicine!); *taqwā* = keeping a **shield** between yourself and harm.
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, for **those who shielded themselves**…"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ *taqwā* is not "fear" in the cowering sense — it is **armour**. A *muttaqī* is a person who went through life **wearing a shield**: avoiding what harms, guarding what matters. And the word arrives right after Hell's ambush (*mirṣād*, v.21): **the ambush was waiting; the muttaqī was armoured**.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ Form VIII with assimilation: *iwtaqā* → ***ittaqā*** — the wāw melts into a doubled tāʾ. Participle: *muttaqī*, plural *muttaqīn*.
- **Why this exact word:** *muttaqīn* names the saved by their **method** — what they did, not just what they felt.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***wiqāyah* = protection (prevention).** *Taqwā* = **wearing a shield**, not trembling behind one.

**36. مَفَازًا — *mafāzā*** · v.31
- **Root feeling:** the "**winning / escaping to safety**" family — *fāza* = to win, to attain; *fawz* = **victory**; *fāʾiz* = a **winner** (everyday Arabic!).
- **Here it means:** "…**a place of triumph** / of safe escape."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ *fawz* carries **both** meanings at once: a great **win** and a safe **escape**. They escaped the ambush of v.21 **and** won the Garden of v.32. One word for being **rescued** and **rewarded** together.
- **Grammar bite:** *mafʿal* noun — place (or time) of winning; *ism* of *inna*, fronted *li'l-muttaqīn* = "**for the muttaqīn** [specifically]."
- **Why this exact word:** *mafāz* — because for them, the Day is not just survived; it is **won**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***fāʾiz* = a winner.** *Mafāz* = where you **win and escape** at the same time.

**37. حَدَائِقَ وَأَعْنَابًا — *ḥadāʾiqa wa aʿnābā*** · v.32
- **Root feelings:** *ḥadāʾiq* — plural of *ḥadīqah*: a **walled, enclosed garden** (*ḥadaqa* = to surround; ⭐ ***ḥadaqah*** = the **pupil** of the eye, encircled by the white). *aʿnāb* — **grapes** (*ʿinab*).
- **Here it means:** "**walled gardens and grapevines**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ remember v.16: the world had *jannātin alfāfā* — dense gardens. Now Paradise has *ḥadāʾiq* — gardens **with walls**: protected, private, **yours**. The world's gardens were open to weather and loss; these are **enclosed**. And grapes are named alone — the fruit that turns into **drink**, which arrives in v.34.
- **Grammar bite:** *ḥadāʾiqa* is a **diptote** (no tanwīn) — plural patterns like *faʿāʾil* usually are. It explains *mafāzā* (a *badal*, substitute).
- **Why this exact word:** *ḥadīqah* stresses **enclosure** — safety, after the ambush.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ḥadaqah* = the pupil of your eye** — protected inside a ring. A *ḥadīqah* is a garden **held safe inside a wall**.

**38. أَتْرَابًا — *atrābā*** · v.33
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**dust / earth**" family — *turāb* = dust; *tirb* = someone **of the same age** as you — classically, because you **played in the dust together** as children.
- **Here it means:** "and youthful companions **of equal age**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ hold this root — it comes back as the surah's **very last word**: *yā laytanī kuntu **turābā*** — "I wish I were **dust**" (v.40). **The same root gives Paradise's companions and Hell's despair.** For one, dust became **shared childhood**; for the other, dust is the **non-existence** he begs for.
- **Grammar bite:** adjective of *kawāʿib* (youthful maidens), accusative plural.
- **Why this exact word:** *atrāb* stresses **equality and companionship** — nobody old, nobody left out.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***tirb* = a playmate from the same dust.** And *turāb* (dust) is the surah's **last word** (v.40). One root, two endings.

**39. كَأْسًا دِهَاقًا — *kaʾsan dihāqā*** · v.34
- **Root feelings:** *kaʾs* — a **cup** (a full drinking-cup). *dihāq* — the "**filling to the brim**" family: *dahaqa* = to fill completely, to pour without stopping.
- **Here it means:** "and **a cup, full to the brim**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the mirror again: in v.24–25 the people of the Fire got **no drink** — then *except* scalding and foul fluid. Here: a cup **brimming over**, poured again and again. **Thirst answered with overflow.**
- **Grammar bite:** *dihāq* is a verbal noun used as an **adjective** ("a cup of overflowing") — Arabic often describes with a maṣdar for emphasis.
- **Why this exact word:** *dihāq* means **never empty** — the opposite of every cup in this world.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **v.24: no drink. v.34: a cup that's always full.** *Dihāq* = poured **until it spills**.

**40. لَغْوًا — *laghwan*** · v.35
- **Root feeling:** the "**empty, worthless talk**" family — *laghā* = to talk nonsense; *laghw* = idle chatter; and ⭐ modern Arabic ***ilghāʾ*** = **cancellation** — making something **void**, as worthless as empty talk.
- **Here it means:** "they will **not hear therein any idle talk**, nor lying."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the mirror's second half.** v.24: *lā **yadhūqūna** fīhā bardan wa lā sharābā* — they won't **taste** relief. v.35: *lā **yasmaʿūna** fīhā laghwan wa lā kidhdhābā* — they won't **hear** nonsense or lies. **Same frame, same rhythm, opposite meaning.** And Paradise is defined, again, by what is **missing**: no gossip, no deceit, no noise.
- **Grammar bite:** object of *yasmaʿūna*; *lā* repeated before *kidhdhāban* to make **each** absent separately.
- **Why this exact word:** *laghw* — because half the pain of this world is **what you have to listen to**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ilghāʾ* = cancellation.** Paradise has **no *laghw*** — no talk that should have been cancelled. **v.24 ↔ v.35**: the mirror.

**41. عَطَاءً حِسَابًا — *ʿaṭāʾan ḥisābā*** · v.36
- **Root feelings:** *ʿaṭāʾ* — the "**giving**" family: *aʿṭā* = to give; *ʿaṭāʾ* = a **gift**; *ʿaṭiyyah* = a present. *ḥisāb* — the **counting** root of v.27; here it means **amply sufficient** (*aḥsabahu* = to give someone until he says *ḥasbī*, "**enough!**").
- **Here it means:** "a recompense from your Lord — **a gift, amply reckoned**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the mirror's masterstroke.** v.26 gave the Fire *jazāʾan wifāqā* — a recompense that exactly **fits**. v.36 gives the Garden *jazāʾan* — **and then adds *ʿaṭāʾan***: a **gift**. **Justice for one; justice plus generosity for the other.** And *ḥisābā*, the very word the deniers never expected (v.27), now means "**enough to satisfy**" — the reckoning they feared became a gift **beyond counting**.
- **Grammar bite:** *jazāʾan* and *ʿaṭāʾan* are both accusatives of the same meaning, the second a *badal* (restatement) that **upgrades** the first.
- **Why this exact word:** *ʿaṭāʾ* reminds you that Paradise is **never just earned** — it is **given**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **v.26: *jazāʾ* that fits. v.36: *jazāʾ* + ʿ*aṭāʾ*.** The Fire gets justice; the Garden gets **justice and a gift**. And *ḥasbī* = "**enough!**"

## §6 — The Silence (v.37–40)

**42. خِطَابًا — *khiṭābā*** · v.37
- **Root feeling:** the "**addressing / speaking to**" family — *khaṭaba* = to address; *khuṭbah* = a **sermon**; *khiṭāb* = a **speech**, a formal address (also a letter); *khaṭīb* = a preacher (and, in everyday Arabic, a **fiancé** — the one who "addressed" the family!).
- **Here it means:** "…the Most Merciful: **none possess [the right of] address** from Him."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ even in the middle of naming Him **ar-Raḥmān** — the Most Merciful — the verse says no one **owns** a right to speak to Him on that Day. Mercy is His to give; **speech is His to permit**. It sets up v.38's silence.
- **Grammar bite:** *lā yamlikūna minhu* = "they do not **own** from Him" — the verb of ownership, for speech.
- **Why this exact word:** *khiṭāb* is **formal** address — like addressing a king. Not even that is allowed without leave.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *khuṭbah* = **a sermon**. On that Day, **nobody gives a khuṭbah** before Him — not without permission.

**43. الرُّوحُ / صَفًّا — *ar-rūḥu / ṣaffā*** · v.38
- **Root feelings:** *ar-Rūḥ* — **the Spirit** (Jibrīl, according to most). *ṣaff* — a **row, a rank** (*ṣaffa* = to line up); ⭐ the rows you stand in for prayer: *sawwū ṣufūfakum* — "straighten your **rows**."
- **Here it means:** "the Day **the Spirit** and the angels stand **in rows**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the scene is pure **stillness**. The greatest of the angels — the one who carried revelation — stands **in line**, silent, waiting. If **he** waits for permission, imagine everyone else. And the image of rows is **your own prayer posture**: every ṣalāh, you rehearse **standing in a row before your Lord**.
- **Grammar bite:** *ar-Rūḥ* is named **separately** from *al-malāʾikah* although he is an angel — *ʿaṭf al-khāṣṣ ʿala'l-ʿāmm*: mentioning the special one alongside the group **to honour him**.
- **Why this exact word:** *ṣaff* — order, discipline, waiting: the opposite of the crowds' chaos in v.18.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***ṣaff* = the row you stand in for prayer.** On that Day, **even the Rūḥ stands in a row** — silent.

**44. أَذِنَ / صَوَابًا — *adhina / ṣawābā*** · v.38
- **Root feelings:** *adhina* — the "**permission**" family, which ⭐ comes from ***udhun*** = **the ear** (permission is what you **give ear to**); *adhān* = the **call to prayer**. *ṣawāb* — the "**hitting the target**" family: *aṣāba* = to hit (the mark); *ṣawāb* = **what is correct**; *muṣībah* = a calamity (something that **hits** you).
- **Here it means:** "they will not speak — **except whom the Most Merciful permits**, and he says **what is right**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ two conditions to speak on that Day: **permission** (*adhina*), and **correctness** (*ṣawāb*). Not even a permitted speaker may say anything he likes — only what **hits the mark**. Compare Mursalāt 77:35–36: *lā yanṭiqūn · wa lā yuʾdhanu lahum* — they don't speak, and they are **not permitted**.
- **Grammar bite:** *illā man* = "except whoever" — an exception that **names the condition**; *ṣawāban* is the object of *qāla*.
- **Why this exact word:** *ṣawāb* over "truth" — it is speech that **lands exactly**, like an arrow.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***udhun* = ear → *idhn* = permission → *adhān* = the call.** And ***ṣawāb* = hitting the target** — on that Day, words must **hit the mark**.

**45. الْيَوْمُ الْحَقُّ — *al-yawmu'l-ḥaqq*** · v.39
- **Root feeling:** the "**truth / firmly established / due**" family — *ḥaqq* = truth, a **right**, what is **due**; *ḥaqīqah* = **reality**; *istiḥqāq* = **entitlement** (and a bill's **due date**!).
- **Here it means:** "**That is the True Day.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ *dhālika* — "**that**" — the far demonstrative, pointing at the Day as something **massive, standing apart**. They called it doubtful (v.3); it is called ***al-ḥaqq***: not just true, but **due** — owed, inevitable, like a debt that has reached its date. (You'll meet its peak in Ḥāqqah 69:1 and 69:51: *ḥaqq al-yaqīn*.)
- **Grammar bite:** *dhālika* (subject) + *al-yawmu* + *al-ḥaqqu* (adjective). Definite article on both: **THE** Day, **THE** true one.
- **Why this exact word:** *ḥaqq* answers the whole surah's opening: the "news" they **disputed** is the day that is **due**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***istiḥqāq* = a due date.** The Day is ***al-ḥaqq*** — the payment that has **fallen due**.

**46. أَنذَرْنَاكُمْ — *andharnākum*** · v.40
- **Root feeling:** the "**warning in advance**" family — *andhara* = to warn; *nadhīr* = a **warner**; and ⭐ ***nadhr*** = a **vow** — both bind something **in advance**.
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, **We have warned you** of a punishment **near at hand**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ after forty verses, the surah **addresses you directly** — "We have warned **you**." And the punishment is ***qarīb*** — **near**. Not near in years, perhaps, but near the way **death** is near: a breath away. The warning is the mercy: **you were told**.
- **Grammar bite:** Form IV past + *-kum* (you, plural); *ʿadhāban qarīban* = the thing warned of (second object).
- **Why this exact word:** *indhār* — a warning given **before** it is too late; that's the whole function of the surah.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***nadhr* = a vow; *indhār* = a warning** — both **bind the future in advance**. The surah ends: *you have been warned.*

**47. الْمَرْءُ / مَا قَدَّمَتْ يَدَاهُ — *al-marʾu / mā qaddamat yadāh*** · v.40
- **Root feelings:** *al-marʾ* — **the person**, a human (*imraʾah* = a woman; ⭐ ***muruwwah*** = **decency**, manliness — the quality of being a real person). *qaddama* — the "**sending forward**" family (*qaddama* = to put ahead; *muqaddimah* = an **introduction**!). *yadāh* — **his two hands**.
- **Here it means:** "the Day **a person** will look at **what his two hands have sent ahead**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the camera narrows from the whole universe to **one unnamed person**, looking at **one thing**: his own deeds, sent ahead of him like **luggage on a flight** — waiting for him at the destination. The hands are named because hands are **what do things**. Compare Qiyāmah 75:13: *bimā qaddama wa akhkhar* — what he sent ahead **and left behind**.
- **Grammar bite:** *yadāhu* = dual *yadāni* + pronoun — the **nūn drops** in iḍāfah (*yadā-hu*). *Mā* = "what" (relative), not "not."
- **Why this exact word:** *qaddamat* — deeds are **already there** before you arrive.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***muqaddimah* = an introduction** — what comes **ahead**. Your deeds are your **muqaddimah**: they arrive before you.

**48. يَا لَيْتَنِي كُنتُ تُرَابًا — *yā laytanī kuntu turābā*** · v.40
- **Root feelings:** *layta* — a particle for a wish that **cannot** come true ("if only…"). *turāb* — **dust**, earth — the root of *atrāb* (v.33).
- **Here it means:** "and the disbeliever will say: **Oh, I wish I were dust!**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the most devastating last line in the juzʾ.** Classical reports describe the scene: on that Day the animals are judged between one another and then told *be dust* — and the disbeliever, watching, **wishes he could be dust too**. The one who denied the Resurrection now begs for **non-existence**. And the root closes the mirror: *atrāb* (v.33) was Paradise's **shared childhood in the dust**; *turāb* (v.40) is the denier's **wish to be nothing**.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***yā laytanī*** = "Oh, would that I…" — *layta* expresses an **impossible** wish (contrast *laʿalla*, a **possible** hope, in ʿAbasa 80:3). *Turāban* is the *khabar* of *kuntu*.
- **Why this exact word:** *turāb* — the lowest, most nothing-like thing he can imagine; and he would **gladly** be it.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***layta* = an impossible wish.** The surah that began with people **asking** (v.1) ends with one person **wishing** — to be **dust**.

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐ **عَمَّ = عَنْ + مَا** ("about what?") — ⚠️ **not *ʿamm* (uncle).** And the rule: ***mā* loses its alif after a preposition** (*ʿamma, fīma, bima, lima, mimma*).
2. ⭐⭐ **The mirror** — **v.24 ↔ v.35** (*lā yadhūqūna fīhā / lā yasmaʿūna fīhā*), **v.26 ↔ v.36** (*jazāʾ* that fits / *jazāʾ* + *ʿaṭāʾ*), **v.27 ↔ v.36** (*ḥisāb* feared / *ḥisāb* given), **v.28 ↔ v.35** (*kidhdhāb* done / *kidhdhāb* absent). ⚠️ **Learn them as pairs.**
3. ⭐ **مَآبًا appears twice** — v.22 (Hell, for the transgressors) and v.39 (**to his Lord**, for whoever wills). Same word, opposite homes.
4. ⭐ **سَيَعْلَمُونَ** — *sa-* = **will** (a separate particle). ⚠️ Don't hunt for it in the root.
5. ⭐ **سُبَاتًا (v.9) is not "sleep"** — *nawm* is already in the verse. *Subāt* = a **cutting-off, rest** (*as-Sabt*, Saturday).
6. ⭐ **الْمُعْصِرَاتِ = rain-clouds** — same root as *al-ʿAṣr*, but ⚠️ **not "times."** Think *ʿaṣīr*, juice: the clouds are **squeezed**.
7. ⭐ **الصُّورِ (horn) ≠ صُورَة (picture) ≠ سُورَة (chapter).** One letter or one vowel apart.
8. ⭐ **كِذَّابًا** (kasra) is a **verbal noun** (*fiʿʿāl*), not the *faʿʿāl* intensive of *wahhāj, thajjāj, ghassāq*. ⚠️ Same heaviness, different pattern.
9. ⭐ **أَفْوَاجًا** — here, crowds rising to **judgment**; in Naṣr 110:2, crowds entering **Islam**. Same word.
10. ⭐ **يَا لَيْتَنِي (layta)** = an **impossible** wish. ⚠️ Contrast **لَعَلَّ (laʿalla)**, a **possible** hope (ʿAbasa 80:3).
