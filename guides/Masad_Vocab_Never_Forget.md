# Surah Al-Masad (111) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–5) · *(also called Sūrat al-Lahab / Tabbat)*

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**His nickname was "Father of FLAME" — and the surah tells him his destination is a fire "of FLAME."**

⭐ **v.1:** *tabbat yadā **abī LAHAB*** — "perish the hands of **Abū LAHAB** (Father of Flame)"
⭐ **v.3:** *sa-yaṣlā nāran dhāta **LAHAB*** — "he will burn in a fire **of FLAME**"

⭐⭐⭐ **SAME WORD — *lahab*.** ⭐ **His nickname (*Abū Lahab*, given for his ruddy, glowing face) becomes the description of his fire.** ⭐ **His name was his prophecy.**

⭐⭐ **And notice two more firsts:**
- ⭐ **He is the ONLY enemy of the Prophet ﷺ NAMED in the entire Qurʾān.** ⭐ **Abrahah? unnamed (Fīl 105). The hater in Kawthar 108? unnamed.** ⭐ **Abū Lahab — the Prophet's ﷺ own UNCLE — named.**
- ⭐ **It was revealed roughly a decade before his death.** ⭐ **All he had to do to "disprove" it was say the shahādah — even falsely.** ⭐ **He never did.** ⭐ **A living prophecy he could have broken any day, and didn't.**

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐⭐ ***ABŪ LAHAB* → *DHĀTA LAHAB*.** ⭐ **Father of Flame → a fire of flame.** ⭐ **The one enemy the Qurʾān names — and it names him by his destination.**

---

## The Big Picture

1. **The curse returned** (v.1) — ⭐ **may the hands of Abū Lahab perish — and he has perished.**
2. **Nothing helps** (v.2) — ⭐ **his wealth and what he earned avail him nothing.**
3. **His fire** (v.3) — ⭐ **a fire of flame.**
4. **His wife** (v.4–5) — ⭐ **carrier of firewood, a rope of palm-fibre on her neck.**

⭐ **Context: when the Prophet ﷺ first proclaimed his message publicly from Mount Ṣafā, his uncle Abū Lahab shouted: ⭐ ***"Tabban laka!"*** — "**May you PERISH!** Is this what you gathered us for?"** ⭐ **This surah answers with his own word.**

## §1 — The Curse Returned (v.1)

**1. تَبَّتْ ... وَتَبَّ — *tabbat… wa tabb*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**PERISHING / ruin / loss that ends in destruction**" family — *tabba* = **to perish, to be lost, to come to ruin**; ⭐ ***tabāb* = utter loss and destruction*** (⭐ **40:37: *wa mā kaydu Firʿawna illā fī **tabāb*** — "Pharaoh's plot was only in **ruin**"!).
- **Here it means:** "**Perish the hands of Abū Lahab — and he has perished.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **his own curse, sent back word for word — and the verb appears TWICE in two different functions.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **At Ṣafā, Abū Lahab shouted at the Prophet ﷺ: ⭐ ***"TABBAN laka!"*** ("**May you PERISH!**").** ⭐ **The surah's very first word: ⭐ ***TABBAT***.** ⭐ **The same root, returned to sender.** ⭐ **Compare Kawthar 108:3: they called him *abtar* (cut off) → "your hater is the *abtar*."** ⭐⭐ **Two consecutive-ish surahs, two insults thrown back.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **And *tabba* appears TWICE — with two different jobs:**
  - ⭐ ***TABBAT yadā* = a CURSE (*duʿāʾ*)** — ⭐ "**may** his hands perish"
  - ⭐ ***wa TABBA* = a STATEMENT OF FACT (*khabar*)** — ⭐ "**and he HAS perished**"
  
  ⭐⭐ **First a wish, then a verdict.** ⭐ **The curse isn't hoping — it's announcing what's already decided.** ⭐ **Past tense for a future certainty — the device you've seen all juzʾ.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Fīl 105:2's *taḍlīl* and Ṭāriq's *kayd*** — ⭐ **and note 40:37, Pharaoh's *kayd* "in *tabāb*."** ⭐ **The tyrant's plot and the uncle's hands — both *tabāb*.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***tabbat*** — past tense, **feminine** (agreeing with *yadā*, hands — feminine in Arabic). ⭐ ***wa tabba*** — past, masculine (him). ⚠️ ⭐ **Doubled root (ت-ب-ب).** ⭐ **The past tense as *duʿāʾ* is standard Arabic for curses and blessings** (*raḥimahu'llāh* = "may Allah have mercy on him").
- **Why this word:** *tabba* — ⭐ **because it was HIS word.** ⭐ **Returning an insult verbatim is the strongest possible reply.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **At Ṣafā he shouted "*TABBAN laka!*" → the surah's first word: *TABBAT*.** ⭐ **His own curse, returned.** ⭐⭐ ***Tabbat* = a CURSE ("may…"); *wa tabba* = a FACT ("and he HAS…").** ⭐ **Like Kawthar 108:3: *abtar* returned to sender.**

**2. يَدَا أَبِي لَهَبٍ — *yadā abī lahab*** · v.1
- **Root feelings:** *yadā* — ⭐ **the TWO HANDS** (dual of *yad*). *Abū Lahab* — ⭐ **"Father of FLAME"** — ⭐ **a *kunyah* (nickname), given for his **glowing, ruddy, handsome face**; his actual name was ***ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā*** ("servant of al-ʿUzzā," an idol).** ⭐ ***Lahab* = a FLAME, the leaping tongue of a fire** (⭐ *iltahaba* = to blaze up; ⭐ **modern *iltihāb* = INFLAMMATION — a word you use clinically!**).
- **Here it means:** "**the two hands of Abū Lahab**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **three things are happening in three words.**
  
  ⭐ **ONE — "HANDS" = his deeds, his efforts.** ⭐ **The hands are what DO things.** ⭐ **You own this from Nabaʾ 78:40** — *mā **qaddamat yadāh*** ("what his **two HANDS** sent forth"). ⭐ **Same dual, same meaning: the hands as the record of deeds.** ⭐ **Classical reports also mention he raised his hand to throw a stone at the Prophet ﷺ at Ṣafā.**
  
  ⭐ **TWO — the KUNYAH, not the NAME.** ⭐⭐ **His real name was *ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā* — "**servant of an idol**."** ⭐ **The Qurʾān will not call him "servant of al-ʿUzzā" — ⭐ **it uses his nickname instead.** ⭐ **Classical note: the Qurʾān avoids attaching *ʿabd* (servant) to an idol.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **THREE — *LAHAB* (FLAME) returns in v.3.** ⭐ ***Abū Lahab* (v.1) → *nāran dhāta lahab* (v.3).** ⭐ **The nickname given for his beautiful glowing face becomes the word for his fire.** ⭐ **The thing he was praised for becomes what he's punished in.**
  
  ⭐ **The clinical hook: ⭐ ***iltihāb* = INFLAMMATION*** — ⭐ **literally a "flaring up."** ⭐ ***Iltihāb al-zāʾidah* = appendicitis.** ⭐ **Same root as *lahab*.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And the naming itself is unique: ⭐ **the only enemy of the Prophet ﷺ named in the Qurʾān.** ⭐ **Compare Fīl 105:1 (Abrahah unnamed), Kawthar 108:3 (the hater unnamed).**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***yadā*** — **dual NOMINATIVE**, ⚠️ **the *nūn* of the dual (*yadāni*) is DROPPED in *iḍāfah*.** ⭐ ***Abī*** — **genitive of *Abū*** — ⭐ **one of the FIVE SPECIAL NOUNS (*al-asmāʾ al-khamsah*): *abū/abā/abī*** — ⭐ **case shown by the long vowel** (⭐ **same family as *dhū/dhā/dhī*, Takwīr 81:20, Fajr 89:5, Balad 90:14–15**).
- **Why this word:** *Abū Lahab* — ⭐ **because the nickname *is* the verdict.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***ABŪ LAHAB* (v.1) → *DHĀTA LAHAB* (v.3).** ⭐ **Father of Flame → a fire of flame.** ⭐⭐ ***ILTIHĀB* = INFLAMMATION** (clinical!) — same root. ⭐⭐ **Real name *ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā* ("servant of an idol") — the Qurʾān won't say it.** ⭐ **The ONLY enemy NAMED in the Qurʾān.** ⚠️ ***Abī* = one of the FIVE NOUNS** (like *dhī*).

## §2 — Nothing Helps (v.2)

**3. مَا أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُ مَالُهُ — *mā aghnā ʿanhu māluh*** · v.2
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**richness / sufficiency**" family — *ghaniya* = to be rich; ⭐ ***aghnā ʿan* = to AVAIL / suffice FOR someone***.
- **Here it means:** "**His wealth did not avail him.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **you own this phrase almost VERBATIM from Layl 92:11 — and the *ghinā* root is your most-developed thread.**
  
  ⭐ **Layl 92:11 — *wa **mā yughnī ʿanhu māluhu** idhā taraddā*** ("his wealth **will not avail him** when he falls")
  ⭐ **Masad 111:2 — ***mā aghnā ʿanhu māluhu*** ("his wealth **did not avail him**")
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **Same verb, same preposition, same subject.** ⭐ **One change: ⭐ **Layl = PRESENT (*yughnī*, "will not avail"), Masad = PAST (*aghnā*, "did not avail")**.** ⭐ **Layl stated the principle; Masad shows it already happened to a named man.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **And the whole *ghinā* thread — now SIX surahs:**
  - **ʿAbasa 80:5** — *mani'**staghnā*** (⭐ the chief who **considered himself needing nothing**)
  - **ʿAbasa 80:37** — *shaʾnun **yughnīh*** (⭐ a concern that **leaves room for nothing**)
  - **Ghāshiyah 88:7** — *lā **yughnī** min jūʿ* (⭐ Hell's food **doesn't suffice**)
  - **Layl 92:8, 11** — *istaghnā… mā **yughnī** ʿanhu māluh* (⭐ **self-sufficient → wealth won't avail**)
  - **Ḍuḥā 93:8** — *fa-**aghnā*** (⭐ **HE enriched you** — the redemption)
  - **ʿAlaq 96:7** — *an raʾāhu'**staghnā*** (⭐ **the CAUSE** of transgression)
  - ⭐ **Masad 111:2** — ***mā aghnā** ʿanhu māluh* (⭐ **didn't avail a NAMED man**)
  
  ⭐⭐ **The thread ends on a specific name.** ⭐ **Abū Lahab was wealthy and powerful in Quraysh; ⭐ **none of it helped.**
  
  ⭐ ⚠️ **Note: *mā* here is NEGATION ("did NOT"), not "what."** ⭐ **(Some read it as interrogative: "what did his wealth avail him?" — same effect.)**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **Form IV past** (*aghnā*), weak-final. ⭐ ***aghnā ʿan*** = "avail/suffice **for**" — ⚠️ **note the fixed preposition.** ⭐ ***Māluhu*** is the **subject**.
- **Why this word:** *aghnā* — ⭐ **to close the juzʾ's great thread about the illusion of self-sufficiency on one real, named person.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **Layl 92:11: *mā YUGHNĪ ʿanhu māluh* (PRESENT — "WILL not avail"). ⭐ **Masad 111:2: *mā AGHNĀ ʿanhu māluh* (PAST — "DID not avail").** ⭐ **Principle → named case.** ⭐⭐ **The *ghinā* thread across SIX surahs ends on a NAME.**

**4. وَمَا كَسَبَ — *wa mā kasab*** · v.2
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**EARNING / acquiring**" family — *kasaba* = **to earn, to gain, to acquire** (by effort); ⭐ ***kasb* = earnings, income***; ⭐ ***maksab* = a gain/profit.**
- **Here it means:** "**…nor what he earned.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **classical scholars read "what he EARNED" as something beyond money — and the reading is moving.**
  
  ⭐ **v.2 already said "his WEALTH" (*māluhu*).** ⭐ **So what's left for "what he EARNED" (*mā kasab*)?** ⭐⭐ **Many classical commentators: ⭐ **his CHILDREN.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **Because of a well-known hadith: ⭐ ***"The best of what a man eats is from his own earning, and his children are of his earning."*** ⭐ **Children counted as a man's *kasb*.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **And here's the irony: ⭐ **Kawthar 108:3** mocked the Prophet ﷺ as ***abtar*** — **sonless, cut off** — ⭐ **by people like Abū Lahab, who HAD sons.** ⭐ **Masad: ⭐ **his sons — "what he earned" — won't help him.**
  ⭐ **The man with sons mocked the man without; ⭐ **the sons availed nothing.**
  
  ⭐ **(Other readings: his status, his deeds, his power.)**
  
  ⭐ **You own *kasaba* from Muṭaffifīn 83:14** — *bal rāna ʿalā qulūbihim **mā kānū yaksibūn*** ("**what they used to EARN** has rusted over their hearts"). ⭐⭐ **Muṭaffifīn: what they earned RUSTED their hearts. Masad: what he earned didn't SAVE him.** ⭐ **Earning that corrodes, earning that fails.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***mā*** — relative ("what") — ⭐ **or a second negation ("nor did he earn anything [useful]").** ⭐ ***Kasaba*** — past tense.
- **Why this word:** *mā kasab* — ⭐ **broad enough to include his sons**, which completes the reply to the *abtar* mockery.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **"His wealth AND what he earned" — ⭐ "earned" = his CHILDREN** (hadith: "your children are of your earning"). ⭐⭐⭐ **He mocked the Prophet ﷺ as SONLESS (*abtar*, 108:3) — ⭐ **his own sons availed him nothing.** ⭐⭐ **Muṭaffifīn 83:14: what they EARNED rusted their hearts.**

## §3 — His Fire (v.3)

**5. سَيَصْلَىٰ نَارًا ذَاتَ لَهَبٍ — *sa-yaṣlā nāran dhāta lahab*** · v.3
- **Root feelings:** *yaṣlā* — ⭐ the "**being ROASTED / burned in**" family (*ṣaliya*) — ⚠️ **not the *ṣalāh* root.** *dhāt* — ⭐ **"possessor of / full of"** (feminine of *dhū*). *lahab* — ⭐ **FLAME.**
- **Here it means:** "**He will burn in a Fire of [blazing] flame.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **THE PAYOFF — and two threads close at once.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ ***LAHAB* — his name.** ⭐ **v.1: *Abū **LAHAB*** (Father of Flame). ⭐ **v.3: *nāran dhāta **LAHAB*** (a fire full of flame).** ⭐ **The nickname given in admiration of his glowing face becomes the substance of his fire.** ⭐ **"Father of Flame" — ⭐ **literally, the flame's father; now its fuel.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **And *ṣaliya* — your longest-running Hell-verb — now in EIGHT surahs:**
  - **Infiṭār 82:15** · **Muṭaffifīn 83:16** · **Inshiqāq 84:12** · **Aʿlā 87:12** · **Ghāshiyah 88:4** · **Layl 92:15** · ⭐ **Masad 111:3**
  
  ⭐ **⚠️ Watch: Aʿlā 87:12 has *yaṣlā* (roasted) and 87:15 *ṣallā* (prayed) — three verses apart. ⭐ **Same letters, different roots.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And note *sa-* (near future):** ⭐ **revealed roughly ten years BEFORE he died.** ⭐ **A man told "you WILL burn" — ⭐ **who had a decade to falsify the prophecy by simply embracing Islam, even outwardly.** ⭐ **He never did.** ⭐ **The *sa-* is a living, testable prediction.**
  
  ⭐ **The *dhāt* construction you own: ⭐ **Burūj 85:1** (*as-samāʾi **dhāti**'l-burūj*), **85:5** (*an-nāri **dhāti**'l-waqūd* — ⭐ **"the fire FULL OF fuel"**), **Fajr 89:7** (*Iram **dhāti**'l-ʿimād*), **Ṭāriq 86:11–12**.** ⭐⭐ **Burūj 85:5 — the persecutors' *nār dhāt al-waqūd* (fire full of FUEL); ⭐ **Masad 111:3 — the persecutor's *nār dhāt lahab* (fire full of FLAME).**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **سَـ + present = near future.** ⭐ ***dhāta*** — accusative of *dhāt* (feminine "possessor of"), describing *nāran*. ⚠️ ⭐ **Weak-final *yaṣlā*.**
- **Why this word:** *lahab* — ⭐ **because his name was already the word for flame.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **FATHER OF FLAME (v.1) → FIRE OF FLAME (v.3).** ⭐ **His nickname became his fire.** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Ṣaliya* — EIGHT surahs now.** ⚠️ **Not the *ṣalāh* root.** ⭐⭐ ***Sa-* = "WILL" — said ~10 years before his death; he could have falsified it any day.** ⭐ **Burūj 85:5: fire full of FUEL. Masad 111:3: fire full of FLAME.**

## §4 — His Wife (v.4–5)

**6. وَامْرَأَتُهُ حَمَّالَةَ الْحَطَبِ — *wa'mraʾatuhu ḥammālata'l-ḥaṭab*** · v.4
- **Root feelings:** *imraʾah* — **a woman/wife** (⭐ **same root as *al-marʾ*, "the person," Nabaʾ 78:40, ʿAbasa 80:34**). *ḥammālah* — ⭐ the "**CARRYING**" family — *ḥamala* = to carry; ⭐ ***ḥammāl* = a PORTER*** (one who carries loads for a living!); ⭐ ***ḥammālah* = a habitual/professional carrier** (the *faʿʿālah* intensive). *ḥaṭab* — ⭐ **FIREWOOD, kindling, dry sticks for burning.**
- **Here it means:** "**And his wife — the carrier of firewood.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **a phrase with two meanings, both classical, both devastating — and an unusual grammatical case.**
  
  ⭐ **His wife was Umm Jamīl (Arwā bint Ḥarb), sister of Abū Sufyān — ⭐ **a woman of high status in Quraysh.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **READING ONE — LITERAL:** ⭐ **she gathered thorny branches and **scattered them on the Prophet's ﷺ path** at night, to hurt his feet.** ⭐ **A "carrier of firewood" — the thorny kind.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **READING TWO — IDIOMATIC:** ⭐ **in Arabic, *ḥammālat al-ḥaṭab* ("the firewood-carrier") was an established expression for ⭐ **a TALEBEARER — one who spreads gossip and slander to kindle hostility between people.** ⭐ **She carries the "kindling" that sets people against each other.** ⭐ **Rumours as firewood.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **And reading two connects to Humazah 104:1** — ⭐ ***wayl li-kulli humazatin lumazah*** — ⭐ **woe to every SLANDERER, FAULT-FINDER.** ⭐ **Humazah named the type; Masad gives it a face — ⭐ **a talebearer who lit fires between people.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And READING THREE (eschatological, often combined):** ⭐ **in Hell, she will carry the firewood for her husband's fire.** ⭐ **The couple who fuelled hostility in life fuel each other's flame.** ⭐ **v.3: *his* fire of flame. v.4: *she* carries the wood.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **The *faʿʿālah* intensive (*ḥammālah*) — a habitual carrier** — ⭐ **the same shape you've tracked: *lawwāmah* (75:2), *tawwāb* (110:3), *wahhāj* (78:13).** ⭐ **It's a character, not a single act.**
  
  ⭐⭐ ⚠️ **THE GRAMMAR GEM: *ḥammālata* is ACCUSATIVE** — ⭐ **in the standard Ḥafṣ reading, where you'd expect nominative.** ⭐ **Classical grammarians call this ***naṣb ʿala'dh-dhamm*** — ⭐ **accusative of CENSURE**: ⭐ **"and his wife — **I mean/I condemn** the carrier of firewood."** ⭐ **The grammar itself points a finger.** ⭐ **(Other recitations read *ḥammālatu*, nominative — both established.)**
  
  ⭐ **Free hook: ⭐ ***ḥammāl* = a PORTER*** — someone who carries loads for pay.
- **Grammar bite:** ⚠️ ⭐ ***ḥammālata*** = **accusative of censure** (*naṣb ʿala'dh-dhamm*) in Ḥafṣ. ⭐ ***Imraʾatuhu*** — nominative, joined to the subject of *sa-yaṣlā* (she burns too).
- **Why this word:** *ḥammālat al-ḥaṭab* — ⭐ **one phrase covering her literal cruelty, her gossip, and her fate.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Ḥammālat al-ḥaṭab* = a TALEBEARER** (Arabic idiom: carrying KINDLING between people). ⭐ **Also: she strewed THORNS on his path. Also: she carries wood for her husband's fire.** ⭐⭐ **Humazah 104:1 named the type; Masad gives it a FACE.** ⭐⭐ ⚠️ ***Ḥammālata* = ACCUSATIVE OF CENSURE** — the grammar points a finger. ⭐ ***Ḥammāl* = a PORTER.**

**7. فِي جِيدِهَا حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍ — *fī jīdihā ḥablun min masad*** · v.5
- **Root feelings:** *jīd* — ⭐ **THE NECK** — ⭐ **specifically a long, graceful neck, the place where JEWELLERY is worn** (different from *raqabah* or *ʿunuq*). *ḥabl* — ⭐ **A ROPE** (⭐ ***ḥabl Allāh* = the rope of Allah, 3:103**; ⭐ ***ḥabl al-warīd* = the jugular vein, 50:16** — "closer than the jugular"). *masad* — ⭐ **coarse rope of twisted PALM-FIBRE** — ⭐ **the roughest, cheapest rope; from *masada* = to twist tightly.**
- **Here it means:** "**Around her neck is a rope of twisted palm-fibre.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **the final image of the surah is a NECKLACE replaced by a ROPE — and it's historically specific.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **Classical reports: ⭐ **Umm Jamīl owned a valuable necklace, and swore she would spend it in opposing the Prophet ﷺ.** ⭐ **And *jīd* is specifically ⭐ **the neck AS A PLACE FOR ORNAMENT** — ⭐ **the word you'd use for a beautiful neck with jewellery on it.**
  
  ⭐⭐⭐ **So the verse says: ⭐ **where the precious necklace hung — ⭐ a rope of the COARSEST palm-fibre.** ⭐ **The finest ornament → the roughest rope.** ⭐ **And it fits the firewood-carrier image (v.4): ⭐ **a rope like the ones used to bind bundles of firewood on a labourer's back.** ⭐ **The noblewoman ends with a porter's rope.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **The NECK thread across your surahs:**
  - **Insān 76:4** — ***aghlāl*** (⭐ **neck-collars** in Hell)
  - **Balad 90:13** — *fakku **raqabah*** (⭐ **freeing a NECK** — the steep climb)
  - ⭐ **Masad 111:5** — *fī **jīdihā** ḥablun* (⭐ **a rope on a NECK**)
  
  ⭐ **Free the neck of another (Balad), or find a rope on your own (Masad).**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And the surah is NAMED after this last, humblest word — *al-Masad*.** ⭐ **Like Māʿūn (107), named after the smallest kindness; Masad named after the coarsest rope.**
  
  ⭐ **And *ḥabl* has a beautiful opposite: ⭐ ***ḥabl Allāh*** — **"hold fast to the ROPE of Allah"** (3:103).** ⭐ **One rope saves; this one binds.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **a nominal sentence with fronted predicate**: *fī jīdihā* (predicate, fronted) + *ḥablun* (subject, indefinite). ⭐ **Fronting = emphasis: "**ON HER NECK** — a rope."** ⭐ ***Min masad*** = "**made of** palm-fibre" (*min* of material).
- **Why this word:** *jīd* (**the ornamented neck**) rather than *ʿunuq* — ⭐ **to set the jewellery against the rope.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***JĪD* = the neck WHERE JEWELLERY HANGS.** ⭐ **She vowed her precious NECKLACE against the Prophet ﷺ → ⭐ **a rope of the COARSEST palm-fibre.** ⭐⭐ ***Ḥabl Allāh* (3:103) = the rope that SAVES; *ḥabl min masad* = the rope that BINDS.** ⭐⭐ **Neck thread: collars (76:4), FREE a neck (90:13), a ROPE on hers (111:5).** ⭐ **The surah is named after its humblest word.**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **لَهَبٍ appears TWICE (v.1, v.3)** — ⚠️ **his NICKNAME and his FIRE.** ⭐ ***Abū Lahab* → *dhāta lahab*.** **Read them together.**
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **حَمَّالَةَ (v.4)** — ⚠️ **ACCUSATIVE in Ḥafṣ = *naṣb ʿala'dh-dhamm* (accusative of CENSURE).** ⭐ **Not a mistake — the grammar points a finger.** ⭐ **(Other recitations: nominative.)**
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **حَمَّالَةَ الْحَطَبِ** — ⚠️ **an Arabic IDIOM for a TALEBEARER** (carrying kindling between people), **plus** the literal thorns, **plus** her fate. **Don't flatten it to one.**
4. ⭐⭐ **تَبَّتْ ... وَتَبَّ (v.1)** — ⚠️ **first a CURSE (*duʿāʾ*), then a STATEMENT (*khabar*).** ⭐ **Same verb, two functions.**
5. ⭐⭐ **وَمَا كَسَبَ (v.2)** — ⚠️ **widely read as "his CHILDREN"** (hadith: children are of a man's earning). ⭐ **Not just a repeat of "wealth."**
6. ⭐⭐ **جِيدِهَا (v.5)** — ⚠️ **specifically the neck AS A PLACE FOR JEWELLERY**, not just "neck." ⭐ **Necklace → rope.**
7. ⭐ **أَبِي لَهَبٍ** — ⚠️ **a KUNYAH (nickname)**, not his name (*ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā*). ⭐ **And *abī* is one of the FIVE NOUNS** (genitive by long vowel).
8. ⭐ **يَدَا (v.1)** — ⚠️ **DUAL nominative with the *nūn* DROPPED** (in *iḍāfah*).
9. ⭐ **سَيَصْلَىٰ (v.3)** — ⚠️ ***ṣaliya* (roasted), NOT *ṣalāh*.** ⭐ **And *sa-* = a testable prophecy, ~10 years before his death.**
10. ⭐ **مَا أَغْنَىٰ (v.2)** — ⚠️ ***mā* = NEGATION** ("did not") — or rhetorical "what did it avail?" — ⭐ **not the relative "what."**
