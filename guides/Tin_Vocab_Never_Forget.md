# Surah At-Tīn (95) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–8)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**Two verses hold the highest and lowest points in the Qurʾān's description of a human being — back to back.**

⭐ **v.4:** *laqad khalaqna'l-insāna fī **AḤSANI TAQWĪM*** — "**the BEST possible form**."
⭐ **v.5:** *thumma radadnāhu **ASFALA SĀFILĪN*** — "**the LOWEST of the low**."

⭐ **Both are superlatives.** ⭐ ***Aḥsan* = the most beautiful. *Asfal* = the very lowest.** ⭐ **And *sāfilīn* is itself a plural of "low ones" — ⭐ **so it's "the lowest **of those who are already low**." ⭐ **A superlative stacked on a plural.**

⭐⭐ **One verse apart, and the only thing connecting them is *thumma* ("then").** ⭐ **No explanation. No transition. Just: made at the top → returned to the bottom.**

⭐ **And then v.6 carves out the exception — the only escape hatch in the surah.**

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐⭐ **Highest form, then lowest of the low, one verse apart.** ⭐ **The distance a human can travel is the surah's entire subject.**

---

## The Big Picture

1. **Four oaths** (v.1–3) — ⭐ **the fig, the olive, Mount Sinai, and this secure city.**
2. **The height** (v.4) — ⭐ **created in the best possible form.**
3. **The fall** (v.5) — ⭐ **returned to the lowest of the low.**
4. **The exception** (v.6) — ⭐ **except those who believed and did righteous deeds.**
5. **Two closing questions** (v.7–8) — ⭐ *so what makes you deny the Reckoning? Is Allah not the most just of judges?*

## §1 — Four Oaths (v.1–3)

**1. وَالتِّينِ وَالزَّيْتُونِ — *wa't-tīni wa'z-zaytūn*** · v.1
- **Root feelings:** *tīn* — ⭐ **the FIG**. *zaytūn* — ⭐ **the OLIVE** (⭐ *zayt* = **oil**! — the olive is literally "the oil-thing") — ⭐ **you own it from ʿAbasa 80:29** (*wa **zaytūnan** wa nakhlā*).
- **Here it means:** "**By the fig and the olive.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the most-discussed oath in this juzʾ, and both main readings are worth holding.**
  
  ⭐ **READING ONE — the literal fruits.** ⭐ **Two of the most nourishing, useful plants known to that world.** ⭐ **The fig is eaten whole, seed and skin — nothing wasted. ⭐ **The olive gives food, oil, fuel, light, and medicine.** ⭐ **Two plants that give everything they have.**
  
  ⭐ **READING TWO — two LANDS.** ⭐ **Many classical scholars read *tīn* and *zaytūn* as pointing to the regions famous for them — ⭐ **the land of Damascus/Jerusalem** — ⭐ **which makes all four oaths a **map of revelation**:
  - **v.1** — ⭐ **fig & olive** → the land of **ʿĪsā** (peace be upon him)
  - **v.2** — ⭐ **Mount Sinai** → **Mūsā** (peace be upon him)
  - **v.3** — ⭐ **this secure city** → **Muḥammad ﷺ**
  
  ⭐⭐ **Read that way, the oaths trace prophethood across three places, ending at Makkah.** ⭐ **A geographical chain, sworn in order.**
  
  ⭐ **And *zayt* = OIL is a free hook** — ⭐ **you know it from *zayt zaytūn* (olive oil) in any Arabic kitchen.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **the *wāw* is *wāw al-qasam*** (the "by" of oaths), so both nouns are **genitive**. ⭐ **Note the surah's rhyme: *-īn / -ūn / -īm* — ⭐ **a nasal ending sustained across all eight verses.**
- **Why this word:** starting with **two humble fruits** and ending at **the Kaʿbah** ⭐ **moves from the ordinary to the sacred**, which is this juzʾ's signature method.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***Zayt* = OIL** (*zayt zaytūn* = olive oil). ⭐⭐ **And the four oaths may be a MAP: ⭐ fig & olive (ʿĪsā's land) → Sinai (Mūsā) → this city (Muḥammad ﷺ).** ⭐ **Prophethood, in geographical order.**

**2. وَطُورِ سِينِينَ — *wa ṭūri sīnīn*** · v.2
- **Root feelings:** *ṭūr* — ⭐ **a MOUNTAIN** (a Syriac-origin word absorbed into Arabic, used specifically for **Mount Sinai** in the Qurʾān). *Sīnīn* — **Sinai** (also appears as *Sīnāʾ*).
- **Here it means:** "**and by Mount Sinai.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the mountain where Mūsā (peace be upon him) received revelation and spoke with his Lord.**
  
  ⭐ **You own the Mūsā-at-the-mountain scene from Nāziʿāt 79:16** — *idh nādāhu rabbuhu bi'l-wādi'l-muqaddasi **Ṭuwā*** ("when his Lord called him in the sacred valley of **Ṭuwā**"). ⭐ **Same event, two surahs — ⭐ **Nāziʿāt names the valley, Tīn names the mountain.**
  
  ⭐ **And note the escalation of the oath-objects:** ⭐ **v.1 = two **plants** (small, everyday). ⭐ **v.2 = a **mountain** (huge, singular, historic). ⭐ **v.3 = a **city** (inhabited, sacred).** ⭐ **Plant → mountain → city: from what grows, to what stands, to where people live.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***iḍāfah*** ("the mountain **of** Sinai") — *ṭūri* is genitive after the oath-*wāw*, and *Sīnīn* is genitive by *iḍāfah*. ⚠️ ⭐ **Note *Sīnīn* keeps its *-īn* ending to carry the rhyme.**
- **Why this word:** naming ⭐ **the specific mountain of revelation** ties this surah's argument to the chain of prophets.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ ***Ṭūr* = MOUNTAIN** (specifically Sinai in the Qurʾān). ⭐ **Nāziʿāt 79:16 named the VALLEY (Ṭuwā); Tīn 95:2 names the MOUNTAIN.** ⭐ **Same event.** ⭐ **Oaths escalate: plants → mountain → city.**

**3. وَهَٰذَا الْبَلَدِ الْأَمِينِ — *wa hādha'l-baladi'l-amīn*** · v.3
- **Root feelings:** *balad* — ⭐ **a city/town** — ⭐ **you own it from Balad 90:1–2** (*lā uqsimu bi-**hādha'l-balad***). *amīn* — ⭐ the "**safety / security / trust**" family — ***amn* = security***; ***amānah* = a trust***; ***īmān* = FAITH***; ⭐ **and ***al-Amīn* — the Prophet's ﷺ own title*** before prophethood — ⭐ **you own it from Takwīr 81:21** (*muṭāʿin thamma **amīn***).
- **Here it means:** "**and by this secure city.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **you own this phrase almost exactly, and the comparison is illuminating.**
  
  ⭐ **Balad 90:1 — *lā uqsimu bi-**hādha'l-balad*** ("I swear by **THIS city**").
  ⭐ **Tīn 95:3 — *wa **hādha'l-baladi'l-amīn*** ("and by **THIS SECURE city**").
  
  ⭐ **Same demonstrative (*hādhā*), same noun (*al-balad*) — ⭐ **Tīn adds *al-amīn* (**the secure**).**
  
  ⭐⭐ **And the contrast is remarkable.** ⭐ **Balad 90:2 immediately said *wa anta **ḥillun** bi-hādha'l-balad* — ⭐ **"you are UNBOUND / treated as fair game in this city."** ⭐ **Tīn calls the same city ***al-amīn*** — **the SECURE one.**
  
  ⭐ **The city is *secure* by divine designation — and yet its own Prophet ﷺ wasn't safe in it.** ⭐ **Two surahs, one city, two truths held together.**
  
  ⭐ **And the ***amīn*** hook is superb:** ⭐ ***amn* (security), *amānah* (a trust), *īmān* (FAITH), *al-Amīn* (his title), and *āmīn* (the word you say after al-Fātiḥah)*** — ⭐ **all from one root of **security and trustworthiness**.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***al-amīn*** is an adjective agreeing with *al-balad* in **case, definiteness, gender, and number** — ⭐ **the four-way agreement rule for Arabic adjectives.** ⭐ **Both genitive after the oath.**
- **Why this word:** *amīn* over "sacred" — ⭐ **because *security* is what the sanctuary actually *provides*, and it's the same root as *faith*.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Amn* (security), *amānah* (a trust), *ĪMĀN* (FAITH), *al-Amīn* (his title), *āmīn*** — ⭐ **ALL ONE ROOT.** ⭐ **Faith and safety are the same word-family.** ⭐ **And Balad 90:1 swore by "THIS city" too.**

## §2 — The Height and the Fall (v.4–5)

**4. فِي أَحْسَنِ تَقْوِيمٍ — *fī aḥsani taqwīm*** · v.4
- **Root feelings:** *aḥsan* — ⭐ **superlative of *ḥasan* (good/beautiful)** = **the BEST/most beautiful** — ⭐ **you own the family from *al-ḥusnā*** (Layl 92:6) and ***al-asmāʾ al-ḥusnā***. *taqwīm* — ⭐ **Form II verbal noun from *qāma* (to stand)** = ⭐ **the act of making something STAND UPRIGHT, straightening it, giving it proper form and proportion** — ⭐ **the same root as *mustaqīm* (straight — as in *aṣ-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*!)** and ***taqwīm* = a CALENDAR*** in modern Arabic (something set in proper order!).
- **Here it means:** "**We have certainly created man in the best form / stature.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the root is about *standing upright*, and that's not decoration.**
  
  ⭐ ***Taqwīm* = making something stand straight and properly proportioned.*** ⭐ **So *aḥsan taqwīm* isn't just "beautiful" — ⭐ **it's **optimally structured, uprightly built, correctly ordered.**
  
  ⭐ **And you say this root **seventeen times a day**:** ⭐ ***ihdina'ṣ-ṣirāṭa'l-**MUSTAQĪM*** — "guide us to the **STRAIGHT** path."** ⭐ **Same root.** ⭐ **You were made *upright* (v.4); you ask to be kept on the *upright* road.**
  
  ⭐ **You also own it from Takwīr 81:28** — *li-man shāʾa minkum an ***yastaqīm*** ("for whoever wills to **go straight**"). ⭐ **Made upright → asked to stay upright.**
  
  ⭐ **And this connects to the *sawwā* thread:** ⭐ **Aʿlā 87:2 (*khalaqa fa-sawwā*), Infiṭār 82:7 (*fa-sawwāka fa-ʿadalak*), Shams 91:7.** ⭐ **Different root, same claim: **you were built to spec.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **لَقَدْ + past = strong affirmation.** ⭐ ***Aḥsani*** is a superlative in *iḍāfah* with *taqwīm* — ⭐ **"the best OF forming."** ⭐ **Note *fī* = "**in**" (immersed in that state), same as Balad 90:4's *fī kabad*.
- **Why this word:** *taqwīm* (**uprightness/proper standing**) over a word for beauty — ⭐ **because the fall in v.5 is precisely a *collapse from upright*.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Taqwīm* ← *qāma* (to STAND).* ⭐⭐ **Same root as *aṣ-ṣirāṭ al-MUSTAQĪM* — you say it 17× a day.** ⭐ **You were made UPRIGHT; you ask to stay UPRIGHT.** ⭐ **And *taqwīm* = a CALENDAR in modern Arabic (properly ordered).**

**5. ثُمَّ رَدَدْنَاهُ أَسْفَلَ سَافِلِينَ — *thumma radadnāhu asfala sāfilīn*** · v.5
- **Root feelings:** *radadnā* — ⭐ the "**returning / sending back**" family — *radda* = to send back; ⭐ **you own it from Nāziʿāt 79:10** (*a-innā la-**mardūdūna** fi'l-ḥāfirah*). *asfal* — ⭐ **superlative of *sāfil* (low)** = **the LOWEST**; *safala* = to be low, to sink; ⭐ ***sufl* = the bottom part.***
- **Here it means:** "**Then We returned him to the lowest of the low.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **the harshest sentence in the surah, and the grammar makes it worse than any translation shows.**
  
  ⭐ **ONE — it's a SUPERLATIVE ON A PLURAL.** ⭐ ***Asfala sāfilīn*** = "**the lowest of [those who are already] low ones**."** ⭐ **Not "very low" — ⭐ **the bottom of a group that is itself at the bottom.**
  
  ⭐ **TWO — the verb is *radadnā*, "we RETURNED him."** ⭐ **Not "we lowered him" — ⭐ **RETURNED**, as if the low place is where he came from, or where he belongs once he abandons the upright form.**
  
  ⭐ **THREE — the connector is just *thumma* ("then").** ⭐ **No explanation. No "because." ⭐ **Highest form, *then*, lowest of the low.** ⭐ **The silence between the verses is deliberate — you supply the reason from v.6.**
  
  ⭐ **And note the perfect *muqābala* (mirror-antithesis):**
  | v.4 | v.5 |
  |---|---|
  | ***AḤSANI*** (best) | ***ASFALA*** (lowest) |
  | *taqwīm* (standing UPRIGHT) | *sāfilīn* (the LOW ones) |
  
  ⭐ **Two superlatives, opposite directions, one verse apart.**
  
  ⭐ **Classical readings of *asfala sāfilīn*:** ⭐ **the lowest depths of Hell; or decrepit old age (a reversal of strength); or moral degradation.** ⭐ **The exception in v.6 supports the *moral/eschatological* reading most strongly.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***asfala*** is a **superlative in *iḍāfah***, accusative. ⚠️ ⭐ **Note *sāfilīn* is a sound masculine plural in the genitive (*-īn*), carrying the rhyme.** ⭐ **And *radadnā* is a **doubled verb** (ر-د-د) — the doubled letters separate in the past tense with *-nā*.
- **Why this word:** *radadnā* (**returned**) rather than "cast down" — ⭐ **it implies a place he is sent *back* to, which is more damning.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Asfala sāfilīn* = "the LOWEST of the [already] LOW."** ⭐ **A superlative stacked on a plural.** ⭐⭐ **And the only word between v.4 and v.5 is *thumma* — "then." ⭐ No explanation given.** ⭐ **Best form → lowest of the low.**

## §3 — The Exception (v.6)

**6. أَجْرٌ غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍ — *ajrun ghayru mamnūn*** · v.6
- **Root feelings:** *ajr* — **a wage/reward** (*ajīr* = a hired worker — ⭐ **wage language, not gift language**). *mamnūn* — ⭐ the "**favour / cutting off**" family — ⭐ **a *magnificent* double root**: *minnah* = ⭐ **a favour held over someone's head, a reminder of obligation** (*mann* = to remind someone of what you did for them); ⭐ **AND *manna* = to CUT OFF / diminish.** ⭐ **Both senses live in *mamnūn*.**
- **Here it means:** "**Except those who believed and did righteous deeds — for them is a reward uninterrupted / without reproach.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the double meaning of *mamnūn* gives you two promises for the price of one, and both are classical.**
  
  ⭐ **READING ONE — "**never cut off**."** ⭐ **From *manna* = to sever/diminish. ⭐ **The reward never stops, never shrinks, never runs out.**
  
  ⭐ **READING TWO — "**without a favour held over you**."** ⭐ **From *minnah*. ⭐ **This is the deeper one: ⭐ **you will never be *reminded* of the debt.** ⭐ **Nobody will say "look what I gave you."** ⭐ **The reward comes with no strings, no obligation, no reminder attached.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **Think about how rare that is.** ⭐ **Almost every human gift comes with an invisible invoice.** ⭐ **This one doesn't.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **AND — you own this ENTIRE PHRASE verbatim from Inshiqāq 84:25:**
  ⭐ **Inshiqāq 84:25 — *illa'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti lahum **ajrun ghayru mamnūn***.**
  ⭐ **Tīn 95:6 — *illa'lladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilu'ṣ-ṣāliḥāti fa-lahum **ajrun ghayru mamnūn***.**
  ⭐ **ALMOST WORD-FOR-WORD IDENTICAL** (only the *fa-* differs). ⭐ **Free vocabulary.**
  
  ⭐ **And note: this is the ONLY escape hatch in the surah.** ⭐ **The fall in v.5 was universal until *illā* ("except") arrived.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***ghayru*** = "**other than / without**" — ⭐ **it takes the following noun in the genitive** (*ghayru mamnūn**in***). ⭐ **A very common construction: *ghayr* + genitive = negation of an adjective.**
- **Why this word:** *mamnūn* over "endless" — ⭐ **because the double sense covers **both** duration **and** dignity.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Mamnūn* = (a) CUT OFF and (b) a FAVOUR HELD OVER YOUR HEAD.** ⭐ **So: **never-ending AND never thrown back in your face.** ⭐⭐ **And this phrase is IDENTICAL to Inshiqāq 84:25.** ⭐ ***Minnah*** = reminding someone what you did for them.

## §4 — Two Closing Questions (v.7–8)

**7. فَمَا يُكَذِّبُكَ بَعْدُ بِالدِّينِ — *fa-mā yukadhdhibuka baʿdu bi'd-dīn*** · v.7
- **Root feelings:** *yukadhdhibu* — ⭐ **Form II of the "lying" root** = **to call something a lie / to deny** — ⭐ **you own it across many surahs** (Nabaʾ 78:28, Qiyāmah 75:32, Layl 92:16). *dīn* — ⭐ **NOT "religion" here** — ⭐ **the root *d-y-n* means DEBT and its SETTLEMENT**: ***dayn* = a DEBT***; *dāna* = to judge/requite. ⭐ **You own this from Infiṭār 82:9, 15, 17, 18.**
- **Here it means:** "**So what makes you deny the Reckoning after [this]?**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **the question is the argument, and *dīn* is the key.**
  
  ⭐ ***Yawm ad-Dīn* = "SETTLEMENT DAY"*** — ⭐ **the day the accounts close.** ⭐ **Not "the Day of Religion."** ⭐ **The root is the same as ***dayn*, a DEBT.**
  
  ⭐ **And the logic of *baʿdu* ("after [this]") is devastating:** ⭐ **"after **what**?"** ⭐ **After everything just said: ⭐ **after being made in the best form, after seeing how far you can fall, after the exception being offered.** ⭐ **The evidence is your own design and your own capacity for ruin.**
  
  ⭐ **You own this exact question-shape from Infiṭār 82:6** — *mā **gharraka** bi-rabbika'l-karīm* ("**what deceived you** about your Lord, the Generous?"). ⭐ **Two surahs, two unanswerable questions aimed straight at the reader.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⚠️ ⭐ ***baʿdu*** with a **ḍammah and no tanwīn** = ⭐ **"after [that]" with the object DELETED but understood.** ⭐ **A known construction (*mabnī ʿala'ḍ-ḍamm*).** ⭐ **And *kadhdhaba* + **بـ** is the fixed pairing.
- **Why this word:** *dīn* (**debt-settlement**) over a word for religion — ⭐ **it makes the denial **financial and concrete**, not theological.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Dayn* = A DEBT.* ⭐ ***Yawm ad-Dīn* = "SETTLEMENT DAY."** ⭐ **Not "religion."** ⭐ **And *baʿdu* = "after **all that**" — with the object deleted.** ⭐ **Compare Infiṭār 82:6: "what DECEIVED you?"**

**8. أَلَيْسَ اللَّهُ بِأَحْكَمِ الْحَاكِمِينَ — *a-laysa'llāhu bi-aḥkami'l-ḥākimīn*** · v.8
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**judging / wisdom / restraining**" family — ⭐ **a superb triple root**: *ḥakama* = **to judge**; ***ḥākim* = a JUDGE/ruler***; ***ḥikmah* = WISDOM***; ⭐ **and originally *ḥakamah* = ⭐ **THE BIT/BRIDLE in a horse's mouth** — the thing that **restrains** it! ⭐ **So judgment, wisdom, and restraint are one idea in Arabic.**
- **Here it means:** "**Is Allah not the most just / wisest of judges?**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **the final verse, and the root is a whole philosophy.**
  
  ⭐ **The bridle-image is the key: ⭐ ***ḥikmah* (wisdom) comes from the thing that HOLDS A HORSE BACK.*** ⭐ **So wisdom in Arabic isn't cleverness — ⭐ **it's the capacity to *restrain*, to hold something in its proper place.** ⭐ **And a *ḥākim* (judge) is one who **puts things back in their proper place.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **Now connect it to the whole surah:** ⭐ **v.4 said you were made in *aḥsan taqwīm* — **properly standing, correctly ordered**.** ⭐ **v.5 said you were returned to *asfala sāfilīn* — **out of place**.** ⭐ **v.8 asks: **is the One who puts things in their proper place not the best at it?**
  
  ⭐ **The surah began with things in their right place (a fig, an olive, a mountain, a sanctuary) and ends by naming Him **the best at putting things right**.
  
  ⭐ **And note the superlative-on-plural again:** ⭐ ***aḥkami'l-ḥākimīn*** = "**the most judging of the judges**" — ⭐ **exactly the same shape as *asfala sāfilīn* in v.5!** ⭐ **The surah's lowest point and its highest name use ⭐ **the identical grammatical construction.**
  
  ⭐ **You own the *ḥ-k-m* family from *al-Ḥakīm*, one of the Names.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **أَلَيْسَ = "is [he] not?"** — a rhetorical question expecting "yes, He is." ⭐ ⚠️ **The *bi-* on *bi-aḥkam* is a *bāʾ zāʾidah* (EXTRA *bāʾ*) after the negative *laysa*** — ⭐ **for emphasis.** ⭐ **You own this from Takwīr 81:22, 24, 25 and Infiṭār 82:16.**
- **Why this word:** ending on ⭐ **a question, not a statement**, makes the reader supply the answer — ⭐ **which is far harder to argue with.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***Ḥakamah* = THE BIT/BRIDLE in a horse's mouth.** ⭐⭐ **So *ḥikmah* (WISDOM) = the power to RESTRAIN and put things in their proper place.** ⭐⭐ **And *aḥkami'l-ḥākimīn* has the SAME shape as *asfala sāfilīn* (v.5)** — ⭐ **superlative on a plural, the surah's lowest depth and highest Name.**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **أَسْفَلَ سَافِلِينَ (v.5)** — ⚠️ **a SUPERLATIVE stacked on a PLURAL**: "the lowest of [those already] low." ⭐ **Not just "very low."** ⭐ **And note **v.8 uses the identical construction** for Allah's Name.
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **مَمْنُونٍ (v.6)** — ⚠️ **DOUBLE meaning**: (a) **cut off** and (b) **a favour held over your head**. ⭐ **The reward is endless AND never thrown back at you.**
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **الدِّينِ (v.7)** — ⚠️ **means "the RECKONING / settlement of accounts," NOT "religion."** ⭐ **Root = ***dayn*, A DEBT.**
4. ⭐⭐ **تَقْوِيمٍ (v.4)** — ⚠️ **from *qāma*, TO STAND.** ⭐ **Same root as *al-mustaqīm*** (the straight path). **Not merely "beautiful form" — *properly upright*.**
5. ⭐⭐ **ثُمَّ (v.5)** — ⚠️ **the ONLY word connecting the highest and lowest verses.** ⭐ **No explanation is given. That silence is deliberate.**
6. ⭐ **بِأَحْكَمِ (v.8)** — ⚠️ **the *bāʾ* is EXTRA** (emphasis after *laysa*), not "with." ⭐ **Same as Takwīr 81:22, 24, 25 and Infiṭār 82:16.**
7. ⭐ **بَعْدُ (v.7)** — ⚠️ **ḍammah, no tanwīn** = "**after [that]**" with the object deleted but understood. **A known construction.**
8. ⭐ **غَيْرُ (v.6)** — ⚠️ **takes the following noun in the GENITIVE** (*ghayru mamnūnin*). **A very common negating construction.**
9. ⭐ **الْأَمِينِ (v.3)** — ⚠️ **same root as *ĪMĀN* (faith).** ⭐ **Security and faith are one word-family in Arabic.**
10. ⭐ **The four oaths (v.1–3)** — ⚠️ **may be a GEOGRAPHICAL MAP** of prophethood (ʿĪsā's land → Sinai/Mūsā → Makkah/Muḥammad ﷺ), not just a list of good things.
