# Surah An-Nāziʿāt (79) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–46)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**It has two completely different rhymes, and the switch is the point.**

- **Verses 1–14:** endings in **-ah / -irah** — *rājifah, rādifah, wājifah, khāshiʿah, ḥāfirah, nakhirah, khāsirah, sāhirah.* Sharp, quick, agitated. It sounds like **a heart pounding**.
- **Verses 15–46:** endings in **-ā** (alif maqṣūrah) — *Mūsā, ṭuwā, ṭaghā, tazakkā, takhshā, ʿaṣā, yasʿā, nādā, aʿlā, ūlā…* Long, open, unhurried. It sounds like **a story being told calmly**.

🧠 **The hook:** the surah **panics first, then calms down to explain.** Terror in section one; a patient history lesson in section two. The rhyme carries the mood before you even understand the words.

## The Big Picture

1. **Five mysterious oaths** (v.1–5) — beings who *pull out, untie, glide, race, and manage*. Most say: angels, especially those taking souls.
2. **The Two Blasts** (v.6–14) — the first shakes, the second follows right behind. Hearts pound, eyes drop.
3. **The Mockery** (v.10–14) — *"We'll be brought back after we're crumbled bones?"* Answer: **one shout, and you're standing on open ground.**
4. **The Mūsā Flashback** (v.15–26) — the whole story of Pharaoh, compressed into 12 short verses. **You've seen this movie before.**
5. **Which is harder — you or the sky?** (v.27–33) — the argument from creation.
6. **The Overwhelming** (v.34–41) — two people, two choices, two destinations.
7. **"When is it?"** (v.42–46) — *That's not your department. You're just the warner.*

**Memory spine:** The surah panics first, then calms down to explain — the rhyme itself carries the mood.

**1. النَّازِعَاتِ — *an-nāziʿāt*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** the "**pulling out / wrenching away**" family — *nazaʿa* = to yank something out of where it's lodged. Modern Arabic: *nizāʿ* = a conflict (a tug-of-war), *nazʿ* = removal.
- **Here it means:** "By those who **pull out** violently."
- **Word magic:** the verb is **physical and forceful** — the kind of pulling that meets **resistance**. Nothing is being handed over; something is being **extracted**. And notice: this is oath #1, so the surah **opens on a struggle**.
- **Grammar bite:** active participle, **feminine plural** (*fāʿilāt* pattern). The described noun is **deleted** — Arabic often drops the obvious noun and lets the adjective stand alone.
- **Why this word:** *nazaʿa* over a gentle word for "taking" — because for some souls, leaving is a **wrench**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *nizāʿ* = a **tug-of-war**. Picture something being **pulled out against its will**, like a deep-rooted weed that fights back.

**2. غَرْقًا — *gharqan*** · v.1
- **Root feeling:** the "**drowning / submerging**" family — same root as *ghariqa* (to drown) and *ighrāq* (immersion).
- **Here it means:** pulling out **from the very depths** — reaching all the way in.
- **Word magic:** a stunning *istiʿāra*. To describe *how deep* the pulling goes, the Qur'an uses **drowning language** — the extractor plunges in **so far it's like submerging**. Not "pulled hard" but "**pulled from drowning-depth**."
- **Grammar bite:** *mafʿūl muṭlaq* (**cognate accusative**) — a verbal noun reinforcing the participle. Arabic's underline key. You'll see this **four times in five verses** here.
- **Why this word:** it measures **depth**, not force — the soul is drawn from the deepest place in the body.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** gharq = drowning. Picture someone reaching **shoulder-deep into water** to grab something at the very bottom.

**3. النَّاشِطَاتِ نَشْطًا — *an-nāshiṭāt nashṭan*** · v.2
- **Root feeling:** the "**untying a knot**" family. Old Arabic: *nashaṭa'l-ʿuqdah* = **he untied the knot**. From that came *nashāṭ* = **energy, briskness** (a freed thing moves lively).
- **Here it means:** "and those who **draw out gently / untie**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **this is the surah's most beautiful contrast, and it's easy to miss.** Verse 1 = **violent wrenching**. Verse 2 = **gently untying a knot**. **Same job, two completely different experiences.** The soul of one person is *torn out*; the soul of another is *slipped free like a loosened knot*. **Two deaths, two verbs, back to back** — and the Qur'an doesn't say which is which. You already know.
- **Grammar bite:** another **cognate accusative** (*nashṭan*) — matching the rhythm of v.1.
- **Why this word:** the knot-image conveys **ease without effort** — untying, not cutting.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **v.1 = ripping a knot apart. v.2 = untying it.** Same knot. Two ways to leave. *That's* the pair — hold onto it.

**4. السَّابِحَاتِ سَبْحًا — *as-sābiḥāt sabḥan*** · v.3
- **Root feeling:** the "**swimming / gliding effortlessly**" family — *sabaḥa* = to swim; *sibāḥah* = swimming. And the noble cousin: **tasbīḥ** (glorifying Allah) — from the idea of **moving swiftly away** from any flaw attributed to Him.
- **Here it means:** "and those who **glide** [through space]."
- **Word magic:** the motion **upgrades**. Verses 1–2 were *pulling* (effortful). Now: **swimming** — smooth, weightless, unresisted. Once the soul is free, the journey becomes **gliding**. The oaths are telling a **sequence**.
- **Grammar bite:** third cognate accusative in a row — the repetition is deliberate **rhythm**, like oars in water.
- **Why this word:** swimming implies **a medium you move through easily** — the perfect verb for beings crossing the heavens.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** you say **subḥānallāh** every day — same root. Picture the angels **swimming through the sky** the way you'd glide underwater.

**5. السَّابِقَاتِ سَبْقًا — *as-sābiqāt sabqan*** · v.4
- **Root feeling:** the "**racing ahead / getting there first**" family — *sabaqa* = to precede; *musābaqah* = a race/competition; *sābiq* = a front-runner.
- **Here it means:** "and those who **race ahead**."
- **Word magic:** the escalation completes: **pull → untie → glide → RACE**. Speed keeps increasing across four verses. And note the near-identical sound of *sābiḥāt* / *sābiqāt* — one letter apart (ق / ح) — a **jinās** (near-pun) that makes the pair feel like one accelerating motion.
- **Grammar bite:** fourth cognate accusative. Notice also the particle shift: v.1–3 use وَ (and), but v.4–5 use فَ (*then*, immediately). **The "fa" signals sequence** — these last two follow *directly* from the first three.
- **Why this word:** racing implies **urgency and precedence** — the command is being delivered *fast*.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *sābiḥāt* (ح) = **swimming**. *sābiqāt* (ق) = **racing**. **One letter apart** — and the second is faster. ⚠️ *Classic mix-up: flag it.*

**6. الْمُدَبِّرَاتِ أَمْرًا — *al-mudabbirāt amran*** · v.5
- **Root feeling:** the "**back / rear end**" family — *dubur* = the back of something. From that: *tadbīr* = **managing an affair by looking at how it ends up**. And the one you know: **tadabbur** — deep reflection on the Qur'an, i.e. thinking **through to the consequences**.
- **Here it means:** "then those who **arrange the affair**" — carrying out the command.
- **Word magic:** the perfect closing oath. The sequence ends not in chaos but in **administration** — everything is being **managed**. And here's the internal gem: this root comes back in **v.22**, where Pharaoh **"adbara"** — *turned his back*. **The angels use their "back-sense" to manage; Pharaoh uses his to flee.** Same root, opposite postures.
- **Grammar bite:** Form II participle, feminine plural. *Amran* here is a **direct object** (not a cognate accusative) — the pattern **breaks** on the fifth oath, which is exactly how you know the list has landed.
- **Why this word:** *tadbīr* means **purposeful management**, not random force — the universe is being *run*, not just happening.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** tadabbur = what you do when you reflect on the Qur'an — thinking **through to the end**. Same root. The angels are the universe's **project managers**.

**7. تَرْجُفُ / الرَّاجِفَةُ — *tarjufu / ar-rājifah*** · v.6
- **Root feeling:** the "**violent trembling / quaking**" family. Related: *irjāf* = spreading alarming rumors — because they **make hearts tremble**.
- **Here it means:** "the Day the **Quaker** quakes" — the first blast of the trumpet.
- **Word magic:** the **cognate structure** (*tarjufu'r-rājifah* — "the shaker shakes") is Arabic at maximum emphasis: the verb and the subject are **the same root**, hammering the idea. English can't do this gracefully; Arabic does it constantly.
- **Grammar bite:** *rājifah* is an active participle, feminine, used as a **name** for the event. The described noun is deleted.
- **Why this word:** naming the event "**The Quaker**" makes it an **entity**, not just an occurrence.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *irjāf* = **rumors that make hearts shake**. The first blast is **The Shaker** — and everything shakes.

**8. الرَّادِفَةُ — *ar-rādifah*** · v.7
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ this one is a gift. *Ridf* = **the person riding behind you on the same mount** (like a passenger on the back of a camel or motorbike). *Radifa* = to follow immediately behind.
- **Here it means:** "**the Follower** follows it" — the second blast.
- **Word magic:** the image is precise and terrifying. The second blast isn't just *later* — it's **riding pillion on the first one**, on the same animal. **No gap.** Compare: if a friend rides behind you on one bike, how far apart are you? **Zero.** That's the spacing between the two blasts.
- **Grammar bite:** another feminine participle used as a name; parallel construction to *rājifah*, which is why they rhyme.
- **Why this word:** *rādifah* conveys **immediate succession** in a way that "the second one" never could.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **ridf = the person sitting behind you on the same bike.** Blast two is **riding on the back of blast one**. That's how close.

**9. وَاجِفَةٌ — *wājifah*** · v.8
- **Root feeling:** the "**rapid throbbing / palpitating**" family — *wajafa* = to beat fast, to pound.
- **Here it means:** "Hearts, that Day, will be **pounding**."
- **Word magic:** notice the **rhyme chain**: *rājifah* → *rādifah* → *wājifah*. Three verses, three words that sound almost identical (ر-ا-ج / ر-ا-د / و-ا-ج). The **earth shakes → the second blast follows → your heart shakes.** The **sound itself transmits the tremor** from the cosmos into your chest. This is one of the finest sound-effects in the Qur'an.
- **Grammar bite:** active participle, feminine (agreeing with *qulūb*, hearts). Note *qulūbun* is **indefinite** — "*some* hearts," leaving room for the other kind.
- **Why this word:** *wājifah* is a **physical, medical** word for a racing heart — not "afraid" (an emotion) but the **body's actual response**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **rājifah → rādifah → wājifah.** Say them fast. The **shaking travels from the earth into your chest** through the rhyme.

**10. خَاشِعَةٌ — *khāshiʿah*** · v.9
- **Root feeling:** the "**humbled, lowered, submissive**" family — and you know it: **khushūʿ**, the humility you're meant to have in ṣalāh.
- **Here it means:** "their eyes **downcast**."
- **Word magic:** a sharp **contrast in the same body**: the **heart** is pounding wildly (*wājifah*, v.8), but the **eyes** are utterly still and lowered (*khāshiʿah*, v.9). **Chaos inside, frozen outside** — exactly what terror looks like.
- **Grammar bite:** participle agreeing with *abṣār* (eyes); *abṣāruhā* — the pronoun refers back to the hearts.
- **Why this word:** *khushūʿ* is the same word used for **worship posture** — a bitter irony: they lower their eyes *now*, when it no longer counts.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** khushūʿ = what you're supposed to have in prayer. On that Day, **everyone finally has it** — too late.

**11. مَرْدُودُونَ — *mardūdūn*** · v.10
- **Root feeling:** the "**returning / sending back**" family — *radda* = to send back; *mardūd* = something returned (in commerce: a returned item!).
- **Here it means:** "They say: *Are we really going to be* ***sent back****?*"
- **Word magic:** the passive form does the sneering. Not "will we return?" but "**will we be returned?**" — like a package. Their mockery treats resurrection as **absurd logistics**.
- **Grammar bite:** passive participle , plural. Note the doubled question-marker: *a-innā la-mardūdūn* — hamzah of questioning + *inna* + *lām* of emphasis = "**are we REALLY, TRULY** going to be…?" Layered sarcasm.
- **Why this word:** the passive strips them of agency — which is, ironically, **exactly true**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *mardūd* = **a returned item** (like a package sent back to the shop). They're mocking: *"We're getting shipped back?"*

**12. الْحَافِرَةِ — *al-ḥāfirah*** · v.10
- **Root feeling:** the "**digging / hollowing out**" family — *ḥafara* = to dig; *ḥufrah* = a pit.
- **Here it means:** two classical readings, both good: (a) **our former state** — from the idiom *rajaʿa ʿalā ḥāfiratih*, "he went back **the way he came**" (retracing your own dug-in footprints); or (b) **the grave** — the dug hollow.
- **Word magic:** the footprint-image is lovely: your path has left **hollows in the sand**, and going back means **stepping into your own old prints**. Either reading, it's a **rare, hard word** — chosen partly because deniers were being showy with their sarcasm.
- **Grammar bite:** genitive after *fī*.
- **Why this word:** *ḥāfirah* is unusual and slightly archaic — it gives their mockery a **smug, literary** tone.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ḥafara = to dig. Picture **walking backwards into your own footprints in the sand** — that's "returning to the *ḥāfirah*."

**13. نَخِرَةً — *nakhirah*** · v.11
- **Root feeling:** the "**hollow, crumbling, rotted**" family — *nakhira* = to become hollow and brittle. Related: *nakhīr* = the **snoring/whistling sound** wind makes passing through a hollow thing.
- **Here it means:** "when we are **crumbling** bones?"
- **Word magic:** the sound-origin is the horror. *Nakhirah* bones are **so hollow the wind whistles through them**. It's not "old bones" — it's bones that have become **flutes**. They chose the most extreme word available to make resurrection sound ridiculous.
- **Grammar bite:** adjective for *ʿiẓāman* (bones), accusative, *khabar* of *kunnā*.
- **Why this word:** maximum decay — the strongest possible version of their objection.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *nakhīr* = **the whistle of wind through something hollow**. Picture bones so rotted the **wind plays them like a flute**. That's the word.

**14. كَرَّةٌ خَاسِرَةٌ — *karratun khāsirah*** · v.12
- **Root feelings:** *karrah* — the "**turning back / charging again**" family. War idiom: *al-karr wa'l-farr* = **charge and retreat**. *khāsirah* — the "**loss**" family (*khusrān* = loss; *khasira* = to lose out).
- **Here it means:** "They said: *That, then, would be a* ***losing return****.*"
- **Word magic:** *karrah* is **battle vocabulary** — a **charge back into the fight**. So they're saying: *if we do get sent back, it's a losing round.* And here's the devastating irony: **they're right.** For them, it *is* a losing return. Their sarcasm accidentally states the truth. The Qur'an lets their own words convict them.
- **Grammar bite:** *khāsirah* is an adjective for *karrah*; both indefinite, both carrying the surah's -irah rhyme.
- **Why this word:** military language makes their mockery sound **tough**, which makes the collapse in v.13–14 hit harder.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *al-karr wa'l-farr* = **charge and retreat**. They sneer, *"a losing comeback."* ⭐ **And they're correct.**

**15. زَجْرَةٌ — *zajrah*** · v.13
- **Root feeling:** the "**driving away with a shout / scolding**" family — *zajara* = to shout at an animal to make it move; *zajr* = a stern rebuke.
- **Here it means:** "It will be but **one shout**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **this is the surah's knockout punch, and it's structural.** They just spent **three long verses** (10–12) building an elaborate objection — *sent back? crumbled bones? a losing return?* The answer is **five words**: *"It's just one shout."* **Their whole argument gets dismissed in less time than it took to make.** And *zajrah* is the sound you make **to a herd animal** — the disdain is built in. And note **"wāḥidah" (one)** — not even repeated. Once.
- **Grammar bite:** *fa-innamā* = "it is nothing but" — the **restriction particle**, cutting off all alternatives.
- **Why this word:** a **herding shout** for the resurrection of humanity — devastatingly dismissive.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Three verses of objection. One word of answer: "a shout."** Picture a shepherd's single call — and everyone stands up.

**16. السَّاهِرَةِ — *as-sāhirah*** · v.14
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**staying awake at night**" family — *sahira* = to stay up; *sahar* = wakefulness before dawn.
- **Here it means:** "and suddenly they are on the **open plain**" — the flat, bare surface of the earth.
- **Word magic:** why is a plain called "**the wakeful one**"? Two beautiful explanations: (a) it's **the place where nobody sleeps** — every single person is standing, awake, forever; (b) a barren open plain **shimmers and never rests**. Either way, the earth's name on that Day is "**Insomnia**." And look at the transition: **"fa-idhā hum"** — "and *suddenly* they are…" — with **no verb at all**. No "they were raised," no "they came out." **The shout, then they're just there.** The grammar itself skips the process.
- **Grammar bite:** fa-idhā al-fujāʾiyyah — "the *idhā* of surprise." It means *and all at once, there they are*. Learn this construction; it appears whenever the Qur'an wants **instant** transition.
- **Why this word:** *sāhirah* over "the earth" or "the plain" — it names the location by its **defining condition**: nobody sleeps here.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ *sahar* = **staying awake**. The gathering-place is called **"The Wakeful Plain."** From the grave to standing — **no in-between**, just *"and suddenly, there they are."*

**17. طُوًى — *ṭuwā*** · v.16
- **Root feeling:** the name of the sacred valley. The root *ṭ-w-y* also means "**to fold**" (*ṭawā* = to fold up a scroll) — so some read the name as "**folded/blessed twice over**," others simply as a proper name.
- **Here it means:** "when his Lord called him in the sacred valley of **Ṭuwā**."
- **Word magic:** none forced — but note the *ṭuwā* / *ṭaghā* (v.17) near-rhyme: **the sacred place** and **the tyrant** named in consecutive verses, sounding almost alike. Immediate contrast.
- **Grammar bite:** a **proper name**, and one that doesn't take tanwīn in some readings (diptote) — but here it carries the rhyme.
- **Why this word:** naming the exact place **grounds the story in history** — this happened somewhere real.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** Ṭuwā — the valley where Mūsā was called. It sits right next to **ṭaghā** (v.17), the tyrant. **Holy ground, then the tyrant's name.**

**18. طَغَىٰ — *ṭaghā*** · v.17 (and v.37)
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**overflowing past all limits**" family — original image: **water bursting its banks and flooding**.
- **Here it means:** "Go to Pharaoh — indeed, **he has transgressed all bounds**."
- **Word magic:** you **already own this root** from Nabaʾ 78:22 (*aṭ-ṭāghīn*, "those who transgress"). Here it gets a **face and a name**: Pharaoh is *the* case study. And ⭐ it comes back in **v.37** — *"as for the one who* ***ṭaghā****"* — applying **Pharaoh's exact verb** to any ordinary person. **The surah says: the difference between you and Pharaoh is scale, not category.**
- **Grammar bite:** past tense, weak-final root (ط-غ-ي); the final *alif maqṣūrah* carries the section's rhyme.
- **Why this word:** it defines tyranny as **a boundary problem**, not a power problem.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **v.17: Pharaoh ṭaghā. v.37: "whoever ṭaghā."** Same verb, twenty verses apart. **The label is transferable.**

**19. تَزَكَّىٰ — *tazakkā*** · v.18
- **Root feeling:** the "**purifying and growing**" family — and you know it: **zakāh** (the purifying charity), *zakiyy* (pure). Key insight: this root means **purity AND growth at once** — because removing what's rotten lets a thing *grow*.
- **Here it means:** "Say: *Would you [like] to* ***purify yourself****?*"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **look at how Allah tells Mūsā to address Pharaoh** — the man who claimed godhood, who enslaved a nation. Not a threat. Not a condemnation. A **gentle question**: *"Would you like to be purified?"* — and Form V (*tazakkā*) is **reflexive**: *purify **yourself***, i.e. **you'd still be the one choosing.** The most tyrannical man in history gets offered **a dignified invitation**. (Elsewhere the Qur'an makes this explicit: *speak to him gently.*) That's the balāgha here — **the mercy is in the tone**.
- **Grammar bite:** Form V = reflexive, doing something to yourself. *Hal laka ilā an…* = a soft, courteous idiom, roughly "*would you care to…?*"
- **Why this word:** *tazakkā* offers **growth**, not just cleansing — it frames repentance as an **upgrade**, not a humiliation.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **zakāh = purify + grow.** And remember *who* this gentle offer was made to: **Pharaoh.** If he got that tone, so does everyone.

**20. تَخْشَىٰ — *takhshā*** · v.19 (and v.26, 45)
- **Root feeling:** the "**awe-filled fear**" family — *khashyah* is fear that comes from **knowing** something's greatness, not from panic. (Compare *khawf* = plain fear.)
- **Here it means:** "and I will guide you to your Lord, **so you would be in awe**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the **order** is the lesson: **guidance first, awe second.** Not "fear Him so you'll obey" but "**let me show you Him — then the awe follows naturally.**" *Khashyah* is presented as the **result of knowledge**, not a substitute for it. And this root becomes the surah's **thread**: v.19 (Pharaoh could have had it), v.26 (a lesson **for whoever has it**), v.45 (you warn **whoever has it**). Three appearances, one filter.
- **Grammar bite:** weak-final verb; the ف here is *fā' as-sababiyyah* — "**so that** you would" — marking a **result**.
- **Why this word:** *khashyah* over *khawf* — because this is **reverence**, the fear of someone you've **come to know**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **khashyah = fear that comes from KNOWING.** First you're shown, *then* you're in awe. **Guidance → awe**, never the reverse.

**21. عَصَىٰ — *ʿaṣā*** · v.21
- **Root feeling:** the "**disobeying**" family — and here's a delicious accident of Arabic: *ʿaṣā* (he disobeyed) is spelled identically to *ʿaṣā* (**a staff/stick**) — the very thing Mūsā was holding.
- **Here it means:** "But he **denied** and **disobeyed**."
- **Word magic:** the pair *kadhdhaba* (denied with his tongue) + *ʿaṣā* (disobeyed with his actions) covers **both channels of rejection** — *ṭibāq*-style completeness. Say it in your head and say it with your hands.
- **Grammar bite:** weak-final past verb.
- **Why this exact word:** two verbs instead of one — because **rejecting is not the same as disobeying**, and Pharaoh did both.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** Mūsā held an *ʿ***aṣā** (staff); Pharaoh *ʿ***aṣā** (disobeyed). Same letters. Easy hook, and the pairing sticks.

**22. أَدْبَرَ يَسْعَىٰ — *adbara yasʿā*** · v.22
- **Root feelings:** *adbara* — the "**back / rear**" family (*dubur* = the back) → **to turn your back and leave**. *yasʿā* — the "**striving, hurrying**" family — the **same root as Saʿī**, the walk between Ṣafā and Marwah.
- **Here it means:** "Then he **turned his back**, **hurrying**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **two beautiful root-echoes in one verse.** (a) *adbara* is the **same root as *al-mudabbirāt*** (v.5) — the angels who **"manage the affair" by seeing the end**; Pharaoh uses the same root to **run from it**. **Same faculty, opposite use.** (b) *yasʿā* is the **Saʿī root** — and it comes back in **v.35** (*mā saʿā*, "what he strove for"). Pharaoh's *saʿy* is **running away**; on that Day, everyone's *saʿy* gets shown.
- **Grammar bite:** *yasʿā* is a *ḥāl* (state) — "he turned his back **while hurrying**." Two simultaneous actions.
- **Why this exact word:** "hurrying" adds **panic** — he doesn't walk away with dignity, he **scurries**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **v.5: angels *manage* (dabbara). v.22: Pharaoh *turns his back* (adbara).** Same root, opposite direction. And *yasʿā* = **the Saʿī root** — he's *striving*, just the wrong way.

**23. حَشَرَ — *ḥashara*** · v.23
- **Root feeling:** the "**gathering people together**" family — the same word used for **the Gathering of the Day of Judgment** (*al-ḥashr*).
- **Here it means:** "So he **gathered** and called out."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **razor-sharp irony.** Pharaoh performs a **mini-Judgment-Day**: he **gathers** (*ḥashara*) the people and **calls out** (*nādā*) a proclamation — mimicking exactly what will be done to him. He plays God, using **God's own verbs**. Then v.25: God **seizes** him. **The counterfeit gathering is answered by the real one.**
- **Grammar bite:** notice the chain of فَ particles across v.21–24 (*fa-kadhdhaba, fa-ḥashara, fa-nādā, fa-qāla*) — each "fa" means "**and then immediately**." The verses **speed up** as his arrogance accelerates.
- **Why this exact word:** using the Judgment-Day verb for Pharaoh's rally makes his imitation **explicit**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ *al-Ḥashr* = **the Gathering** of Judgment Day. Pharaoh **staged his own version**. Note how the "fa-fa-fa" makes the verses **accelerate** — arrogance picking up speed right before the crash.

**24. نَكَالَ — *nakāl*** · v.25
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**shackling / holding back**" family — a *nikl* is a **shackle or restraint**. So *nakāl* is a punishment that **acts as a restraint on other people** — an **exemplary** punishment, made public to stop others.
- **Here it means:** "So Allah seized him with the **exemplary punishment** of the Hereafter and the first [life]."
- **Word magic:** the word's **whole purpose is deterrence** — it's a punishment shaped like **a warning sign**. And it explains the odd order: "**the Hereafter and the first**" — *ākhirah* named **before** *ūlā*, reversing chronology. Why? Because **the lasting one matters more**; the drowning was just the preview.
- **Grammar bite:** accusative — a *mafʿūl li-ajlih*-style construction: seized him **as an exemplary punishment**.
- **Why this exact word:** *nakāl* over a plain word for punishment — this one is **meant to be seen by others**. It's on display for you, right now, in this surah.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ *nikl* = **a shackle.** A *nakāl* punishment is one **put on public display to restrain everyone else.** Pharaoh's ending is a **warning sign with a body attached.**

**25. عِبْرَةً — *ʿibrah*** · v.26
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**crossing over**" family — *ʿabara* = to cross (a river); *ʿubūr* = a crossing; *ʿibārah* = an expression (words that **carry** meaning across). And: *taʿbīr ar-ruʾyā* = **dream interpretation** — crossing from the *image* to the *meaning*.
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, in that is a **lesson** for whoever is in awe."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **this is the most useful word in the surah for a student.** A *ʿibrah* isn't information — it's a **bridge you walk across**, from *what happened to Pharaoh* to *what that means for me*. **The story is one bank; your life is the other.** Most people read the story and **stand on the near bank**. And note the condition: a lesson **only for whoever has *khashyah*** (v.20's root again). **The bridge exists; not everyone crosses.**
- **Grammar bite:** *inna fī dhālika la-ʿibratan* — a **triple-emphasis** frame (*inna* + fronted *fī dhālika* + emphatic *lām*). Arabic underlining, three times over.
- **Why this exact word:** *ʿibrah* over "a story" or "a warning" — because the **whole value is in the crossing**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ *ʿ***abara = to cross a river.** A *ʿibrah* is a **bridge from someone else's story to your own life.** Reading isn't crossing.

**26. سَمْكَهَا — *samkahā*** · v.28
- **Root feeling:** the "**height / thickness**" family — *samk* = the vertical dimension of a structure, its **roof-height**. (Not to be confused with *samak*, fish — different word, same letters.)
- **Here it means:** "He raised its **ceiling-height** and proportioned it."
- **Word magic:** construction vocabulary again (as in Nabaʾ 78:12's "*We built*"). The sky is described as **architecture with a measured ceiling** — you'd only describe a *roof height* if someone **designed** it.
- **Grammar bite:** direct object of *rafaʿa*; note the argument frame: *a-antum ashaddu khalqan ami's-samāʾ* = "**Are you harder to create, or the sky?**" A rhetorical question with only one answer.
- **Why this exact word:** *samk* is a **builder's measurement** — it makes the sky sound **specced out**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *samk* = **ceiling height**. Picture an architect's drawing of the sky with **the roof height labeled**.

**27. فَسَوَّاهَا — *fa-sawwāhā*** · v.28
- **Root feeling:** the "**making even, proportioning, perfecting**" family.
- **Here it means:** "and **proportioned it**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ you **already own this root twice** from Qiyāmah: 75:4 (*nusawwiya* — He can re-proportion your **fingertips**) and 75:38 (*sawwā* — He **proportioned** you from a clot). Now it's used for **the sky**. **Same verb for your fingertip and for the heavens.** That's the entire argument of v.27 compressed into one repeated word: *the precision is identical at both scales*.
- **Grammar bite:** Form II, weak-final root; the **fa-** shows **sequence**: raised, *then* perfected.
- **Why this exact word:** repeating the human-creation verb for the cosmos makes the comparison **airtight**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **sawwā** built your **fingertips** (Qiy 75:4) and the **sky** (79:28). **Same craftsman, same verb, different scale.**

**28. أَغْطَشَ — *aghṭasha*** · v.29
- **Root feeling:** the "**darkening**" family — *ghaṭash* = darkness, specifically the **dimming of vision** or deep gloom. A rare word.
- **Here it means:** "and He **darkened** its night and brought out its morning-brightness."
- **Word magic:** the verb is **Form IV** = **causative**: not "the night got dark," but "**He darkened it**" — an active, deliberate act. Night doesn't just *happen*; it's **switched on**. Paired with *akhraja* (He brought out) in the same verse: **a perfect ṭibāq** — He darkens, He brings out. **Two switches, one hand.**
- **Grammar bite:** Form IV (أَفْعَلَ) = making something happen. Recognize the shape: *aghṭasha, akhraja, arsā, amāta* — all "He caused X."
- **Why this exact word:** the rare word makes you **notice** an event you've ignored ~10,000 times.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** He didn't *let* night fall — He **turned it off**, then **turned morning on**. Two switches, same verse.

**29. ضُحَاهَا — *ḍuḥāhā*** · v.29 (and v.46)
- **Root feeling:** the "**forenoon brightness**" family — *ḍuḥā* is the **mid-morning**, when the sun is well up and everything is lit. (You know it as **Ṣalāt aḍ-Ḍuḥā**, and as **Sūrat aḍ-Ḍuḥā**.)
- **Here it means:** "and brought out its **morning light**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ it comes back as **the surah's final word** (v.46): *"as if they had lingered only an evening or its* ***morning****."* So *ḍuḥā* opens as **creation's daily gift** and closes as **the measure of an entire lifetime**. Your whole life = one *ḍuḥā*.
- **Grammar bite:** object of *akhraja*; the *-hā* refers back to the sky/night.
- **Why this exact word:** *ḍuḥā* names the **brightest, most ordinary** part of your day — used to measure both creation and mortality.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ you pray **Ṣalāt aḍ-Ḍuḥā** in that light. This word **opens the creation passage (v.29) and closes the whole surah (v.46)** — where your entire life gets measured as **one morning**.

**30. دَحَاهَا — *daḥāhā*** · v.30
- **Root feeling:** the "**spreading out / expanding**" family — *daḥā* = to spread wide. Related noun: *udḥiyy* = **an ostrich's nest**, the hollow it spreads out in the ground (and by extension some connect *daḥya* to an **egg**).
- **Here it means:** "And the earth, after that, He **spread out**."
- **Word magic:** the verb means **spreading and making usable** — preparing a surface for living, exactly like an ostrich **flattens out a nest**. And note the sequence: **sky first (v.27–29), then earth (v.30–32)** — big to small, ceiling before floor. (People debate what the ostrich-nest link implies about shape; the **core, agreed meaning is "spread out for use"** — that's what to hold onto.)
- **Grammar bite:** weak-final past verb; note **"the earth"** is fronted for emphasis before its verb.
- **Why this exact word:** *daḥā* implies **preparation for habitation**, not mere creation.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *udḥiyy* = **an ostrich's spread-out nest**. Picture the earth being **smoothed flat like a nest being prepared** — for you to live in.

**31. مَرْعَاهَا — *marʿāhā*** · v.31
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**shepherding / grazing**" family — *raʿā* = to graze or **to tend**; *rāʿī* = **a shepherd**; and **riʿāyah** = care, guardianship. The famous hadith: *"each of you is a **rāʿī** (shepherd) and responsible for his flock."*
- **Here it means:** "He brought out from it its **water** and its **pasture**."
- **Word magic:** the root's double life is the point: the same letters give **"grazing land"** and **"taking care of someone."** So *marʿā* isn't just grass — it's **the place where care happens**. And notice the pairing: **water and pasture** — *drink and food*, the two things a living creature needs. Nothing decorative.
- **Grammar bite:** noun of place (*mafʿal* pattern) from a weak-final root; the *alif maqṣūrah* carries the rhyme.
- **Why this exact word:** *marʿā* frames the earth's produce as **provision under a shepherd's care**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **rāʿī = shepherd, riʿāyah = care.** The pasture is literally **"the care-place."** Water and pasture: **drink and food**, nothing extra.

**32. أَرْسَاهَا — *arsāhā*** · v.32
- **Root feeling:** the "**anchoring, being firmly fixed**" family — *rasā* = to be firm/anchored; **mirsāh/marsā** = **a harbour or anchorage** where ships are moored.
- **Here it means:** "and the mountains — He **anchored them**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **this root sets up the surah's finest long-range echo.** Here: mountains are **anchored** (*arsāhā*, v.32). Then at **v.42**, the mockers ask about the Hour: *"****ayyāna mursāhā****"* — "**when will it be anchored / when does it dock?**" **Same root.** The mountains have an anchor-point; **so does the Hour** — you just don't get to know it. ⭐ **The surah answers a question in v.42 using a word it planted in v.32.**
- **Grammar bite:** Form IV (causative): "He **made them** firm." Weak-final root (ر-س-و).
- **Why this exact word:** *arsā* is **nautical** — anchoring — which makes the mountains sound like **stabilizers on a moving vessel**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ *marsā* = **a harbour where ships anchor.** Mountains = **the earth's anchors** (v.32). Then in **v.42** they ask when the Hour **"drops anchor."** Same root, ten verses apart.

**33. مَتَاعًا لَّكُمْ وَلِأَنْعَامِكُمْ — *matāʿan lakum wa li-anʿāmikum*** · v.33
- **Root feeling:** *matāʿ* — the "**enjoyment / temporary provision**" family; *matāʿ* is goods you **use for a while** (in modern Arabic, *amtiʿah* = **luggage!** — things you carry temporarily). *anʿām* — grazing livestock; related to *niʿmah* (a blessing).
- **Here it means:** "as **provision** for you and your livestock."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **this entire verse appears again, word for word, in Sūrat ʿAbasa (80:32).** Not a similar verse — **identical**. Two consecutive surahs, the same sentence closing the same kind of passage. Worth noticing when you get there. And *matāʿ* carries a **built-in expiry date**: it's *temporary* enjoyment. Even while listing gifts, the word whispers *this doesn't last*.
- **Grammar bite:** *maf'ūl li-ajlih* (accusative of purpose) — "[He did all that] **as provision**."
- **Why this exact word:** *matāʿ* over a permanent word for "provision" — it keeps the **dunya frame** intact.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ *amti'ah* = **luggage** — stuff you carry **temporarily**. And this **exact verse repeats in ʿAbasa 80:32.** Same words, next surah.

**34. الطَّامَّةُ الْكُبْرَىٰ — *aṭ-ṭāmmatu'l-kubrā*** · v.34
- **Root feeling:** the "**overwhelming / submerging completely**" family — *ṭamma'l-māʾ* = **the water rose and buried everything**. A *ṭāmmah* is a calamity that **covers over** whatever it meets.
- **Here it means:** "But when **the Greatest Overwhelming** comes."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ note the **root-cousin** to *ṭaghā* (v.17, 37) — both are **water-overflow** images. **Pharaoh overflowed his limits (ṭaghā); the Day overflows everything (ṭāmmah).** And there's grim symmetry: **the man who flooded past his boundaries is answered by a flood that covers all boundaries.** Adding *al-kubrā* (the Greatest) implies there are lesser *ṭāmmahs* — every personal disaster is a **small rehearsal**.
- **Grammar bite:** participle used as a name, with a superlative adjective (*al-kubrā*, feminine of *akbar*).
- **Why this exact word:** *ṭāmmah* = something that **submerges** — you don't survive it by standing taller.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ *ṭamma* = **water rising over everything.** Pharaoh **overflowed** (*ṭaghā*) — and he was **drowned**. Now the whole world meets **The Great Overflowing.**

**35. يَتَذَكَّرُ / مَا سَعَىٰ — *yatadhakkaru / mā saʿā*** · v.35
- **Root feeling:** *tadhakkara* — the "**remembering**" family, Form V (reflexive: **calling it back to yourself**). *saʿā* — the **Saʿī** root again (striving, hurrying).
- **Here it means:** "the Day man will **remember what he strove for**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ two hooks. (a) *saʿā* is the **third appearance of this root** in the surah — **v.8 of ʿAbasa** aside, here: **v.22** (Pharaoh *hurrying* away), **v.35** (everyone remembering their *striving*). **Your Saʿī gets replayed.** (b) The verb is **"remember," not "be told"** — the horror isn't new information, it's **recall**. **You already know. You just haven't been looking.**
- **Grammar bite:** Form V = doing it to yourself. *Mā* = "**what**" (relative), not "not."
- **Why this exact word:** *tadhakkara* (remember) instead of *ʿalima* (come to know) — nothing gets **added**; the file was always yours.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ Not "he'll be **informed**" — "he'll **remember**." **The memory was always there.** And *saʿā* is the **Ṣafā–Marwah root**: every bit of striving, replayed.

**36. بُرِّزَتِ — *burrizat*** · v.36
- **Root feeling:** the "**emerging into full view / standing out**" family — *baraza* = to come out prominently; *bāriz* = **prominent**; *mubārazah* = stepping out for single combat.
- **Here it means:** "and **Hellfire is displayed** for whoever sees."
- **Word magic:** the verb means **made prominently visible** — Hell isn't *revealed* (as if hidden) so much as **brought out into the open, put on display**. Combined with **"li-man yarā" (for whoever sees)**, there's a chilling nuance: it's out in plain view — **the question is who's looking.**
- **Grammar bite:** Form II passive (intensive + passive) — "was made thoroughly to appear." The **doer is unnamed**, which is standard for cosmic events.
- **Why this exact word:** *burrizat* over "appeared" — this is an **exhibition**, deliberately staged.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *bāriz* = **prominent, standing out**. Hell gets **wheeled out into the open** — and it's visible **"for whoever sees."**

**37. آثَرَ — *āthara*** · v.38
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**trace / footprint**" family — *athar* = **a mark left behind** (archaeology = *āthār*!). From that: *āthara* = **to prefer one thing over another**, and **īthār** = the beautiful virtue of **preferring others over yourself** (selflessness).
- **Here it means:** "and **preferred** the life of this world."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the root's noble meaning makes this cutting. **Īthār** — putting others first — is one of the highest virtues in Arabic. Here the **same verb** describes putting **the dunya** first. **The machinery of preference is identical; only the object changed.** He was capable of *īthār*. He aimed it at the wrong thing.
- **Grammar bite:** Form IV , past. Paired with *ṭaghā* in v.37 by *wa*: **overstepping + misplaced preference** = the full diagnosis.
- **Why this exact word:** *āthara* frames the failure as a **choice of priority**, not ignorance. He knew. He picked.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ *athar* = **a footprint** (as in *āthār*, archaeological remains). *Īthār* = **putting others first**. Here: he put **the dunya** first. **Same verb, wrong object.**

**38. الْمَأْوَىٰ — *al-maʾwā*** · v.39 (and v.41)
- **Root feeling:** the "**taking shelter / seeking refuge**" family — *awā* = to take shelter; *maʾwā* = **the place you end up sheltering**. (Related: *āwā* = to give someone refuge.)
- **Here it means:** v.39 — "then indeed **Hellfire is the refuge**." v.41 — "then indeed **Paradise is the refuge**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **exactly the same structural move as *maʾāban* in Nabaʾ 78:22 & 39 — one word, used twice, for two opposite destinations.** But it's sharper here: the two verses are **grammatically identical** (*fa-inna X hiya'l-maʾwā*), separated by only one verse. And the irony is in the word: *maʾwā* means **the place you take shelter**. **Hell described as a shelter.** For those who sought refuge in the dunya, the "shelter" they chose becomes the one they get.
- **Grammar bite:** *hiya* here is *ḍamīr al-faṣl* (**the separating pronoun**) — it adds **restriction**: "Hell is **the** refuge — *that specifically*." Small word, big emphasis.
- **Why this exact word:** *maʾwā* over "destination" — because **shelter is what everyone is actually looking for.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Same word, v.39 and v.41. Hell = "the shelter." Paradise = "the shelter."** Just like *maʾāb* in Nabaʾ. **Everyone finds shelter somewhere.**

**39. مَقَامَ رَبِّهِ — *maqāma rabbihi*** · v.40
- **Root feeling:** the "**standing**" family — *qāma* = to stand; *maqām* = **a standing-place** or **a station/rank**.
- **Here it means:** "as for whoever **feared the standing before his Lord**."
- **Word magic:** two valid readings, both beautiful: (a) **the place where you'll stand** before Him — i.e. the standing-place of judgment; (b) **His station/majesty** — awe at *who He is*. The ambiguity enriches: you fear **the meeting** and **the One you're meeting**.
- **Grammar bite:** *iḍāfah* (possessive): "the standing-place **of** his Lord." Accusative as object of *khāfa*.
- **Why this exact word:** *maqām* makes the fear **specific and located** — not vague anxiety, but a **scheduled standing**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** *maqām* = **a standing-spot**. Picture **the exact patch of ground where you'll stand** in front of Him. Fear *that* spot.

**40. نَهَى النَّفْسَ عَنِ الْهَوَىٰ — *nahā'n-nafsa ʿani'l-hawā*** · v.40
- **Root feeling:** *nahā* — the "**forbidding / bringing to an end**" family (*nahy* = a prohibition; *nihāyah* = **the end**). *hawā* — ⭐ the "**falling down**" family: *hawā* = **to fall, to plunge**; *hāwiyah* = **the abyss**; *hawāʾ* = **air** (empty space you fall through).
- **Here it means:** "and **restrained the soul from desire**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the root of "desire" is "to FALL."** Arabic doesn't name whims neutrally — it names them by **what they do to you**. *Hawā* isn't "wanting"; it's **the thing you drop through**. And there's a perfect internal echo: *nahā* (restrain / bring to an end) shares its root with **v.44's** *muntahāhā* ("its **final end**"). ⭐ **The one who put an END to his desires (v.40) is answered by the One who holds the END of all things (v.44).** Same root, two scales.
- **Grammar bite:** *nahā X ʿan Y* = "**restrained X from Y**" — the preposition *ʿ**an*** is fixed with this verb. Learn the pairing.
- **Why this exact word:** *hawā* over a neutral word for desire — it builds the **consequence into the noun**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ *hāwiyah* = **the abyss**; *hawāʾ* = **air**. **Desire is named after falling.** Following your *hawā* = **stepping off an edge.**

**41. أَيَّانَ مُرْسَاهَا — *ayyāna mursāhā*** · v.42
- **Root feelings:** *ayyāna* — an interrogative of time reserved for a **dreaded or momentous** event (not everyday *matā*). *mursā* — the **anchoring** root from v.32.
- **Here it means:** "They ask you about the Hour: **when is its arrival / when does it dock?**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **two hooks, both excellent.** (a) *Ayyāna* is **the exact same word as Qiyāmah 75:6** (*ayyāna yawmu'l-qiyāmah*) — **same mocking question, two surahs.** (b) *Mursāhā* is the **anchoring root planted in v.32** (the mountains were *anchored*). So the question is literally "**when does it drop anchor?**" — and the answer (v.44) is: **that harbour belongs to your Lord.**
- **Grammar bite:** *mursā* is a noun of time/place from Form IV, weak-final. *Ayyāna* is *mabnī* (indeclinable).
- **Why this word:** *ayyāna* smuggles in **dread** even when asked sarcastically — the word itself concedes the Hour's enormity.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Same question word as Qiyāmah 75:6.** And *mursā* = **"drops anchor"** — from **v.32**, where the *mountains* were anchored. **The surah planted this word ten verses earlier.**

**42. فِيمَ أَنتَ مِن ذِكْرَاهَا — *fīma anta min dhikrāhā*** · v.43
- **Root feeling:** *dhikrā* — the "**mentioning / reminding**" family (you own this from Insān 76:29's *tadhkirah*).
- **Here it means:** "**In what [position] are you** to mention it?" — i.e. *this isn't yours to announce.*
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **grammar hook you already own.** *Fīma* = مَا + فِي, with the alif of *mā* **dropped** — exactly the rule from Nabaʾ 78:1 (*ʿamma* = عن + ما). **Two consecutive surahs open a key verse with this same contraction.** Learn the rule once, collect it twice.
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **interrogative مَا loses its alif after any preposition**: *ʿamma, fīma, bima, lima, mimma.* This is the single highest-yield small rule in this juzʾ.
- **Why this word:** the phrasing is a **gentle relief**, not a rebuke — *you don't carry this weight.*
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Nabaʾ opened with** *ʿamma* **(عن+ما). Nāziʿāt uses** *fīma* **(في+ما).** Same rule. **The alif drops.**

**43. مُنتَهَاهَا — *muntahāhā*** · v.44
- **Root feeling:** the "**ending / final limit**" family — *nihāyah* = the end; *muntahā* = **the utmost point, where something terminates**. (*Sidrat al-Muntahā* — the Lote Tree of the **Utmost Boundary** — same word.)
- **Here it means:** "**To your Lord is its final end**" — its ultimate knowledge and destination.
- **Word magic:** ⭐ the internal rhyme with **v.40's** *nahā* (same root): **the righteous man puts an *end* to his desires; Allah holds the *end* of the Hour.** And structurally: **"ilā rabbika" is fronted** — "**To your Lord** [alone] is its end" — a restriction. You already saw this exact fronting in **Qiyāmah 75:12** (*ilā rabbika yawmaʾidhin al-mustaqarr*) and **75:30** (*al-masāq*). **Three surahs, same move: everything terminates at Him.**
- **Grammar bite:** noun of place from Form VIII (*intahā*), weak-final.
- **Why this word:** *muntahā* means **the point past which there is nothing** — the strongest possible way to close the question.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Sidrat al-Muntahā** = the tree at **the Utmost Limit**. Same word. And notice the fronting — **"To your Lord…"** — the same construction as Qiyāmah 75:12 & 75:30.

**44. مُنذِرُ — *mundhir*** · v.45
- **Root feeling:** the "**warning**" family — and ⭐ you already own it: **the same root as *andharnāhu*** (Nabaʾ 78:40) and ***an-nadhr*****, the vow** (Insān 76:7). All share: **binding something in advance.**
- **Here it means:** "You are only a **warner** to whoever fears it."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **"innamā" (only)** is a **restriction particle** — it *limits* the Prophet's ﷺ job description. And that's the **comfort**: he is **not** responsible for the outcome, only the delivery. Combined with *man yakhshāhā* ("whoever is in awe of it") — the **third and final appearance** of the *khashyah* thread (v.19 → v.26 → v.45). ⭐ **The surah's filter, stated three times: awe is what makes the message land.**
- **Grammar bite:** Form IV active participle; *innamā* = restriction ("nothing but").
- **Why this word:** *mundhir* (warner) rather than "guide" — because **guidance isn't his to give**, only warning is his to deliver.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **khashyah appears 3 times: v.19 (Pharaoh could have), v.26 (the lesson is for them), v.45 (the warning reaches them).** Same filter, three checkpoints.

**45. لَمْ يَلْبَثُوا — *lam yalbathū*** · v.46
- **Root feeling:** the "**staying / lingering**" family — ⭐ **the same root as *lābithīn*** in Nabaʾ 78:23 ("**remaining** therein for ages").
- **Here it means:** "as if they had **not lingered** except an evening or its morning."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the contrast is exquisite and deliberate.** In **Nabaʾ 78:23**, this root described staying in Hell for *aḥqāban* — **ages upon ages**. Here, the same root describes their **entire earthly life** as **less than one day**. ⭐ **Same verb: your whole life = an afternoon. Your stay there = ages.** The two surahs use one root to set the exchange rate.
- **Grammar bite:** **لَمْ + jussive = past negation** ("did not"). Note *lam yalbathū* drops the final *nūn* — that's the jussive at work.
- **Why this word:** *labitha* (linger) rather than "live" — it makes the whole lifespan sound like **waiting in a lobby**.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ **Nabaʾ 78:23:** *lābithīn* **for AGES. Nāziʿāt 79:46:** *lam yalbathū* **— barely an evening.** Same root. **That's the trade.**

**46. عَشِيَّةً أَوْ ضُحَاهَا — *ʿashiyyatan aw ḍuḥāhā*** · v.46
- **Root feelings:** *ʿashiyyah* — **late afternoon into evening**; *ḍuḥā* — **mid-morning** (from v.29).
- **Here it means:** "…except **an evening or its morning**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐ **the most quietly devastating ending imaginable.** He doesn't say "a day." He says **"an evening — or its morning."** That's **half a day**, and then he **halves it again** with "or." Your entire life — every plan, every worry, every year — reduced to **the part of a day you barely notice passing.** And it closes the loop with **v.29**, where *ḍuḥā* was **creation's daily gift**. ⭐ **The word that opened as a blessing closes as a measurement of how little time you had.**
- **Grammar bite:** *aw* here means "**or**" in the sense of *even less than that* — a downward correction, not a real alternative.
- **Why this word:** naming the **smallest natural unit of a day** — and then offering an even smaller one.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐ Your whole life: **"an evening — or just the morning of it."** And *ḍuḥā* was **v.29's gift of daylight**. **First word of creation's kindness; last word of your lifespan.**
