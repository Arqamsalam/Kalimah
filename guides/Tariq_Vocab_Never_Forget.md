# Surah Aṭ-Ṭāriq (86) — Never-Forget Vocabulary Guide

**Level:** intermediate, explained super simple · **Order:** mushaf (v.1–17)

**Layers per word:** Root feeling · Here it means · Word magic (balāgha, simplified) · Grammar bite · Why this exact word · 🧠 Never forget

---

## ⭐ The One Thing That Makes This Surah Special

**Seventeen verses, and it runs the argument for resurrection in a perfect circle — using ONE root twice.**

- **v.8:** *innahu ʿalā **RAJʿIHI** la-qādir* — "**He is able to RETURN him.**"
- **v.11:** *wa's-samāʾi dhāti'r-**RAJʿ*** — "By the sky **possessing RETURN**" (the rain that comes back every year).

⭐ **Same root, three verses apart.** ⭐ **The sky RETURNS its rain. The earth SPLITS for the seed. Therefore: you will be RETURNED, and the earth will SPLIT for you.**

⭐ **The oath in v.11–12 isn't decoration — it's the EVIDENCE for v.8.** ⭐ **Every year, in front of your eyes, the whole cycle rehearses your resurrection.**

🧠 **The hook:** ⭐ **Rain comes back. Seeds split the ground. So will you.**

---

## The Big Picture

1. **The Night-Knocker** (v.1–4) — a piercing star, ⭐ **and: every soul has a guard.**
2. **Look At Your Origin** (v.5–8) — ⭐ **"Let man look at what he was made from"** — a gushing drop. **He who made it can return it.**
3. **The Day Secrets Are Tested** (v.9–10) — ⭐ **no power, no helper.**
4. **Rain and Seed** (v.11–14) — ⭐ **the evidence.** And: **this is a decisive word, not a joke.**
5. **Two Plots** (v.15–17) — ⭐ **they scheme; I scheme.** *Give them a little time.*

## §1 — The Night-Knocker (v.1–4)

**1. الطَّارِقِ — *aṭ-ṭāriq*** · v.1–2
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ **a gorgeous, tactile root** — *ṭaraqa* = ⭐ **TO KNOCK, to strike, to beat**. ***Ṭarq*** = a knock/blow. ***Miṭraqah*** = ⭐ **a HAMMER**! ***Ṭarīq*** = ⭐ **a ROAD** (the path **beaten** by feet!). And classically, ***ṭāriq*** = ⭐ **anyone who arrives AT NIGHT** — because in the old world, a night visitor had to **KNOCK** to be let in.
- **Here it means:** "By the sky and **the NIGHT-COMER** — and what will make you know what the night-comer is?"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **This word is a whole atmosphere, and every layer of the root matters.**
  
  ⭐ **A *ṭāriq* is a NIGHT VISITOR — someone who arrives when you're asleep and KNOCKS.** ⭐ **Think about how that feels: a knock at your door in the dead of night.** ⭐ **Unexpected. Startling. It could be anything.**
  
  ⭐ **The word carries that entire emotional payload before it's even defined.** ⭐ **And then the Qur'an doesn't define it immediately — v.2 asks *"and what will make you know what the ṭāriq is?"*** ⭐ **It makes you WAIT one more verse.** ⭐ **The suspense IS the technique.**
  
  ⭐ **And here's the connection to the whole surah:** ⭐ **v.4 says every soul has a GUARD.** ⭐ **A night-knocker is exactly what a guard exists for.** ⭐ **The oath sets up the theme: something comes in the night, and someone is watching.**
  
  ⭐ **You already own *ṭāriq*'s cousins:** ⭐ ***ṭarīq* = a ROAD** — literally "the beaten path." ⭐ **And *miṭraqah* = a HAMMER.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***wa mā adrāka mā…*** — **you own this construction from Infiṭār 82:17–18** and **Muṭaffifīn 83:8, 19**. ⭐ **Remember the rule: PAST tense *adrāka* → an explanation FOLLOWS.** ⭐ **And here it does, in v.3.** ⚠️ **Contrast with *mā yudrīka* (present, ʿAbasa 80:3) → no explanation.**
- **Why this word:** *ṭāriq* over "star" — because ⭐ **the KNOCK is the point**, not the astronomy.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***ṭaraqa* = TO KNOCK; *miṭraqah* = a HAMMER; *ṭarīq* = a ROAD (a beaten path).*** ⭐ **A *ṭāriq* = someone who arrives at night and KNOCKS on your door.** ⭐ **And the surah answers: there's a guard on duty (v.4).**

**2. النَّجْمُ الثَّاقِبُ — *an-najmu'th-thāqib*** · v.3
- **Root feelings:** *najm* — **a star** (also *najm* = a plant without a stem, and *nujūm* = instalments — things that appear in stages). *thāqib* — ⭐ the "**piercing / boring through**" family — *thaqaba* = **to bore a hole, to pierce**; ***thuqb*** = **a hole**; *mithqab* = ⭐ **a DRILL**.
- **Here it means:** "**The PIERCING star.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **The answer to the suspense — and *thāqib* is a startlingly physical word.**
  
  ⭐ ***Thaqaba* means TO DRILL A HOLE THROUGH something.*** ⭐ **So the star doesn't "shine" — ⭐ **it BORES THROUGH the darkness**, punching a hole in it.
  
  ⭐ **Picture it: not a glow spread over the sky, but ⭐ **a single point of light PUNCTURING the black**, the way a drill goes through a board.
  
  ⭐ **And connect the two words:** ⭐ **v.1: a KNOCKER (something that strikes). v.3: a PIERCER (something that bores through).** ⭐ **Both are words of FORCE and PENETRATION.** ⭐ **This star doesn't visit; it breaks through.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare the star-vocabulary you own:** ⭐ **Takwīr 81:2** (*an-nujūmu **inkadarat*** — stars go **murky and fall**), ⭐ **Infiṭār 82:2** (*al-kawākibu **intatharat*** — stars **scattered**), ⭐ **Takwīr 81:15–16** (*al-khunnas… al-kunnas* — stars that **hide and sweep**). ⭐ **Four surahs, four different star-verbs.**
- **Grammar bite:** **active participle** (*thāqib*) describing *an-najm*. ⭐ **Both definite** — "**THE** piercing star," a specific, known thing.
- **Why this word:** *thāqib* over "bright" — because ⭐ **piercing implies the darkness RESISTS and loses.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***mithqab* = a DRILL; *thuqb* = a HOLE.*** ⭐ **The star doesn't glow — it DRILLS through the dark.** ⭐ **v.1 KNOCKS, v.3 PIERCES. Both are force-words.**

**3. لَّمَّا عَلَيْهَا حَافِظٌ — *lammā ʿalayhā ḥāfiẓ*** · v.4
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**guarding / preserving / memorizing**" family — ***ḥāfiẓ*** = ⭐ **one who has memorized the Qur'an**; *maḥfūẓ* = preserved; *ḥifẓ* = memorization. ⭐ **You now own this root FOUR times: Infiṭār 82:10, Muṭaffifīn 83:33, Burūj 85:22, and here.**
- **Here it means:** "**There is NO soul but that it has over it a GUARDIAN.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **The payoff of the oath, and it's a double negative for maximum coverage.**
  
  ⭐ **"There is no soul EXCEPT that…"*** ⭐ **The construction covers EVERY single person, with zero exceptions.** ⭐ **Not "most people have," not "believers have" — ⭐ **there is no one who doesn't.**
  
  ⭐ **And the tone is deliberately DOUBLE-EDGED:**
  - ⭐ **A guard means you're WATCHED** (nothing goes unrecorded)
  - ⭐ **A guard means you're PROTECTED** (*ḥifẓ* = preservation, safekeeping)
  
  ⭐ **After a *ṭāriq* — a night-knocker — which reading do you want?** ⭐ **Something arrives in the dark, and someone is on duty.** ⭐ **The surah lets both senses stand.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Infiṭār 82:10–11:** ⭐ **there the guardians were named as *kirāman kātibīn* — NOBLE, RECORDING.** ⭐ **Infiṭār tells you WHO they are; Ṭāriq tells you that NOBODY is without one.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐⭐ ***in* here = *in an-nāfiyah* — it means "NOT," not "if."*** ⭐ **You own this from Takwīr 81:27** (*in huwa illā dhikr* = "**it is NOTHING BUT** a reminder"). ⭐ **And *lammā* here = *illā* ("except").** ⚠️ **So: *in kullu nafsin lammā ʿalayhā ḥāfiẓ* = "there is NOT a soul EXCEPT that over it is a guardian."** ⚠️ **Misreading *in* as "if" destroys the sentence.**
- **Why this word:** *ḥāfiẓ* over "watcher" — because ⭐ **the preservation sense makes the record PERMANENT, and offers protection at the same time.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ **A *ḥāfiẓ* preserves the Qur'an perfectly — these preserve YOU.** ⭐ ⚠️ ***in* = NOT (not "if"); *lammā* = EXCEPT.*** ⭐ **"There is NO soul WITHOUT a guardian."** ⭐ **Fourth surah with this root.**

## §2 — Look At Your Origin (v.5–8)

**4. فَلْيَنظُرِ الْإِنسَانُ مِمَّ خُلِقَ — *fal-yanẓuri'l-insānu mimma khuliq*** · v.5
- **Root feelings:** *yanẓur* — the "**looking**" family — *naẓara* + **ilā** = to look AT. *mimma* — ⭐ **مِنْ + مَا** = "**from what?**"
- **Here it means:** "**So let man look at what he was created from.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **TWO major hooks in one verse, and you own both already.**
  
  ⭐ **First: *fal-yanẓuri'l-insān* is the EXACT SAME opening as ʿAbasa 80:24** — *fal-**yanẓuri'l-insānu** ilā ṭaʿāmih* ("**So let man look at his FOOD**"). ⭐⭐ **Identical four words, two surahs apart.**
  - ⭐ **ʿAbasa: look OUTWARD** — at your lunch, the rain, the split earth, the crops.
  - ⭐ **Ṭāriq: look INWARD** — at where you came from.
  ⭐ **Same command, opposite directions. Two halves of one argument.**
  
  ⭐ **Second: *mimma* = مِنْ + مَا, with the alif of *mā* DROPPED.*** ⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE THIRD TIME you've met this rule:**
  - ⭐ **Nabaʾ 78:1** — ***ʿamma*** (عن + ما) — "**about what?**"
  - ⭐ **Nāziʿāt 79:43** — ***fīma*** (في + ما) — "**in what?**"
  - ⭐ **Ṭāriq 86:5** — ***mimma*** (من + ما) — "**from what?**"
  
  ⭐⭐ **Three surahs, three prepositions, one rule.** ⭐ **You now own *ʿamma, fīma, mimma, bima, lima* completely.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***lām al-amr*** (the **command lām**) on a third-person verb: *fal-yanẓur* = "**let him look**" — ⭐ **you own it from ʿAbasa 80:24** and **Muṭaffifīn 83:26** (*fal-yatanāfas*). ⭐ **Note the verb goes JUSSIVE.**
- **Why this word:** *yanẓur* (physical looking) over "reflect" — because ⭐ **the evidence is meant to be OBSERVED, not theorized.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **ʿAbasa 80:24: "let man look at his FOOD" (outward). Ṭāriq 86:5: "let man look at his ORIGIN" (inward).** ⭐ **Same four words, opposite directions.** ⭐⭐ **And *mimma* = من+ما — THIRD instance of the alif-dropping rule** (*ʿamma*, *fīma*, *mimma*).

**5. مَّاءٍ دَافِقٍ — *māʾin dāfiq*** · v.6
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**gushing / pouring forth with force**" family — *dafaqa* = **to pour out forcefully, to gush**; ***dafq*** = a gushing outflow; *tadaffuq* = ⭐ **a surge/flow** (modern Arabic uses it for **cash flow, traffic flow**).
- **Here it means:** "He was created from a **GUSHING fluid**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **The word emphasizes FORCE and lack of control, and that's the argument.**
  
  ⭐ ***Dāfiq* = gushing, surging, propelled.*** ⭐ **Not a drop that sits — ⭐ **fluid in forceful motion**, going where it's driven.
  
  ⭐ **And that's the humbling point: ⭐ **your origin was something that GUSHED — it wasn't placed, it wasn't chosen, it was propelled.** ⭐ **You had no agency in your own beginning whatsoever.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare the origin-vocabulary you own:**
  - ⭐ **Qiyāmah 75:37** — *nuṭfatan min maniyyin **yumnā*** (a drop **emitted** — passive)
  - ⭐ **Insān 76:2** — *nuṭfatin **amshāj*** (a **MIXED** drop)
  - ⭐ **ʿAbasa 80:19** — *min **nuṭfatin** khalaqahu fa-**qaddarah*** (a drop, **precisely measured**)
  - ⭐ **Ṭāriq 86:6** — *māʾin **dāfiq*** (a **GUSHING** fluid)
  
  ⭐⭐ **Four surahs, four angles on the same origin: emitted, mixed, measured, gushing.** ⭐ **The Qur'an keeps returning to this because it's the argument that can't be dodged — you were THERE, in a sense, and you contributed nothing.**
- **Grammar bite:** **active participle** (*dāfiq*) describing *māʾ*; both **indefinite**. ⭐ **Note *khuliqa* is PASSIVE** ("he **WAS** created") — ⭐ **matching the passivity of the origin itself.**
- **Why this word:** *dāfiq* over "a drop" — because ⭐ **gushing conveys FORCE without control**, which is the humbling part.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***tadaffuq* = a SURGE/FLOW** (cash flow, traffic flow). ⭐ **You began as something that GUSHED — propelled, not chosen.** ⭐ **Fourth surah on your origin: *yumnā* (75), *amshāj* (76), *qaddarah* (80), *dāfiq* (86).**

**6. الصُّلْبِ وَالتَّرَائِبِ — *aṣ-ṣulbi wa't-tarāʾib*** · v.7
- **Root feelings:** *ṣulb* — ⭐ the "**hardness / firmness**" family — *ṣalaba* = to be hard; ***ṣulb*** = ⭐ **the BACKBONE / spine** (the body's hard part), and *ṣulb* also = **hard/solid** in modern Arabic (*ṣulb* = **steel**!). *tarāʾib* — ⭐ **the RIBS / the chest-bones**, plural of *tarībah* — the front of the torso.
- **Here it means:** "**Emerging from between the BACKBONE and the RIBS.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **The anatomical precision is the point, and the two words are a deliberate pair.**
  
  ⭐ ***Ṣulb* = the BACK (behind). *Tarāʾib* = the CHEST/RIBS (front).*** ⭐ **So "from between the backbone and the ribs" means ⭐ **from the very CORE of the body** — the deepest, most protected, most central place.
  
  ⭐ **It's a *merism*: naming the back and the front to mean EVERYTHING BETWEEN THEM** — ⭐ **the same technique as *qaddama wa akhkhara* (Qiyāmah 75:13 / Infiṭār 82:5), where "forward and back" means the whole ledger.**
  
  ⭐ **And connect *ṣulb* to Qiyāmah 75:25** — *tazunnu an yufʿala bihā **FĀQIRAH*** ("expecting a **BACK-BREAKING** calamity" — from *faqār*, the **spine**). ⭐ **Two surahs, two spine-words, opposite scenes: one is where life begins, one is where strength breaks.**
  
  ⭐ **And *ṣulb* = STEEL in modern Arabic** — ⭐ **the hardest thing, naming the hardest part of you.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***min bayni…*** = "from **between**" — the preposition *bayn* (between) is the same root as ***bayān*** (**clarity**, Qiyāmah 75:19) and *mubīn* (Takwīr 81:23). ⭐ **Clarity = distinguishing between things.**
- **Why this word:** naming **two bones** rather than an organ — because ⭐ **bones are what everyone can feel on themselves.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***ṣulb* = the SPINE (and STEEL in modern Arabic); *tarāʾib* = the RIBS.*** ⭐ **Back + front = "from your very CORE."** ⭐ **Compare Qiyāmah 75:25's *fāqirah* — the other spine-word.**

**7. عَلَىٰ رَجْعِهِ لَقَادِرٌ — *ʿalā rajʿihi la-qādir*** · v.8
- **Root feelings:** *rajʿ* — ⭐ the "**returning / bringing back**" family — *rajaʿa* = **to return**; ***rujūʿ*** = a return; ⭐ ***rājiʿūn*** — as in *innā lillāhi wa innā ilayhi **rājiʿūn***, which you say when someone dies! *qādir* — ⭐ the "**power AND precise measuring**" family — ⭐ **you own it from Qiyāmah 75:4** (*balā **qādirīna** ʿalā an nusawwiya banānah*) and **Insān 76:16** (*qaddarūhā*) and **ʿAbasa 80:19** (*fa-qaddarah*).
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, He is **ABLE to RETURN him**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **The hinge of the surah, and both words are ones you already own.**
  
  ⭐ **First, *rajʿ*: ⭐ **this word comes back in v.11*** (*wa's-samāʾi dhāti'r-**rajʿ***). ⭐⭐ **The claim is made in v.8; the EVIDENCE for it arrives in v.11, using the SAME ROOT.** ⭐ **"He can RETURN you" … "by the sky that RETURNS [its rain]."** ⭐ **The proof is built into the vocabulary.**
  
  ⭐ **Second, *qādir*: ⭐ **the root means BOTH "powerful" AND "precisely measuring."*** ⭐ **You met it in Qiyāmah 75:4, where the same word answered the same objection** (*"does man think We won't gather his bones?"* → *"Yes — **qādirīn** — able even to re-proportion his fingertips"*).
  
  ⭐⭐ **So Ṭāriq 86:8 and Qiyāmah 75:4 are the SAME ARGUMENT with the SAME WORD.** ⭐ **Qiyāmah: He can rebuild your fingertips. Ṭāriq: He can return you.** ⭐ **And both use *qādir* — power that MEASURES.**
  
  ⭐ **And note *rajʿ* is what you SAY at a funeral:** ⭐ ***innā ilayhi rājiʿūn* — "to Him we are RETURNING."*** ⭐ **You already affirm this verse every time someone dies.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***inna* + *la-*** = the **double-emphasis frame** — ⭐ **fourth instance you own** (82:10, 83:7, 85:12, here). ⭐ **Note *ʿalā rajʿihi* is FRONTED before *la-qādir*** — ⭐ **spotlighting "returning him" as the thing He's able to do.**
- **Why this word:** *rajʿ* (a plain "return") over a technical resurrection term — because ⭐ **it makes the claim ordinary**, and then v.11 shows it happening annually.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***innā ilayhi RĀJIʿŪN* — you say this root at every funeral.*** ⭐ **And *qādir* = the SAME word answering the SAME objection in Qiyāmah 75:4.** ⭐⭐ **This claim (v.8) gets its evidence in v.11 — same root.**

## §3 — The Day Secrets Are Tested (v.9–10)

**8. تُبْلَى السَّرَائِرُ — *tublā's-sarāʾir*** · v.9
- **Root feelings:** *tublā* — ⭐ the "**testing / trying / examining**" family — *balā* = **to test**; ***balāʾ*** = **a trial**; ⭐ **you own it from Insān 76:2** (*na**btalīhi*** — "**We TEST him**"). *sarāʾir* — ⭐ **plural of *sarīrah*: the "SECRET" family** — ***sirr*** = **a secret**; *asrār* = secrets; ⭐ ***sarīrah*** = ⭐ **what you keep INSIDE — your inner reality, your private self.**
- **Here it means:** "**The Day when the SECRETS will be TESTED.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **A phrase worth memorizing, and the verb choice is remarkable.**
  
  ⭐ **It does NOT say the secrets will be "revealed" or "exposed."** ⭐ **It says they will be ***TESTED*** (*tublā*)** — ⭐ **the metallurgy/assay word again, the same family as *ibtilāʾ*.**
  
  ⭐⭐ **Compare Burūj 85:10's *fatanū* (putting gold in fire to test purity).** ⭐ **Consecutive surahs, the same idea: ⭐ **what's inside you gets ASSAYED, not just displayed.**
  
  ⭐ **And ⭐ *sarāʾir* is beautifully specific:*** *sarīrah* is ⭐ **not just a secret you're hiding — it's your INNER REALITY**, the version of you that only you know. ⭐ **Your actual intentions. What you're really like when nobody's looking.**
  
  ⭐ **So the verse says: on that Day, the thing being examined is ⭐ **not your public record but your PRIVATE SELF** — and it isn't merely shown, it's **put to the test.**
  
  ⭐ **Connect to Inshiqāq 84:23:** *wa'llāhu aʿlamu **bimā yūʿūn*** — "Allah knows best **what they store up inside**" (*wiʿāʾ* = a container). ⭐ **Inshiqāq says He KNOWS what's in the container; Ṭāriq says the contents get TESTED.**
  
  ⭐ **And Insān 76:2 said the whole point of giving you hearing and sight was *nabtalīhi* — TO TEST YOU.** ⭐ **The test that began at your creation (76:2) gets graded here (86:9).**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **passive present** (*tublā*), weak-final root (ب-ل-و). ⭐ **The doer is unnamed, as always in these Day-scenes.**
- **Why this word:** *tublā* over "exposed" — because ⭐ **testing implies a STANDARD**, and exposure alone doesn't.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***sirr* = a SECRET; *sarīrah* = your INNER REALITY.*** ⭐ **They aren't merely REVEALED — they're TESTED (assayed).** ⭐⭐ **Insān 76:2: the test BEGINS (*nabtalīhi*). Ṭāriq 86:9: the test is GRADED (*tublā*).** ⭐ **Same root, first surah to last.**

**9. مِن قُوَّةٍ وَلَا نَاصِرٍ — *min quwwatin wa lā nāṣir*** · v.10
- **Root feelings:** *quwwah* — **strength/power** — ⭐ **you own it from Takwīr 81:20** (*dhī **quwwatin***, describing the noble messenger). *nāṣir* — ⭐ the "**helping / granting victory**" family — *naṣara* = to help/support; ***naṣr*** = **victory/help** (*Sūrat an-Naṣr*!); *anṣār* = ⭐ **the ANṢĀR of Madīnah** — "**the Helpers**."
- **Here it means:** "Then he will have **no POWER and no HELPER**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **A perfect two-part denial, covering every possible resource.**
  
  ⭐ ***Quwwah* = strength FROM WITHIN yourself.*** ⭐ ***Nāṣir* = help FROM OUTSIDE yourself.*** ⭐⭐ **Between them, there is nothing left.** ⭐ **You can't save yourself, and no one can save you.**
  
  ⭐ **And *nāṣir* is the ANṢĀR root** — ⭐ **the honoured title of the people of Madīnah who took in the Muhājirūn.** ⭐ **The word for the greatest historical example of people helping people — ⭐ **negated on that Day.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Infiṭār 82:19:** *yawma **lā tamliku nafsun li-nafsin shayʾā*** ("a Day when **no soul will possess anything for another soul**"). ⭐⭐ **Same doctrine, two surahs.** ⭐ **Infiṭār denies OWNERSHIP; Ṭāriq denies POWER and HELP.**
  
  ⭐ **And Qiyāmah 75:11:** *kallā **lā wazar*** ("**No! There is no refuge**"). ⭐ **Three surahs closing the same door.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***mā lahu min quwwatin*** — the ***min*** here is *min zāʾidah* (**extra *min* after negation for emphasis**) = "**not ANY** power at all." ⭐ **Same emphatic-particle logic as the *bāʾ zāʾidah* you own from Takwīr 81:22, 24, 25 and Infiṭār 82:16.**
- **Why this word:** naming **both** inner and outer resources — because ⭐ **denying one alone would leave a door open.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***anṣār* = THE HELPERS of Madīnah.*** ⭐ **No power (inside) + no helper (outside) = nothing left.** ⭐ **Compare Qiyāmah 75:11 (*lā wazar*) and Infiṭār 82:19 (*lā tamliku nafsun li-nafs*).** **Three surahs, one closed door.**

## §4 — Rain and Seed: The Evidence (v.11–14)

**10. ذَاتِ الرَّجْعِ — *dhāti'r-rajʿ*** · v.11
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ **the RETURNING root from v.8** — *rajaʿa* = to return; ***rujūʿ*** = a return.
- **Here it means:** "By the sky **possessing RETURN**" — i.e. ⭐ **the sky that sends rain back, season after season**.
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE SURAH'S MASTERSTROKE, and if you catch nothing else, catch this.**
  
  ⭐ **v.8 claimed: *innahu ʿalā **RAJʿIHI** la-qādir* — "He is able to RETURN him."**
  ⭐ **v.11 swears by: *as-samāʾi dhāti'r-**RAJʿ*** — "the sky possessing RETURN."**
  
  ⭐⭐ **SAME ROOT. THREE VERSES APART. The claim and its proof share a word.**
  
  ⭐ **The argument, spelled out:** ⭐ **"You doubt I can bring you back? Look up. Every single year, the sky BRINGS BACK the rain.** ⭐ **The same water that evaporated, gone, invisible — ⭐ **RETURNS.** ⭐ **It has done this your entire life, in front of you.**
  
  ⭐ **And ⭐ *dhāt* — "POSSESSING return"*** — makes it an **attribute of the sky**, not an event. ⭐ **Returning is what the sky IS.** ⭐ **You own *dhāt* from Burūj 85:1, 5, 15** (*dhāti'l-burūj, dhāti'l-waqūd, dhu'l-ʿArsh*) — ⭐ **consecutive surahs, same construction.**
  
  ⭐ **And it links to your funeral phrase:** ⭐ ***innā ilayhi rājiʿūn*.** ⭐ **The sky returns its rain; we return to Him.** **Same root.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***dhāt* + genitive** = "possessor of." **Feminine because *samāʾ* is feminine.**
- **Why this word:** *rajʿ* rather than a word for "rain" — because ⭐ **using the SAME ROOT as v.8 makes the evidence unmissable.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **v.8: "He can RETURN you." v.11: "the sky POSSESSING RETURN."** ⭐ **SAME ROOT — the claim and its proof.** ⭐ **The rain comes back every year, in front of your eyes.** ⭐ **And *innā ilayhi rājiʿūn* — same root.**

**11. ذَاتِ الصَّدْعِ — *dhāti'ṣ-ṣadʿ*** · v.12
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**splitting / cracking open**" family — *ṣadaʿa* = **to split, to crack**; ***ṣadʿ*** = **a crack/fissure**; ⭐ ***ṣudāʿ*** = ⭐ **a HEADACHE** (a "splitting" head!); and *ṣadaʿa bi'l-ḥaqq* = ⭐ **to declare the truth openly** (to "split" the silence).
- **Here it means:** "And by the earth **possessing SPLITTING**" — ⭐ **the earth that cracks open for shoots to emerge.**
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **The second half of the evidence, and the pair is perfect.**
  
  ⭐ **v.11: the SKY returns (rain comes down).**
  ⭐ **v.12: the EARTH splits (life comes up).**
  ⭐⭐ **Together: the complete annual resurrection you watch every single year.**
  
  ⭐ **And you OWN this root already:**
  - ⭐ **ʿAbasa 80:26** — *thumma **shaqaqna**'l-arḍa **shaqqā*** ("then We **SPLIT** the earth, a splitting") — same idea, different root (ش-ق-ق).
  - ⭐ **Inshiqāq 84:1** — *idha's-samāʾu **inshaqqat*** (the sky **splits**).
  
  ⭐ **So across three surahs: the earth splits for SEEDS (80:26, 86:12), and the sky splits for the DAY (84:1).** ⭐ **The same action, small scale and cosmic scale.**
  
  ⭐ **And *ṣudāʿ* = a HEADACHE** is the hook you'll never lose — ⭐ **a "splitting" head.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ **third *dhāt*** in two consecutive surahs (85:1, 85:5, 85:15, 86:11, 86:12). ⭐ **Five instances — the construction is fully yours now.**
- **Why this word:** *ṣadʿ* over a growth-word — because ⭐ **it emphasizes the FORCE required**: hard ground must be **broken from below**, which is exactly what resurrection is.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***ṣudāʿ* = a HEADACHE (a "splitting" head).*** ⭐ **v.11 sky RETURNS (rain down) + v.12 earth SPLITS (life up) = the annual resurrection.** ⭐ **Compare ʿAbasa 80:26 — the earth split for seeds.**

**12. لَقَوْلٌ فَصْلٌ — *la-qawlun faṣl*** · v.13
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**cutting apart / separating / deciding**" family — *faṣala* = to separate; ⭐ ***faṣl*** = **a school TERM or a CHAPTER** (a cut-off section); ***fāṣilah*** = ⭐ **a COMMA / a separator** — and the technical term for **a Quranic VERSE-ENDING** (the very thing you've been studying!). ⭐ **You own it from Nabaʾ 78:17** (*inna **yawma'l-faṣli** kāna mīqātā*).
- **Here it means:** "Indeed, it is **a DECISIVE word**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **The word means "a word that CUTS," and that's the claim.**
  
  ⭐ ***Faṣl* = separating, cutting cleanly between things.*** ⭐ **So *qawlun faṣl* = ⭐ **speech that DECIDES** — that draws the line between truth and falsehood and leaves no blur.
  
  ⭐ **And the cross-link is beautiful:** ⭐ **Nabaʾ 78:17 called the Day itself *yawm al-faṣl* — "the Day of SEPARATION."** ⭐⭐ **So: the DAY separates people (78:17), and the QUR'AN separates truth (86:13).** ⭐ **Same root, same function, two objects.** ⭐ **The Book does now what the Day will do then.**
  
  ⭐ **And the *fāṣilah* hook is one you'll appreciate:** ⭐ **the technical Arabic name for a Quranic verse-ending is *fāṣilah* — "a separator."** ⭐ **The very feature you set out to master is named from this root.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***inna* + *la-*** — **fifth instance** of the double-emphasis frame. ⭐ **Note *faṣl* is a MAṢDAR (verbal noun) used as an ADJECTIVE** — ⭐ **which in Arabic is more emphatic than a normal adjective**: not "a decisive word" but "a word that IS decisiveness."
- **Why this word:** *faṣl* over "true" — because ⭐ **it claims a FUNCTION (it decides), not just a property.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***faṣl* = a school TERM, a CHAPTER; *fāṣilah* = a COMMA — and the name for a QURANIC VERSE-ENDING.*** ⭐ **Nabaʾ 78:17: the DAY separates people. Ṭāriq 86:13: the QUR'AN separates truth.**

**13. بِالْهَزْلِ — *bi'l-hazl*** · v.14
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**joking / triviality / lack of seriousness**" family — *hazala* = **to jest, to joke**; ***hazl*** = **jesting** — and it is ⭐ **the direct opposite of *jidd* (seriousness)**. The classic Arabic pair: ***al-jidd wa'l-hazl*** = **the serious and the jocular**.
- **Here it means:** "And it is **NOT a jest**."
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐ **A perfect *ṭibāq* (antithesis) with v.13, and the pairing is razor-sharp:**
  
  ⭐ **v.13: *qawlun **FAṢL*** — a word that **CUTS/DECIDES**.
  ⭐ **v.14: *wa mā huwa bi'l-**HAZL*** — and it is **NOT a JOKE**.
  
  ⭐⭐ **Decisive vs. trivial. The two verses define the Qur'an by stating what it is and what it isn't.**
  
  ⭐ **And *hazl* is a specific accusation being pre-empted.** ⭐ **A "joke" is something you can enjoy and then set down.** ⭐ **The verse forecloses the most common way people neutralize a serious message: ⭐ **treating it as entertainment.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare Muṭaffifīn 83:13** — *qāla **asāṭīru'l-awwalīn*** ("he says: **legends of the ancients!**"). ⭐ **Same dismissal-move: reduce it to a story, and you don't have to obey it.** ⭐ **Ṭāriq answers it head-on.**
  
  ⭐ **And note the *bāʾ zāʾidah* again** (*bi'l-hazl* after negation) — ⭐ **you own this from Takwīr 81:22, 24, 25 and Infiṭār 82:16.** ⭐ **Sixth instance.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***mā huwa bi-…*** = the standard **emphatic negation frame** with the extra *bāʾ*. ⚠️ **Recognize it instantly now.**
- **Why this word:** *hazl* over "false" — because ⭐ **the danger isn't that people will call it a LIE; it's that they'll treat it LIGHTLY.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐ ***al-jidd wa'l-hazl* = the serious and the jocular.*** ⭐ **v.13 DECISIVE ↔ v.14 NOT A JOKE.** ⭐ **And compare Muṭaffifīn 83:13 — "just old legends." Same dismissal, answered here.**

## §5 — Two Plots (v.15–17)

**14. يَكِيدُونَ كَيْدًا ... وَأَكِيدُ كَيْدًا — *yakīdūna kaydan… wa akīdu kaydā*** · v.15–16
- **Root feeling:** ⭐ the "**plotting / scheming / contriving**" family — *kāda* = **to plot, to devise a stratagem**; ***kayd*** = **a scheme, a plan devised against someone**. ⭐ **Note: *kayd* isn't always negative in Arabic — it can mean skilful planning; in Sūrat Yūsuf, Allah's arranging is called *kidnā li-Yūsuf*.**
- **Here it means:** v.15 — "**Indeed, they are plotting a plot.**" v.16 — "**And I am planning a plan.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **One of the most quietly terrifying pairs of verses in the Qur'an — and the balance is exact.**
  
  ⭐ **Look at the perfect symmetry:**
  
  | **v.15** | **v.16** |
  |---|---|
  | *innahum **yakīdūna** * | *wa **akīdu** * |
  | ***kaydā*** | ***kaydā*** |
  | **they** (plural) | ⭐ **I** (singular) |
  
  ⭐⭐ **Identical verb. Identical cognate accusative. And the numbers are the whole point: ⭐ **THEY (many) vs. I (One).**
  
  ⭐ **The Qur'an doesn't say "their plot will fail" or "I will punish them."** ⭐ **It simply places the two plots side by side, in matching grammar, and lets you do the arithmetic.** ⭐ **The confidence is in the SYMMETRY.**
  
  ⭐ **And note the shift to the FIRST PERSON SINGULAR — *akīdu*, "I."*** ⭐ **Most of these surahs use "We" (*naḥnu*) — the plural of majesty.** ⭐ **Here it's ⭐ **"I."** ⭐ **Intensely personal, and directly matched against their "they."**
  
  ⭐ **The cognate accusative (*kaydan*) appears TWICE** — ⭐ **you own this construction from Nāziʿāt 79:1–4** (*gharqan, nashṭan, sabḥan, sabqan*), **ʿAbasa 80:25–26** (*ṣabban, shaqqan*), **Inshiqāq 84:6** (*kadḥan*). ⭐ **Seventh and eighth instances.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐ ***kāda* is a HOLLOW verb*** (weak middle ي): *kāda → yakīdu → akīdu*. ⭐ **Same hollow pattern as *yaḥūr* (84:14), *dhāqa/yadhūqu* (78:24), *ẓanna*… ⭐ **Watch how the weak middle letter behaves.**
- **Why this word:** using ⭐ **THE SAME WORD for both plots** — rather than a nobler word for His — is what makes the verse land: ⭐ **the matching makes the mismatch obvious.**
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ **Same verb, same noun, both verses. THEY plot… ⭐ **I** plot.** ⭐ **The Qur'an doesn't argue — it just puts the two side by side.** ⭐ **And note: "I," not "We."**

**15. فَمَهِّلِ ... أَمْهِلْهُمْ رُوَيْدًا — *fa-mahhili… amhilhum ruwaydā*** · v.17
- **Root feelings:** *mahhil / amhil* — ⭐ the "**granting respite / giving time / going gently**" family — *mahala* = to give time; ***muhlah*** = ⭐ **a grace period, an extension** (modern Arabic: *muhlah* = a deadline extension!); ***tamahhal*** = **take your time, slow down**. *ruwaydan* — ⭐ **a DIMINUTIVE** — from *rūd* (gentleness/slowness): ⭐ **"just a LITTLE while," "gently, softly."**
- **Here it means:** "**So give the disbelievers respite — grant them respite, [just] a LITTLE.**"
- **Word magic:** ⭐⭐⭐ **The final verse, and it's a masterpiece of restraint.**
  
  ⭐ **After "I am planning a plan" (v.16), you expect a threat.** ⭐ **Instead: ⭐ **"give them time."**
  
  ⭐ **And the command is given TWICE, with two different verb forms:** ⭐ ***mahhil* (Form II) and *amhil* (Form IV)*** — ⭐ **both meaning "grant respite," repeated for emphasis.** ⭐ **The repetition is a calming gesture: *let it go, let it go.***
  
  ⭐⭐ **Then the last word: *ruwaydan* — a DIMINUTIVE.*** ⭐ **Arabic diminutives (*taṣghīr*) shrink a word to make it small, or gentle, or affectionate.** ⭐ **So the surah's final word means ⭐ **"a teeny little while."**
  
  ⭐ **Read the whole effect:** ⭐ **"They're plotting. I'm planning. Give them time — just a tiny bit."** ⭐ **The confidence is TOTAL, and the tone is almost casual.** ⭐ **You only speak this calmly when the outcome isn't in doubt.**
  
  ⭐ **And it's the perfect close for the surah that began with a *ṭāriq* — a night-knocker.** ⭐ **Something is coming. It knocks. Wait a little.**
  
  ⭐ **Compare the endings you've collected:** ⭐ **Takwīr 81:29 (*Rabb al-ʿālamīn*), Infiṭār 82:19 (*lillāh*), Inshiqāq 84:25 (*ghayru mamnūn*), Burūj 85:22 (*lawḥin maḥfūẓ*), ⭐ **Ṭāriq 86:17 (*ruwaydan*).** ⭐ **Not one of them ends on a threat.**
- **Grammar bite:** ⭐⭐ ***ruwaydan* is a DIMINUTIVE (اسم تصغير)*** — ⭐ **Arabic shrinks words to signal smallness, gentleness, or endearment** (*kutayyib* = a booklet, *bunayy* = "my dear little son"). ⭐ **A rare and precious example in the Qur'an.** ⭐ **Note also the two imperative forms: *mahhil* (Form II) and *amhil* (Form IV) — ⭐ **both causative-ish, both "give respite."**
- **Why this word:** ending on a **diminutive** — ⭐ **the smallest possible word** — after the biggest possible claim, is the ultimate expression of composure.
- 🧠 **Never forget:** ⭐⭐⭐ ***muhlah* = a GRACE PERIOD / deadline extension.*** ⭐ **After "I am planning a plan," the answer is: "give them time — just a LITTLE."** ⭐⭐ ***ruwaydan* is a DIMINUTIVE — "a teeny while."** ⭐ **Total confidence, delivered in the smallest word available.**

## ⚠️ Never-Forget Traps

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **رَجْعِهِ (v.8) and الرَّجْعِ (v.11)** — **same root, three verses apart.** ⚠️ **The claim and its evidence share a word. Miss this and you miss the surah.**
2. ⭐⭐ **إِنْ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ لَّمَّا (v.4)** — ⚠️ ***in* = "NOT" (not "if"); *lammā* = "EXCEPT."*** **"There is NO soul EXCEPT that it has a guardian."** ⚠️ **The single easiest misreading in the surah.**
3. ⭐⭐ **مِمَّ (v.5)** — **من + ما**, alif dropped. ⭐ **Third instance:** *ʿamma* (78:1), *fīma* (79:43), *mimma* (86:5).
4. ⭐⭐ **رُوَيْدًا (v.17)** — a **DIMINUTIVE** ("a teeny little while"). ⚠️ **Translating it as just "a while" loses the tone entirely.**
5. ⭐⭐ **تُبْلَى (v.9)** — the secrets are **TESTED**, ⚠️ **not merely "revealed."** Same test-family as *nabtalīhi* (76:2) and *fatanū* (85:10).
6. ⭐ **الطَّارِقِ (v.1)** — ***ṭaraqa* = TO KNOCK** (*miṭraqah* = a hammer, *ṭarīq* = a beaten road). ⚠️ **Not just "night star."**
7. ⭐ **الثَّاقِبُ (v.3)** — ***mithqab* = a DRILL.*** ⚠️ **The star PIERCES; it doesn't merely shine.**
8. ⭐ **فَصْلٌ (v.13)** — a **maṣdar used as an adjective** = more emphatic than a normal adjective. And ⭐ ***fāṣilah*** is the name for a **Quranic verse-ending**.
9. ⭐ **أَكِيدُ (v.16)** — ⚠️ **first person SINGULAR ("I"), not the usual "We."** The shift is deliberate.
10. ⭐ **مَهِّلِ / أَمْهِلْ (v.17)** — **two different forms** (II and IV) of the same command, repeated for emphasis. ⚠️ **Not a typo.**
